# Phosphorated Carbohydrate Solution

**Type:** Active Ingredient
**Drug Class:** Hyperosmolar carbohydrate/phosphoric-acid antiemetic
**Verification Status:** AI-compiled from primary sources, unverified — not yet reviewed by a pharmacist/physician

## Indication
OTC: relief of upset stomach/nausea associated with overindulgence in food or drink (per label — a mild, self-limited-nausea indication, not general antiemetic use for more serious causes of nausea/vomiting).

## Mechanism
A hyperosmolar solution of dextrose, fructose, and phosphoric acid. Thought to act directly on the GI tract wall to reduce smooth-muscle contraction and delay gastric emptying — a local/mechanical mechanism, distinct from the CNS-acting antihistamine mechanisms of [[dimenhydrinate]]/[[meclizine]] or bismuth subsalicylate's antisecretory/anti-inflammatory mechanism ([[bismuth-subsalicylate]]).

## Dosing (per Emetrol FDA label)
Adults: 1-2 tablespoons (15-30mL) at first sign of nausea, repeated every 15 minutes until nausea subsides, not exceeding 5 doses without medical advice. Each 5mL contains 3.74g total sugar (dextrose/fructose) and 21.5mg phosphoric acid.

## Efficacy Evidence — a genuinely unusual regulatory history
**The FDA's final antiemetic OTC rulemaking classified phosphorated carbohydrate solution as Category I for safety but Category III for antiemetic effectiveness** — meaning the FDA's own review process found insufficient evidence of effectiveness for this ingredient, and it consequently **did not achieve full OTC monograph status** on efficacy grounds. Despite this, the product remains commercially marketed (a status this database did not fully resolve — possibly via a different regulatory pathway or grandfathered marketing status; flagged as a gap). Separately, a 1989 manufacturer-sponsored study (Adria Laboratories) reported effectiveness for upset-stomach-associated nausea relief, and more recent secondary sourcing describes a 60-70% nausea-score reduction within 30-45 minutes of the first two doses in patients with acute viral gastroenteritis or dietary nausea — but **neither Emetrol nor comparable products are recommended as first-line agents for nausea under current clinical guidelines**, which prefer evidence-based antiemetics instead, per general reference sourcing reviewed in this pass.

## Contraindications
**Hereditary fructose intolerance (HFI)** — a specific, real, genetic-disease-related contraindication given the product's fructose content, distinct from any other GI-category ingredient in this database.

## Key Safety Data
