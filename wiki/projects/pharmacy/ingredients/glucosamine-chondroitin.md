## Indication
Marketed for knee osteoarthritis pain/joint health.

## Mechanism
Glucosamine and chondroitin sulfate are proposed to be structural building blocks of joint cartilage, theoretically supporting cartilage maintenance/repair — this page's source does not detail the specific biochemical mechanism, focusing instead on the clinical trial outcome data.

## Efficacy Evidence — Clegg et al. 2006 GAIT trial (PMID 16495392, NIH-funded landmark trial)
1,583 patients with symptomatic knee osteoarthritis, randomized to glucosamine 1500mg/day, chondroitin sulfate 1200mg/day, both combined, celecoxib 200mg/day (active/positive control), or placebo, for 24 weeks. Primary outcome: 20% pain reduction from baseline.

- **Placebo response:** 60.1%
- **Glucosamine:** +3.9 points over placebo (p=0.30) — **not significant**
- **Chondroitin:** +5.3 points (p=0.17) — **not significant**
- **Combined:** +6.5 points (p=0.09) — **not significant** (borderline)
- **Celecoxib (positive control):** +10.0 points (p=0.008) — **significant**, confirming the trial design was sensitive enough to detect a real drug effect when present — this rules out "the trial just wasn't powered to see anything" as an excuse for the null glucosamine/chondroitin result
- **Subgroup: moderate-to-severe baseline pain specifically:** combined therapy response 79.2% vs. placebo 54.3% (p=0.002) — a **significant** benefit in this specific subgroup, though the authors themselves flag this as an exploratory (not pre-specified primary) finding
- Adverse events: mild, infrequent, evenly distributed across all arms — no safety signal

## Authors' Conclusion
Glucosamine and chondroitin sulfate, alone or combined, did NOT effectively reduce pain in the overall knee osteoarthritis population. The combination MAY be effective specifically in patients with moderate-to-severe knee pain — an exploratory finding, not a confirmed primary result, and one that later literature has not uniformly replicated (per general context — not independently re-verified with a specific follow-up study in this pass).

**This vault preserves the honest nuance:** this is a well-known negative trial for the general population, with one real but statistically exploratory silver lining for a specific pain-severity subgroup — not a flat "it doesn't work" or "it works" claim.

## Contraindications

## Key Safety Data
Adverse events in GAIT were mild, infrequent, and evenly distributed across all 5 treatment arms (including placebo) — no specific safety signal was identified for either glucosamine or chondroitin in this trial.
