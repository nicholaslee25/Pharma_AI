# Homeopathic Arnica (Arnica montana, topical)

**Type:** Active Ingredient
**Drug Class:** Homeopathic preparation (HPUS), low-dilution herbal-derived — a meaningfully different evidence category from ultra-high-dilution homeopathics like [[oscillococcinum]] (200CK, no original molecule plausibly remaining), since arnica's typical OTC dilutions (1X-3X) retain measurable original-plant material
**Verification Status:** AI-compiled from primary sources, unverified — not yet reviewed by a pharmacist/physician
**Evidence Grade:** Mixed and dose-dependent — a real, if methodologically flawed, trial base exists for higher-concentration/fresh-plant arnica preparations (positive open-label knee-osteoarthritis trial), but a dedicated systematic review found **efficacy at 10% concentration and below is NOT supported by available evidence** — and the homeopathic dilutions actually sold in this category (1X-3X HPUS) are well within or below that unsupported range
**Last updated:** 2026-08-27
**Sources:** WebSearch (DARE/NCBI systematic review "Systematic Review on the Efficacy of Topical Arnica montana for the Treatment of Pain, Swelling and Bruises," Knuesel et al. Arnica montana gel open multicenter osteoarthritis trial, Advances in Therapy), DailyMed brand labels (Boiron Arnicare Gel, MagniLife Arnica Pain Relief Gel)

---

## Indication
FDA-unevaluated homeopathic OTC use: temporary relief of muscle pain, stiffness, and bruise-related symptoms (pain, swelling, discoloration) from minor injuries, overexertion, or falls; also marketed for minor arthritis/joint pain.

## Mechanism
Arnica montana (a flowering plant) contains sesquiterpene lactones (notably helenalin) with documented anti-inflammatory and analgesic activity in laboratory/pharmacological studies at sufficient concentration. Homeopathic OTC products use HPUS-labeled decimal dilutions (1X = 1:10, 3X = 1:1,000) of arnica tincture — far less diluted than classical high-potency homeopathy (e.g., 30C, 200C), meaning some actual plant-derived material remains, unlike [[oscillococcinum]]'s essentially molecule-free 200CK dilution. This places topical arnica in a genuinely different, more defensible evidence category than duck-extract-style ultra-dilution homeopathics — but as detailed below, the dilutions actually used in most OTC products may still be too low for the concentration-dependent effect the trial literature suggests is needed.

## Efficacy Evidence — genuinely dose-dependent, a real nuance worth holding onto
- **Systematic review (DARE/NCBI-indexed, Brito/Knipschild et al.):** identified 11 controlled trials (5 in volunteers, 6 in patients) with high heterogeneity in health conditions, arnica dosage, co-interventions, and outcome measures, plus important methodological flaws in many studies. **Explicit conclusion: "the efficacy of Arnica in doses of 10% and below is not supported by the available evidence"** — the review calls for further research at higher concentrations rather than validating the low-dose products actually sold OTC.
- **Knuesel et al. open multicenter trial (Adv Ther)** — Arnica montana FRESH PLANT gel (a markedly more concentrated preparation than the 1X-3X HPUS dilutions in this database's tracked brands), applied twice daily in 79 patients (26 men, 53 women) with mild-to-moderate knee osteoarthritis: significant reductions in WOMAC total score, pain, stiffness, and function subscales at 3 and 6 weeks. Local adverse-event rate 7.6% (one allergic reaction); 87% rated tolerability "good"/"fairly good," 76% willing to reuse. **This trial's positive result used a fresh-plant (higher-concentration) preparation, not the diluted HPUS homeopathic gels this category actually tracks** — a real, easily-missed distinction.
- **Net finding:** the trial evidence that exists for arnica is concentration-dependent and points toward higher-concentration/fresh-plant preparations, not the low-dilution (1X-3X) homeopathic gels actually sold as "Arnicare" and "MagniLife Arnica" in this database — a meaningful gap between the marketing positioning ("arnica works, per studies") and what the cited studies actually tested.

## Key Safety Data
Generally well-tolerated at the low OTC dilutions used; the fresh-plant-gel trial's 7.6% local adverse-event rate (mostly non-allergic irritation, one allergic reaction) is the most concrete safety data located, though it concerns a more concentrated preparation than the homeopathic products tracked here. Undiluted/high-concentration arnica extract is a known contact allergen and can cause more significant irritation — not directly applicable to the dilute HPUS products but relevant context for the plant generally.

## Contraindications
Per Boiron Arnicare Gel's own label: external use only; avoid eyes, mucous membranes, damaged/broken skin; do not wrap tightly or apply heat/ice immediately before or after use; discontinue if condition persists beyond 3 days or worsens. Standard homeopathic-product FDA disclaimer applies: "The FDA has not evaluated this homeopathic product for safety or efficacy, and is not aware of scientific evidence supporting homeopathy's effectiveness."

## Special Population Flags
No specific pregnancy/lactation/pediatric data was located in this pass beyond the general external-use warnings above.

## Formulation Caveat
This category's two arnica brands use different dilutions — Boiron Arnicare Gel is Arnica montana **1X HPUS 7%** (less dilute); MagniLife Arnica Pain Relief Gel is **3X HPUS** (more dilute, roughly 1/100th the concentration of 1X by the decimal-dilution convention) — a real, confirmed potency difference between the two leading brands in this category, neither of which reaches the concentration used in the one positive-outcome trial located (a fresh-plant, non-diluted preparation).
