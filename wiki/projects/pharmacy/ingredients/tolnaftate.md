# Tolnaftate

**Type:** Active Ingredient
**Drug Class:** Thiocarbamate antifungal (topical OTC)
**Verification Status:** AI-compiled from primary sources, unverified — not yet reviewed by a pharmacist/physician
**Evidence Grade:** Moderate — an older, well-established agent with real historical efficacy data (93% mycologic eradication in small studies, roughly equivalent to clotrimazole in older head-to-head comparisons), but the literature is aging and the drug has been largely superseded in dermatology practice by newer agents ([[terbinafine]], [[butenafine]]) with stronger comparative trial performance
**Last updated:** 2026-08-27
**Sources:** WebSearch (ScienceDirect/Taylor & Francis/Drugs.com monograph summaries), DailyMed (Tinactin labels)

## Indication
FDA OTC monograph: mild-to-moderate athlete's foot (tinea pedis), jock itch (tinea cruris), ringworm (tinea corporis), and tinea versicolor. **Explicitly NOT effective for onychomycosis (nail fungus)** — the drug does not penetrate the nail plate, the same limitation already documented for [[clotrimazole]].

## Mechanism
Inhibits squalene epoxidase — the same target enzyme as [[terbinafine]] (allylamine) and [[butenafine]] (benzylamine) — but tolnaftate is chemically a distinct thiocarbamate. Blocking this enzyme disrupts fungal ergosterol synthesis and cell-membrane formation, increasing membrane permeability and causing fungal cell death. High specificity to fungal cell membranes accounts for its generally low toxicity to human cells.

## Efficacy Evidence
Older but real clinical data: tolnaftate 1% cream applied twice daily for 3-4 weeks was found **as effective as clotrimazole 1% cream** for tinea pedis, tinea corporis, and tinea cruris caused by *T. rubrum*, *T. mentagrophytes*, and *E. floccosum* in comparative studies. Small studies report **93% mycologic eradication** and **53-70% clinical cure**, with cure rates generally cited in the 60-90% range against dermatophytes. **Important context, per this database's newer comparative-trial findings:** [[butenafine]] achieved substantially higher clinical/mycological cure rates than clotrimazole in a modern RCT (e.g., 61.7% vs. 17.6% mycological cure at 1 week), and clinical dermatology practice has moved away from tolnaftate specifically because newer agents (terbinafine, naftifine, butenafine) outperform it — tolnaftate's own literature base is older and has not been updated with comparably rigorous modern trials in the sources located.

## Key Safety Data
Low toxicity to human cells given its fungal-membrane-specific mechanism; no dedicated systemic-toxicity data was located in this pass beyond standard topical-antifungal local irritation precautions.

## Contraindications
Not for use on nails (ineffective due to lack of nail-plate penetration) or scalp infections. External use only. Not for children under 2 without doctor guidance/supervision per Tinactin's own label.

## Special Population Flags
Per Tinactin's own DailyMed label: not for children under 2 years except under a doctor's advice and supervision.

## Formulation Caveat
Tolnaftate's clinical use has genuinely declined since the introduction of more potent antifungal compounds (terbinafine, naftifine) — this is stated plainly in the pharmacology literature reviewed, not merely inferred from this database's own comparative trial findings, though both point the same direction.
