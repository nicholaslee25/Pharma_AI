# Permethrin / Pyrethrin (Head Lice Pediculicides)

**Type:** Active Ingredient (class)
**Drug Class:** Pyrethroid (permethrin, synthetic) / pyrethrin (natural, combined with piperonyl butoxide synergist) — neurotoxic insecticides, FDA OTC monograph pediculicides
**Verification Status:** AI-compiled from primary sources, unverified — not yet reviewed by a pharmacist/physician
**Evidence Grade:** High for original clinical-trial efficacy (97-99% cure in classic trials) — but this is now sharply undercut by a separate, extremely well-documented real-world finding: near-universal genetic resistance (kdr mutations) in actual US head lice populations, meaning classic trial cure rates likely no longer reflect real-world performance

## Indication
FDA OTC monograph: treatment of head lice (Pediculus humanus capitis) infestation, including nits (eggs).

## Mechanism
Both act on insect nerve cell membranes, interrupting signal transmission between nerve and muscle, causing paralysis and death (lice die by asphyxiation, unable to breathe once paralyzed). **Permethrin** (synthetic pyrethroid) has residual activity, remaining on treated hair for at least 10 days post-application. **Pyrethrin** (natural, always combined with the synergist piperonyl butoxide to block the enzyme that would otherwise rapidly metabolize it) lacks this residual activity, which is part of why RID's label requires two applications 7 days apart rather than Nix's single-application regimen.

## Efficacy Evidence — classic trials vs. real-world resistance, a critical distinction
**Classic trial data:** permethrin achieved a **97-99% cure rate** in patients tested 14 days after a single application in the original pivotal trials, and outperformed lindane (97% vs. 67-85% eradication) in a Cochrane-indexed comparison. Pyrethrin+piperonyl-butoxide was found similarly effective to permethrin in older synergized-pyrethrin trials.

**Real-world resistance — a major, extremely well-documented and quantified finding:** genetic (knockdown resistance, "kdr") mutations conferring permethrin/pyrethroid resistance have become **nearly universal in US head lice populations**:
- A US surveillance study (138 collection sites across 48 states, July 2013-May 2015) found an overall mean resistance-allele frequency of **98.3% ± 10%**; **132 of 138 sites (95.6%) showed a mean resistance-allele frequency of 100%**; 42 of 48 states (88%) had a mean of 100%; only a single site showed no mutations at all.
- A separate historical study (291 samples, 12 states) found the kdr mutation frequency rising from **84.4% in 1999 to 99.6% in 2009** — a clear, accelerating trend, not a static baseline.
- Resistance-allele frequency did not vary meaningfully by the size of the human population the lice were collected from — i.e., this is a broad, geographically uniform phenomenon, not confined to specific regions or demographics.

**Net finding, stated plainly:** the 97-99% cure rates from classic trials almost certainly no longer reflect real-world outcomes against the actual, now near-universally resistant US head lice population — the labeled products have not changed, but their target organism has evolved past them. This is directly analogous to antibiotic resistance and should be weighted heavily against these two brands' combined 84% share of this category's pharmacist recommendations.

**Direct supporting evidence — an alternative mechanism outperforming permethrin head-to-head:** a randomized trial (Pharmacology & Pharmacy, 2013; n=42) found **1% sodium chloride spray significantly more effective than 1% permethrin crème rinse** for treating active head lice infestation — a real, controlled trial result plausibly explained by the resistance data above, since sodium chloride's desiccation mechanism (see [[Licefreee!]]) is not subject to kdr-mutation resistance at all.

## Key Safety Data
Generally well-tolerated topically; standard pediculicide precautions (avoid eyes, external use only). No major new safety signal was located in this pass beyond the efficacy/resistance finding above, which is an effectiveness issue, not a safety one.

## Contraindications
External use only; avoid eyes and mucous membranes; not all products approved for use under a specified minimum age (varies by brand — see individual DailyMed labels).

## Special Population Flags
No specific new pregnancy/pediatric data was located in this pass beyond standard OTC pediculicide age-restriction conventions (varies by brand).

## Formulation Caveat
"Permethrin" and "pyrethrin" are chemically related (both pyrethroid-class, acting on the same sodium-channel target) but distinct molecules — permethrin is synthetic with residual activity; pyrethrin is natural, always synergized with piperonyl butoxide, and requires a second application. Both share the same kdr-mutation resistance mechanism and are therefore both affected by the resistance finding above.

## Pharmacy Times Context (recommendation share — NOT efficacy)
**Lice Treatments** (Topicals, 870,378/month category volume): Nix 71%, RID 13%, Licefreee! 7%, Lice Shield 6%, Vamousse 3%. These are pharmacist recommendation shares, not cure rates. **A genuine, well-documented tension exists here:** the two brands with 84% combined recommendation share (Nix, RID) use a mechanism now facing near-universal real-world genetic resistance, while a smaller-share brand (Licefreee!, 7%) uses a mechanism (sodium chloride) shown in a direct RCT to outperform permethrin, and another (Vamousse, 3%) uses a different resistance-proof physical mechanism (dimethicone) — see [[Licefreee!]] and [[Vamousse]].

## Brands Using This Ingredient (in this database — formulation not individually verified per SKU)
**[[Nix]]** confirmed 2026-08-27 — permethrin 1%, single-application cream rinse, category leader (71%).
**[[RID]]** confirmed 2026-08-27 — pyrethrins 0.33% + piperonyl butoxide 4%, two applications 7 days apart (13%).

**Dimeticone (Vamousse's mechanism) direct comparison:** a 100-patient RCT found a dimeticone-based pediculicide (10-minute application) achieved **98% cure at day 1** vs. **84% for 0.5% permethrin (45-minute application)** — both reached 100% by day 10 after a second application. In vitro, 10 minutes of dimeticone exposure rendered all treated lice non-viable, with 96.8% ovicidal activity. The authors concluded dimeticone's short application time and resistance-independent physical mechanism are key advantages "in the case of resistance" — directly relevant given the kdr-mutation data above.
