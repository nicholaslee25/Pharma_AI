# Topical Diclofenac

**Type:** Active Ingredient
**Drug Class:** Topical NSAID
**Verification Status:** AI-compiled from primary sources, unverified — not yet reviewed by a pharmacist/physician
**Evidence Grade:** Moderate-to-high specifically for osteoarthritis pain — the central advantage over oral NSAIDs is a much smaller systemic exposure and correspondingly lower systemic (cardiovascular/GI/renal/hepatic) risk, at the cost of a slower onset (up to 7 days) and a narrower indication (arthritis only, not acute strains/sprains)

## Indication
OTC (1% gel, since a 2020 Rx-to-OTC switch): temporary relief of arthritis pain in the hand, wrist, elbow, foot, ankle, or knee. **Not indicated for strains, sprains, bruises, or sports injuries** — this is an arthritis-specific product, not a general topical NSAID for acute injury (a distinction the label makes explicitly). Prescription-only 3% gel formulation exists for actinic keratosis (a distinct dermatological indication, different concentration and use case, not the OTC arthritis product).

## Mechanism
Same COX-1/COX-2 inhibition pathway as oral diclofenac, reducing prostanoid synthesis; modestly COX-2-selective (~4x greater COX-2 vs. COX-1 inhibition in vitro) — though far less selective than dedicated COX-2 inhibitors. Applied topically, it reaches local joint tissue with much lower systemic absorption than the oral form, which is the central rationale for its improved systemic safety profile.

## Dosing (per Voltaren Arthritis Pain FDA OTC label)
Use **4 times per day, every day, for up to 21 days**. Upper body areas (hand, wrist, elbow): 2.25 inches (2g) per application. Lower body areas (foot, ankle, knee): 4.5 inches (4g) per application. **Do not use on more than 2 body areas simultaneously.** StatPearls' broader dosing reference: 1–2% gel, up to 16g/day for a single joint or up to 32g/day for multiple joints (polyarthritis) — consistent with, but framed slightly differently than, the consumer label's per-application-site instructions.

**Important onset note: may take up to 7 days to work — not for immediate relief**, a meaningfully different use pattern than an oral NSAID taken PRN.

## Pharmacokinetics
Topical application produces much lower systemic drug exposure than oral diclofenac — explicitly described in StatPearls as carrying "less risk of systemic exposure." (For contrast: oral diclofenac is rapidly absorbed, binds plasma albumin, and concentrates in synovial fluid at the inflammation site — none of which applies at the same magnitude to the topical route.)

## Contraindications
Allergy to pain relievers/fever reducers; NSAID/aspirin hypersensitivity; use for strains/sprains/bruises/sports injuries (not the approved indication); use before/after heart surgery; application to more than 2 body areas at once; application to eyes, nose, or mouth. StatPearls additionally notes class-wide absolute contraindications relevant mainly to oral use (cardiovascular history, severe renal insufficiency, active GI bleeding/ulcers) and that diclofenac carries an FDA boxed warning for cardiovascular risk and appears on the Beers List for elderly avoidance — these are strongest for the oral/systemic form, and StatPearls does not separately caveat how much less they apply to the topical gel specifically.

## Key Safety Data
- **Topical-specific:** mild-to-moderate skin irritation at the application site is the main reported local effect, with minimal systemic exposure concern
- **Label warnings (carried over from the class, even at low systemic exposure):** liver damage risk if overused or combined with other diclofenac products; stomach bleeding risk (higher if age 60+, ulcer history, anticoagulant/steroid use, ≥3 alcoholic drinks/day); heart attack/heart failure/stroke risk (NSAID class warning)
- **Systemic NSAID risks generally** (cardiovascular, GI, renal, hepatic, hematologic) are all much less likely with topical use given limited absorption — this is the entire rationale for choosing this product over an oral NSAID when the pain is localized to a joint

## Drug Interactions
Not itemized topical-gel-specifically in the sources reviewed; StatPearls notes oral diclofenac is sometimes combined with gastric acid reducers (PPIs) or misoprostol for GI protection — not directly relevant to topical use, included here as class context only.

## Special Population Flags
- **Elderly:** Beers Criteria caution applies mainly to oral/systemic NSAID use; topical diclofenac's lower systemic exposure is specifically why it's often considered a safer alternative for older patients who need arthritis pain relief but carry cardiovascular/GI/renal risk factors
- **Pregnancy:** StatPearls flags misoprostol-COMBINATION diclofenac products as contraindicated in pregnancy — this is a misoprostol-specific concern, not necessarily applicable to the plain topical gel, though independent confirmation for topical diclofenac specifically in pregnancy was not found in this pass
- **Renal insufficiency, H. pylori history, cardiovascular disease:** StatPearls' general precautions for diclofenac as a drug — again, most directly relevant to oral/systemic exposure levels rather than the topical gel

## Overdose / Toxicity
Not addressed with topical-gel-specific overdose data in the sources reviewed — the label's dosing/body-area limits (4g per lower-body application, max 2 body areas) function as the practical ceiling on topical exposure.
