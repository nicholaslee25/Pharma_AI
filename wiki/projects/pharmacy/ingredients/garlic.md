## Indication
Marketed for blood pressure support, cholesterol management, and immune support — all three have real surrogate-marker evidence (see Efficacy Evidence), though not confirmed hard-outcome evidence.

## Mechanism
Garlic's organosulfur compounds have proposed cardiovascular-protective and immunomodulatory properties; specific molecular mechanism for blood pressure/lipid effects not detailed in the source reviewed. Immunomodulation involves increased macrophage activity, natural killer cell activity, and T/B cell production.

## Efficacy Evidence — Ried 2016 meta-analysis and review (PMID 26764326)

**Blood pressure** (updated meta-analysis, 20 trials, 970 participants, MEDLINE search 1955–Dec 2013):
- Overall: systolic BP decreased **5.1±2.2 mmHg** (p<0.001); diastolic BP decreased **2.5±1.6 mmHg** (p<0.002) vs. placebo
- **Hypertensive subgroup specifically** (baseline ≥140/90): larger effect — SBP decreased **8.7±2.2 mmHg** (p<0.001); DBP decreased **6.1±1.3 mmHg** (p<0.001) — garlic's blood-pressure effect is meaningfully larger in people who are actually hypertensive, not a flat effect across all users

**Cholesterol** (citing a separate 39-RCT, 2,300-adult meta-analysis, ≥2-week minimum treatment):
- Total and LDL cholesterol reduced by **~10%** — but specifically when taken for **>2 months**, and specifically in people with **slightly elevated baseline cholesterol** (>200 mg/dL) — both duration and baseline level are real preconditions for this effect, not a universal claim

**Immunity:**
- Clinical trials cited show garlic significantly reduces the number, duration, and severity of upper respiratory infections (specific trial numbers not detailed in this review's own abstract — flagged as a less quantified claim than the BP/cholesterol data)

**Important caveat (the review author's own words):** these are surrogate marker improvements (blood pressure, cholesterol) — "future long-term trials are needed to elucidate the effect of garlic on cardiovascular morbidity and mortality" (i.e., actual heart attacks, strokes, deaths). This vault preserves that caveat explicitly rather than implying surrogate-marker improvement equals proven disease prevention.

## Key Safety Data
Garlic supplements were described as "highly tolerated" in the review. Per broader context (not from this specific abstract): side effects reported by roughly one-third of participants across garlic trials generally, described as mild — burping, flatulence, reflux, mainly in the first few weeks of use.

## Drug Interactions
Not addressed in the source reviewed in this pass. (Garlic has historically been discussed elsewhere as having potential antiplatelet/bleeding-risk interactions, but this was not independently confirmed via a primary source here and is therefore not asserted as established.)

## Special Population Flags
- **Hypertensive individuals specifically:** larger blood-pressure benefit than the general population
- **People with slightly elevated cholesterol (>200 mg/dL):** the specific population where the cholesterol-lowering effect was demonstrated, taken for >2 months
