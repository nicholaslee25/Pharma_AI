# Topical Counterirritants (Menthol / Camphor / Methyl Salicylate)

**Type:** Active Ingredient (class)
**Drug Class:** Topical counterirritant / external analgesic
**Verification Status:** AI-compiled from primary sources, unverified — not yet reviewed by a pharmacist/physician
**Evidence Grade:** Moderate-to-good specifically for Salonpas Pain Relief Patch's own 10%/3% formulation (a real FDA NDA-approved product backed by its own N=208 pivotal RCT); weaker/unestablished for the higher-concentration competitor formulations (Icy Hot, BENGAY, Tiger Balm) marketed under the OTC monograph system without brand-specific trials at their actual concentrations. Methyl salicylate carries a genuine, FDA-labeled NSAID-class systemic risk profile despite being "just a rub," and the class as a whole carries an FDA-reviewed rare-but-serious burn risk at higher concentrations.
**Last updated:** 2026-08-26
**Sources:** raw/primary-sources/topical-counterirritants/fda-safety-and-efficacy-sources.md

## Indication
Temporary relief of minor aches and pains of muscles and joints associated with simple backache, arthritis, strains, sprains, bruises, and cramps. Marketed heavily as sports/exercise recovery and general musculoskeletal pain products (Thermal Relief Products category).

## Mechanism
Counterirritants (menthol, camphor, methyl salicylate) work by activating and then desensitizing epidermal nociceptors — menthol specifically activates cold-sensing TRPM8 receptors, producing a cooling sensation that competes with/masks underlying pain signaling (same mechanism already documented on [[Biofreeze]]'s brand page). This is fundamentally a **sensory distraction/masking mechanism**, not an anti-inflammatory or disease-modifying one — mechanistically distinct from both lidocaine's sodium-channel blockade ([[topical-anesthetics]]) and capsaicin's TRPV1/substance-P depletion mechanism ([[capsaicin]]).

## Efficacy Evidence

**Higashi et al. 2010 (PMID 20171409, Clinical Therapeutics 32(1):34-43)** — randomized, double-blind, parallel-group, placebo-controlled, multicenter RCT sponsored by Hisamitsu Pharmaceutical: a patch containing **methyl salicylate 10% + l-menthol 3%** vs. placebo patch, adults with clinically diagnosed mild-to-moderate muscle strain, single 8-hour application, N=208 per secondary reporting.
- **Result: 40% additional pain relief with the active patch vs. placebo; 58% of patients rated satisfaction as good/very good/excellent** (figures per the manufacturer's own clinical-studies summary page, not independently re-derived from the full paywalled paper in this pass — cited as company-reported figures from a real peer-reviewed study, not independently re-verified statistics).
- **This is NOT a generic ingredient-class trial — it is the actual pivotal trial for a specific commercial product: Salonpas Pain Relief Patch**, which used this exact 10% methyl salicylate + 3% menthol formulation. This trial directly supported Salonpas Pain Relief Patch's **FDA New Drug Application (NDA) approval in February 2008** — making it, per Hisamitsu's own claim, the first OTC topical analgesic patch approved via the full NDA pathway rather than the OTC monograph system most other products in this database (including every other Thermal Relief Products brand) are marketed under. This is a genuinely higher regulatory bar than typical OTC status, worth flagging as a real positive differentiator for this specific product. See `drugs/Salonpas.md`.
- No comparably designed RCT specific to camphor alone, or to the higher-concentration formulations several OTHER brands in this category actually sell (e.g., BENGAY Ultra Strength's 30% methyl salicylate/10% menthol, or Icy Hot's 29-30%/7.6-10% — roughly 3x the Salonpas-tested concentrations), was located in this pass. The Higashi trial's positive result applies specifically to Salonpas's own 10%/3% formulation, not to the higher-concentration competitors.

## Key Safety Data — TWO real, quantified risk findings

**1. FDA Drug Safety Communication, September 13, 2012 — rare but serious burns:**
- Reviewed 43 cases (1969-2011) of burns linked to OTC topical products containing menthol, methyl salicylate, and/or capsaicin.
- Burns ranged from first- to third-degree chemical burns; some occurred after a SINGLE application, with severe burning/blistering within 24 hours; some required hospitalization.
- **Named brands in FDA/press coverage of this communication: Bengay, Capzasin, Flexall, Icy Hot, Mentholatum** — representative examples, not necessarily an exhaustive list.
- **Quantified risk factor: a majority of the more severe burns involved products with menthol >3% or methyl salicylate >10%.** Several brands in this exact database category exceed BOTH thresholds by a wide margin: Icy Hot Balm (menthol 7.6-10%, methyl salicylate 29-30%), BENGAY Ultra Strength (menthol 10%, methyl salicylate 30%), Tiger Balm Active Muscle Rub (menthol 5%, methyl salicylate 15%), Salonpas Deep Relieving gel (menthol 10%, methyl salicylate 15%).

**2. Methyl salicylate is a salicylate — real NSAID-class systemic risk, not just a topical irritant:**
Per Salonpas's own FDA label: methyl salicylate carries the SAME stomach-bleeding warning class as oral NSAIDs (risk factors: age 60+, ulcer/bleeding history, blood thinner/steroid/other-NSAID use), an aspirin/NSAID-allergy contraindication, and — most strikingly — an explicit pregnancy warning: **"It is especially important not to use methyl salicylate at 20 weeks or later in pregnancy unless definitely directed to do so by a doctor,"** directly analogous to oral NSAID pregnancy warnings about premature ductus arteriosus closure. This is a genuinely surprising, real systemic-absorption-driven risk that many consumers likely do not associate with "a muscle rub."

## Contraindications
Do not apply to wounds, damaged/broken skin, rashes, or the face; do not use with a heating pad (compounds the burn risk above via increased absorption/local heat); do not bandage tightly; aspirin/salicylate-allergic individuals should consult a doctor before using methyl salicylate-containing products specifically (per Tiger Balm and Salonpas labels).

## Drug Interactions
Methyl salicylate: additive bleeding risk with blood thinners, oral NSAIDs, or corticosteroids (same interaction class as oral aspirin/NSAIDs, per the Salonpas label).

## Special Population Flags
- **Pregnancy:** methyl salicylate specifically should not be used at 20+ weeks gestation without explicit doctor direction — a real, labeled warning, not a generic caution.
- **Age 60+:** elevated stomach-bleeding risk from methyl salicylate's systemic NSAID-class absorption.
- **Children:** most SKUs restricted to age 12+ (BENGAY, Tiger Balm) or require doctor consultation under that age; not to be confused with the separate, lower age threshold (2+) common to menthol-only anti-itch products like [[Nature's Willow Bug Bite Balm]].
- **Anyone using a heating pad concurrently:** explicitly contraindicated across every brand reviewed — a real, specific, repeated warning tied directly to the FDA's 2012 burn-risk finding.

## Overdose / Toxicity
Swallowing concentrated methyl salicylate products is a genuine pediatric poison-control concern (methyl salicylate is highly concentrated salicylate — small volumes can be equivalent to a large aspirin overdose); every label reviewed carries a "contact Poison Control immediately if swallowed" warning. Topical overuse/misuse (heating pads, tight bandaging, high-concentration products) is the FDA-documented mechanism for the serious burn cases above.

## Formulation Caveat
This category is genuinely heterogeneous: brands mix and match menthol, camphor, and methyl salicylate in different combinations and wildly different concentrations (menthol alone at 1% in mild anti-itch products vs. 10% in BENGAY Ultra Strength; methyl salicylate absent from some SKUs entirely, present up to 30% in others). A consumer should check the SPECIFIC product's concentrations, not assume "menthol rub" implies a fixed low-risk product — the FDA's own burn-risk data is concentration-dependent, not a blanket ingredient-class judgment.

## Pharmacy Times Context (recommendation share — NOT efficacy)
Per this database's own subcategory file (Pharmacy Times 2026 OTC Guide, ~1,700 pharmacists surveyed): **Thermal Relief Products** (First Aid, 1,665,756/month volume): Biofreeze 23%, Icy Hot 20%, Salonpas 19%, ThermaCare 14%, BENGAY 12%, Beyond BodiHeat 8%, Tiger Balm 4%. These are pharmacist recommendation shares, not efficacy or safety-incident rates. Note: ThermaCare and Beyond BodiHeat (22% combined share) are heat-generating DEVICES with no counterirritant chemical ingredient at all — see their own brand pages.

## Brands Using This Ingredient Class (in this database — formulation not individually verified per SKU beyond what's noted)
**Biofreeze** (23%) — menthol only (4-10.5% depending on format), no methyl salicylate or camphor; see `drugs/Biofreeze.md`.
**Icy Hot** (20%) — menthol 7.6-10% + methyl salicylate 29-30% depending on SKU (Balm/Stick/Cream), exceeding BOTH FDA burn-risk thresholds; see `drugs/Icy Hot.md`.
**Salonpas** (19%) — spans FIVE+ distinct formulations: menthol 3% + methyl salicylate 10% (Pain Relief patch — the FDA NDA-approved, RCT-backed formulation described above; also Arthritis Pain patch, Pain Relieving Jet spray); menthol 10% + methyl salicylate 15% (Deep Relieving gel); camphor + menthol + methyl salicylate (plain "Salonpas" patch); and a separate capsaicin + menthol "Pain Relieving HOT" SKU with no methyl salicylate at all — see `drugs/Salonpas.md` and [[capsaicin]].
**BENGAY** (12%) — camphor 4% + menthol 10% + methyl salicylate 30% (Ultra Strength Non-Greasy), the highest methyl salicylate concentration confirmed in this category, well above both FDA burn-risk thresholds; explicitly named in the FDA's 2012 burn-risk communication; see `drugs/BENGAY.md`.
**Tiger Balm** (4%) — spans multiple SKUs: camphor + menthol only (Neck and Shoulder Rub); methyl salicylate 15% + menthol 5% + camphor 3% (Active Muscle Rub, Muscle Rub, Pain Relieving Muscle Spray); methyl salicylate + menthol only, no camphor (Active Muscle Gel); camphor + menthol (Ultra Strength) — see `drugs/Tiger Balm.md`.
**ThermaCare** (14%) — NOT a counterirritant chemical product at all; an FDA Class II heat-generating DEVICE (iron-oxidation/exothermic chemistry), with no menthol/camphor/methyl salicylate. Genuinely strong positive RCT evidence (Nadler 2002, PMID 12004166) shows it outperformed ibuprofen and acetaminophen for acute low back pain; a real FDA recall (6 lots, 2017-2018, overheating/leaking wraps) also exists. See `drugs/ThermaCare.md`.
**Beyond BodiHeat** (8%) — also a heat-generating DEVICE (activated carbon + iron powder + vermiculite), no counterirritant chemical ingredient — see `drugs/Beyond BodiHeat.md`.
