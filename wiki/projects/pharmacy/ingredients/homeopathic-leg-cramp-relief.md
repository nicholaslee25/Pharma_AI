## Indication
Marketed for temporary relief of leg/foot/calf cramps (Hyland's Leg Cramps) or the "urge to jerk or move legs"/restless sensations (Hyland's Restful Legs, MagniLife). None of these products are FDA-evaluated or FDA-approved to treat Restless Leg Syndrome as a diagnosed condition — Hyland's Restful Legs' own label explicitly states it is **not intended to treat Restless Leg Syndrome**, a real, brand-name-vs-label tension worth flagging given the product's name directly evokes RLS.

## The Quinine Finding — Real, Significant, and Brand-Specific
**Hyland's Leg Cramps contains Cinchona Officinalis 3X HPUS** (cinchona bark, the natural source of quinine) — the LEAST diluted ingredient in its formula (a 3X dilution is 1 part in 1,000, several orders of magnitude less dilute than the 6X/12X/30X dilutions used for the formula's other ingredients and for the other two brands on this page). This matters because **prescription quinine sulfate (Qualaquin) carries an FDA boxed warning and safety communication (issued 2010) against off-label use for leg cramps specifically**, due to serious, sometimes fatal reactions: severe thrombocytopenia, hemolytic uremic syndrome/thrombotic thrombocytopenic purpura, and cardiac arrhythmias (QT prolongation). At a true 3X dilution, the quinine-molecule content per dose remains far below any pharmacologically active threshold established for these reactions — so this is NOT the same exposure as therapeutic-dose quinine sulfate. The product's own label reflects awareness of this ancestry: it directs users to "ask a doctor before use if you are sensitive to quinine." This is presented here as a real, sourced tension (a genuine historical safety signal tied to the same underlying molecule, at a dose understood to be far below pharmacological activity) rather than a claim that the homeopathic product itself is dangerous — the two facts (real FDA quinine-sulfate warning; real homeopathic-dose dilution of the same source material) are both true and both worth a user's awareness.

## Brand-by-Brand Formula Comparison
- **Hyland's Leg Cramps:** Cinchona Officinalis 3X (quinine source — see above), Viscum Album 3X, Gnaphalium Polycephalum 3X, Rhus Toxicodendron 6X, Aconitum Napellus 6X, Ledum Palustre 6X, Magnesia Phosphorica 6X.
- **Hyland's Restful Legs:** Arsenicum Album 12X, Lycopodium 6X, Pulsatilla 6X, Rhus Toxicodendron 6X, Sulphur 6X, Zincum Metallicum 12X — **no quinine/Cinchona at all**, a genuinely different formula from its sibling "Leg Cramps" product despite the shared "Hyland's" brand.
- **MagniLife Relaxing Legs (tablet form):** Magnesia Phosphorica, Cuprum Metallicum, Colocynthis, Phytolacca Decandra, Veratrum Album, Causticum, Stannum Metallicum, Rhus Toxicodendron (each at 12X/30X) — explicitly marketed "Quinine-Free" on-label, a direct competitive contrast to Hyland's Leg Cramps.

## Key Safety Data
Per standard homeopathic-dilution safety logic already established elsewhere in this database: dilutions at 6X and above are generally considered to contain negligible-to-no measurable amount of the original substance (beyond Avogadro's-number dilution limits for anything above ~12C/24X). The 3X Cinchona ingredient in Hyland's Leg Cramps is the one exception worth naming specifically in this category, per the Quinine Finding above.

## Special Population Flags
Per Hyland's own label: ask a doctor before use if sensitive to quinine (Leg Cramps product specifically). No other population-specific flags located in this pass beyond the standard "ask a doctor if pregnant/nursing" homeopathic-product convention.
