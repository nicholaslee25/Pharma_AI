# Bismuth Subsalicylate

**Type:** Active Ingredient
**Drug Class:** Antidiarrheal / antacid with antimicrobial activity (salicylate + bismuth salt)
**Verification Status:** AI-compiled from primary sources, unverified — not yet reviewed by a pharmacist/physician
**Evidence Grade:** Moderate — established OTC symptomatic treatment for diarrhea/dyspepsia; off-label H. pylori quadruple-therapy role has notably strong reported success rates
**Last updated:** 2026-08-25
**Sources:** raw/primary-sources/bismuth-subsalicylate/statpearls-bismuth-subsalicylate.md, raw/primary-sources/bismuth-subsalicylate/dailymed-pepto-bismol.md

## Indication
FDA-approved OTC: diarrhea, dyspepsia, heartburn, indigestion, nausea, upset stomach (gas, belching, fullness), traveler's diarrhea. Off-label: H. pylori eradication (as part of quadruple therapy), traveler's diarrhea prevention, microscopic colitis in patients unable to tolerate budesonide.

## Mechanism
Hydrolyzes in the stomach into bismuth and salicylic acid. Salicylate is almost completely absorbed systemically; bismuth remains largely in the GI tract, forming bactericidal salts that block bacterial adhesion to mucosal cells, reducing inflammation and promoting ulcer healing. Antidiarrheal effect via three pathways: reduced prostaglandin formation (COX inhibition), stimulated fluid/sodium/chloride reabsorption, and inhibited intestinal secretions. Against H. pylori specifically: blocks bacterial adhesion to gastric epithelium and inhibits phospholipase, protease, and urease activity.

## Dosing (per Pepto-Bismol FDA OTC label and StatPearls)

| Population | Dose |
|---|---|
| Adults/children ≥12y | 30mL (525mg) every ½h, or 60mL every 1h PRN; max 8 doses (240mL)/24h |
| Children 9–12y (off-label/StatPearls) | 262mg q30min–1h |
| Children 6–9y | 175mg q30min–1h |
| Children 3–6y | 87mg q30min–1h |
| H. pylori quadruple therapy (off-label, adult) | 300mg four times daily x10–14 days |
| Traveler's diarrhea prophylaxis | 524mg four times daily with meals/bedtime |

**Off-label H. pylori success rate:** up to 90% with quadruple therapy (StatPearls). **Traveler's diarrhea prophylactic efficacy:** 62%, vs. 80% for antimicrobial prophylaxis — bismuth subsalicylate is a real but less-effective prevention option than antibiotics specifically for this use.

## Pharmacokinetics
Salicylate component almost completely absorbed into the bloodstream; bismuth salt minimally absorbed, remaining mostly in the GI tract. Therapeutic effect typically apparent within 4 hours.

## Contraindications
Concurrent anticoagulants, sulfinpyrazone, probenecid, methotrexate, or other high-salicylate medications; GI ulceration or hemophilia; bleeding problems or bloody/black stools; **flu-like symptoms in children/adolescents (Reye syndrome risk — this is a salicylate, same rule as aspirin)**; salicylate sensitivity/aspirin allergy; concurrent doxycycline for malaria prophylaxis (reduces doxycycline absorption).

## Key Safety Data
- Common: nausea, bitter taste, diarrhea, dark/black stools and tongue discoloration (both temporary and harmless — worth reassuring patients of this explicitly, since it can look alarming)
- **Bismuth toxicity** (from extended overconsumption, uncommon but serious): blackening of tongue/teeth, fatigue, mood changes, deteriorating mental status; potentially fatal neurotoxicity in rare cases; hearing loss, tinnitus, muscle spasms/weakness, anxiety, confusion, depression, headaches, slurred speech
- Since this is a salicylate, it carries the same Reye syndrome risk profile as aspirin in children/teens with viral illness

## Drug Interactions
Avoid concurrent use with: anticoagulants, sulfinpyrazone, probenecid, methotrexate, other high-salicylate-content products, doxycycline (malaria prevention — reduced absorption).

## Special Population Flags
- **Pediatric:** Reye syndrome risk with concurrent viral illness (same as aspirin); limited dosing data <12y per StatPearls, other antidiarrheal options may be preferable
- **Pregnancy:** CDC recommends AGAINST use for traveler's diarrhea prevention/treatment specifically
- **Breastfeeding:** alternative treatments preferred, given potential infant salicylate absorption
- **Renal impairment:** use lowest effective dose for shortest feasible period — salicylates may worsen renal function

## Overdose / Toxicity
**Salicylate toxicity threshold:** generally >150 mg/kg salicylates (or >6.5g aspirin equivalent) — a real risk with repeated/excessive OTC dosing given the drug's own salicylate content. **Symptoms:** impaired cognition, tremors, lethargy, somnolence, insomnia, delirium, myoclonus, seizures, depressed mood, anxiety. **Bismuth encephalopathy:** rare, from prolonged overconsumption. **Management:** immediate discontinuation, activated charcoal if within 2h of ingestion, supportive IV fluids, salicylate level monitoring every 1–2h until declining, urine alkalinization if concentration >30 mg/dL, hemodialysis for severe cases with altered mental status/metabolic acidosis. No specific antidote exists; routine therapeutic use does not require salicylate level monitoring.

## Pharmacy Times Context (recommendation share — NOT efficacy)
Pepto-Bismol recommendation shares (Pharmacy Times 2026 OTC Guide, ~1,700 pharmacists surveyed): Upset Stomach Remedies 55%, Nausea Remedies 16%, Antidiarrheals 10%. These are pharmacist recommendation shares, not response/cure rates — note this is a multi-category product given bismuth subsalicylate's broad symptom coverage (diarrhea + upset stomach + nausea all in one ingredient).
