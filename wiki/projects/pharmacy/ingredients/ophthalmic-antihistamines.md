# Ophthalmic Antihistamines (Ketotifen / Olopatadine / Alcaftadine)

**Type:** Active Ingredient (class)
**Drug Class:** Dual-acting H1-receptor antagonist + mast-cell stabilizer, topical ophthalmic
**Verification Status:** AI-compiled from primary sources, unverified — not yet reviewed by a pharmacist/physician
**Evidence Grade:** Moderate-to-high — multiple RCTs and a meta-analysis support efficacy for allergic-conjunctivitis itch relief; head-to-head differences between the three drugs in this class are small

## Indication
Temporary relief of itchy eyes due to allergic conjunctivitis (pollen, ragweed, grass, animal hair/dander). All three drugs in this class treat the itch symptom, not the underlying allergic response systemically.

## Mechanism
All three are "dual-acting" agents: direct H1-histamine receptor antagonism (blocks the itch signal immediately) plus mast-cell stabilization (reduces future histamine release from conjunctival mast cells with continued use) — mechanistically distinct from the vasoconstrictor decongestants covered on [[ophthalmic-decongestants]], which reduce visible redness via blood-vessel constriction rather than blocking the allergic itch response.
- **Ketotifen fumarate** (Zaditor, Alaway): 0.025%, dosed twice daily (every 8-12h)
- **Olopatadine HCl** (Pataday): 0.2% (Once Daily Relief, ~16h duration) or 0.7% (Extra Strength, 24h duration), dosed once daily
- **Alcaftadine** (Lastacaft): 0.25%, dosed once daily — the newest to switch Rx-to-OTC (December 2021)

## Dosing
- Zaditor/Alaway: adults and children 3+, 1 drop per affected eye twice daily, every 8-12h, no more than twice/day
- Pataday: adults and children 2+, 1 drop per affected eye once daily (frequency per specific SKU's approved dosing)
- Lastacaft: 1 drop per affected eye once daily

## Efficacy Evidence

**Olopatadine vs. ketotifen meta-analysis (PMC10258714, 2023)** — systematic review of 7 RCTs (PubMed, Embase, Web of Science, EBSCO, Cochrane) comparing olopatadine vs. ketotifen in allergic conjunctivitis:
- Olopatadine showed **significantly lower hyperemia (redness)** than ketotifen: MD −0.77 (95% CI −1.24 to −0.30, p=0.001)
- **No significant difference between the two drugs for itching, tearing, or papillae** — the two are comparably effective for the itch symptom itself, the primary reason patients use this drug class
- Both are described in the underlying literature as "new generation multiple-action topical antiallergic agents" recommended as first-line allergic-conjunctivitis treatment

**Bottom line:** within this class, expect comparable itch relief regardless of which specific drug is chosen; olopatadine may have a modest edge for visible redness reduction specifically, per this one meta-analysis. No head-to-head trial of alcaftadine (the newest OTC entrant) against ketotifen or olopatadine was located in this pass — its efficacy data comes from its own placebo-controlled approval trials rather than a direct within-class comparison.

## Pharmacokinetics
Topical ophthalmic application; minimal systemic absorption expected for all three at labeled OTC concentrations. Not independently detailed in the sources reviewed in this pass.

## Contraindications
Hypersensitivity to the specific drug or its excipients (e.g., benzalkonium chloride, a preservative present in Alaway and likely others). Not for use while wearing contact lenses if the eye is red (general class caution on most ophthalmic antihistamine labels).

## Key Safety Data
No serious adverse events identified across the sources reviewed; mild, transient eye irritation/stinging on instillation is the most commonly reported effect for this drug class generally. "If swallowed, get medical help or contact Poison Control" is standard labeling language across Zaditor/Alaway (pediatric-ingestion caution, not evidence of a specific poisoning event).

## Drug Interactions
Not established with ophthalmic-specific data in the sources reviewed in this pass — minimal systemic absorption at labeled doses makes systemic drug interactions unlikely but not independently confirmed here.

## Special Population Flags
**Pediatric:** Zaditor/Alaway approved 3+; Pataday approved 2+ for its itch indication. Lastacaft's OTC-approved pediatric age cutoff was not independently confirmed in this pass.

## Overdose / Toxicity
Not established with ophthalmic-specific overdose data in the sources reviewed; standard "if swallowed, contact Poison Control" caution applies given these are liquid drops accessible to children.
