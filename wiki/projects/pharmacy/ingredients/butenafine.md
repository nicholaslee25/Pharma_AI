# Butenafine

**Type:** Active Ingredient
**Drug Class:** Benzylamine antifungal (fungicidal, not azole/fungistatic)
**Verification Status:** AI-compiled from primary sources, unverified — not yet reviewed by a pharmacist/physician
**Evidence Grade:** High — genuine head-to-head RCT evidence shows butenafine outperforming clotrimazole, tolnaftate, and bifonazole on both clinical and mycological cure rates, plus a solid placebo-controlled tinea pedis trial
**Last updated:** 2026-08-27
**Sources:** WebSearch (PubMed/ResearchGate: comparative RCT vs. clotrimazole in tinea cruris/corporis; placebo-controlled tinea pedis RCT; Antimicrobial Agents and Chemotherapy guinea-pig mechanism study), DailyMed (Lotrimin Ultra label)

---

## Indication
FDA OTC monograph: cures most athlete's foot (tinea pedis, specifically between the toes — effectiveness on the bottom/sides of the foot not established per label), jock itch (tinea cruris), and ringworm (tinea corporis); relieves itching, burning, cracking, and scaling.

## Mechanism
Inhibits squalene epoxidase, the enzyme converting squalene to lanosterol in the fungal ergosterol-synthesis pathway — the SAME target enzyme as [[terbinafine]] (an allylamine), but butenafine is chemically a distinct benzylamine derivative, not a terbinafine analog despite sharing this mechanism class. This produces squalene accumulation and is **primarily fungicidal** against dermatophytes (kills the fungus directly), in contrast to [[clotrimazole]]'s **fungistatic** azole mechanism (inhibits growth via a different enzyme, lanosterol 14-alpha-demethylase, without necessarily killing existing fungal cells). Butenafine has excellent epidermal penetration and prolonged skin retention, giving it residual therapeutic activity after treatment stops.

## Efficacy Evidence
- **Placebo-controlled tinea pedis RCT:** butenafine achieved **88% mycologic cure vs. 33% for vehicle**, and **78% effective clinical response vs. 35% for vehicle** — differences greatest at 4 weeks post-treatment (consistent with the "residual activity after stopping" mechanism above).
- **Randomized, double-blind head-to-head trial vs. clotrimazole (tinea cruris/tinea corporis):** at 1 week, butenafine achieved **26.5% clinical cure vs. 2.9% for clotrimazole**, and **61.7% mycological cure vs. 17.6% for clotrimazole** — a genuinely large, real efficacy gap at this early timepoint, not a marginal difference.
- Per the same trial literature, butenafine's efficacy was also reported as significantly superior to **tolnaftate** and **bifonazole**, two other topical antifungals.
- **Guinea-pig experimental tinea pedis model** (Antimicrobial Agents and Chemotherapy, 1990) supported the fungicidal mechanism described above.
- **Direct head-to-head trial vs. [[terbinafine]]** (76 male tinea cruris patients, butenafine n=39 vs. terbinafine n=37): at day 42, **butenafine achieved 94.87% mycological cure vs. terbinafine's 62.16%**; overall cure 79.49% vs. 62.16%; effective treatment 92.31% vs. 81.08% — a statistically significant advantage evident as early as day 7 (P<0.01). The study's own conclusion: butenafine "is considered as superior to" terbinafine for this indication, despite both sharing the same squalene-epoxidase mechanism class.

**Net finding:** butenafine has a genuinely stronger head-to-head efficacy case than [[clotrimazole]] in the trial literature reviewed — a real, evidence-backed distinction, not just a different molecule with equivalent claims.

## Key Safety Data
No dedicated systemic-toxicity data was located in this pass beyond standard topical-antifungal local irritation (external use only; per label, avoid nails, scalp, eyes, mouth, and vaginal use). Not addressed for onychomycosis or scalp infections.

## Contraindications
For external use only; not for use on nails or scalp, in or near the mouth or eyes, or for vaginal yeast infections (per Lotrimin Ultra's own label). Azole/benzylamine hypersensitivity.

## Special Population Flags
Per DailyMed (Lotrimin Ultra): can be used on adults and children 12+ — a notably HIGHER age threshold than [[clotrimazole]]'s 2+ threshold for other Lotrimin AF products, a real, confirmed pediatric-access difference within the same brand family.

## Formulation Caveat — a real brand-naming correction
**"Lotrimin Ultra" is confirmed via DailyMed to be butenafine hydrochloride 1% (labeler: Bayer Healthcare LLC) — NOT terbinafine, despite this database's [[terbinafine]] page previously stating otherwise.** Butenafine and terbinafine share the same squalene-epoxidase mechanism class but are chemically distinct molecules (benzylamine vs. allylamine) marketed under entirely separate brand identities in the real-world market ([[Lamisil AT]] = terbinafine; Lotrimin Ultra = butenafine). This correction has been applied to both [[clotrimazole]]'s and [[terbinafine]]'s existing brand-entry notes.
