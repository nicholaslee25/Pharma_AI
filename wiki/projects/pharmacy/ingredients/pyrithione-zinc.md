# Pyrithione Zinc (Zinc Pyrithione)

**Type:** Active Ingredient
**Drug Class:** Antifungal/antiseborrheic (zinc ionophore)
**Verification Status:** AI-compiled from primary sources, unverified — not yet reviewed by a pharmacist/physician
**Evidence Grade:** Moderate-to-high — multiple RCTs support efficacy at the standard 1% OTC concentration, though most located trials are industry-sponsored cosmetic-science studies (formulation/vehicle comparisons) rather than large independent Cochrane-style reviews.
**Last updated:** 2026-08-27
**Sources:** DailyMed (Head & Shoulders, Dove DermaCare Scalp, DHS Zinc, Selsun Blue Itchy Dry Scalp labels), PubMed/search-summarized efficacy literature (Pierard-Franchimont et al. 2002 comparative RCT, PMID 12476017; Schwartz et al. 2013 vehicle-comparison RCT, PMID 23614401). Brand-page research 2026-08-27.

## Indication
Dandruff (flaking, itching) and its recurrence prevention — confirmed across every brand-specific label pulled this pass with near-identical Drug Facts wording ("helps prevent recurrence of flaking and itching associated with dandruff" / "seborrheic dermatitis").

## Mechanism
Pyrithione acts as a zinc ionophore, facilitating cellular zinc uptake; the resulting mechanism has antifungal activity against *Malassezia* (*Pityrosporum*) yeast species implicated in dandruff, alongside some direct antimicrobial and cytostatic effects on skin cells. Mechanistically related to, but a genuinely different chemical entity from, [[ketoconazole]] (a true imidazole antifungal) and [[selenium-sulfide]] (which acts via a different antifungal/cytostatic pathway) — all three target the same underlying *Malassezia* biology from different angles.

## Dosing
Standard OTC concentration confirmed across most brands: **1%**. One brand in this database's category (DHS Zinc Shampoo) is confirmed at **2%** — double the standard concentration — see Discrepancy Flag below. Directions are uniform: wet hair, massage into scalp/hair, rinse; use at least twice weekly or as directed.

## Efficacy Evidence

**Head-to-head vs. ketoconazole (Pierard-Franchimont et al. 2002, multicenter RCT, PMID 12476017):** zinc pyrithione 1% achieved **67% improvement** in total dandruff severity score at week 4, vs. **73% for ketoconazole 2%** — a real but modest gap favoring ketoconazole at its higher (Rx-only in the US) concentration; see [[ketoconazole]] for the important caveat that this is not a fair 1%-vs-1% comparison.

**Vehicle/formulation comparison (Schwartz et al. 2013, RCT, PMID 23614401):** a "potentiated" zinc-pyrithione formulation outperformed a "dual active" (zinc pyrithione/climbazole combination) product across all four weekly measurement points for both flaking and epidermal-histamine endpoints — search-summarized finding indicating formulation/delivery-vehicle differences can meaningfully affect real-world efficacy even at the same nominal active-ingredient concentration, a genuinely under-appreciated point for a consumer comparing "1% zinc pyrithione" products across brands.

**In vitro antifungal activity:** zinc pyrithione and selenium disulfide both show good in vitro efficacy against *Pityrosporum*, per search-summarized general literature — consistent with, though less rigorously trial-quantified than, the head-to-head ketoconazole comparison above.

## Contraindications
Known hypersensitivity to pyrithione zinc or shampoo components. No other contraindications specifically detailed across the labels checked this pass.

## Key Safety Data
- Uniformly mild safety profile across every label checked this pass: for external use only, avoid eye contact (rinse with water if contact occurs), stop use and consult a doctor if condition worsens or doesn't improve
- No systemic toxicity, hepatotoxicity, or serious-adverse-event signal was located in the sources gathered this pass, in contrast to some of ketoconazole's oral-formulation-associated (not topical) safety history
- No specific data on long-term daily-use safety beyond the standard "twice weekly or as directed" usage pattern was located

## Brands Using This Ingredient (in this database — formulation not individually verified per SKU beyond what's noted)
**Head & Shoulders** (brand-page research 2026-08-27) — confirmed pyrithione zinc 1% across its major SKU lines (Classic Clean, Deep Moisture, Itchy Scalp Care, Dry Scalp Care, Scalp Restore, Bare Minimal Ingredients), P&G. **Dove DermaCare Scalp** (brand-page research 2026-08-27) — confirmed pyrithione zinc 1.0% across its line (Pure Daily Care, Invigorating Mint, Dryness and Itch Relief, Men+Care Dandruff Defense/Deep Clean) — the **same active ingredient and concentration as Head & Shoulders**, a genuine finding that two of this category's top-five brands by recommendation share (28% + 6% = 34% combined) are pharmacologically identical, marketed under entirely different brand identities (P&G's Head & Shoulders vs. Unilever's Dove). **DHS Zinc Shampoo** (brand-page research 2026-08-27) — confirmed zinc pyrithione **2%**, double the concentration of Head & Shoulders/Dove — see Discrepancy Flag below. **Selsun Blue** (brand-page research 2026-08-27) — the brand's flagship line is [[selenium-sulfide]]-based, but a specific "Selsun Blue Itchy Dry Scalp" SKU is confirmed pyrithione-zinc-based instead — a within-brand dual-active split, see `drugs/Selsun Blue.md`.

## Discrepancy Flag (real, current — 2026-08-27): DHS Zinc Shampoo's 2x concentration
**DHS Zinc Shampoo is confirmed at zinc pyrithione 2%** — double the 1% concentration confirmed for Head & Shoulders and Dove DermaCare Scalp, the two much-higher-recommendation-share brands using the identical active ingredient. No head-to-head trial comparing 1% vs. 2% zinc pyrithione specifically was located in this pass (unlike [[ketoconazole]], where a 1%-vs-2% dose-response trial was located) — so whether DHS's 2x concentration translates to meaningfully better real-world efficacy, or simply a different manufacturer's formulation choice within the same FDA-monograph allowable range, is a genuine open question this pass could not resolve either way.
