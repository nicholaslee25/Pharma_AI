# Antacids (Calcium Carbonate / Magnesium Hydroxide / Aluminum Hydroxide)

**Type:** Active Ingredient (class)
**Drug Class:** Antacids (calcium carbonate, aluminum hydroxide, magnesium hydroxide/trisilicate, sodium bicarbonate)
**Verification Status:** AI-compiled from primary sources, unverified — not yet reviewed by a pharmacist/physician
**Evidence Grade:** Established for rapid symptomatic relief of mild intermittent heartburn/GERD; specific salt determines the tradeoff profile (aluminum → constipation, magnesium → diarrhea) and each has distinct toxicity risks with chronic/excessive use
**Last updated:** 2026-08-25
**Sources:** raw/primary-sources/antacids/statpearls-antacids.md, raw/primary-sources/antacids/dailymed-tums.md

**Coverage note:** This page uses calcium carbonate (Tums) as the representative agent for FDA-label data, matching this database's highest-recommendation-share brand in this class.

---

## Indication
Relief of mild intermittent GERD-associated heartburn, acid indigestion, sour/upset stomach. Context: North American heartburn prevalence is 18–28%, with 25% of adults reporting daily heartburn (StatPearls) — a large, mostly self-treated population. Other uses across the class: duodenal/gastric ulcers, stress gastritis, non-ulcer dyspepsia, bile-acid diarrhea, biliary reflux, and (for calcium specifically) osteoporosis/phosphate binding in renal failure.

## Mechanism
Neutralize gastric acid and inhibit pepsin (a proteolytic enzyme), relieving pain and pylorospasm and preventing acid-mediated mucosal damage. **Calcium salts** specifically raise gastric/duodenal pH, inhibit pepsin above pH 4, increase lower esophageal sphincter tone, and increase esophageal peristalsis (helping push acid back down). **Aluminum hydroxide** neutralizes acid/inhibits pepsin and also binds GI phosphate (used separately for hyperphosphatemia). Different salts across the class also bind bile acids, promote angiogenesis, and (in some formulations) suppress H. pylori growth.

## Dosing (representative — Tums Regular Strength, calcium carbonate 500mg/200mg elemental calcium)
Adults/children ≥12y: chew 2–4 tablets as symptoms occur. Max 15 tablets/24h (10 tablets/24h if pregnant). Do not use maximum dosage for more than 2 weeks without medical supervision.

**Class-wide dosing (StatPearls):** aluminum hydroxide up to 640mg 5–6x/day (max 3840mg/24h) as antacid; calcium carbonate max 8000mg/day for up to 2 weeks symptomatic relief. Note the calcium carbonate maximum for hyperphosphatemia use (2000mg/day total) is far LOWER than the antacid-use maximum (8000mg/day) — these are genuinely different indication-specific limits, not interchangeable.

## Pharmacokinetics
Not extensively covered with systemic PK data in the sources reviewed — antacids act locally in the stomach, with calcium and aluminum having some systemic absorption relevant to their respective toxicity profiles below.

## Contraindications
Hypersensitivity to any formulation component. Caution required in: renal failure, heart failure, edema, cirrhosis, low-sodium diets, uremia, GI hemorrhage, hyperparathyroidism, renal calculus, achlorhydria.

## Key Safety Data
- **Aluminum hydroxide:** increased toxicity risk in renal failure patients and infants — osteopenia/osteomalacia, microcytic anemia, neurotoxicity, constipation/fecal impaction, nausea/vomiting/abdominal cramps, hypomagnesemia, hypophosphatemia
- **Calcium carbonate:** abdominal pain, anorexia, constipation, acid rebound, nausea, vomiting, flatulence, xerostomia, headache, hypercalcemia, hypophosphatemia, and **milk-alkali syndrome** (a real, named toxicity syndrome from excess calcium+alkali intake — historically associated specifically with heavy antacid use)
- StatPearls explicitly notes adverse effects are prominent in infants and the elderly, and chronic use is not recommended for either population due to safety concerns

## Drug Interactions
- **Acidic drugs (digoxin, chlorpromazine, isoniazid):** antacids impair their absorption, reducing blood levels/therapeutic effect
- **Pseudoephedrine, levodopa:** antacids increase their absorption — possible toxicity from elevated serum levels
- **Tetracycline, fluoroquinolone antibiotics:** magnesium-containing antacids (trisilicate, hydroxide) bind these drugs and impede absorption
- **Sodium bicarbonate specifically:** alters urine pH — inhibits excretion of basic drugs (amphetamines, quinidine) while increasing excretion of acidic drugs (aspirin)
- Tums OTC label: ask a doctor/pharmacist before use if taking any prescription drug, given this broad interaction pattern across the antacid class

## Special Population Flags
- **Pregnancy:** aluminum-containing antacids considered safe, including during labor for aspiration prophylaxis; calcium-containing antacids need caution — avoid excessive use since calcium crosses the placenta; Tums label specifically caps pregnant use at 10 tablets/24h vs. 15 for the general population
- **Breastfeeding:** aluminum-containing antacids not well studied in lactation (aluminum is endogenous to breast milk regardless); calcium-containing antacids considered safe during breastfeeding
- **Infants/elderly:** adverse effects prominent in both groups; chronic use not recommended

## Overdose / Toxicity
Not covered with a dedicated overdose section in the sources reviewed beyond the milk-alkali syndrome and aluminum-toxicity data above — periodic calcium/phosphorus monitoring is suggested for patients on chronic antacid therapy (StatPearls).

## Pharmacy Times Context (recommendation share — NOT efficacy)
