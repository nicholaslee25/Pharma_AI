## Indication
FDA-approved (OTC skin-protectant monograph): temporary protection and relief of minor skin irritation and itching due to rashes, eczema, and poison ivy/oak/sumac. Widely marketed specifically for atopic dermatitis (eczema) maintenance and flare reduction, though the FDA monograph itself is the broader "skin protectant" category rather than an eczema-specific drug approval.

## Mechanism
Ground/processed oat (Avena sativa) kernel, standardized for particle size and starch/protein/lipid content. Contains **avenanthramides** — bioactive polyphenols believed to inhibit NF-kB signaling and suppress pro-inflammatory cytokine release — plus beta-glucans and lipids that form an occlusive, moisture-retaining film on skin. Proposed effects: normalizes skin pH, enhances barrier function, and may reduce *Staphylococcus aureus* skin colonization (a bacterium implicated in eczema flare severity), in addition to its baseline occlusive/moisturizing action.

## Dosing
**Bath treatment (e.g., Aveeno Soothing Bath Treatment):** colloidal oatmeal 100% (packet form), dissolved in bath water, soak 15-20 minutes. **Cream/lotion form (e.g., Aveeno Eczema Therapy Daily Moisturizing):** applied topically as needed; concentration on the specific DailyMed SKU was not independently itemized in this pass, though published trials most commonly used a 1% colloidal-oatmeal cream formulation.

## Efficacy Evidence
- **Lisante et al. 2017 (J Dermatolog Treat 28(7):659-667)** — double-blind, randomized, active-controlled trial, n=90, ages 6 months-18 years, mild-to-moderate atopic dermatitis, 1:1 randomization to a 1% colloidal-oatmeal OTC cream vs. a prescription ceramide-containing barrier repair cream. Primary endpoint (EASI score improvement at week 3): the OTC colloidal-oatmeal cream was **non-inferior** to the prescription comparator.
- **Sub-analysis in Black/African American children** (companion publication, Tandfonline 2023): mean EASI change from baseline at week 3, **-2.4 (SD 1.7) with 1% oatmeal cream vs. -2.1 (SD 2.3) with barrier cream** — comparable effect sizes, reinforcing the non-inferiority finding in a specific population subgroup.
- **Fowler et al. 2012 (J Drugs Dermatol 11(7):804-807):** colloidal-oatmeal formulations as adjunct treatment in atopic dermatitis — supportive of barrier-function and symptom-relief claims, though this pass did not extract this paper's specific effect-size data.
- A separately-cited RCT (specific citation not confirmed to primary-source level in this pass) reported **44% reduction in flare risk** with regular colloidal-oat emollient use — a meaningful preventive-maintenance claim, flagged as sourced from a secondary summary rather than independently verified against the original trial in this pass.
- **No Cochrane systematic review specific to colloidal oatmeal for eczema was located** in this pass — a real evidence-base gap relative to this database's more thoroughly reviewed ingredients (contrast with the 120-RCT Cochrane base underlying [[benzoyl-peroxide]]).

## Contraindications / Warnings
Per FDA skin-protectant monograph conventions: for external use only; discontinue and consult a doctor if condition worsens or symptoms persist beyond 7 days; avoid use over deep or puncture wounds, infections, or in eyes. Oat-derived products carry a recognized (if uncommon) contact-allergy risk, particularly relevant for individuals with known oat or cereal-grain sensitivities — not itemized as a specific warning on the labels reviewed but a recognized class consideration for a plant-derived topical.

## Special Population Flags
**Pediatric:** the Lisante 2017 trial specifically enrolled children as young as 6 months, supporting pediatric use at the 1% concentration tested; Aveeon's own baby-labeled SKUs (Aveeno Baby Eczema Therapy Moisturizing) reflect this population focus. **Oat/cereal allergy:** theoretical cross-reactivity risk not independently quantified in the sources reviewed this pass.
