# Ophthalmic Decongestants/Vasoconstrictors (Naphazoline / Tetrahydrozoline / Brimonidine)

**Type:** Active Ingredient (class)
**Drug Class:** Topical ophthalmic vasoconstrictor (alpha-adrenergic agonist) — often combined with the antihistamine pheniramine maleate
**Verification Status:** AI-compiled from primary sources, unverified — not yet reviewed by a pharmacist/physician
**Evidence Grade:** Established mechanism/long market history for naphazoline/tetrahydrozoline; brimonidine has genuine placebo-controlled trial data specifically supporting its low-rebound claim — a materially different evidence situation within the same broad "redness reliever" category

## Indication
Temporary relief of eye redness due to minor irritation. Combination products (naphazoline + pheniramine) also treat itching from allergic conjunctivitis in one step.

## Mechanism — two genuinely different subclasses within one Pharmacy Times category
- **Naphazoline HCl and tetrahydrozoline HCl** (Naphcon-A, Opcon-A, [[Clear Eyes]], [[Visine]]): **non-selective, mixed alpha-1/alpha-2 adrenergic agonists.** Alpha-1 activation is specifically what drives the well-documented **rebound hyperemia** risk with regular/prolonged use — redness worsens once the drug wears off, prompting more frequent use.
- **Brimonidine tartrate** ([[Lumify]]): a **selective alpha-2 adrenergic agonist** — a real, FDA-approved pharmacological distinction, not just marketing language. Per the mechanism described in the reviewed literature, the rebound/tachyphylaxis problem is specifically attributed to alpha-1 receptor activity, which brimonidine's selectivity avoids.
- **Pheniramine maleate** (paired with naphazoline in Naphcon-A/Opcon-A): a first-generation H1-antihistamine, added to treat itching alongside the naphazoline's redness-reducing action — mechanistically closer to [[ophthalmic-antihistamines]]'s class than to the vasoconstrictor it's paired with, but reviewed here since it never appears as a standalone product in this database.

## Dosing
- Naphcon-A: naphazoline HCl 0.025% + pheniramine maleate 0.3%, 1-2 drops up to 4x daily
- Opcon-A: pheniramine maleate 0.315% + naphazoline HCl 0.02675% (near-identical to Naphcon-A, marginally different concentrations)
- Lumify: brimonidine tartrate 0.025%, dosed per label (onset within 60 seconds per manufacturer trial data, duration up to 8 hours)

## Efficacy Evidence

**Brimonidine tartrate placebo-controlled trials (cited in FDA approval package and tandfonline 2024 real-world safety review):** significantly reduced ocular redness for up to 8 hours; **negligible rebound redness at 1 week post-discontinuation (1.28%)**; clinical trials found **no tachyphylaxis or rebound redness** after month-long use in the trials reviewed. This is a genuinely different, better-characterized safety profile than naphazoline/tetrahydrozoline, for which the rebound-hyperemia risk is well-established but the FDA approval package did not include a comparably designed low-rebound claim.

**Real-world safety review (tandfonline 2024):** post-marketing adverse-event data shows "few reports" of tachyphylaxis/rebound redness for brimonidine — reassuring, but the same review explicitly cautions that **overuse/abuse potential exists with any OTC topical vasoconstrictor**, brimonidine included, and stresses that use should be clinically targeted rather than assumed risk-free.

**Naphazoline/tetrahydrozoline:** no dedicated brand-specific RCT was located in this pass beyond the long-established mechanism and market history; the rebound-hyperemia risk for this subclass is treated in the pharmacology/clinical literature as an established, well-known effect rather than something requiring a fresh trial to confirm — see also the discrepancies already flagged on [[Clear Eyes]] and [[Visine]].

## Contraindications
Narrow-angle glaucoma; use with MAOIs (general vasoconstrictor-class cautions, applies to naphazoline/tetrahydrozoline); known hypersensitivity to the specific active or preservatives. Not for use for more than 72 hours continuously without consulting a doctor (naphazoline/tetrahydrozoline-class labeling).

## Key Safety Data
**Rebound hyperemia** is the single most important safety fact for the naphazoline/tetrahydrozoline subclass — redness can worsen once the drug wears off, potentially prompting a cycle of increasing use. **Brimonidine's selective alpha-2 mechanism is specifically designed to avoid this failure mode**, and its own trial data supports that distinction (1.28% rebound at 1 week vs. the well-known, if not precisely quantified in these sources, rebound pattern for naphazoline/tetrahydrozoline). Masking redness rather than treating an underlying cause is a caution that applies across the whole class regardless of subclass — persistent redness still warrants medical evaluation.

## Drug Interactions
MAOIs increase vasoconstrictor effect/hypertensive risk with naphazoline/tetrahydrozoline-class agents (general alpha-agonist class caution).

## Special Population Flags
Pediatric use: general OTC eye-drop pediatric dosing/age cutoffs apply per each specific brand's label; not independently itemized per drug in the sources reviewed in this pass.

## Overdose / Toxicity
Not established with ophthalmic-specific overdose data in the sources reviewed; standard "keep out of reach of children, contact Poison Control if swallowed" caution applies to all eye-drop products in this class given their liquid, child-accessible form.
