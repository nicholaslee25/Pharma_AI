# Simethicone

**Type:** Active Ingredient
**Drug Class:** Antiflatulent (anti-gas), non-systemic surfactant
**Verification Status:** AI-compiled from primary sources, unverified — not yet reviewed by a pharmacist/physician
**Evidence Grade:** Low-to-moderate — one old small positive trial for adult gas/bloating symptoms; StatPearls explicitly states it is NOT effective for infant colic despite common marketing/use for that purpose
**Last updated:** 2026-08-25
**Sources:** raw/primary-sources/simethicone/statpearls-simethicone.md, raw/primary-sources/simethicone/dailymed-gas-x-max.md

---

## Indication
FDA-approved (1952): flatulence and bloating discomfort from excess GI gas. Not suitable for ileus, small bowel obstruction, or constipation — and symptoms suggesting gallstones or heart disease warrant further medical evaluation rather than simethicone self-treatment.

## Mechanism
Non-systemic surfactant — reduces surface tension of gas bubbles in the GI tract, allowing them to coalesce and disperse for easier passage via flatulence or belching. **Important limitation: does not reduce actual gas production** — it cannot prevent gas increases from lactose intolerance or medication side effects, since it only affects bubble physics, not the underlying gas-generating process.

## Dosing (per Gas-X FDA OTC label and StatPearls)

| Population | Dose | Max |
|---|---|---|
| Adults/children ≥12y | 40–125mg (or per product, e.g. 1–2 Gas-X Max 250mg softgels) after meals/bedtime | 500mg/day (StatPearls); Gas-X label max 2 softgels (500mg)/24h without physician guidance |
| Children 2–12y | 40mg four times daily | 480mg/day |
| Infants/children <2y | 20mg four times daily | 240mg/day — but see Efficacy note below |

## Efficacy Evidence
**Positive:** a 1974 double-blind, placebo-controlled trial (n=41, cited by StatPearls) found simethicone significantly improved gas, heartburn, bloating, and distension vs. placebo at days 5 and 10 — an old, small trial not independently re-verified against the original publication in this pass.

**Negative — infant colic:** StatPearls states plainly that studies found simethicone **NOT effective** for infantile colic — a clinically important negative finding given how frequently simethicone drops are marketed and used for this exact purpose in infants. This vault flags this explicitly rather than letting the "presumed safe in infants" framing imply it also works for colic.

## Pharmacokinetics
Not absorbed orally; excreted entirely in feces. This zero-systemic-absorption profile is the basis for simethicone's essentially benign safety profile.

## Contraindications
Simethicone allergy.

## Key Safety Data
Minimal toxicity profile: occasional mild diarrhea and nausea. No kidney injury, hypertension, or hyperglycemia (mechanistically ruled out by lack of systemic absorption). No oral-administration silicosis cases reported.

## Drug Interactions
Generally minimal. One case report of altered levothyroxine absorption in a child (judged minimal concern). One case report of potential carbamazepine toxicity elevation when combined with simethicone — warrants some caution though the mechanism/frequency is unclear.

## Special Population Flags
- **Pregnancy/breastfeeding:** considered safe given lack of systemic absorption
- **Infants:** presumed safe from a toxicity standpoint, but NOT shown effective for colic specifically (see Efficacy Evidence above) — an important distinction between "safe to give" and "proven to help" for this particular infant use case

## Overdose / Toxicity
Not meaningfully applicable given zero systemic absorption — no serum monitoring is feasible or needed, and no toxicity mechanism exists beyond the mild GI effects noted above.

## Pharmacy Times Context (recommendation share — NOT efficacy)
Gas-X (Pharmacy Times 2026 OTC Guide, ~1,700 pharmacists surveyed): Antiflatulence Products category 72%. This is a pharmacist recommendation share, not a response/cure rate — and per the mixed/limited efficacy evidence above (especially the infant-colic negative finding), a high recommendation share should not be read as strong proof of therapeutic benefit for every use case this ingredient is marketed for.

## Brands Using This Ingredient (in this database — formulation not individually verified per SKU)
Gas-X Maximum Strength Softgels — confirmed simethicone 250mg per its own DailyMed label pulled above. Other Gas-X line products (Extra Strength, Ultra Strength, Ultimate Strength, Total Relief combination with calcium carbonate) exist in the real-world market at varying simethicone doses; individual labels beyond the Maximum Strength softgel were not independently pulled in this pass.

**Cross-category finding (2026-08-27, Pediatrics/Infant Gas Products research): Infants' Mylicon Gas Relief** (category-dominant, 63% share) confirmed simethicone 20mg/0.3mL, labeled "Antigas" for gas discomfort from air swallowing or diet — **its label does NOT mention colic anywhere**, correctly avoiding the colic-efficacy gap this page's Efficacy Evidence section flags (StatPearls: simethicone NOT effective for infant colic despite common marketing for that purpose). See `drugs/Infants' Mylicon Gas Relief.md`. **Little Remedies Gas Relief Drops** (18% share) confirmed at an essentially identical dose (simethicone 20mg/0.3mL) and dosing schedule, also with no colic claim — the category's two highest-share brands (81% combined) both avoid the colic over-claim. See `drugs/Little Remedies Gas Relief Drops.md`.

**Major finding — Hyland's Baby Gas Drops (10% share) is NOT simethicone-based at all.** Confirmed via DailyMed: nine homeopathic dilutions (Argentum Nitricum, Asafoetida, Carbo Vegetabilis, Chamomilla, Cinchona Officinalis, Colocynthis, Lycopodium, Nux Moschata, Sepia), filed in this same category as the two genuine simethicone drugs above. Its own label explicitly claims relief of "colic" — the exact use case this page's Efficacy Evidence section already documents StatPearls stating simethicone is NOT effective for, except this product has no simethicone at all, so the claim rests purely on homeopathic tradition. The label carries the standard FDA homeopathic disclaimer verbatim ("has not been evaluated by the Food and Drug Administration for safety or efficacy"). See `drugs/Hyland's Baby Gas Drops.md` and [[homeopathic-cold-products]] for related general homeopathic evidence framing (though that page is scoped to cold/flu, not gas/colic — a data gap).

**Second homeopathic (non-simethicone) product confirmed — Colic Calm Plus (3% share), with a real 2023 FDA warning letter.** A 12-constituent homeopathic liquid (CalmCo LLC, manufactured by Denison Pharmaceuticals, LLC); the manufacturer's own marketing explicitly confirms it contains NO simethicone. More seriously: the FDA issued a warning letter to CalmCo LLC on 2023-08-09 citing illegal unapproved-drug claims for "Colic Calm"/"Colic Calm +" (e.g., claiming to ease "gas, colic, hiccups and teething"), referencing a related CGMP warning letter issued to manufacturer Denison Pharmaceuticals on 2023-07-26 for failing to adequately investigate batch/specification discrepancies — the FDA explicitly called this combination "particularly concerning from a public health perspective" given the product's infant-directed use. See `drugs/Colic Calm Plus.md`.

**Mommy's Bliss Baby Gas Relief** (3% share) confirmed at the same simethicone 20mg/0.3mL dose as Mylicon/Little Remedies, correctly scoped to "gas" with no colic claim. Markets "real ginger extract" prominently despite ginger being an inactive, not active, ingredient. See `drugs/Mommy's Bliss Baby Gas Relief.md`.
