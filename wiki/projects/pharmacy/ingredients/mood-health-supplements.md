# Mood Health Supplements (St. John's Wort, SAM-e, and Stress-Relief Botanical/Amino Acid Blends)

**Type:** Active Ingredient (product class — covers three distinct, unrelated mechanisms: Hypericum perforatum/St. John's Wort, S-adenosylmethionine/SAM-e, and GABA/L-theanine/ashwagandha/lemon balm "stress relief" combination products)
**Drug Class:** Herbal antidepressant (St. John's Wort); endogenous-compound dietary supplement (SAM-e); amino acid/botanical dietary supplement blends (GABA, L-theanine, ashwagandha, lemon balm) — none FDA-approved as a drug for any mood indication
**Verification Status:** AI-compiled from primary sources, unverified — not yet reviewed by a pharmacist/physician
**Evidence Grade:** Moderate-to-good for St. John's Wort specifically in mild-to-moderate major depression (real, multi-trial Cochrane-level evidence, though genuinely complicated by product-standardization variability and serious drug-interaction risk); Moderate for SAM-e in major depressive disorder (large trial volume, but limited head-to-head comparison with modern SSRIs, and commercial doses often below trial-tested amounts); Low/Very Low for most branded "stress relief" combination products, and Low specifically for the general "occasional stress" (non-clinical-depression/anxiety) indication most OTC products in this category actually market to, versus the clinical-depression populations most of the strongest evidence above was generated in

**Sources:** Cochrane review of Hypericum perforatum for depression (29 trials, 5,489 patients); general SAM-e clinical trial literature (50+ published trials); manufacturer ingredient disclosures for KSM-66 ashwagandha, GABA, L-theanine, lemon balm combination products

## Indication
St. John's Wort: mild-to-moderate major depressive disorder (the population studied in essentially all positive trials). SAM-e: major depressive disorder. GABA/L-theanine/ashwagandha/lemon balm blends: marketed for "occasional stress" in a general (non-clinically-diagnosed) population — a meaningfully different, less rigorously studied use case than clinical depression/anxiety disorder treatment.

## Efficacy Evidence

**St. John's Wort (Hypericum perforatum):** a Cochrane review of 29 trials (5,489 patients) found it more effective than placebo and roughly comparable to standard antidepressants for mild-to-moderate depression, with a **significantly lower discontinuation/dropout rate than SSRIs** (fewer side effects driving people to stop treatment) — a real, meaningful positive finding. However, independent sourcing notes the evidence is genuinely "inconsistent and confusing" across more recent trials (some finding minimal benefit, others finding SSRI-comparable benefit), and **evidence for severe major depression remains insufficient**. A critical, real-world caveat: **hypericum extract composition varies considerably by raw material, extraction process, and solvent — meaning a specific trial-tested extract is not automatically interchangeable with a given marketplace capsule**, a genuine product-standardization concern this database has found recurring across herbal supplements generally.

**SAM-e (S-adenosylmethionine):** more than 50 published clinical trials exist; a synthesis of 13 placebo-controlled and 19 tricyclic-antidepressant-comparison trials (1,400+ patients) found SAM-e superior to placebo and equivalent to older tricyclic antidepressants as monotherapy. However, **only one published trial has compared SAM-e against a modern SSRI, and it found no advantage for either SAM-e or the SSRI (escitalopram) over placebo** — a real, important evidence gap for the comparison most relevant to today's standard-of-care antidepressants. SAM-e has no FDA review of its effectiveness (dietary supplement status).

**GABA/L-theanine/ashwagandha/lemon balm combination products:** ashwagandha (particularly the KSM-66 branded extract) has real, independently-cited clinical research supporting a "healthy response to occasional stress." GABA and L-theanine both have plausible calming mechanisms (GABA is the brain's primary inhibitory neurotransmitter; L-theanine promotes alpha-wave brain activity) with some supporting literature, though this pass did not locate large, definitive RCTs specifically for the combination products as marketed. These products are generally positioned for a milder, non-clinical "occasional stress" use case rather than diagnosed depression or anxiety disorder — a real, important distinction from the clinical populations most of the SJW/SAM-e evidence above was generated in.

## Real, Serious Drug Interaction Risk (St. John's Wort specifically)
**St. John's Wort is a strong inducer of CYP3A4 and P-glycoprotein** (via its hyperforin component activating the pregnane X receptor) — this causes faster metabolism/clearance of a wide range of commonly co-prescribed medications, including (per general pharmacology literature) hormonal contraceptives, anticoagulants (warfarin), immunosuppressants (cyclosporine, tacrolimus), some HIV antiretrovirals, and other antidepressants (serotonin syndrome risk if combined with SSRIs/SNRIs/MAOIs). This is a real, serious, well-documented interaction profile — one of the more consequential herbal-drug interaction risks in this entire database — directly relevant to any of this category's three St. John's Wort brands.

## Contraindications
St. John's Wort: should not be combined with SSRIs/SNRIs/MAOIs (serotonin syndrome risk) or with any of the CYP3A4/P-glycoprotein-substrate medications listed above without physician guidance.

## Key Safety Data
St. John's Wort is generally well-tolerated on its own (the Cochrane review's lower-dropout finding supports this), but its interaction profile is the primary safety concern, not direct toxicity. SAM-e side effects are reported as milder than SSRIs (less nausea, headache, dry mouth, activation).

## Special Population Flags
St. John's Wort: any patient on hormonal contraceptives, anticoagulants, immunosuppressants, other antidepressants, or HIV antiretrovirals should consult a physician before use given the real CYP3A4/P-glycoprotein interaction risk.
