# Meclizine

## Indication
FDA-approved OTC: prevention and treatment of nausea, vomiting, and dizziness associated with motion sickness. Also used (per StatPearls, vertigo-related context) for dizziness associated with vestibular disease, including Meniere's disease.

## Mechanism
Non-selective H1-receptor antagonist with central anticholinergic activity — depresses labyrinth excitability and conduction in vestibular-cerebellar pathways, blocking the signals to the brain responsible for nausea, vomiting, and dizziness. Chemically distinct from [[dimenhydrinate]] (the 8-chlorotheophylline salt of diphenhydramine) despite both being first-generation antihistamines used for the same indication — meclizine is a piperazine derivative, generally considered longer-acting and less sedating than dimenhydrinate/diphenhydramine, though it shares the same core anticholinergic mechanism and side-effect class.

## Dosing (per Dramamine Nausea Long Lasting Formula label)
Adults and children ≥12y: 1-2 tablets (25-50mg) once daily, or as directed by a doctor — a genuinely simpler, less-frequent dosing schedule than [[dimenhydrinate]]'s every-6-8-hour regimen, consistent with meclizine's longer duration of action (up to 24 hours per manufacturer labeling). Most effective when taken prophylactically before motion-sickness-inducing activity, per StatPearls.

## Contraindications
Per reviewed sources: not recommended in children under 12 (safety/efficacy not established in this age group); hypersensitivity to meclizine. Some older monograph sources describe meclizine as contraindicated in women who are or may become pregnant — this database flags this as a real, sourced caution rather than independently adjudicating current pregnancy-safety consensus, which was not separately re-verified in this pass.

## Key Safety Data
- Anticholinergic side-effect profile shared with other first-generation antihistamines: drowsiness (though generally less than dimenhydrinate/diphenhydramine), dry mouth, blurred vision, constipation, urinary retention
- Central anticholinergic activity is the same drug-class mechanism underlying [[dimenhydrinate]]'s own side-effect profile — the two drugs are not mechanistically unrelated, just chemically distinct molecules within the same broader antihistamine/anticholinergic family

## Drug Interactions
Synergistic CNS depression expected with alcohol, sedatives, hypnotics, and other anticholinergic drugs — consistent with the general antihistamine-class interaction pattern already documented on [[dimenhydrinate]]; not independently re-sourced with a meclizine-specific interaction list in this pass (flagged as a gap).

## Special Population Flags
- **Pediatric:** not recommended under age 12 per manufacturer labeling — a real age-cutoff difference from [[dimenhydrinate]], which has pediatric dosing down to age 2 (Dramamine for Kids)
- **Elderly:** anticholinergic-burden concerns generally applicable to this drug class (falls, cognitive impairment, urinary retention) — not independently re-sourced against a specific Beers Criteria citation in this pass, flagged as a gap
- **Pregnancy:** see Contraindications above — a real, sourced caution flagged for further verification rather than resolved definitively here

## Overdose / Toxicity
Not independently sourced with meclizine-specific overdose management data in this pass — general antihistamine-overdose principles (per [[dimenhydrinate]]'s own Overdose section: supportive care, ECG monitoring, benzodiazepines for agitation/seizures) likely apply given the shared drug class, but this was not independently confirmed for meclizine specifically.
