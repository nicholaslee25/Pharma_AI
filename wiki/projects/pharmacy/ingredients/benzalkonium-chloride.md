# Benzalkonium Chloride

**Type:** Active Ingredient
**Drug Class:** Quaternary ammonium compound (cationic surfactant/antiseptic)
**Verification Status:** AI-compiled from primary sources, unverified — not yet reviewed by a pharmacist/physician
**Evidence Grade:** Genuinely unresolved at the regulatory level — the FDA has never issued a final GRASE (Generally Recognized As Safe and Effective) determination for this ingredient in OTC consumer antiseptic wash products; it remains in an open-ended "deferred" category rather than either approved or banned, a real and important status distinct from a normal monograph ingredient.
**Last updated:** 2026-08-27
**Sources:** FDA Federal Register (Safety and Effectiveness of Consumer Antiseptics; Topical Antimicrobial Drug Products for OTC Human Use, 2016 final rule and 2017 renewal), DailyMed labels for Dial/Dove/Softsoap/Cetaphil brand-page research (2026-08-27), AJIC/ScienceDirect efficacy and pharmacokinetic studies (search-summarized, not full-text pulled)

---

## Indication
Topical antiseptic/antibacterial use in consumer wash-off products — bar soaps, liquid hand soaps, and body washes — "for washing to decrease bacteria on the skin," per the standard Drug Facts wording used across every brand confirmed on this page. Also used in leave-on hand sanitizer/rub formulations and ophthalmic preparations (as a preservative) — those are regulated as a **separate FDA category** ("Consumer Antiseptic Rub") from the wash-off products this page focuses on, and should not be assumed to carry the same regulatory status.

## Mechanism
Cationic surfactant — the positively charged quaternary ammonium molecule disrupts bacterial cell membranes, causing leakage of cellular contents. Broad-spectrum activity against many gram-positive and some gram-negative bacteria. Chemically related to, but distinct from, benzethonium chloride (used in some Dial liquid-hand-wash SKUs — see Discrepancy Flag below) and cetrimonium chloride (listed as an inactive ingredient on at least one Softsoap SKU checked this pass).

## Dosing
Typical OTC wash concentrations confirmed this pass: **0.10%** (Dial bar soaps) to **0.13%** (Dove Care & Protect, Softsoap, Cetaphil antibacterial soap). Directions are uniform across brands: wet, lather vigorously, wash/cover skin, rinse and dry thoroughly.

## Efficacy Evidence
No large Cochrane-style systematic review specific to benzalkonium-chloride wash-off soap (as distinct from leave-on hand sanitizer/rub formulations) was located in this pass — a genuine evidence gap, not papered over. What was found, all for leave-on rub/sanitizer formulations rather than the rinse-off soap products documented on this page:
- A benzalkonium chloride (0.12%) hand sanitizer produced 3.75–4.16 log10 reductions in *S. aureus* fingertip colony counts, with antibacterial activity persisting up to 4 hours post-application, outperforming an ethanol comparator's <1 log10 reduction in the cited study (AJIC, search-summarized, not independently verified against the full text this pass).
- A pharmacokinetic study of a 0.13% benzalkonium-chloride consumer antiseptic wash found systemic absorption "low even under exaggerated use conditions" (ScienceDirect, search-summarized).
- This rub/sanitizer-specific efficacy data should **not** be assumed to transfer directly to the wash-off soap formulations that are the actual subject of every brand on this page — a rinsed-off product has a fundamentally shorter skin contact time than a leave-on rub, and the FDA itself regulates the two product types under separate rulemakings for exactly this reason.

## Regulatory Status — the central finding for this ingredient (real, current, unresolved)
The FDA's September 2016 final rule on OTC Consumer Antiseptic Wash products **banned 19 active ingredients** (including triclosan and triclocarban) from marketing, on grounds manufacturers had not demonstrated they were safe and effective for long-term daily use. **Benzalkonium chloride, benzethonium chloride, and chloroxylenol were explicitly carved out ("deferred") from that ban** — not approved, not banned, but given a one-year window (renewed at least once, March 2017) for manufacturers to submit the safety/effectiveness data the FDA said was missing. Per FDA and legal-analysis sources located in this pass, **as of the most recent search (2024–2025 reporting), no final GRASE determination has been issued for benzalkonium chloride in consumer antiseptic wash products** — meaning the ingredient behind roughly half of this database's "Antibacterial Soaps" category by recommendation share remains in indefinite regulatory limbo, legally marketable but never affirmatively confirmed safe-and-effective for this specific use, nearly a decade after the deferral began. This is a genuinely significant, underappreciated regulatory fact, not an alarmist inference — it is exactly the situation the 2016 rule was designed to resolve, and it remains unresolved.

## Contraindications
Per every label checked this pass: for external use only; avoid contact with eyes (flush with water if contact occurs); discontinue and consult a doctor if irritation or redness develops; keep out of reach of children; contact Poison Control if swallowed.

## Key Safety Data
- Systemic absorption reported as low under normal and exaggerated use conditions (per the pharmacokinetic study cited above)
- Contact dermatitis and skin/eye irritation are the primary described risks across labels, consistent with any surfactant-class antiseptic
- Broader public-health literature (not independently verified via primary sources in this pass) has raised concerns about quaternary-ammonium-compound resistance selection pressure and environmental/aquatic toxicity — flagged as context, not confirmed against a primary source here
- No triclosan/triclocarban-style hormonal-disruption or bacterial-resistance data specific to benzalkonium chloride was located to the same depth as those banned ingredients' documented evidence base — this cuts both ways: less evidence of harm, but also less evidence of the safety the FDA said it wants demonstrated

## Drug Interactions

## Special Population Flags
No specific pregnancy, breastfeeding, or pediatric guidance beyond the standard "keep out of reach of children" warning was located on any label checked this pass.

## Overdose / Toxicity
If swallowed: contact Poison Control Center immediately, per every label checked this pass. No further overdose-management detail was located.

## Brands Using This Ingredient (in this database — formulation not individually verified per SKU beyond what's noted)
**Dial** (brand-page research 2026-08-27) — confirmed benzalkonium chloride 0.10% across its current bar-soap line (Dial Advanced Clean Antibacterial Gold, Dial Antibacterial and Sensitive Hypoallergenic Bar, Dial Antibacterial with Vitamins); its liquid **Dial Antibacterial Hand Wash uses benzethonium chloride 0.1% instead** — a different, related quaternary-ammonium active, not individually detailed on this page. A historical "Dial Gold Bar Antibacterial Deodorant Soap" SKU used triclocarban 0.60% and is confirmed DISCONTINUED (NDC inactivated per FDA compliance action), consistent with triclocarban's 2016 ban — see `drugs/Dial.md`. **Dove** (brand-page research 2026-08-27) — confirmed benzalkonium chloride 0.13% but ONLY in the "Care & Protect" sub-line; the standard Dove Beauty Bar and Sensitive Skin Bar carry no antibacterial active/Drug Facts panel at all — see `drugs/Dove.md`. **Softsoap** (brand-page research 2026-08-27) — confirmed benzalkonium chloride 0.13% (Kitchen Fresh Hands and other antibacterial-labeled SKUs); non-antibacterial Softsoap SKUs exist without this active — see `drugs/Softsoap.md`. **Cetaphil** (brand-page research, prior pass) — confirmed benzalkonium chloride 0.13% v/v for its antibacterial soap line, see `drugs/Cetaphil.md`.

## Discrepancy Flag (real, current — 2026-08-27): pHisoderm category-placement mismatch
**pHisoderm (4% share of this category) does not currently sell a benzalkonium-chloride or any other antibacterial-active soap.** Confirmed via DailyMed: pHisoderm's current product line (Anti-Blemish Body Wash, Clean/Clinical facial cleansers) is built entirely around **salicylic acid 2%** as an acne treatment, manufactured by The Mentholatum Company (the same manufacturer confirmed for [[OXY]] elsewhere in this pass). No current pHisoderm SKU with an antibacterial (benzalkonium chloride or otherwise) Drug Facts active ingredient was located. This is a genuine mismatch between this database's Pharmacy Times-sourced "Antibacterial Soaps" category placement and the brand's actual current formulation — pHisoderm today is an acne-cleanser brand, not an antibacterial-soap brand, despite the category placement. See `drugs/pHisoderm.md` for the full discussion, including pHisoderm's historical hexachlorophene-related FDA action (1972).
