# Magnesium Hydroxide (Saline Laxative Use)

**Type:** Active Ingredient
**Drug Class:** Saline/osmotic laxative (distinct pharmacological use of magnesium from the [[magnesium]] dietary-supplement page)
**Verification Status:** AI-compiled from primary sources, unverified — not yet reviewed by a pharmacist/physician
**Evidence Grade:** Established, long-standing OTC laxative (Milk of Magnesia); real, documented hypermagnesemia/toxicity risk in renal impairment at laxative-level doses, which are far higher than dietary-supplement doses
**Last updated:** 2026-08-27
**Sources:** DailyMed (Phillips' Milk of Magnesia), general drug-interaction/case-report literature reviewed in this pass

**New page, added 2026-08-27:** built specifically because Phillips' Milk of Magnesia's laxative-dose magnesium hydroxide (1200mg/15mL) is pharmacologically distinct from — and uses doses far exceeding — the dietary-supplement-dose context [[magnesium]] is built around (adult RDA 310-420mg, supplemental Tolerable Upper Intake Level 350mg). Applying [[magnesium]]'s dietary-supplement safety framing to this laxative use would understate the real toxicity risk at laxative doses.

---

## Indication
OTC: relief of occasional constipation (as a saline/osmotic laxative, "Milk of Magnesia"). Also used, at lower doses, as an antacid (see [[antacids]]'s own brief magnesium-hydroxide mention in aluminum/magnesium-combination antacids like Gaviscon/Mylanta/Gelusil, confirmed elsewhere in this database).

## Mechanism
Poorly absorbed magnesium salt draws water into the small intestine via osmosis, increasing intraluminal fluid volume and stimulating peristalsis — a saline/osmotic laxative mechanism, distinct from bulk-forming fiber ([[fiber]]), stimulant laxatives ([[bisacodyl]], [[senna]]), stool softeners ([[docusate]]), or PEG-based osmotic agents ([[peg-3350]]).

## Dosing (per Phillips' Milk of Magnesia FDA OTC label)
Magnesium hydroxide 1200mg/15mL liquid. Adults/children ≥12y: 30-60mL/day (single dose or divided), preferably at bedtime. Onset: bowel movement in ½ to 6 hours — notably faster than PEG-3350's 1-3 day onset per [[peg-3350]], making this a genuinely different use-case fit (fast, single-episode relief vs. PEG's slower, more sustained approach).

**Critical dosing-context note:** at the labeled maximum adult dose (60mL/day), this delivers approximately 4,800mg of magnesium hydroxide — vastly exceeding [[magnesium]]'s cited adult Tolerable Upper Intake Level of 350mg for supplemental (dietary) magnesium. This is intentional and expected for a short-term saline-laxative use case, not a labeling error, but it means [[magnesium]]'s general safety/toxicity framing (built around dietary-supplement doses) does not directly apply here.

## Key Safety Data
- **Real, documented hypermagnesemia/toxicity risk, especially in renal impairment**: because magnesium is renally eliminated, patients with reduced kidney function cannot adequately clear the large magnesium load a laxative dose delivers, risking accumulation to toxic levels
- Multiple published case reports (reviewed in this pass) document severe, sometimes fatal hypermagnesemia from Milk-of-Magnesia/magnesium-laxative use, particularly in elderly patients and those with renal failure or bowel disorders, sometimes from unsupervised/repeated use
- **Hypermagnesemia symptoms**: CNS depression, muscle weakness/paralysis, hypotension, respiratory depression, prolonged cardiac conduction time, irregular heartbeat, and in severe cases cardiac arrest
- Generally well-tolerated in patients with normal renal function at labeled doses

## Contraindications
Kidney disease/impaired renal function (real risk of magnesium accumulation to toxic levels); patients on a magnesium- or sodium-restricted diet should consult a doctor; abdominal pain, nausea, vomiting (may indicate a condition where laxative use is inappropriate).

## Drug Interactions
Per [[antacids]]'s general magnesium-containing-antacid interaction note: may impair absorption of tetracycline/fluoroquinolone antibiotics. Not independently expanded beyond that shared class note in this pass.

## Special Population Flags
- **Renal impairment / CKD / dialysis patients: should avoid magnesium hydroxide laxative products unless specifically directed by a physician**, per case-report literature reviewed in this pass — a real, clinically significant contraindication-adjacent risk
- **Elderly:** unsupervised/chronic use carries elevated hypermagnesemia risk even without diagnosed renal failure, per reviewed case reports
- **Pregnancy/breastfeeding:** ask a health professional before use, per standard OTC laxative labeling convention (not independently detailed with magnesium-hydroxide-specific data in this pass)

## Overdose / Toxicity
Hypermagnesemia from excessive or repeated dosing, particularly with impaired renal clearance — can progress from muscle weakness/hypotension to respiratory depression and cardiac arrest in severe cases. Management in reviewed case reports generally involved IV fluids/calcium gluconate and, in renal failure, dialysis to remove excess magnesium.

## Pharmacy Times Context (recommendation share — NOT efficacy)
Phillips' (Pharmacy Times 2026 OTC Guide, ~1,700 pharmacists surveyed): Laxatives, Nonfiber category 5%. This is a pharmacist recommendation share, not a response/cure rate.
