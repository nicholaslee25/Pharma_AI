# Adapalene

**Type:** Active Ingredient
**Drug Class:** Third-generation topical retinoid (vitamin A derivative, retinoic acid receptor modulator)
**Verification Status:** AI-compiled from primary sources, unverified — not yet reviewed by a pharmacist/physician
**Evidence Grade:** Moderate-High — multiple RCTs and a network meta-analysis support efficacy for mild-to-moderate acne, with adapalene+benzoyl-peroxide combination outperforming either monotherapy; less extensively documented in this pass than the Cochrane-level evidence base already built for [[benzoyl-peroxide]] and [[salicylic-acid]]
**Last updated:** 2026-08-27
**Sources:** StatPearls (NBK482509, adapalene), FDA Differin Gel label (accessdata.fda.gov 021753s004lbl.pdf), FDA NDA 020380Orig1s010 pharmacology review, network meta-analysis of acne treatments (BJD/PMC9804728), drugs/Differin Gel.md (brand-page cross-check, 2026-08-27)

## Indication
FDA-approved (topical, since 1996 Rx; OTC 0.1% strength since 2016): mild-to-moderate acne vulgaris, primarily comedonal (blackheads/whiteheads) acne. Prescription-only formulations exist at higher strength (0.3% gel) and in fixed-dose combination with benzoyl peroxide or clindamycin for more severe or inflammatory acne.

## Mechanism
Adapalene binds retinoic acid receptors (RARs), modulating cellular differentiation, keratinization, and inflammatory signaling. This **normalizes abnormal follicular keratinization** — the process that forms microcomedones, the earliest acne lesion — rather than killing bacteria (benzoyl peroxide's mechanism) or dissolving existing keratin plugs (salicylic acid's keratolytic mechanism). It also carries direct anti-inflammatory activity independent of its comedolytic effect. Because it targets lesion *formation* rather than treating existing lesions, benefit typically takes 8-12 weeks to become fully apparent — a materially slower onset than benzoyl peroxide or salicylic acid.

## Pharmacokinetics
Minimal systemic absorption: less than 0.01% of applied topical dose reaches systemic circulation, with plasma clearance within 72 hours — a favorable safety margin compared to oral retinoids (e.g., isotretinoin), whose teratogenicity risk is systemic and well-established.

## Dosing (per Differin Gel FDA OTC label)
Apply a thin layer to affected areas of the skin **once daily** (typically at night, though the OTC label does not mandate nighttime use the way earlier prescription labeling emphasized). Adapalene is photostable (unlike tretinoin), so same-day sun exposure does not degrade the molecule the way it does older retinoids — though sun-sensitivity warnings still apply to the skin itself.

## Efficacy Evidence
- **Network meta-analysis (BJD/PMC9804728), 40 RCTs, topical/oral/physical/combined acne treatments:** adapalene + benzoyl peroxide combination ranked as the most effective topical regimen studied, though with a somewhat higher withdrawal rate than monotherapy due to irritation.
- **Adapalene vs. tazarotene 0.1% (Cochrane-indexed trial):** roughly equivalent efficacy (adapalene 0.3% vs. tazarotene 0.1%), with **adapalene producing less irritation** than tazarotene — a real tolerability advantage among retinoids.
- **Adapalene vs. tretinoin (multicenter US/Europe trials):** comparable efficacy between adapalene gel and tretinoin gel, historically adapalene's main competitive claim (similar results, generally better tolerated).
- Retinoid monotherapy generally underperforms retinoid + antibiotic or retinoid + benzoyl peroxide combination therapy — a consistent finding across the acne literature, relevant since Differin Gel (adapalene monotherapy) is used standalone by OTC consumers who may not realize combination therapy is more effective per the literature.
- No large Cochrane review specific to adapalene monotherapy (analogous to the benzoyl-peroxide or salicylic-acid Cochrane reviews already in this database) was located in this pass — evidence here is drawn from a network meta-analysis and head-to-head trials rather than one dedicated systematic review, hence the "Moderate-High" rather than a Cochrane-anchored "High" grade.

## Contraindications / Warnings
External use only; avoid eyes, lips, mucous membranes, eczematous or sunburned skin. Photosensitizing — use sunscreen/protective clothing (though adapalene itself is photostable, treated skin can still be more UV-sensitive). Common initial skin irritation (erythema, dryness, scaling, a "purging" phase) in the first 2-4 weeks, generally milder than with tazarotene but present with any retinoid. **Pregnancy:** adapalene is teratogenic in animal studies at high oral doses (25 mg/kg, 32-65x the max recommended human topical dose by mg/m² comparison); it was classified Pregnancy Category C under the prior FDA system and product labeling advises caution/avoidance in pregnancy, though a systematic review of first-trimester topical-retinoid exposure found no clear increase in retinoid embryopathy risk — a genuinely mixed picture the label resolves conservatively (avoid) despite topical systemic absorption being minimal.

## Special Population Flags
**Pregnancy:** avoid per label, despite low systemic absorption — see Contraindications above for the nuance between animal-study teratogenicity data (oral, high-dose) and human topical-exposure epidemiology (no clear signal found, but not large enough to fully clear the drug). **Pediatric:** OTC label age cutoffs apply per Differin Gel packaging (consult label; not independently itemized in the sources reviewed this pass).
