# Sodium Phosphate (Saline Laxative / Enema Use)

**Type:** Active Ingredient
**Drug Class:** Saline/osmotic laxative (rectal enema and, historically, oral formulations)
**Verification Status:** AI-compiled from primary sources, unverified — not yet reviewed by a pharmacist/physician

**Last updated:** 2026-08-27

## Indication
Relief of occasional constipation; bowel cleansing before rectal examinations/procedures — via a rectal enema route, distinct from every oral laxative in this database.

## Mechanism
Monobasic and dibasic sodium phosphate salts draw water into the bowel osmotically upon rectal administration, producing rapid bowel evacuation — among the fastest-onset laxative mechanisms in this database (label: bowel movement in 1-5 minutes, vs. hours-to-days for oral agents).

## Dosing (per Fleet Enema FDA OTC label)
Monobasic sodium phosphate 19g + dibasic sodium phosphate 7g per 118mL adult enema (concentrations vary by product — Fleet for Children uses a lower-volume/dose formulation). Adults: one enema as needed; **do not use more than one enema in 24 hours** — this specific limit is safety-critical, not merely a dosing suggestion, given the real overdose/toxicity risk below.

## Key Safety Data
- **Real, FDA-documented acute kidney injury risk ("acute phosphate nephropathy")**: calcium-phosphate crystal deposits become trapped in renal tubules, causing potentially permanent kidney damage that can require dialysis or transplant in severe cases
- **FDA issued two safety communications** (2006 and 2014) specifically warning that oral sodium phosphate products, and repeated/excessive enema dosing, carry rare but serious risk of kidney/heart harm and death; a boxed warning was added in 2008 to related prescription bowel-prep products (Visicol, OsmoPrep) for the same underlying risk
- **Risk factors**: diabetes, age over 65, dehydration, and concurrent use of NSAIDs, ACE inhibitors, ARBs, or diuretics — all of which reduce the kidney's ability to handle the phosphate/fluid-shift load
- Using more than one enema in 24 hours is explicitly flagged by the FDA as capable of causing serious harm, including death, from resulting electrolyte disturbances

## Contraindications
Kidney disease; sodium-restricted diets (significant sodium load per dose); congestive heart failure; children age 5 and under should not receive sodium phosphate products without consulting a health professional first, per general drug-safety guidance reviewed in this pass. Abdominal pain, nausea, vomiting — do not use if present, consult a doctor.

## Drug Interactions
Concurrent NSAID, ACE inhibitor, ARB, or diuretic use raises acute-kidney-injury risk when combined with sodium phosphate, per the FDA risk-factor data above — a real, clinically relevant combination risk not documented on any other laxative ingredient page in this database.

## Special Population Flags
- **Renal impairment:** contraindicated/high risk — sodium phosphate's kidney-injury mechanism directly targets renal tubules, compounding any pre-existing impairment
- **Elderly (>65y):** an independently identified FDA risk factor for acute phosphate nephropathy
- **Pediatric:** Fleet markets a separate, lower-dose "Fleet for Children" formulation; children ≤5 should not receive sodium phosphate products without professional guidance
- **Dehydration:** an independent FDA-identified risk factor — a patient already fluid-depleted (e.g., from the constipation/bowel-prep context itself) may be at elevated risk exactly when using this product

## Overdose / Toxicity
Excessive or repeated dosing (more than one enema/24h) can cause serious electrolyte disturbances (hyperphosphatemia, hypocalcemia, hypernatremia) and acute phosphate nephropathy, potentially requiring dialysis or kidney transplant in severe cases, and has been associated with death in FDA-reviewed cases.
