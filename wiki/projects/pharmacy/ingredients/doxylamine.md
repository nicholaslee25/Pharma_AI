# Doxylamine Succinate

**Type:** Active Ingredient
**Drug Class:** First-generation ethanolamine antihistamine, used as an OTC nighttime sleep aid (also as the antihistamine component of Rx Bonjesta/Diclegis for nausea of pregnancy, not covered here)
**Verification Status:** AI-compiled from primary sources, unverified — not yet reviewed by a pharmacist/physician
**Evidence Grade:** Same low/mixed OTC-sleep-aid evidence tier as [[oral-diphenhydramine-sleep-aid]] — FDA recognizes doxylamine 25mg as a safe/effective OTC nighttime sleep aid, but independent reviews of OTC sleep aids broadly (including doxylamine) describe the evidence as lacking robust efficacy support
**Last updated:** 2026-08-28
**Sources:** WebSearch (DailyMed Unisom SleepTabs label, general first-generation-antihistamine literature already reviewed for [[oral-diphenhydramine-sleep-aid]])

## Indication
OTC nighttime sleep aid for occasional sleeplessness.

## Mechanism
First-generation H1-antihistamine; crosses the blood-brain barrier producing central sedation, the basis of its OTC sleep-aid use. Shares the core anticholinergic (antimuscarinic) activity of the broader first-generation antihistamine class, the same mechanistic family as [[diphenhydramine|oral-diphenhydramine-sleep-aid]] and [[dimenhydrinate]], though doxylamine is chemically an ethanolamine, a different subclass from diphenhydramine (also an ethanolamine derivative, but a distinct molecule) and from meclizine (a piperazine).

## Dosing
Per Unisom SleepTabs label: doxylamine succinate 25mg, one tablet approximately 30 minutes before bedtime, for adults and children 12 years and over. Not for use in children under 12.

## Efficacy Evidence
No doxylamine-specific RCT with hard efficacy numbers was independently located in this pass (a real evidence gap, flagged rather than filled with diphenhydramine's numbers as if interchangeable). The same systematic-review finding already documented on [[oral-diphenhydramine-sleep-aid]] — that OTC sleep aids as a class lack robust efficacy evidence — is understood to apply to doxylamine as a member of the same first-generation-antihistamine OTC sleep-aid class, but this is an inference from the class-level review, not an independently sourced doxylamine-specific trial.

## Key Safety Data
Same anticholinergic toxidrome class-risk as [[oral-diphenhydramine-sleep-aid]]: sedation, dry mouth, blurred vision, constipation, urinary retention; cumulative anticholinergic burden linked to falls/delirium/dementia risk in the geriatric-pharmacology literature broadly. The AGS Beers Criteria flag first-generation antihistamines AS A CLASS (not diphenhydramine specifically) for avoidance in most/all older adults — this applies to doxylamine too, not just diphenhydramine, a point worth stating explicitly since Beers-Criteria discussion elsewhere in this database has so far centered on diphenhydramine by name.

## Contraindications
Per standard OTC label conventions: not for children under 12; avoid combining with other doxylamine- or diphenhydramine-containing products (additive sedation/anticholinergic risk); do not use with alcohol or other sedatives without consulting a doctor.

## Special Population Flags
- **Older adults:** AGS Beers Criteria class-level avoidance recommendation applies (see Key Safety Data above).
- **Pregnancy:** doxylamine (combined with vitamin B6/pyridoxine) is the active ingredient in the FDA-approved prescription antiemetic Diclegis/Bonjesta for nausea/vomiting of pregnancy — a genuinely different, higher-evidence-tier use than the OTC sleep-aid indication covered on this page, and not further researched here since it falls outside this database's OTC scope.
- **Children:** not for use under age 12 per OTC labeling.

## Overdose / Toxicity
Anticholinergic toxidrome, same class-level presentation as [[oral-diphenhydramine-sleep-aid]]: sedation progressing to agitation/delirium, dry mouth, blurred vision, tachycardia, and in severe cases seizures or cardiac conduction abnormalities. No specific antidote for routine OTC-dose overdose; supportive care is standard.
