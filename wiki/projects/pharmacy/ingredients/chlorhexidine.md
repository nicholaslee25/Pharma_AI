# Chlorhexidine

**Type:** Active Ingredient
**Drug Class:** Antiseptic mouthwash (biguanide)
**Verification Status:** AI-compiled from primary sources, unverified — not yet reviewed by a pharmacist/physician
**Evidence Grade:** High-quality Cochrane evidence exists for both benefit (plaque reduction) and harm (tooth staining) — but the review authors themselves judged the gingivitis-reduction effect, while statistically real, NOT clinically relevant in mild disease. Also important: the standard U.S. chlorhexidine mouthrinse (Peridex) is a PRESCRIPTION product, not truly OTC, despite being filed in this database's OTC mouthwash category.

## Indication
Treating gingivitis between dental visits (redness/swelling of gingivae, gingival bleeding on probing), as an adjunct to mechanical oral hygiene (toothbrushing ± flossing) — NOT tested for acute necrotizing ulcerative gingivitis, and NOT a routine daily cosmetic mouthwash.

**Important formulation/access note:** the standard U.S. chlorhexidine gluconate 0.12% oral rinse (Peridex) is **prescription-only**, not OTC. This is a real regulatory distinction worth flagging since chlorhexidine-family products appear in this database's OTC "Therapeutic Mouthwashes/Oral Rinses" category — actual chlorhexidine content should be verified per specific product before assuming OTC accessibility.

## Mechanism
Biguanide antiseptic; positively charged chlorhexidine interacts with the bacterial cell wall. At low concentration (0.02–0.06%) it is bacteriostatic; at higher concentration (>0.1%, matching the 0.12% Peridex formulation) it causes cell leakage and cytoplasmic coagulation, becoming bactericidal. Broad-spectrum activity against gram-positive bacteria, gram-negative bacteria, and yeast.

## Dosing
Per Peridex label: rinse with 15mL undiluted product twice daily for 30 seconds after toothbrushing. Begin after professional dental cleaning; reevaluate at intervals not exceeding six months (with professional dental prophylaxis at least that often).

## Efficacy Evidence

**Cochrane review (James et al. 2017, PMID 28362061)** — 51 RCTs, 5,345 participants, chlorhexidine mouthrinse as adjunct to mechanical hygiene vs. hygiene alone or plus placebo/control rinse, ≥4 weeks:
- **Gingivitis:** Gingival Index reduced by **0.21** (95% CI 0.11–0.31) at 4–6 weeks vs. control (10 trials, 805 participants with mild gingivitis, mean baseline GI ~1; high-quality evidence) — similar effect at 6 months. **The review authors themselves state this reduction, while statistically real, was NOT considered clinically relevant** in mild disease — a genuinely important caveat, not a minor footnote.
- **Plaque:** standardized mean difference **1.45** (95% CI 1.00–1.90) SD lower plaque with chlorhexidine (12 trials, 950 participants, high-quality evidence) — a LARGE, clinically meaningful reduction, similarly large at 6 months.
- **Extrinsic tooth staining (harm):** SMD **1.07** (95% CI 0.80–1.34) SD HIGHER staining with chlorhexidine (8 trials, 415 participants, moderate-quality evidence) — a LARGE increase, persisting through 6 months.
- **Calculus:** results inconclusive.
- No evidence that any particular chlorhexidine concentration or rinsing frequency (once vs. twice daily) outperforms another.
- Other reported adverse effects: taste disturbance (11 studies), oral mucosal effects — soreness, irritation, mild desquamation, ulceration (13 studies), burning sensation/tongue (9 studies).

**Bottom line:** the honest picture from the strongest evidence located is "large, real plaque reduction + large, real staining increase + a gingivitis benefit too small to matter clinically in mild disease" — a genuinely mixed risk/benefit profile, not simply "very effective."

## Pharmacokinetics
Per Peridex label: ~30% of the active ingredient is retained in the oral cavity after rinsing and slowly released into oral fluids; poorly absorbed from the GI tract. Microbiological sampling showed bacterial count reductions (aerobic and anaerobic) ranging 54–97% through six months of use.

## Contraindications
Hypersensitivity to chlorhexidine gluconate or other formula ingredients.

## Key Safety Data
- **Tooth staining:** 56% of Peridex users showed measurable staining increase, with 15% developing heavy stain, vs. 35% in the control group after six months (label Precautions section) — consistent with the large Cochrane-quantified staining effect above
- Supragingival calculus increase observed in clinical testing vs. controls (label Warnings)
- Anaphylaxis and serious allergic reactions have been reported with chlorhexidine-containing dental products
- Oral mucosal effects (aphthous ulcers, ulceration) at <1% frequency per label; post-marketing reports of stomatitis, glossitis, salivary gland inflammation
- Effect on periodontitis (as distinct from gingivitis) remains undetermined per label

## Drug Interactions
Not addressed with specific interaction data in the sources gathered.

## Special Population Flags
- **Pregnancy:** animal studies at high doses showed no fetal harm; adequate human studies not completed (label).
- **Breastfeeding:** excretion in human milk undetermined; rat studies at 100x typical-use doses showed no adverse effects on offspring.
- **Pediatric:** safety/effectiveness not established under age 18 (Peridex label).
- **Carcinogenesis/Mutagenesis:** no carcinogenic effect in rats up to 38mg/kg/day; no mutagenic effects in mammalian studies (label).

## Overdose / Toxicity
