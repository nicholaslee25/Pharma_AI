# Multivitamins

**Type:** Active Ingredient (product class)
**Drug Class:** Multi-nutrient supplement (dietary supplement, not FDA-regulated as a drug)
**Verification Status:** AI-compiled from primary sources, unverified — not yet reviewed by a pharmacist/physician
**Evidence Grade:** Strong rationale for correcting nutrient deficiency; NOT established for cancer or cardiovascular disease prevention per a large 2022 RCT; a companion 2023 RCT from the same study population found a real, statistically significant cognitive benefit in older adults — genuinely mixed, outcome-dependent evidence rather than a single verdict

## Indication
Correction of nutrient-deficiency gaps from diet. Widely marketed for general wellness, disease prevention, and cognitive health — the actual trial evidence for these broader claims is outcome-specific (see Efficacy Evidence).

## Mechanism
Not a single mechanism — a combination product delivering multiple vitamins/minerals at RDA-range doses. Each individual nutrient's mechanism is covered on its own ingredient page in this database (e.g. [[vitamin-d]], [[vitamin-c]], [[iron]], [[calcium]], [[magnesium]]).

## Efficacy Evidence — COSMOS trial (same 21,442-participant RCT population, two published outcome papers)

**Sesso et al. 2022 (PMID 35294969)** — cancer/cardiovascular outcomes, median 3.6-year follow-up, 21,442 older US adults:
- **Total invasive cancer:** HR 0.97 (95% CI 0.86–1.09, p=0.57) — **no significant effect**
- Breast cancer HR 1.06, colorectal cancer HR 1.30 — no significant effects
- Lung cancer HR **0.62** (95% CI 0.42–0.92) — a significant protective signal, though this was a secondary/exploratory outcome and should be interpreted cautiously rather than as a headline finding
- **Composite cardiovascular disease:** HR 0.98 (95% CI 0.86–1.12) — no significant effect
- **All-cause mortality:** HR 0.93 (95% CI 0.81–1.08) — no significant effect
- No safety concerns identified
- **Authors' conclusion:** daily MVM did not significantly reduce total cancer incidence

**Baker et al. 2023 COSMOS-Mind (PMID 36102337)** — cognitive outcomes, 3-year follow-up, 2,262 participants from the same overall study:
- **Global cognition:** significant benefit with MVM vs. placebo (mean z=0.07, 95% CI 0.02–0.12, p=0.007)
- Benefit was LARGER in participants with a cardiovascular disease history (z=0.14 vs. 0.06 without CVD history; interaction p=0.01)
- Benefits also seen for memory and executive function composites specifically
- Cocoa extract (the trial's other arm) showed NO cognitive benefit
- **Authors' conclusion:** this is the first evidence from a large, long-term, pragmatic RCT supporting potential MVM efficacy for cognition in older adults — though confirmation in a more diverse cohort is still needed, and the mechanism is unclear

**Synthesis for this page:** the honest, current picture is genuinely split by outcome — daily multivitamin use has NOT been shown to reduce cancer, cardiovascular disease, or overall mortality risk in a large modern RCT, but the SAME trial population showed a real, statistically significant cognitive benefit (global cognition, memory, executive function), most pronounced in those with cardiovascular disease history. This is a more nuanced and more current picture than a flat "multivitamins don't do anything beyond correcting deficiency" claim would suggest.

## Contraindications

## Key Safety Data
COSMOS (Sesso 2022) reported no safety concerns over its 3.6-year median follow-up in >21,000 participants — a reassuring, well-powered safety signal for typical multivitamin-multimineral dosing in older adults, though this does not substitute for checking individual nutrient upper limits (especially iron and fat-soluble vitamins) in higher-potency products.

## Drug Interactions
Not itemized as a combination product — see individual nutrient pages for specific interactions (e.g. iron with levothyroxine/levodopa, calcium with several drug classes).

## Special Population Flags
- **Older adults with cardiovascular disease history:** per COSMOS-Mind, this subgroup showed the largest cognitive benefit from daily MVM use — a genuinely specific, sourced finding rather than a general claim
- **Pregnancy:** a distinct, better-evidenced multivitamin use case exists specifically via prenatal formulations — see [[prenatal-vitamins]] for the strong folic acid/neural-tube-defect evidence, which should not be conflated with general adult multivitamin evidence

## Overdose / Toxicity
Not addressed as a combination product in the sources reviewed — risk depends on the specific formulation's doses of fat-soluble vitamins (A, D, E, K) and iron, which can accumulate to toxic levels with excessive multivitamin use, especially when combined with additional single-nutrient supplements. See individual nutrient pages for specific Tolerable Upper Intake Levels.
