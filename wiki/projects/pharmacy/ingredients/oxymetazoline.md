## Indication
Temporary relief of nasal congestion from the common cold, hay fever, or upper respiratory allergies; temporary relief of sinus congestion/pressure.

## Mechanism
Imidazoline-class alpha-2 adrenergic agonist — more potent and longer-acting than phenylethylamine decongestants (e.g. phenylephrine), acting on both capacitance (venous plexus) and resistance (arteriolar) vessels in the nasal mucosa to produce vasoconstriction and reduce swelling.

## Dosing (per Afrin FDA OTC label)
Adults/children ≥6y: 2–3 sprays per nostril every 10–12 hours, max 2 doses/24h. Children <6y: ask a doctor. **Critical limit: do not use for more than 3 consecutive days** — this is the central safety fact for this ingredient class.

## Efficacy / Mechanism of the Central Safety Issue — Rhinitis Medicamentosa (StatPearls)
Oxymetazoline is an effective short-term decongestant, but prolonged/repeated use (typically **5–7 days**, per StatPearls) causes rebound congestion (rhinitis medicamentosa, RM) via one or more proposed mechanisms:
- **Ischemic theory:** chronic vasoconstriction causes mucosal ischemia, predisposing to interstitial edema
- **Tachyphylaxis:** constrictor mechanisms fatigue, adrenoreceptors become refractory (correlating with reduced sensitivity to the body's own catecholamines), requiring escalating doses
- **Vascular permeability changes** and **beta-adrenergic rebound** (beta effects outlasting alpha effects, causing rebound vasodilation) are also proposed
- Prolonged use may also suppress the body's own norepinephrine release via negative feedback, leaving nerves unable to sustain vasoconstriction once the drug is stopped

**Epidemiology:** RM accounts for ~14% of nonallergic rhinitis cases generally, and ~49% of self-medicating patients with persistent rhinitis overuse intranasal decongestants despite awareness of duration limits — i.e., this is a common, not rare, real-world problem.

## Contraindications

## Key Safety Data
- **Label's own rebound warning (quoted verbatim):** "Do not use for more than 3 days. Use only as directed. Frequent or prolonged use may cause nasal congestion to recur or worsen."
- Temporary burning, stinging, sneezing, or increased nasal discharge can occur with use
- **Rhinitis medicamentosa complications if overused:** mucosal hyperplasia, chronic rhinosinusitis (with or without polyps), atrophic rhinitis, septal perforation, increased intraoperative bleeding risk, hypertension/other CV effects, psychological dependency/withdrawal anxiety
- **Benzalkonium chloride** (a preservative in the Afrin formulation) is flagged by StatPearls as a possible contributing factor to rebound congestion, though clinical-significance evidence is described as mixed
- **Recovery:** subjective rebound symptoms may resolve within 48h if intranasal corticosteroids are used alongside discontinuation, vs. >1 week without; objective mucosal abnormalities (edema/inflammation) often take 7–14 days to resolve
- **Weaning strategy for RM (StatPearls):** discontinue one nostril at a time, or dilute with saline before slow withdrawal, to reduce abrupt rebound

## Special Population Flags
- **Ask a doctor before use if:** heart disease, high blood pressure, diabetes, thyroid disease, or trouble urinating due to enlarged prostate (per label — consistent with sympathomimetic/vasoconstrictor pharmacology)
- **Pregnancy/breastfeeding:** ask a health professional before use
- **Pediatric:** not for children <6y without medical guidance

## Overdose / Toxicity
Label directs seeking immediate medical help if swallowed — consistent with sympathomimetic systemic toxicity risk if ingested rather than sprayed nasally, though specific overdose management data was not located in the sources reviewed in this pass.
