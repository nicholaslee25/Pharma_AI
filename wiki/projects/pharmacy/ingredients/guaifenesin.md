# Guaifenesin

## Indication
FDA-approved: productive cough from the common cold, chest congestion, productive cough from allergies. Off-label: chronic bronchitis, productive cough in pneumonia.

## Mechanism
Expectorant via two proposed mechanisms: relaxes bronchial smooth muscle tone, and irritates the respiratory tract to increase respiratory-tract fluid volume — both intended to reduce mucus viscosity and ease productive coughing.

## Dosing (per Mucinex FDA OTC label / StatPearls)

| Form | Adult dose | Max |
|---|---|---|
| Extended-release tablet (600mg) | 1–2 tablets q12h | 4 tablets/24h (2400mg) |
| Syrup (200mg/10mL) | 10 mL q4h | 60 mL/day |
| Granules | 2–4 packets q4h | 6 doses/24h |

**Pediatric:** not for children <12 years per the Mucinex label; StatPearls notes FDA recommends against pediatric use generally and AAP advises avoiding under age 4, though some studies support liquid-form use ≥2y with accurate measurement.

## Efficacy Evidence — Bennett et al. 2015 RCT (PMID 26462765, NCT01114581)
Randomized, parallel-group, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial, non-smoking adults with acute upper RTI, single dose Mucinex 1200mg (2×600mg ER tablets) vs. placebo, n=19/arm:
- **Mucociliary clearance (60min):** placebo 8.3%→11.8%, drug 9.7%→11.1% — **not significant**
- **Cough clearance:** placebo 9.9%→9.1%, drug 10.8%→5.6% — **not significant**
- No significant difference in sputum biophysical properties (rheology, interfacial tension) between drug and placebo
- **Conclusion: no significant effect of a single 1200mg guaifenesin dose on objective mucociliary/cough clearance or sputum properties vs. placebo**

This negative objective-outcome result is consistent with StatPearls' own characterization (in its separate "Cough: Evaluation and Management" article, not separately pulled in this pass) that guaifenesin has "weak evidence" for effectiveness. It should be weighed honestly alongside guaifenesin's long-standing FDA OTC monograph approval, which predates modern RCT standards for expectorants.

## Pharmacokinetics
Well absorbed in the small intestine; mean volume of distribution 116 L. Hepatic metabolism to two inactive metabolites (β-(2-methoxyphenoxy)-lactic acid; hydroxy-guaifenesin), renally excreted.

## Contraindications
Phenylketonuria (some formulations contain phenylalanine); history of kidney stones (guaifenesin increases urine supersaturation/crystallization risk); severe liver or kidney disease; age <4 years; known hypersensitivity; concomitant MAOI use.

## Key Safety Data
- **Renal:** acute renal failure, nephrolithiasis, flank pain, hematuria, oliguria reported
- **GI:** nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, diarrhea
- **Other:** dizziness, drowsiness, tachycardia, hypertension, hypersensitivity reactions
- No specific incidence percentages located in the sources reviewed — flagged as a gap

## Drug Interactions
- Concomitant MAOI use is contraindicated (StatPearls)
- Medications affecting CYP enzyme metabolism (e.g., rolapitant) and medications affecting heart rhythm/causing respiratory depression are flagged in StatPearls, though this guidance is drawn from an article that also covers dextromethorphan and may be more relevant to that component in combination products
- **Lab interference:** guaifenesin can falsely elevate vanillylmandelic acid (VMA) lab test levels

## Special Population Flags
- **Pregnancy:** normal OTC doses considered acceptable (StatPearls)
- **Breastfeeding:** no studies located on breastmilk excretion or infant effects (StatPearls); a separate LactMed database entry (not independently pulled in this pass) reportedly considers usual maternal OTC doses unlikely to harm a nursing infant, especially beyond 2 months of age — not independently verified here
- **Pediatric:** not for <12y per Mucinex label; FDA/AAP guidance against use under age 4 more broadly
- **Hepatic/renal impairment:** no manufacturer dose adjustment recommended, but severe liver/kidney disease is a contraindication and kidney-stone history is a specific contraindication

## Overdose / Toxicity
No guaifenesin-specific toxicity profile distinct from combination products was located in the sources reviewed. Management is symptomatic; activated charcoal may help limit absorption.

## Pharmacy Times Context (recommendation share — NOT efficacy)
Expectorants (Pharmacy Times 2026 OTC Guide, ~1,700 pharmacists surveyed): Mucinex — 74%. This is a pharmacist recommendation share, not a response/cure rate — and per the Bennett 2015 RCT above, does not imply the ingredient has strong objective efficacy data behind it.
