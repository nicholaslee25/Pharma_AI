# Vitamin D

**Type:** Active Ingredient
**Drug Class:** Fat-soluble vitamin / hormone precursor (dietary supplement, not FDA-regulated as a drug)
**Verification Status:** AI-compiled from primary sources, unverified — not yet reviewed by a pharmacist/physician
**Evidence Grade:** High for correcting deficiency/bone health when combined with calcium; a large Cochrane review found vitamin D ALONE does not prevent fractures, and NIH's own review found insufficient evidence for benefit on cancer, cardiovascular disease, depression, MS, diabetes, or weight loss despite extensive research on all of these

## Indication
Prevention/treatment of vitamin D deficiency; prevention of rickets (children) and osteomalacia (adults); with calcium, supports bone health/osteoporosis prevention in older adults. Widely marketed (well beyond its established evidence base — see Efficacy Evidence) for immune function, mood, and general wellness.

## Mechanism
Vitamin D from sun/food/supplements is biologically inert until two hydroxylations: hepatic conversion to 25-hydroxyvitamin D [25(OH)D, calcidiol], then renal conversion to the active 1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D [1,25(OH)2D, calcitriol]. Promotes intestinal calcium absorption, maintains serum calcium/phosphate for bone mineralization, prevents hypocalcemic tetany. Also has anti-inflammatory and immune-modulatory roles, and influences cell growth/differentiation/apoptosis via widely-distributed vitamin D receptors. Two supplement forms: D2 (ergocalciferol) and D3 (cholecalciferol) — D3 raises and sustains serum 25(OH)D more effectively than D2.

## Dosing (NIH ODS RDA/AI and Tolerable Upper Intake Level)

| Population | RDA/AI | Upper Limit |
|---|---|---|
| Infants/children/adolescents | 10–15 mcg (400–600 IU), varies by age | 25–100 mcg (1000–4000 IU), varies by age |
| Adults | 15–20 mcg (600–800 IU) | 25–100 mcg (1000–4000 IU), varies by age |

**Status assessment:** serum 25(OH)D ≥50 nmol/L (20 ng/mL) considered sufficient for most people; deficiency risk rises below 30 nmol/L (12 ng/mL) — though NIH notes these thresholds are not definitively established.

## Efficacy Evidence

**Avenell et al. 2014 Cochrane review (PMID 24729336)** — 53 trials, 91,791 participants, vitamin D ± calcium vs. placebo/control for fracture prevention in older adults:
- **Vitamin D ALONE: high-quality evidence of NO benefit** — hip fracture RR 1.12 (95% CI 0.98–1.29); any fracture RR 1.03 (95% CI 0.96–1.11)
- **Vitamin D PLUS calcium: high-quality evidence of a small real benefit** — hip fracture RR 0.84 (95% CI 0.74–0.96, p=0.01); any fracture RR 0.95 (95% CI 0.90–0.99). In absolute terms: ~1 fewer hip fracture per 1000 community-dwelling older adults/year, or ~9 fewer per 1000/year in institutional (higher-risk) populations
- **Mortality:** not adversely affected by either vitamin D or vitamin D+calcium (RR 0.97, 95% CI 0.93–1.01)
- **Adverse effects of vitamin D+calcium:** hypercalcemia RR 2.28 (worse for calcitriol specifically, RR 4.41), GI symptoms RR 1.04, renal disease RR 1.16 (95% CI 1.02–1.33)

**NIH ODS health-outcomes review (citing FNB and AHRQ syntheses of ~250 studies, 2009–2013):** both bodies concluded evidence was inadequate/contradictory to establish vitamin D benefit for any outcome **other than bone health**. Specifically:
- **Cancer:** most research found no relationship between serum 25(OH)D and cancer risk; clinical trials generally failed to show supplementation reduces cancer incidence
- **Cardiovascular disease:** observational association with lower CVD risk, but clinical trials have NOT confirmed this — several trials found no CVD risk reduction even in low-25(OH)D populations
- **Depression:** low levels associated with depression risk observationally, but supplementation has NOT been shown to prevent or treat depression in trials
- **Multiple sclerosis:** observational association with MS risk/progression, but trial evidence limited and supplementation does NOT appear to reduce signs/symptoms/relapse
- **Type 2 diabetes:** inverse observational association, but supplementation does NOT help glucose homeostasis or diabetes progression/management
- **Weight loss:** evidence does NOT support vitamin D supplements for weight loss despite an observational association between obesity and low vitamin D status

**This is a deliberately preserved, extensively-negative-evidence framing:** vitamin D is genuinely one of the most over-claimed supplements relative to its actual trial evidence, per NIH's own review — its solid ground is bone health (with calcium) and correcting frank deficiency, not the broader wellness/immune/mood claims commonly marketed alongside it.

## Pharmacokinetics
Both D2 and D3 well absorbed in the small intestine via passive diffusion and carrier-protein-mediated mechanisms; dietary fat enhances but is not required for absorption. Neither aging nor obesity alters gut absorption (though obesity is separately associated with lower circulating vitamin D status via volumetric dilution in adipose tissue — not detailed further in the sources reviewed).

## Contraindications
Not itemized specifically in the sources reviewed beyond the excessive-intake toxicity risks below.

## Key Safety Data
- **Toxicity:** hypercalcemia, hypercalciuria, high serum 25(OH)D; extreme cases — renal failure, soft-tissue calcification, cardiac arrhythmias, death. **Almost always from excessive supplement intake, not food or sun exposure.**
- Combining calcium + vitamin D supplements may increase certain adverse effect risks (see Cochrane data above: hypercalcemia, GI symptoms, renal disease)
- **Deficiency risk groups:** lactose intolerance, milk allergy, ovo-vegetarian/vegan diets, limited sun exposure, impaired renal 25(OH)D activation, malabsorption conditions; also breastfed infants, older adults, dark skin, obesity, gastric bypass history

## Drug Interactions
May interact with: **orlistat, statins, steroids, thiazide diuretics** (bidirectional — some of these drugs affect vitamin D levels, and vitamin D status may affect how these drugs perform).

## Special Population Flags
- **Breastfed infants:** at-risk group for inadequacy (breast milk is typically low in vitamin D)
- **Older adults:** at-risk group; also the population studied in the fracture-prevention Cochrane data above
- **Obesity/gastric bypass:** at-risk group for inadequacy
- **Dark skin, limited sun exposure:** at-risk groups given the UV-dependent endogenous synthesis pathway

## Overdose / Toxicity
See Key Safety Data above — Tolerable Upper Intake Level 25–100 mcg (1,000–4,000 IU) depending on age; exceeding this chronically via supplements (not food/sun) risks hypercalcemia/hypercalciuria and, in extreme cases, renal failure, soft tissue calcification, cardiac arrhythmias, and death.
