# Boric Acid

## Indication
Intravaginal use for symptoms of vaginal yeast infection (itching, burning, irritation, discharge, odor). Clinical literature specifically supports it as a second-line/alternative option for **recurrent or non-albicans/azole-resistant** vulvovaginal candidiasis, not as a general first-line treatment the way clotrimazole or miconazole are used for uncomplicated cases (see [[clotrimazole]]).

## Mechanism
Fungistatic (not fungicidal) activity against *Candida* species; its effectiveness is unrelated to pH, per in vitro data cited in the 1981 Van Slyke trial. Minimal vaginal absorption occurs, with a blood boron half-life under 12 hours.

## Dosing
Per the "THE KILLER" 600mg suppository OTC label: insert 1 suppository vaginally, preferably at bedtime, for 7 days (up to 14 days for chronic irritation), or use as a spot treatment. The classic 1981 RCT protocol used 600mg boric acid powder in a gelatin capsule, once daily for 14 days.

**Formulation warning — read before assuming any "boric acid suppository" contains a real dose:** not every product marketed this way contains a pharmacological dose. A DailyMed-registered product ("V-BELLA") lists its active ingredient as "Boricum Acidum 3X HPUS" — a **homeopathic dilution**, not equivalent to the 600mg dose used in the clinical trials below — with its own label explicitly stating the FDA has not evaluated it for safety or efficacy. Always check the actual "Active Ingredient" line and mg amount on a specific product's label rather than assuming "boric acid suppository" branding implies the studied dose.

## Efficacy Evidence

**Van Slyke et al. 1981 (PMID 7282789, Am J Obstet Gynecol)** — double-blind RCT, 600mg boric acid vs. 100,000 U nystatin, both as 14-day daily intravaginal capsules, for *Candida albicans* vulvovaginitis:
- Cure rate, boric acid: **92%** at 7–10 days, **72%** at 30 days
- Cure rate, nystatin: **64%** at 7–10 days, **50%** at 30 days
- Speed of symptom relief similar between arms; no untoward side effects reported; cervical cytology unaffected

**Iavazzo et al. 2011 systematic review (PMID 21774671, J Womens Health)** — 14 studies total (only **2 RCTs**, 9 case series, 4 case reports), boric acid vs. nystatin/terconazole/flucytosine/itraconazole/clotrimazole/ketoconazole/fluconazole/buconazole/miconazole, monotherapy in 7 studies:
- **Mycologic cure rates ranged 40%–100%** across the included studies — a wide range reflecting heterogeneous study quality/design, not a single reliable number
- 4 of 9 case series reported statistically significant cure-rate outcomes
- No study found a statistically significant difference in RECURRENCE rates vs. comparators
- Adverse effects: vaginal burning (<10% of cases), watery discharge, vaginal erythema (across 7 studies)
- Authors' conclusion: boric acid is a safe, economical **alternative** specifically for recurrent/chronic vaginitis when conventional azole treatment fails due to non-albicans or azole-resistant *Candida* — not a general first-line recommendation

## Pharmacokinetics
Minimal systemic absorption from vaginal application; blood boron half-life under 12 hours per the 1981 trial's boron analysis.

## Contraindications
**Oral ingestion — toxic if swallowed** (vaginal-use-only product; labels direct contacting Poison Control immediately if swallowed). Not indicated for treating sexually transmitted infections. Per label: consult a doctor before use if pregnant, nursing, or experiencing vaginal bleeding, sores, fever, or pelvic pain.

## Key Safety Data
- Vaginal burning sensation: **<10%** of cases (Iavazzo et al. 2011, pooled across 7 studies)
- Watery discharge during treatment and vaginal erythema also reported across the same 7 studies, without a precise combined incidence given
- No untoward systemic side effects in the 1981 RCT; cervical cytology unaffected
- **Toxic if swallowed** — this is a real, serious risk distinct from its topical/vaginal safety profile, given boric acid's known systemic toxicity via the oral/ingestion route

## Drug Interactions
Not addressed with specific interaction data in the sources gathered. Label warns against concurrent tampons, douches, spermicides, and vaginal intercourse during treatment, and notes latex condoms/diaphragms may be compromised (damaged, reducing contraceptive/STI protection reliability) during use.

## Special Population Flags
- **Pregnancy:** labels direct consulting a doctor before use; this database's prior framing flagged a "specific pregnancy safety concern" — treat as NOT routinely safe in pregnancy without medical guidance, consistent with the conservative label language found here.
- **Pediatric:** "THE KILLER" label directs consulting a doctor if uncertain; specific pediatric dosing was not given in the sources gathered (product appears adult-marketed).

## Overdose / Toxicity
Vaginal-use-only. If swallowed: keep out of reach of children; contact Poison Control (1-800-222-1222) immediately per label. No specific overdose case data was located in this pass beyond this general label warning.
