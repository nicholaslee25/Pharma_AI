# Vaginal Lubricants and Moisturizers (General)

**Type:** Active Ingredient (class — two distinct product types)
**Drug Class:** Lubricant (water-based, silicone-based, oil-based) / vaginal moisturizer
**Verification Status:** AI-compiled from primary sources, unverified — not yet reviewed by a pharmacist/physician
**Evidence Grade:** Established for symptomatic comfort as a class, but real product-specific testing shows meaningful differences in tissue-safety between brands — including three of this database's top four best-selling products in this exact category
**Last updated:** 2026-08-26
**Sources:** raw/primary-sources/vaginal-lubricants-moisturizers/statpearls-gsm.md, raw/primary-sources/vaginal-lubricants-moisturizers/pubmed-edwards-panay-2015-osmolality.md

## Indication
Lubricants: reducing friction during sexual activity. Moisturizers: baseline vaginal dryness, used regularly (not just around sexual activity). Both are considered **first-line, nonhormonal treatment for genitourinary syndrome of menopause (GSM)** per StatPearls, alongside regular sexual activity, pelvic floor physical therapy, and vaginal dilators.

## Scale of the Underlying Problem (StatPearls) — real, quantified undertreatment
GSM affects an estimated 14-87% of postmenopausal women (40-54% report bothersome symptoms); **75% experience vaginal dryness, 40% report painful intercourse.** Yet **over 50% of affected patients use no treatment at all, ~70% never discuss it with a healthcare provider, and fewer than 25% receive appropriate care** — a substantial, quantified care gap that makes accessible OTC options (lubricants/moisturizers) genuinely important, even though they are not the most effective available treatment (local vaginal estrogen achieves 80-90% symptomatic success vs. lubricants/moisturizers' comfort-only role).

## Mechanism
Lubricants act mechanically (reducing friction) via water-based, silicone-based, or oil-based formulations. Moisturizers address baseline tissue hydration on a regular-use basis rather than around sexual activity specifically.

## Efficacy/Safety Evidence — a real, brand-specific finding

**Edwards & Panay 2015 (PMID 26707589, Climacteric)** — reviewed osmolality and pH of commercial vaginal lubricants/moisturizers against WHO safety standards:
- **WHO recommends osmolality NOT exceed 380 mOsm/kg**; in practice, since most commercial products exceed this, an upper limit of 1,200 mOsm/kg is generally deemed acceptable. Normal vaginal pH is 3.8–4.5.
- Using slug mucosal irritation assays (a standard epithelial-irritancy model): hypo-osmotic lubricants (32–316 mOsm/kg) showed no adverse effects.
- **Replens measured 2,143 mOsm/kg and K-Y Jelly measured 2,463 mOsm/kg — both moderately hyperosmotic, causing mild-to-moderate irritation in testing.**
- **Astroglide measured 5,848 mOsm/kg — very hyperosmotic, causing SEVERE irritation and tissue damage in testing.**
- Separate research confirms hyperosmolar lubricants cause epithelial cell damage and INCREASE susceptibility to infections including HSV-2 — a real, biologically-grounded mechanism, not just a discomfort issue.
- For comparison, other tested products measured well within the WHO range: Yes Water-Based (154 mOsm/kg), Balance Activ (309 mOsm/kg) — showing that low-osmolality formulations are commercially available, this is not an unavoidable category-wide problem.

**Why this matters directly for this database:** per this database's own Pharmacy Times subcategory data (below), **K-Y (39%), Replens (16%), and Astroglide (11%) are three of the four top-selling products in this exact category — together representing 66% of all recommendations — and all three measured well above the WHO's recommended osmolality ceiling in independent testing**, with Astroglide specifically showing severe irritation/tissue damage in the cited assay. This is a genuine, specific, evidence-backed safety consideration for the most commonly recommended products in this space, not a generic ingredient-class caveat.

## Key Safety Data
- **Oil-based lubricants can damage latex condoms** — a specific, practical, well-established safety point (material compatibility, not a biological/tissue-safety issue).
- Hyperosmolar products (see above): epithelial cell damage, increased HSV-2 susceptibility, and dose-dependent irritation severity (mild-to-moderate at ~2,100-2,500 mOsm/kg, severe at ~5,800 mOsm/kg in the cited testing).

## Contraindications/Interactions
Oil-based lubricants + latex condoms: material damage/failure risk (condom integrity, not a drug interaction in the pharmacologic sense).

## Special Population Flags
Postmenopausal women with GSM symptoms are the population most directly addressed by this product category; StatPearls notes local vaginal estrogen therapy achieves 80-90% symptomatic success (vs. lubricants/moisturizers' comfort-focused, non-curative role) and should be considered by patients not adequately helped by OTC options — a real escalation pathway worth naming rather than implying OTC products are the ceiling of available treatment.

## Formulation Caveat
Lubricant type (water-based, silicone-based, oil-based) determines condom compatibility and other practical properties. Lubricants and moisturizers are a distinct pair of product types (per-use vs. regular-use), not interchangeable names for the same thing. **Osmolality and pH are real, measurable, product-specific properties that vary substantially between brands** (154 mOsm/kg to >5,800 mOsm/kg among products cited in the source above) — "water-based" alone does not guarantee a low-osmolality, tissue-safe formulation.

## Pharmacy Times Context (recommendation share — NOT efficacy)
Per this database's own subcategory file (Pharmacy Times 2026 OTC Guide, ~1,700 pharmacists surveyed): **K-Y — 39%**, **Replens — 16%**, Vagisil 14%, **Astroglide — 11%**, Good Clean Love 5%, RepHresh 5%, Pre-Seed Fertility-Friendly Lubricant 4%, Luvena 2%, pH-D Boric Acid Moisturizing Gel 2% (see [[boric-acid]] — a different active ingredient entirely), Swiss Navy 1%, Trojan 1% of Vaginal Moisturizers and Lubricants recommendations (737,395/month category volume). **This is a pharmacist recommendation share, not a safety ranking** — and per the osmolality data above, the three highest-recommended brands (K-Y, Replens, Astroglide) are specifically the ones independently measured as hyperosmolar in the cited testing, a real disconnect between market popularity and measured tissue-safety data worth surfacing rather than glossing over.

## Brands Using This Ingredient (in this database — formulation not individually verified per SKU)
K-Y, Replens, Astroglide — directly named and osmolality-tested in the Edwards & Panay 2015 source above (2,463, 2,143, and 5,848 mOsm/kg respectively), all measured above the WHO-recommended 380 mOsm/kg ceiling. Vagisil, Good Clean Love, RepHresh, Pre-Seed Fertility-Friendly Lubricant, Luvena, Swiss Navy, Trojan — filed under this category by real-world market identity, not individually osmolality-tested in the source reviewed here. pH-D Boric Acid Moisturizing Gel is a boric-acid product, a different active ingredient and mechanism — see [[boric-acid]].

**K-Y confirmed 2026-08-28 — a real, serious independent safety finding beyond osmolality alone:** Johns Hopkins researchers found K-Y Warming Jelly specifically (osmolality reportedly >30x that of normal body fluid) increased herpes simplex virus transmission more than NINEFOLD in a mouse model compared to no lubricant, and separate research on hyperosmolar lubricants as a class (K-Y included) documents disruption of the vaginal epithelial barrier and toxicity to protective lactobacilli — a proposed real mechanism connecting hyperosmolality to increased STI/bacterial-vaginosis susceptibility, not just discomfort. This is the single most concrete brand-specific safety finding located in this pass for this category's largest-share product (39%). See `drugs/K-Y.md`.

**Replens and RepHresh confirmed 2026-08-28 to share the same functional mechanism** — both are polycarbophil/carbomer-based bioadhesive gels (not lactic-acid-based, contrary to some marketing framing) designed to adhere to the vaginal wall and release moisture over time; Replens holds FDA 510(k) clearance (K101098/K101241) as a Class II medical device for vaginal moisturizing/lubrication, not as a drug. RepHresh's own label explicitly states the product "is not intended to prevent or treat vaginal infections" — worth flagging against its own "odor eliminating" marketing claim, since consumers may reasonably associate odor elimination with an infection-related benefit despite the explicit disclaimer. See `drugs/Replens.md` and `drugs/RepHresh.md`.

**Good Clean Love confirmed 2026-08-28** at a genuinely lower, WHO-closer osmolality range (250–400 mOsm/kg, aloe-vera-based) than K-Y/Replens/Astroglide above — a real positive contrast, though the top of its own stated range (400) still exceeds the WHO's strict 380 ceiling even as it sits far below the thousands-of-mOsm/kg figures measured for the category's top three brands. See `drugs/Good Clean Love.md`.

**Pre-Seed Fertility-Friendly Lubricant confirmed 2026-08-28** to carry real, brand-specific, independently published fertility-focused evidence distinct from this page's general osmolality/GSM framework: a 2014 Fertility and Sterility study (SUNY Upstate) found Pre-Seed had no significant adverse effect on sperm motility/health in an in vitro fertilization-mimicking model, while several Astroglide and K-Y products tested in the SAME study showed significant deleterious effects on sperm — a direct, brand-specific head-to-head contrast with two of this category's top-selling competitors. A later 2022 study is more mixed, finding Pre-Seed still caused some reduction in sperm forward progression versus untreated controls (egg white was the only tested substance with no reduction), while remaining superior to some other "fertility-friendly" competitors — a genuinely two-sided, not uniformly positive, evidence picture. See `drugs/Pre-Seed Fertility-Friendly Lubricant.md`.

**Swiss Navy and Trojan confirmed 2026-08-28** to each sell BOTH a silicone-based (Swiss Navy: cyclopentasiloxane/dimethicone, contains no water, will not affect vaginal pH by definition) and a water-based line (Trojan H2O: deionized water/glycerin/hydroxyethylcellulose/sodium hyaluronate) under one brand name — silicone-based formulations are not covered by the osmolality framework above at all (osmolality is a property of aqueous solutions), a real formulation-category distinction from every other brand on this page. See `drugs/Swiss Navy.md` and `drugs/Trojan.md`.
