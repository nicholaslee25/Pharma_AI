# Naproxen

**Type:** Active Ingredient
**Drug Class:** NSAID (propionic acid derivative), non-selective COX-1/COX-2 inhibitor (slightly more COX-1 selective)
**Verification Status:** AI-compiled from primary sources, unverified — not yet reviewed by a pharmacist/physician
**Evidence Grade:** High for acute moderate-to-severe pain (Cochrane postoperative-pain model, 996 participants, favorable NNT); general NSAID-class evidence otherwise
**Last updated:** 2026-08-25
**Sources:** raw/primary-sources/naproxen/statpearls-naproxen.md, raw/primary-sources/naproxen/dailymed-aleve.md, raw/primary-sources/naproxen/cochrane-postop-pain-summary.md

## Indication
FDA-approved: acute gout, ankylosing spondylitis, bursitis, polyarticular juvenile idiopathic arthritis, osteoarthritis, tendonitis, rheumatoid arthritis, pain, primary dysmenorrhea. Off-label: acute migraine treatment and prophylaxis. OTC (naproxen sodium, approved 1994): minor aches — arthritis pain, muscular aches, backache, menstrual cramps, headache, toothache, cold-symptom aches, fever reduction.

## Mechanism
Non-selective COX-1/COX-2 inhibitor, slightly more selective for COX-1. Blocks arachidonate binding, preventing conversion of arachidonic acid to prostaglandin G; reduces prostaglandin E/F production that sensitizes pain receptors — analgesic and anti-inflammatory effect.

## Dosing

| Population | Dose | Max |
|---|---|---|
| Adult OTC (naproxen sodium) | 220 mg (1 caplet) q8–12h; up to 440mg first dose if needed | 660 mg/24h (3 caplets) |
| Adult, mild-moderate arthritis (Rx) | 220–550 mg PO q12h | 1650 mg/day, up to 6 months |
| Adult, acute gouty arthritis | 825 mg initial, then 275 mg q8h until resolution | — |
| Adult, acute severe headache/migraine (Rx) | Naproxen sodium 550 mg q12h, may increase to 825 mg | 1375 mg/day |
| Pediatric ≥12y | 20 mg/kg/day | 1000 mg/day (Rx) / 660 mg/day (OTC) |
| Pediatric 2–12y | 20 mg/kg/day | 1000 mg/day (Rx only — OTC not recommended) |
| Pediatric <2y | Safety/efficacy not established | — |

**Renal:** no adjustment if CrCl ≥30 mL/min; not recommended if CrCl <30 mL/min.

## Efficacy Evidence — Cochrane, single-dose acute postoperative pain (Mason et al. 2004, PMID 15495091)
10 RCTs, 996 adults with moderate-to-severe acute postoperative pain, ≥50% pain relief over 4–6h as outcome:
- **Naproxen sodium 550 mg** (6 trials, n=500): RR 4.2 (95% CI 2.9–6.0); NNT **2.6** (95% CI 2.2–3.2)
- **Naproxen 400mg / naproxen sodium 440mg** (3 trials, n=334): RR 4.8 (95% CI 2.75–8.38)
- Naproxen 200mg / naproxen sodium 220mg: two small studies suggest effective, insufficient data for pooled NNT
- Time to remedication: naproxen sodium 550mg 7.6h vs. placebo 2.6h — reflects naproxen's longer duration of action vs. shorter-acting NSAIDs like ibuprofen
- No significant difference in overall adverse-event rate vs. placebo, though AE reporting was inconsistent across included trials
- **Note:** a 2009 Cochrane update of this review exists (Derry C, et al., PMID 19160232) which was not independently re-verified in this pass — the 2004 data above is what this vault's citation index originally pointed to and what was directly confirmed live against PubMed.

## Pharmacokinetics
Extensive hepatic metabolism; ~95% excreted in urine; half-life 12–17h (notably longer than ibuprofen's ~2h, explaining naproxen's longer dosing interval and longer post-dose AE exposure window). Naproxen sodium absorbed faster than naproxen base. Can be taken with food/milk/antacids/PPI/misoprostol to reduce GI effects.

## Contraindications
- Documented NSAID hypersensitivity
- NSAID/aspirin-induced asthma (aspirin triad)
- Pregnancy ≥30 weeks gestation (StatPearls); OTC label separately warns against use in the last 3 months of pregnancy generally unless directed by a doctor
- Perioperative CABG surgery

## Key Safety Data
- **GI:** COX-1 inhibition reduces gastric mucosal prostaglandin protection more than COX-2 inhibition does — GI ulcer/bleed risk is a class effect shared with other NSAIDs
- **Renal:** NSAIDs can significantly decrease renal blood flow when perfusion is prostaglandin-dependent (dehydration, CHF, pre-existing renal disease) → AKI/renal failure risk; sodium/water retention can raise BP in hypertensive patients
- **Platelet:** COX-1 inhibition in platelets dose-dependently reduces thromboxane A2 and increases bleeding time, reversible on discontinuation; clinical bleeding-risk data are mixed
- **Cardiovascular — real, contested regulatory history (2026-08-27 addition):** per OTC label, NSAIDs (except aspirin) increase risk of heart attack, heart failure, and stroke, which can be fatal — risk increases with longer use. In 2004, the FDA warned after an NIH-halted colon-adenoma-prevention trial (~2,500 patients, naproxen 2 tablets/day for 3 years — above the OTC max dose) found a 50% higher heart attack/stroke risk (~70 of 2,500 affected, 23 deaths), prompting the "not more than 10 days" OTC guidance. However, a 2014 FDA joint advisory committee reviewing post-2005 data found some members believed naproxen showed a comparatively more favorable CV profile than other NSAIDs — but the committee's official (non-unanimous) recommendation was that the evidence did NOT meet the bar to support a formal "naproxen is safer" label claim, and the FDA's July 2015 label update strengthened the warning uniformly across all non-aspirin NSAIDs rather than treating naproxen differently. This remains a genuinely unresolved, debated question — not settled in either direction. See `drugs/Aleve.md`.
- **Serious but rare (StatPearls):** blood dyscrasias, Stevens-Johnson syndrome, MI, stroke, heart failure, anaphylaxis
- No specific incidence percentages were located for naproxen specifically in the sources reviewed (unlike ibuprofen's FDA label, which gives precise numbers) — flagged as a gap rather than filled with an estimate

## Drug Interactions
Not itemized with specific interaction data in the sources reviewed here beyond the general cautions below; see [[ibuprofen]] for detailed same-class NSAID interaction data (aspirin antiplatelet interference, ACE inhibitors, diuretics, lithium, methotrexate, warfarin) that likely applies similarly to naproxen as a class effect, though this was not independently confirmed for naproxen specifically in this pass.
- **Relative-caution conditions (StatPearls):** recent MI/heart disease, hypertension, CHF, fluid retention/edema, dehydration, GI history (PUD/GERD), bleeding/coagulopathy, hepatic/renal disease, asthma, sodium restriction, chronic alcohol/tobacco use, elderly, women actively attempting conception
- **OTC label:** ask a doctor/pharmacist before use if taking a blood thinner, another NSAID/aspirin-containing product, or consuming ≥3 alcoholic drinks/day

## Special Population Flags
- **Pregnancy:** avoid in 1st trimester (StatPearls); contraindicated ≥30 weeks gestation; OTC label separately advises against use in the last 3 months generally unless a doctor directs otherwise
- **Elderly:** start with lower doses — increased risk from cardiovascular disease, CKD, GI ulcer/bleed history; OTC label specifically flags age ≥60 as a stomach-bleeding risk factor
- **Renal impairment:** avoid if CrCl <30 mL/min
- **Hepatic impairment:** no specific dosing guideline in StatPearls; use lowest doses with caution
- **Pediatric:** OTC use not recommended under age 12; prescription dosing exists down to age 2 (StatPearls)

## Overdose / Toxicity
Naproxen overdose is common given OTC availability but usually mild (StatPearls). No specific antidote — supportive care and vital-sign monitoring. Activated charcoal benefit uncertain. Hemodialysis generally not recommended due to high protein binding, though may help select cases with renal impairment or acid-base disturbance. Large overdoses can cause seizures and metabolic acidosis, potentially leading to renal failure.
