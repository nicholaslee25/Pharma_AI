# Aspirin

**Type:** Active Ingredient
**Drug Class:** NSAID (salicylate) / irreversible COX-1 inhibitor and platelet inhibitor
**Verification Status:** AI-compiled from primary sources, unverified — not yet reviewed by a pharmacist/physician
**Evidence Grade:** High for acute analgesia/antipyresis (Cochrane-level dose-response evidence); low-dose cardiovascular primary-prevention benefit applies to a substantially narrower population than once assumed
**Last updated:** 2026-08-25
**Sources:** raw/primary-sources/aspirin/statpearls-aspirin.md, raw/primary-sources/aspirin/dailymed-bayer-genuine-aspirin.md, raw/primary-sources/aspirin/cochrane-derry-2012-postop-pain.md

## Indication
OTC: minor aches/pains (headache, muscle pain, toothache, menstrual pain, cold-symptom aches, minor arthritis pain), fever reduction. Prescription/clinical: acute coronary syndrome, MI treatment/prophylaxis, unstable angina/NSTEMI, secondary ASCVD prevention, selected primary ASCVD prevention, acute ischemic stroke, stroke prophylaxis, post-PCI, dual antiplatelet therapy, ankylosing spondylitis, RA (off-label), acute migraine. Off-label: colorectal cancer prevention, Kawasaki disease, SLE.

## Mechanism
Irreversibly inhibits COX-1 and acetylates/alters COX-2 function — unlike reversible-COX NSAIDs. Because platelets cannot synthesize new COX, aspirin's thromboxane A2 suppression produces a prolonged (7–10 day) antiplatelet effect lasting the platelet lifespan. ~90% COX inhibition achieved at 160–325mg. Also redirects arachidonic acid toward the lipoxygenase pathway (pro-resolving lipoxins/resolvins) while increasing leukotriene production — the mechanistic basis for aspirin-exacerbated respiratory disease (AERD/Samter triad).

## Dosing

| Indication | Dose |
|---|---|
| OTC pain/fever | 325–650mg q4–6h PRN, max 4g/day |
| ACS loading | 162–325mg chewed |
| Secondary ASCVD / post-ACS maintenance | 75–100mg/day |
| Primary ASCVD prevention (selected, ages 40–70) | 75–100mg/day |
| Acute ischemic stroke | 325mg within 24–48h onset (delay 24h if IV alteplase given) |
| RA/inflammation | 2–4g/day divided |
| Acute migraine | 1000mg |
| Colorectal cancer prevention (off-label) | 160mg/day, 3 years post-surgery |
| DAPT (with P2Y12 inhibitor) | 81mg/day, ≥12 months minimum |

Bayer Genuine Aspirin OTC label: 1–2 tablets (325–650mg) q4h, or 3 tablets (975mg) q6h, max 12 tablets (3900mg)/24h.

## Efficacy Evidence — Cochrane, single-dose acute postoperative pain (Derry et al. 2012, PMID 22513905)
68 studies, doses 300–1200mg (majority 600/650mg, n=2409, 64 studies; or 990/1000mg, n=380, 8 studies), ≥50% pain relief over 4–6h as outcome:
- **600/650mg:** NNT **4.2** (95% CI 3.9–4.8)
- **900/1000mg:** NNT **3.8** (95% CI 3.0–5.1)
- **1200mg:** NNT **2.7** (95% CI 2.0–3.8)
- Doses below 600mg were **not significantly different from placebo**
- Fewer needed rescue medication with aspirin over 4–8h; no difference by 12h
- Adverse events not significantly different from placebo at 600/650mg; at 900/1000mg, **NNH 7.5** (95% CI 4.8–17) — mostly dizziness, drowsiness, gastric irritation, nausea, vomiting, mild-to-moderate
- **Authors' conclusion:** effective analgesic; higher doses more effective but more AEs; pain relief "very similar milligram for milligram" to acetaminophen

**Note on this database's "one rule that matters most":** aspirin's cardiovascular role (low-dose 75–325mg for CV prevention) and its OTC analgesic role (325–650mg+ for pain/fever) are pharmacologically related but clinically distinct uses at different doses — this page keeps them separately labeled rather than blending "aspirin works for X" into one undifferentiated claim.

## Pharmacokinetics
Peak plasma 0.3–2h (liquid forms faster than tablets). Extensively albumin-bound; Vd 0.16 L/kg; crosses placenta and enters breast milk. Low-dose kinetics are first-order; at higher/repeated doses, metabolism saturates (pseudo-zero-order), causing a sharp rise in salicylate levels and a narrow therapeutic-toxic margin — directly relevant to why aspirin overdose is more dangerous at high/chronic dosing than the linear-kinetics intuition would suggest.

## Contraindications
NSAID/aspirin hypersensitivity; NSAID-associated bronchospastic asthma; peptic ulcer disease/gastritis; inherited coagulopathies (hemophilia); acquired bleeding diatheses (dengue, yellow fever); G6PD deficiency; **viral infection in children/teens (Reye syndrome risk)**; severe hepatic insufficiency; severe renal impairment (CrCl <10 mL/min). Avoid in gout (reduces uric acid excretion even at low doses); discontinue if tinnitus develops.

## Key Safety Data
- GI irritation is the most common adverse effect, ranging from gastritis to GI bleeding
- Hypersensitivity reactions in ~1–2% of patients, ranging from mild rash to angioedema/anaphylaxis
- AERD (Samter triad — asthma + recurrent nasal polyps + NSAID hypersensitivity): aspirin desensitization may improve pulmonary outcomes in affected patients
- **Reye syndrome:** rare but serious, 30–45% mortality, encephalopathy with fatty liver degeneration; incidence has fallen substantially due to preferential use of acetaminophen for pediatric fever
- Increased intracerebral hemorrhage risk, especially in low-BMI or Asian-ethnicity patients
- Elderly (AGS Beers Criteria): recommend AGAINST initiating aspirin for primary CV prevention — bleeding risk rises substantially with age while benefit is minimal; consider deprescribing in patients already on it for primary prevention only

## Drug Interactions
- **Methotrexate:** decreased renal MTX clearance, increased toxicity — avoid combination
- **Anticoagulants (warfarin, heparin) and antiplatelets (clopidogrel, dipyridamole):** synergistic bleeding risk
- **SSRIs:** synergistic upper GI bleeding risk (platelet serotonin depletion + aspirin)
- **Uricosurics (probenecid, sulfinpyrazone):** aspirin reverses their uricosuric effect
- **Digoxin, lithium:** aspirin reduces renal clearance of both — monitor levels
- **Diuretics, ACE inhibitors:** increased AKI risk
- **Acetazolamide:** may cause severe metabolic acidosis (synergistic bicarbonate lowering)
- **Corticosteroids, other NSAIDs:** synergistic GI bleeding/ulceration
- **Valproate, phenytoin:** aspirin displaces from protein binding — monitor free phenytoin levels specifically
- **Alcohol:** increased GI bleeding risk

## Special Population Flags
- **Pediatric:** contraindicated during/after viral illness due to Reye syndrome risk — this is the single most important pediatric safety rule for this ingredient
- **Kawasaki disease:** standard-risk patients get IV immunoglobulin + aspirin, but evidence shows no significant advantage of medium/high-dose over low-dose aspirin, and similar coronary outcomes even when aspirin is omitted in the acute phase
- **Pregnancy:** low-dose aspirin in the 3rd trimester is NOT associated with premature ductal closure (unlike other NSAIDs); 81mg/day recommended from 12–28 weeks gestation for high preeclampsia risk factors; current evidence does NOT support prophylactic low-dose aspirin for preventing early pregnancy loss, growth restriction, stillbirth, or preterm birth absent preeclampsia risk; OTC label separately warns against use in the last 3 months of pregnancy generally
- **Breastfeeding:** excreted into milk, disproportionately more at higher maternal doses; long-term high-dose maternal use can cause infant metabolic acidosis; use alternatives for high-dose maternal therapy
- **Renal impairment:** avoid if CrCl <10 mL/min; KDIGO supports low-dose aspirin for secondary ASCVD prevention in CKD patients with established disease
- **Hepatic impairment:** use cautiously — impaired metabolism raises salicylate levels and bleeding risk

## Overdose / Toxicity
Mild: tinnitus, dizziness, lethargy, nausea, vomiting. Severe: hyperthermia, tachypnea, respiratory alkalosis, high anion-gap metabolic acidosis, hypokalemia, hypoglycemia, seizures, coma, cerebral edema; death often via cardiopulmonary failure/pulmonary edema. Therapeutic level 150–300 mcg/mL; toxic >300 mcg/mL. **Management:** urinary alkalinization (cornerstone — bicarbonate drip + potassium supplementation), GI decontamination (activated charcoal/bowel irrigation), airway/ventilation support as needed (maintain pH ≤7.5 with bicarbonate if intubated). **Hemodialysis** indicated for: acute level ≥100 mg/dL, chronic level ≥40 mg/dL, neurotoxicity at any level, renal failure, acute pulmonary edema, or cardiovascular compromise.
