# Moisturizers / Ceramides (Therapeutic Skincare)

**Type:** Active Ingredient (class)
**Drug Class:** Therapeutic moisturizer (occlusives, humectants, emollients: ceramides, petrolatum, glycerin, urea)
**Verification Status:** AI-compiled from primary sources, unverified — not yet reviewed by a pharmacist/physician
**Evidence Grade:** Moderate-to-strong for TREATING existing eczema (real Cochrane flare-reduction data) — but a separate, equally real Cochrane finding shows emollients probably do NOT prevent eczema from developing in infants and probably increase local skin infections when used that way. These are two different questions with two different answers; brand recommendation share must not be read as an efficacy percentage.

## Indication
Atopic dermatitis/eczema management, xerosis, ichthyosis, psoriasis, and general dry-skin/therapeutic skincare (StatPearls).

## Mechanism
Three functional categories (StatPearls):
- **Occlusives** (petrolatum, mineral oil, paraffin, squalene, dimethicone) form a physical barrier reducing water evaporation — **petrolatum reduces water loss by ~99%**, the single hardest number in the mechanism literature (see [[petrolatum]]).
- **Humectants** (glycerin, hyaluronic acid, alpha-hydroxy acids) attract/bind water to the skin; AHAs also promote desquamation and increase ceramide levels. Important nuance: humectants used WITHOUT an occlusive can actually increase transepidermal water loss.
- **Emollients** (shea butter, ceramides) smooth/soften skin by filling gaps between skin cells; ceramides specifically are sphingolipids depleted in atopic dermatitis skin, contributing to the barrier dysfunction underlying the disease.

## Efficacy Evidence — TWO DIFFERENT QUESTIONS, TWO DIFFERENT ANSWERS

**van Zuuren et al. 2017 (PMID 28432721, abridged Cochrane review, Br J Dermatol) — TREATING existing eczema:**
- 77 RCTs, 6,603 participants (quality caveat: only 9% low risk of bias, 47% high risk)
- Moisturizer vs. no moisturizer, SCORAD severity: MD -2.42 (95% CI -4.55 to -0.28) — statistically real, but did NOT reach the pre-specified minimal important clinical difference of 8.7
- **Fewer flares with moisturizer: RR 0.40 (95% CI 0.23–0.70)** — a substantial real reduction
- Topical corticosteroid use reduced by 9.30g over 6-8 weeks in moisturizer-using groups — a real steroid-sparing effect
- Glycyrrhetinic acid-, urea-, and glycerol-containing creams outperformed vehicle/placebo/no-moisturizer controls
- Moisturizer ALONE had more flares than moisturizer + twice-weekly fluticasone (RR 2.17, 95% CI 1.55–3.11) — moisturizer is not a substitute for anti-inflammatory treatment when that's indicated, but adding moisturizer to anti-inflammatory treatment beat anti-inflammatory alone

**Kelleher et al. 2021 (PMID 33550675, Cochrane IPD meta-analysis) — PREVENTING eczema onset in healthy-skinned infants (a completely different question):**
- 33 trials identified, 17 with usable data (5,823 participants), 10 in IPD analysis (5,154 participants); infants <1 year, no pre-existing eczema, emollients applied for first 3-12 months
- **Eczema risk by age 1-3: RR 1.03 (95% CI 0.81–1.31, MODERATE certainty) — essentially NO preventive effect**
- Food allergy risk: unclear, possibly increased — RR 2.53 (95% CI 0.99–6.47, VERY LOW certainty)
- **Local skin infection risk PROBABLY INCREASED: RR 1.34 (95% CI 1.02–1.77, MODERATE certainty)**
- **Authors' conclusion: regular emollients during infancy probably do NOT prevent eczema and probably INCREASE local skin infections**

**This distinction matters and should never be collapsed:** moisturizing skin that already has eczema is a real, moderately effective treatment; moisturizing healthy infant skin specifically to try to PREVENT eczema from ever developing does not work and carries a real infection-risk signal. This database's typical OTC customer (someone with existing dry skin/eczema buying CeraVe, Eucerin, etc.) matches the first scenario — but the second finding is directly relevant to a parent asking about prophylactic infant skincare.

## Key Safety Data
No specific adverse-effect data for moisturizers in general was given in StatPearls beyond the humectant-without-occlusive caveat above. The Kelleher 2021 review's local-skin-infection signal (RR 1.34) is the most concrete quantified harm located in this pass, specific to the infant-prevention-use context.

## Formulation Caveat
The evidence is for the functional ingredient categories (occlusives, humectants, emollients/ceramides) and the treat-vs-prevent distinction above — not for any single branded product as a whole. A brand's recommendation share should not be read as validating its specific formulation's efficacy over another brand's.
