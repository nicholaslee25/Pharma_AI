## Indication
**Topical (OTC, per DailyMed Lamisil AT label):** athlete's foot (tinea pedis), jock itch (tinea cruris), ringworm (tinea corporis); relieves itching, burning, cracking, scaling accompanying these conditions.
**Oral (FDA-approved, prescription-only, per StatPearls — included for contrast, not this vault's OTC scope):** onychomycosis from dermatophytes — proximal subungual onychomycosis; distal lateral subungual onychomycosis affecting >50% of the nail plate with matrix involvement and thickness >2mm; ≥3 nails involved.
**Oral off-label:** pediatric onychomycosis (first-line per British Association of Dermatologists); tinea capitis (particularly Trichophyton infections in North America).

## Mechanism
Allylamine antifungal — non-competitive inhibitor of squalene epoxidase, acting early in the ergosterol synthesis pathway. Not directly fungicidal by itself; it's the resulting intracellular accumulation of squalene to toxic levels that kills the fungal cell (mechanistically distinct from azoles like clotrimazole, which block a later step — 14-alpha-lanosterol demethylation).

## Dosing

| Form / Indication | Regimen | Duration |
|---|---|---|
| Topical, athlete's foot (between toes) | Apply twice daily (AM/PM) | 1 week |
| Topical, athlete's foot (bottom/sides of foot) | Apply twice daily | 2 weeks |
| Topical, jock itch or ringworm | Apply once daily | 1 week |
| Oral, onychomycosis (prescription only) | 250mg tablet once daily | 6 weeks (fingernails), up to 12 weeks (toenails) |
| Oral, unapproved pulse regimen | 250mg/day x4 weeks, then 4 weeks off, then 4 weeks on | — |

Directions per label: wash affected area first, wear ventilated shoes, wash hands after application. Notably shorter topical treatment durations than clotrimazole 1% (2–4 weeks for the equivalent indications — see [[clotrimazole]]). Oral absorption/bioavailability is similar regardless of food intake.

## Efficacy Evidence
No product-specific systematic review, RCT, or Cochrane data with hard effect sizes (NNT/RR/CI) for topical terbinafine was located in this pass — only the StatPearls narrative and the FDA OTC label were gathered as primary sources (per the sourcing note carried over from the prior session on this ingredient). StatPearls states "Level-I" evidence shows oral terbinafine's superiority over placebo and alternative systemic antifungals for its approved onychomycosis indication, but this is a general characterization, not a specific study with N/PMID/effect size — and it concerns the oral form, not the topical OTC product this page is primarily about. This is flagged rather than papered over with an invented citation.

## Pharmacokinetics
**Topical:** per label, for external use only — not addressed with detailed systemic absorption data in these sources (see Contraindications; not to be applied to nails, scalp, mouth, eyes, or for vaginal use).
**Oral:** hepatic AND renal clearance; inhibits hepatic CYP2D6.

## Contraindications
**Topical:** for external use only. Do not apply to nails, scalp, mouth, eyes, or use for vaginal yeast infections. Discontinue if excessive irritation develops.
**Oral (prescription form, included for contrast):** chronic or active liver disease; prior allergic reaction to oral terbinafine.

## Key Safety Data
**Topical:** if eye contact occurs, rinse thoroughly with water; contact Poison Control if ingested; children under 12 require doctor consultation before use. No incidence-rate data for topical local irritation was given in the label text extracted.
**Oral (for contrast — not this page's OTC scope):**
- Common (mild, self-limited): headaches, GI symptoms, rash
- Uncommon: visual disturbances, dysgeusia (taste disturbance), mild transaminitis
- Severe/rare: dysgeusia can be severe or **permanent**; reports of fulminant liver failure; Stevens-Johnson syndrome, toxic epidermal necrolysis; cutaneous or systemic lupus erythematosus
- FDA recommends measuring serum transaminases before oral drug initiation; average time to drug-induced liver injury is nearly 30 days, usually within 3 months; liver enzymes >2x normal require immediate discontinuation, with supportive care (including liver transplantation in severe cases) sometimes needed

## Drug Interactions
**Topical:** none reported in the sources gathered (minimal systemic absorption expected with correct external use).
**Oral (for contrast):** CYP2D6 inhibition — potential interactions with cimetidine, fluconazole, cyclosporine, rifampin, caffeine, paroxetine, codeine, metoprolol, simvastatin, nifedipine, digoxin, phenytoin.

## Special Population Flags
- **Pediatric:** topical label requires doctor consultation for children under 12 before use.
- **Pregnancy (oral form, for contrast):** former Category B, but StatPearls states oral terbinafine is NOT recommended for use during pregnancy despite that classification.
- **Breastfeeding (oral form, for contrast):** excreted in breast milk — StatPearls advises use should follow completion of breastfeeding.
- No topical-specific pregnancy/breastfeeding guidance was present in the label text extracted here.

## Overdose / Toxicity
Label directs contacting Poison Control if the topical product is ingested; no dedicated overdose management section (dosing thresholds, case reports) was present in the sources gathered for either form.
