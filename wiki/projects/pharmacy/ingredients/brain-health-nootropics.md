# Brain Health / Nootropic Supplements

**Type:** Active Ingredient (product class — covers apoaequorin, coffee fruit extract, silk protein hydrolysate, citicoline, and other branded "cognitive support" ingredients)
**Drug Class:** Cognitive-support dietary supplement (not FDA-regulated as a drug; no ingredient in this class is FDA-approved for any cognitive indication)
**Verification Status:** AI-compiled from primary sources, unverified — not yet reviewed by a pharmacist/physician
**Evidence Grade:** Very Low to Low for nearly every branded ingredient in this category — this is one of the most consistently poorly-evidenced, most heavily-litigated product categories found across this entire database. Citicoline (as an isolated, generic compound, independent of any specific brand) has the most genuine supporting literature of the ingredients covered here, though even that evidence is described by independent sourcing as limited for the specific patented "Cognizin" form
**Last updated:** 2026-08-28
**Sources:** FTC v. Quincy Bioscience (Prevagen) trial record and 2024 jury verdict; multiple class-action complaints and expert analyses (Neuriva/Reckitt, Natrol Cognium, ONNIT Alpha BRAIN, Focus Factor); general citicoline RCT literature

**Sourcing note:** built specifically for this database's Brain Health category, which previously had no dedicated ingredient-class evidence page. Unlike most ingredient pages in this database, this page's central finding is not a single trial's result but a **cross-brand pattern**: nearly every major branded product in this specific category has been the subject of a real lawsuit, FTC/NAD action, or expert re-analysis concluding the product does not work as marketed.

## Indication
Marketed broadly for memory, focus, attention, and general cognitive support in healthy adults. **No ingredient covered on this page has an FDA-approved cognitive indication**, and (see below) several have real, adjudicated or expert-analyzed findings of no meaningful benefit over placebo.

## The Cross-Brand Pattern (the central finding of this page)
This category shows a genuinely unusual density of real, documented efficacy failures across its market-leading brands:

- **Apoaequorin (Prevagen):** in a 2024 federal jury trial (FTC and NY Attorney General v. Quincy Bioscience), the jury found the company's claims were not supported by science and were misleading. The core scientific problem: apoaequorin is a protein that is very likely digested by stomach pepsin into amino acids before absorption, and even if it survived, its size/poor fat solubility would prevent it crossing the blood-brain barrier — no study has shown orally-administered apoaequorin reaches the human brain. The company's own "Madison Memory Study" failed its primary endpoint; the only positive results came from 30+ post-hoc subgroup analyses, a method widely regarded as unreliable for establishing efficacy (multiple-comparisons/p-hacking risk).
- **Coffee fruit extract + phosphatidylserine (Neuriva):** class-action litigation alleges "clinically tested"/"scientifically proven" claims are false; independent expert re-analysis of the company's own clinical trial data, applying standard trial-analysis principles, concluded the product and its two main ingredients are no better than placebo. An initial $8M settlement was later vacated on procedural (standing) grounds, not because the underlying advertising claims were found valid.
- **Silk protein hydrolysate / "Cera-Q" (Natrol Cognium):** a class-action lawsuit alleges 2 of 9 clinical studies cited on packaging were retracted (one specifically for **data fabrication and falsification**), that the studies supporting the ingredient used doses of 400-600mg/day — 2 to 6 times the amount actually in two Cognium tablets — and that independent experts concluded the ingredient likely works no better than placebo at the labeled dose.
- **Proprietary blend (ONNIT Alpha BRAIN):** the company's own self-funded 2016 study, when its full text is examined, showed a statistically significant difference from placebo in only 1 of 26 tests performed, with the placebo group outperforming the treatment group in several measures — a real, company-sourced, unfavorable result driving current false-advertising litigation.
- **Proprietary blend, "fairy dusting" allegation (Focus Factor):** NAD found insufficient evidence for the brand's "#1 clinically studied" claim; current litigation alleges ingredients are present in amounts too small to plausibly produce the claimed effects (a practice sometimes called "fairy dusting" — including trace amounts of an active ingredient primarily to justify listing it on the label); a 2004 FTC action also found unsubstantiated claims about focus/memory/mood/concentration.
- **Citicoline (Magtein/Cognizin, Qunol Brain Health):** the one comparative exception found in this pass — citicoline has real, independent, non-brand-specific RCT literature (including in healthy older adults), and magnesium L-threonate ("Magtein") has documented brain-magnesium-elevation evidence — though independent sourcing describes the "Cognizin" patented-form-specific clinical research as still limited, and no brand-specific lawsuit was located for this product in this pass.

## Efficacy Evidence
See the brand-specific findings above — this page intentionally does not present a single pooled ingredient-class evidence grade, because "Brain Health" as tracked in this database's Pharmacy Times category spans entirely different, unrelated active compounds (a jellyfish-derived protein, a coffee-fruit botanical extract, a silkworm-derived protein, a proprietary nootropic blend, and citicoline/magnesium) — evidence for one does not transfer to another.

## Pharmacy Times Context (recommendation share — NOT efficacy)
Brain Health (Pharmacy Times 2026 OTC Guide, ~1,700 pharmacists surveyed, 802,941 monthly recommendations): Prevagen 50%, Neuriva Brain Health 24%, Focus Factor 8%, Natrol Cognium 8%, ONNIT Alpha BRAIN 5%, Qunol Brain Health 5%. **This is a genuinely striking finding: the top 5 of 6 brands in this category (97% combined share) each carry a real, documented, and often litigated efficacy problem** — this is one of the largest recommendation-share-vs-evidence-quality gaps found anywhere in this entire database.
