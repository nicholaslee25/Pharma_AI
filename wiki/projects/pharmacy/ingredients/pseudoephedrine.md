# Pseudoephedrine

**Type:** Active Ingredient
**Drug Class:** Sympathomimetic amine (amphetamine/phenethylamine class), oral nasal decongestant
**Verification Status:** AI-compiled from primary sources, unverified — not yet reviewed by a pharmacist/physician

## Indication
OTC: temporary relief of nasal/sinus congestion and pressure from the common cold, hay fever, or other upper respiratory allergies.

## Mechanism
Sympathomimetic amine (amphetamine/phenethylamine class). Increases norepinephrine activity at adrenergic receptors, producing alpha-adrenergic vasoconstriction of nasal mucosal blood vessels and reduced nasal swelling.

## Dosing (per Sudafed FDA OTC label)

| Population | Dose | Max |
|---|---|---|
| Adults & children ≥12y | 60 mg (2×30mg tablets) q4–6h | 8 tablets (240mg)/24h |
| Children 6–11y | 30 mg (1 tablet) q4–6h | 4 tablets (120mg)/24h |
| Children <6y | Do not use | — |

**Regulatory note:** Sold behind-the-counter in the US per the Combat Methamphetamine Epidemic Act (2005) due to its use as a methamphetamine precursor — a legal/purchasing control, not a clinical dosing restriction.

## Efficacy Evidence

**Eccles et al. 2005 RCT (PMID 15794071)** — double-blind, randomized, parallel-group, placebo-controlled, N=238 adults (mean age ~20) with nasal congestion from acute URTI, pseudoephedrine 60 mg:
- Day 1 objective nasal airway resistance (NAR AUC 0–3h, primary endpoint): significantly better than placebo, p=0.006; NAR AUC 0–4h p=0.001
- Day 3 (multiple dosing): NAR AUC 0–3h and 0–4h both p<0.001 vs. placebo
- Day 1 subjective congestion (VAS): AUC 0–3h p=0.029, AUC 0–4h p=0.021
- Day 3 subjective VAS: **not** statistically significant — objective decongestant effect persisted with repeated dosing more reliably than the subjective symptom-score benefit
- Heart rate ~2–4 bpm higher with pseudoephedrine; 5 adverse events in each arm, judged treatment-unrelated

**Gelotte et al. 2019 pediatric RCT (PMID 31274197, NCT01744106)** — multicenter, double-blind, placebo-controlled, children 6–11y, pseudoephedrine 30mg tablets (current OTC monograph dose), up to 7 days as needed:
- Primary endpoint (weighted change in nasal congestion severity, 1–8h post-dose): pseudoephedrine superior, least-squares mean difference 1.2 (p=0.029)
- Day 2 secondary endpoints only numerically favorable, not confirmed significant
- **Industry-affiliated funding:** two authors were paid CHPA (Consumer Healthcare Products Association) consultants; the CRO received Perrigo-funded consulting fees; one author was a Perrigo employee — weigh accordingly

**Not established by any source reviewed here:** meaningful shortening of the underlying viral illness, or cold prevention. Effect is decongestion only.

## Contraindications
Per FDA label: do not use if currently taking a prescription MAOI, or within 2 weeks of stopping one (risk of hypertensive crisis).

## Key Safety Data
- Ask a doctor before use if: heart disease, high blood pressure, thyroid disease, diabetes, or trouble urinating due to enlarged prostate (per label — reflects sympathomimetic cardiovascular/urinary effects)
- Stop use and ask a doctor if nervousness, dizziness, or sleeplessness occur; if symptoms don't improve within 7 days; or if symptoms occur with fever
- Gelotte 2019 (pediatric, 6–11y): somnolence 71.9% (pseudoephedrine) vs. 63.9% (placebo); insomnia 34.4% vs. 38.9%; nervousness 20.0% vs. 23.6% — high rates in *both* arms, illustrating that baseline cold symptoms/AE-reporting thresholds inflate both groups similarly
- Eccles 2005 (adult): heart rate ~2–4 bpm higher on pseudoephedrine vs. placebo

## Drug Interactions
- **MAOIs:** contraindicated — hypertensive crisis risk (see Contraindications)
- No other interaction data located in the FDA OTC label or sources reviewed in this pass

## Special Population Flags
- **Pediatric:** not for children <6y (label); Gelotte 2019 supports efficacy and general safety at the monograph dose for ages 6–11 over up to 7 days
- **Pregnancy/breastfeeding:** label says ask a health professional before use; no further detail located in this pass
- **Cardiovascular/thyroid/diabetes/BPH:** ask a doctor before use per label, consistent with sympathomimetic pharmacology
- **MAOI use:** contraindicated

## Overdose / Toxicity
Not addressed in the FDA OTC monograph label (no Overdosage section, unlike prescription labels) or in the two RCTs reviewed. Not fabricated here — flagged as a gap.
