# Hydrocortisone

**Type:** Active Ingredient
**Drug Class:** Topical corticosteroid — Class VII (least-potent class), 1% OTC strength
**Verification Status:** AI-compiled from primary sources, unverified — not yet reviewed by a pharmacist/physician
**Evidence Grade:** High — topical corticosteroids as a class are among the best-established treatments for inflammatory dermatoses; hydrocortisone 1% specifically sits at the mildest end of the potency spectrum, which shapes both its safety margin and its limits

## Indication
Temporary relief of itching from minor skin irritation/inflammation/rashes: eczema, insect bites, poison ivy/oak/sumac, contact dermatitis from soaps/detergents/cosmetics/jewelry, seborrheic dermatitis, psoriasis; also external genital/feminine/anal itching (OTC label). Broader steroid-responsive dermatoses (atopic dermatitis, lichen planus, alopecia areata) per StatPearls, generally under medical supervision at this or higher potencies.

## Mechanism
Three pathways: (1) anti-inflammatory — vasoconstriction, phospholipase A2 inhibition, direct inhibition of inflammatory transcription factors; (2) anti-mitotic — decreased epidermal proliferation/collagen synthesis (also the basis for its main side effect, skin atrophy); (3) immunosuppressive — suppresses immune cell maturation/differentiation/proliferation.

## Dosing (per Fingertip Unit system and OTC label)
**Fingertip units (FTU) per application:** face/neck 2.5, trunk front 7, trunk back 7, one arm 3, one hand 1. **Frequency: once-to-twice daily is recommended by StatPearls** — research shows more frequent dosing only increases adverse effects without added benefit, though the OTC label itself permits 3–4 times daily for adults/children ≥2y (a real difference between the more conservative dermatology literature guidance and the actual consumer product label — both are preserved here). **Duration: should not exceed 2–4 weeks** regardless of potency.

**Vehicle matters:** creams for acute exudative inflammation/intertriginous areas; lotions/gels for hair-bearing regions; ointments for thick hyperkeratotic lesions (avoid on hairy areas).

## Pharmacokinetics
Absorption is enhanced by inflammation, occlusive dressings, and thin epidermis — up to a **300-fold variation** in absorption between thin- and thick-skin body regions, which is why the same product can behave very differently applied to the face vs. the sole of the foot. Hepatic metabolism, primarily renal excretion with some biliary elimination.

## Contraindications
**Absolute:** bacterial skin infections (impetigo, furuncles/carbuncles, cellulitis, erysipelas, lymphangitis, erythrasma) — the steroid's anti-inflammatory/vasoconstrictive effect masks infection signs, delaying appropriate treatment. **Relative:** Candida/dermatophyte infections — immunosuppressive effect can produce a persistent, atypical-appearing fungal infection ("tinea incognito").

## Key Safety Data
- **Skin atrophy** is the most common adverse effect (from the anti-mitotic mechanism) — epidermal thinning, increased dermal ground-substance resorption; reversible but recovery can take months
- **Tachyphylaxis** (tolerance): capillaries regain vasoconstriction capacity after only ~4 days off treatment — supports intermittent ("pulse") dosing schedules to preserve effectiveness
- Other local effects: striae (permanent), acne, steroid rosacea, perioral dermatitis, hypertrichosis, pigmentation changes, delayed wound healing
- **Systemic effects** (glaucoma, HPA axis suppression, Cushing syndrome, hypertension, hyperglycemia) occur mainly with prolonged HIGH-potency use on thin skin — hydrocortisone 1% (Class VII, least potent) carries a much lower systemic risk than higher-potency prescription steroids, though pediatric patients remain more vulnerable to HPA suppression generally due to higher skin-surface-area-to-body-weight ratio
- **Topical corticosteroid withdrawal:** a poorly characterized condition from chronic misuse — erythema, itchiness, burning, secondary scaling lesions on stopping long-term use

## Drug Interactions
May require careful consideration when combined with: live vaccines, insulin, methotrexate, diuretics — overall interaction severity described as low for hydrocortisone specifically.

## Special Population Flags
- **Pregnancy:** maternal topical corticosteroid use, any potency, not associated with significant adverse pregnancy outcomes
- **Breastfeeding:** short-term application unlikely to pose infant risk; use lowest potency on smallest area, wipe thoroughly before nursing
- **Pediatrics:** very potent topical steroids should not be used in children without specialist advice; mild corticosteroids like OTC 1% hydrocortisone are the appropriate starting point. OTC label: not for children <2y without doctor consultation
- **Elderly:** dose cautiously — increased skin fragility, higher body-surface-area-to-weight ratio

## Overdose / Toxicity
Not addressed with a dedicated overdose section for topical use in the sources reviewed; systemic toxicity risk (HPA suppression, Cushing syndrome) scales with potency, application area, duration, and skin-barrier integrity, all of which are comparatively favorable for OTC 1% hydrocortisone used per label directions.
