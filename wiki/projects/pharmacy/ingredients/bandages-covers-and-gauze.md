## What This Page Is

Adhesive strip bandages (Band-Aid, Nexcare, Curad, Welly, and equivalents) are simple wound covers — a strip of fabric or plastic material coated on one side with pressure-sensitive adhesive, sometimes with an absorbent pad, with no active drug ingredient. "No clinical trial exists comparing bandage brand X vs. brand Y on healing outcomes" is the expected, normal finding for this device class, not a research gap — see FDA Classification below for why manufacturers are not required to generate this kind of evidence.

## FDA Classification

Adhesive bandages are regulated as **Class I devices** under **21 CFR 880.5240** (medical adhesive tape and adhesive bandage), FDA product code **KGX** ("Tape and bandage, adhesive") — 510(k)-exempt, general controls only. The regulation covers a strip of fabric or plastic material coated on one side with adhesive, which may include a pad of surgical dressing without a disinfectant, intended to cover/protect wounds, hold together skin edges of a wound, support an injured body part, or secure objects to the skin. Class I 510(k)-exempt status means manufacturers are not required to submit outcome-trial or comparative-performance evidence to market these products — this is the same regulatory tier as elastic bandages/braces (see [[joint-support-braces]]).

## The One Real Cross-Brand Finding: PFAS Testing (Mamavation/EHN 2024)

An EPA-certified laboratory, commissioned by Mamavation in partnership with Environmental Health News, tested 40 bandage products from 18 brands (including Band-Aid, Curad, 3M/Nexcare, CVS, Walmart/Up & Up, and others) for organic fluorine, a strong indicator of PFAS ("forever chemicals") contamination in the adhesive and/or absorbent pad.

- **26 of 40 tested products (65%) showed detectable organic fluorine**, ranging 11–328 ppm.
- **The single highest result in the entire study was a Band-Aid product** — the OURTONE Flexible Fabric BR65 line (260 ppm on the absorbent pad, 374 ppm on the sticky flaps) — while **other Band-Aid SKUs (e.g. Hello Kitty Assorted Adhesive Bandages) tested non-detect (<10 ppm)**. PFAS presence in this dataset varies by specific SKU/product line within a brand, not by brand as a whole.
- Separately, of bandages specifically marketed for people with darker/melanated skin tones, 63% (10 of 16 tested) showed PFAS indicators — a notable equity-relevant finding independent of overall brand identity.
- **14 products across the full 40-product set tested below the 10 ppm detection limit**, including specific SKUs from Band-Aid, CVS, 3M, TRU COLOUR, and Welly — meaning "PFAS-free" and "contains PFAS" both exist within some of the same parent brands, not as a clean brand-vs-brand split.
- The EPA states PFAS exposure is linked to immune, fertility, developmental, and cancer risk concerns — this is an environmental-chemical exposure question, not a wound-care efficacy question, and is a materially different kind of risk than anything in the drug-ingredient pages elsewhere in this database.

**Practical implication:** a brand's overall PFAS reputation (positive or negative) should not be assumed to apply uniformly across its full SKU lineup — this is a real, source-confirmed, SKU-level finding, not a brand-level one. Welly's PFAS-free marketing position is consistent with its specific tested SKU(s) coming back non-detect in this dataset, but the same lab found some other brands' individual SKUs also came back clean while their broader lineup did not.

## Recall / Adverse-Event History

Routine FDA MAUDE adverse-event and device-recall records exist for this whole product category, consistent with normal post-market surveillance for a widely-sold Class I device rather than a signal of unusual risk — e.g., a Class 2 device recall exists in the FDA database for a Curad Flexible Fabric Bandage product (record ID 99526); the publicly indexed record does not display a specific recall-reason narrative in the search interface used for this pass, so the precise reason is not asserted here. A separate, unrelated Class 2 recall for a Cardinal Health/Curity flexible adhesive bandage (latex adhesive residual contamination on a specific manufacturing lot) illustrates the type of issue this device category occasionally sees — included as context, not as evidence about Curad specifically.

## Material Claims (breathability, waterproofing)

Common cross-brand marketing claims — "breathable," "waterproof," "flexible fabric," "won't stick to the wound" — reflect real, differentiable material engineering choices (porous/perforated adhesive backing for breathability; occlusive plastic film for waterproofing) but are not independently outcome-tested claims in the sense a drug label claim would be; no comparative RCT-style evidence adjudicating "brand X breathes better than brand Y" was located in this pass, and none is expected given the Class I regulatory tier.

## Pharmacy Times Context (recommendation share — NOT efficacy)

**Bandages, Covers, and Gauze** (First Aid category, Pharmacy Times 2026 OTC Guide, ~1,700 pharmacists surveyed, 1,907,143/month volume): Band-Aid 74%, Nexcare 19%, Curad 5%, Welly 2%. This is a pharmacist recommendation share, not a performance ranking — no clinical or comparative-performance evidence exists to support ranking these four brands against each other on wound-care outcomes.
