# Omega-3 / Fish Oil

**Type:** Active Ingredient
**Drug Class:** Polyunsaturated fatty acid dietary supplement (EPA/DHA; ALA from plant sources)
**Verification Status:** AI-compiled from primary sources, unverified — not yet reviewed by a pharmacist/physician
**Evidence Grade:** Genuinely mixed and dose/formulation-dependent — high-dose prescription EPA (4g/day icosapent ethyl) showed a strong, consistent cardiovascular benefit in one major trial (REDUCE-IT), while a similar-dose EPA+DHA formulation showed none in another (STRENGTH); typical low-dose (~1g/day) OTC supplement doses have NOT shown consistent cardiovascular-event reduction across large modern RCTs
**Last updated:** 2026-08-25
**Sources:** raw/primary-sources/omega-3-fish-oil/nih-ods-omega3-health-professional.md

**Sourcing note:** this page is anchored on the NIH ODS Health Professional Fact Sheet, which itself synthesizes an unusually large and well-documented set of major cardiovascular RCTs (JELIS, ORIGIN, Alpha Omega, VITAL, ASCEND, REDUCE-IT, STRENGTH) with specific trial numbers — a strong enough single source that no additional standalone study was independently pulled in this pass.

---

## Indication
General cardiovascular health support (the dominant marketed claim); also studied for infant neurodevelopment, cognitive function, AMD, dry eye, and rheumatoid arthritis (not detailed in depth here — see Efficacy Evidence for the cardiovascular data specifically, which is the best-documented outcome).

## Mechanism
ALA (plant-derived, essential) converts to EPA then DHA in the liver, but conversion is very limited (<15%) — direct EPA/DHA intake from fish/fish oil is the only practical way to meaningfully raise body levels. Omega-3s are structural components of cell-membrane phospholipids (DHA concentrated in retina, brain, sperm) and are precursors of eicosanoids that are generally less pro-inflammatory/pro-clotting than the eicosanoids made from omega-6 fatty acids (arachidonic acid) — raising EPA/DHA relative to arachidonic acid shifts the body's inflammatory/clotting balance.

## Dosing / Forms
Typical OTC fish oil supplement: ~1,000mg fish oil containing ~180mg EPA + 120mg DHA (varies widely by product — always check the specific label for actual EPA/DHA content, not just total "fish oil" mg). Prescription high-dose forms exist: icosapent ethyl (Vascepa) 4g/day EPA-only, and omega-3 carboxylic acid (Epanova) 4g/day EPA+DHA — these are NOT equivalent to typical OTC supplement doses and were tested as adjuncts to statin therapy in specific high-risk populations, not as general-purpose OTC products.

## Efficacy Evidence — Major Cardiovascular RCTs (chronological, per NIH ODS)

**JELIS 2007** — 18,645 hypercholesterolemic patients, 1.8g/day EPA + statin vs. statin alone, mean 4.6 years: **19% fewer major coronary events** overall; in the secondary-prevention (prior CAD) subgroup specifically, 28% reduction in unstable angina and 19% reduction in major coronary events; NO effect in the primary-prevention (no CAD history) subgroup. Also: 20% reduction in recurrent stroke in patients with prior stroke, but no effect on total stroke incidence.

**ORIGIN 2012 and Alpha Omega 2010 (largely null):** 1g/day omega-3 (ORIGIN, 12,536 diabetic/high-risk patients, ~6 years) and low-dose EPA+DHA in margarine (Alpha Omega, 4,837 post-MI patients, 40 months) both showed NO reduction in cardiovascular events vs. placebo, despite ORIGIN lowering triglycerides.

**VITAL 2018** (25,871 adults, no prior CVD/cancer, 1g/day omega-3, median 5.3 years): NO significant reduction in the composite major CV event outcome, BUT a significant **28% reduction in total MI** (77% reduction in African Americans specifically; 40% reduction in those eating <1.5 fish servings/week) plus reductions in fatal MI, total CHD, and revascularization procedures.

**ASCEND 2018** (15,480 diabetic adults, no CVD, same 1g/day dose, mean 7.4 years): NO significant effect on composite serious vascular events, BUT a significant **19% reduction in cardiovascular death**.

**REDUCE-IT 2019** (8,179 CVD/high-risk patients, elevated triglycerides despite statins, 4g/day icosapent ethyl vs. MINERAL OIL placebo, median 4.9 years): significant reductions across the board — composite CV events **25%**, CV death **20%**, stroke **28%**, MI **31%**.

**STRENGTH 2020** (13,078 high-CV-risk patients, 4g/day omega-3 carboxylic acid vs. CORN OIL placebo): stopped early for futility, with a HIGHER atrial fibrillation rate in the treatment group; NO significant benefit on the composite CV endpoint.

**Why REDUCE-IT and STRENGTH conflict despite similar 4g/day doses:** per NIH ODS's own analysis, likely explanations include different omega-3 formulation (EPA-only ethyl ester vs. EPA+DHA carboxylic acid) and — importantly — **different placebo comparators**: mineral oil (REDUCE-IT) is NOT pharmacologically neutral (it affects lipids/inflammatory markers and may impair statin absorption), while corn oil (STRENGTH) is more neutral. This means REDUCE-IT's apparent benefit may be partly inflated by an unfavorable placebo, not purely a true drug effect — a genuinely important methodological caveat this vault preserves rather than presenting REDUCE-IT as an uncomplicated win.

**Syntheses:**
- 2019 meta-analysis (13 trials incl. VITAL/ASCEND/REDUCE-IT, NOT STRENGTH; 127,477 participants): dose-related reduction in MI, CHD death, total CHD, CV death, total CVD — but NO significant stroke reduction except at REDUCE-IT's higher 4g/day dose
- 2020 Cochrane review (86 RCTs, 162,796 participants, 1968–2019): reduced triglycerides ~15%, slightly reduced cardiovascular mortality and CHD events — but NO effect on all-cause mortality, overall cardiovascular events, stroke, or arrhythmia
- Several earlier meta-analyses and a 2016 AHRQ report concluded omega-3 supplements do NOT significantly reduce risk of most cardiovascular events

**Bottom line for this page:** typical OTC fish-oil doses (~1g/day) show a scattered pattern of significant benefit on SOME specific outcomes (MI, CV death) but not the primary composite endpoint in the largest modern trials (VITAL, ASCEND); only high-dose prescription EPA (4g/day, and specifically the ethyl-ester form vs. a flawed placebo) showed a strong, consistent, multi-endpoint benefit in REDUCE-IT, which was NOT replicated by a similar-dose EPA+DHA formulation in STRENGTH.

## Pharmacokinetics
Dietary lipids hydrolyzed in the gut, absorbed via bile-salt micelles into enterocytes (~95% absorption efficiency, similar to other dietary fats), incorporated into chylomicrons, enter circulation via lymphatics. Natural/re-esterified triglycerides and free fatty acids have somewhat higher bioavailability than ethyl esters, though all forms significantly raise plasma EPA/DHA.

## Contraindications
Not itemized specifically for OTC fish oil in the sources reviewed; prescription high-dose formulations carry their own specific prescribing information not detailed here.

## Key Safety Data
- Common mild effects: unpleasant/fishy taste, bad breath, heartburn, nausea, GI discomfort, diarrhea, headache, odoriferous sweat
- Doses of 2–15 g/day EPA/DHA might increase bleeding time via reduced platelet aggregation, though this has not been shown to cause actual bleeding problems in the cited research
- **STRENGTH trial specifically found an increased atrial fibrillation rate** in the high-dose omega-3 carboxylic acid group — a real, trial-documented cardiac safety signal at high prescription doses, not something to assume applies to typical low-dose OTC supplements
- **FDA:** dietary supplements providing ≤5 g/day combined EPA+DHA are considered safe as recommended; EFSA reached a similar conclusion for long-term use up to ~5g/day

## Drug Interactions
Not itemized in the sources reviewed beyond the bleeding-time consideration above (relevant to concurrent anticoagulant/antiplatelet use, though not independently confirmed as a clinically significant interaction in the source).

## Special Population Flags
- **African Americans:** in VITAL, showed a notably larger MI-risk reduction (77%) than the overall study population (28%) — a specific, sourced subgroup finding worth preserving rather than flattening into the overall number
- **Low fish consumers (<1.5 servings/week):** also showed a larger MI-risk reduction (40%) in VITAL than the overall population — suggesting supplementation may matter more for those with lower baseline dietary omega-3 intake
- **Patients already on statins with elevated triglycerides despite therapy:** the specific population REDUCE-IT targeted and where the strongest (if placebo-caveated) benefit was shown

## Overdose / Toxicity
Not addressed with a specific overdose section in the sources reviewed; general safety ceiling is the ≤5g/day EPA+DHA figure noted above.

The 30mg total omega-3 dose is small relative to the adult doses discussed in the efficacy evidence above; this page's VITAL/REDUCE-IT findings should not be assumed to transfer to this pediatric dose.
