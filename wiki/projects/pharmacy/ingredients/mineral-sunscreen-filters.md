# Mineral Sunscreen Filters (Zinc Oxide, Titanium Dioxide)

**Type:** Active Ingredient (class)
**Drug Class:** Inorganic (physical/mineral) UV filters — FDA OTC monograph sunscreen actives
**Verification Status:** AI-compiled from primary sources, unverified — not yet reviewed by a pharmacist/physician
**Evidence Grade:** High for GRASE regulatory status (the only two of 16 FDA-reviewed sunscreen actives affirmed both safe and effective); safety evidence for modern nanoparticle formulations is reassuring but based on absorption/penetration studies rather than long-term outcome RCTs
**Last updated:** 2026-08-27
**Sources:** FDA 2019 proposed rule (Federal Register), nanoparticle skin-penetration literature (Therapeutic Goods Administration literature review; Taylor & Francis/PubMed review PMID 24198489)

---

## Indication
FDA OTC monograph: prevention of sunburn and, with regular use alongside other sun-protection measures, decreased risk of skin cancer and early skin aging caused by the sun — same indication as [[chemical-sunscreen-filters]], via a mechanistically distinct route. Zinc oxide also has a separate, unrelated FDA indication and evidence page in this database for diaper-rash barrier protection — see [[zinc-oxide]] for that different use case; this page covers zinc oxide's UV-filter/sunscreen mechanism specifically.

## Mechanism
Historically described as purely "physical" blockers that reflect and scatter UV radiation off the skin's surface, current mechanistic understanding is more nuanced: at the nanoparticle sizes used in modern cosmetically-elegant (non-whitening) mineral sunscreens, zinc oxide and titanium dioxide particles **both scatter/reflect UV light and absorb it** via semiconductor-band-gap photochemistry, similar in principle (though not in chemical structure) to the organic chromophore absorption used by [[chemical-sunscreen-filters]]. Titanium dioxide covers UVB and short-UVA more effectively; zinc oxide provides the broadest single-ingredient UVA coverage (including long-UVA/UVA1) of any FDA-monograph sunscreen active, chemical or mineral.

## FDA Regulatory Status
Per the FDA's February 2019 proposed rule evaluating all 16 marketed sunscreen active ingredients: **zinc oxide and titanium dioxide are the only two ingredients affirmed GRASE** (Generally Recognized As Safe and Effective), at concentrations up to 25%. This stands in direct contrast to the 12 chemical filters (including all six covered on [[chemical-sunscreen-filters]]) for which FDA found insufficient safety data to make a positive GRASE determination, and to PABA/trolamine salicylate, found **not** GRASE. This is a real, meaningful regulatory distinction: a consumer or pharmacist specifically seeking the sunscreen category's most FDA-confidence-affirmed active ingredients should look for zinc oxide or titanium dioxide on the label.

## Nanoparticle Safety Evidence
Modern mineral sunscreens use nanoparticle-sized zinc oxide/titanium dioxide specifically to avoid the visible white cast of larger-particle mineral formulations, which raised a legitimate safety question (do nanoparticles penetrate skin and reach systemic circulation, unlike larger particles?). The literature reviewed found:
- **The majority of in vitro and in vivo studies show zinc oxide and titanium dioxide nanoparticles do not penetrate, or only minimally penetrate, the stratum corneum** — one cited study found **less than 0.03% of applied zinc content penetrated the epidermis**, suggesting systemic absorption is highly unlikely under normal use.
- **Current evidence assessment: neither nanoparticle is likely to cause harm when used as sunscreen ingredients as directed.**
- **A genuine, if theoretical, caveat exists:** in the presence of UV light, both nanoparticle types can generate reactive oxygen species, and there is a theoretical carcinogenicity concern **if** particles were to enter the body in meaningful quantities — the reviewed literature explicitly frames this as a reason for continued monitoring/caution in new formulation development, not as a demonstrated real-world risk, given the penetration data above.

This is a materially different, and generally more reassuring, systemic-exposure picture than [[chemical-sunscreen-filters]]' JAMA-documented plasma absorption findings — though it should be noted the studies measure different things (skin penetration/absorption studies for minerals vs. direct blood plasma concentration RCTs for chemical filters), so the comparison is suggestive rather than a strict apples-to-apples contrast.

## Environmental/Reef-Safety Context
Neither zinc oxide nor titanium dioxide is subject to Hawaii's oxybenzone/octinoxate sunscreen ban or the (currently overridden) Key West ban — see [[chemical-sunscreen-filters]] for that separate controversy. Mineral sunscreens are frequently marketed as "reef-safe" specifically on this basis, though the reviewed sources for this page did not independently verify mineral-filter coral-reef-safety claims beyond the absence of an equivalent ban.

## Contraindications / Warnings
External use only; avoid eye contact; discontinue if irritation develops. Standard FDA-mandated label language applies (consistent use with other sun-protection measures decreases skin cancer/early-aging risk).
