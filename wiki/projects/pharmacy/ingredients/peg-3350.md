# Polyethylene Glycol 3350 (PEG 3350)

**Type:** Active Ingredient
**Drug Class:** Osmotic laxative
**Verification Status:** AI-compiled from primary sources, unverified — not yet reviewed by a pharmacist/physician
**Evidence Grade:** High for adult occasional/chronic constipation (FDA-approved for ≥17y, a randomized placebo-controlled trial shows significant benefit); pediatric use is off-label with an important FDA neuropsychiatric safety signal to weigh
**Last updated:** 2026-08-25
**Sources:** raw/primary-sources/peg-3350/statpearls-peg3350.md, raw/primary-sources/peg-3350/dailymed-miralax.md

---

## Indication
FDA-approved: constipation treatment in patients ≥17 years — the preferred agent for chronic constipation/disimpaction due to a favorable adverse-effect profile and better taste than other osmotic agents. Non-FDA-approved: colonoscopy preparation, fecal impaction, pediatric constipation (very commonly used off-label despite lacking formal approval for this population).

## Mechanism
Minimally absorbed ethylene glycol polymer that forms hydrogen bonds with water molecules, preventing water reabsorption from stool. This retains water in the stool and raises osmotic pressure, producing softer stool and more frequent bowel movements.

## Dosing

| Population | Dose |
|---|---|
| Adults ≥17y (OTC) | 17g in 4–8oz beverage, once daily; max 7 days without medical guidance (label); StatPearls: up to 2 weeks OTC, up to 6 months if medically directed |
| Colonoscopy prep | 238g in 2L sports drink the day before; split-dose (1L/day) for afternoon procedures |
| Pediatric fecal impaction (off-label, >2y) | 1–1.5 g/kg/day for 3–6 days, max 6 consecutive days |
| Pediatric chronic constipation (off-label) | Initial and maintenance dosing per StatPearls is weight-based and titrated to response; continue minimum 2 months, taper gradually after ≥1 month symptom-free |

Onset: 1–3 days (label) / 2–4 days (StatPearls).

## Efficacy Evidence
A randomized, placebo-controlled, multicenter, double-blind trial (203 patients, 7 days, cited by StatPearls) found the PEG group had significantly less hardened stools and decreased exertion (**p<0.0001**), plus better quality of life, reduced pain, less bloating, and constipation symptom relief. This trial was not independently re-verified against a specific PubMed record in this pass — presented as StatPearls' own cited summary.

## Pharmacokinetics
Minimal GI absorption, poor systemic bioavailability — this is the basis for its favorable safety profile even in pregnancy/breastfeeding.

## Contraindications
Known/suspected bowel obstruction, appendicitis, inflammatory bowel disease, perforated bowel, hypersensitivity to PEG.

## Key Safety Data
- **Common:** flatulence, nausea, stomach cramps, diarrhea, abdominal swelling, rectal hemorrhage
- **Topical:** urticaria documented after PEG electrolyte lavage solution (bowel-prep) use
- **Serious but rare:** metabolic acidosis (mainly IV context), renal proximal tubular necrosis with oliguria/azotemia/renal failure
- **Pediatric neuropsychiatric FDA signal (prolonged use):** seizures, tremors, obsessive-compulsive behaviors (including repetitive chewing/sucking), paranoia, mood swings, tics, headache, sedation, aggression, anxiety, lethargy, rages. **This is a real, FDA-reported signal specific to prolonged pediatric use** and is directly relevant given how commonly PEG 3350 is used off-label in children — stated plainly here rather than downplayed, though it should also not be overstated as proven causation versus a reported-events signal

## Drug Interactions
**Starch-based thickeners** (used for dysphagia): PEG may interact with these, risking viscosity loss; xanthan-gum-based thickeners are the recommended alternative for patients who need thickened liquids. The MiraLAX label itself explicitly warns against combining with starch-based thickeners.

## Special Population Flags
- **Pregnancy:** former FDA Category C; despite limited robust research, AAFP considers it the drug of choice for chronic constipation during pregnancy
- **Breastfeeding:** minimal systemic absorption implies low clinically significant breast milk excretion
- **Hepatic/renal impairment, elderly:** no specific dose-adjustment guidance in manufacturer labeling
- **Pediatric:** off-label use is extremely common in practice, but see the neuropsychiatric FDA signal above — long-term pediatric users specifically warrant monitoring for these events

## Overdose / Toxicity
Exposure/toxicity is uncommon and generally mild — overdose mainly causes diarrhea, potentially leading to dehydration; complications can include confusion and, rarely, seizures. No antidote exists, but high/intermediate molecular weight PEG is considered nontoxic given minimal absorption.

## Pharmacy Times Context (recommendation share — NOT efficacy)
MiraLAX recommendation shares (Pharmacy Times 2026 OTC Guide, ~1,700 pharmacists surveyed): Non-Fiber Laxatives category 63%, Stool Softeners category 18% (likely reflecting the osmotic mechanism being loosely grouped with "stool softening" in consumer/pharmacist framing, even though PEG 3350 is mechanistically an osmotic laxative, not a surfactant stool softener like docusate). These are pharmacist recommendation shares, not response/cure rates.
