# Selenium Sulfide (Selenium Disulfide)

**Type:** Active Ingredient
**Drug Class:** Antifungal/antiseborrheic
**Verification Status:** AI-compiled from primary sources, unverified — not yet reviewed by a pharmacist/physician
**Evidence Grade:** Moderate — real clinical trial support exists at both 1% and 2.5% concentrations, but the sources located this pass were smaller single-center studies rather than a large independent Cochrane-style review.
**Last updated:** 2026-08-27
**Sources:** DailyMed (Selsun Blue multi-SKU label), PubMed/search-summarized (Cureus 2024 single-center study on 2.5% formulation; Karger Skin Appendage Disorders comparative RCT vs. ketoconazole 2%; Taylor & Francis 2025 combination-shampoo study). Brand-page research 2026-08-27.

---

## Indication
FDA-monograph OTC indications: dandruff, seborrheic dermatitis, and (at higher concentration/different formulation) tinea versicolor — confirmed via the DailyMed label's product-name suffixes ("Anti-Dandruff," and for related non-Selsun-Blue store-brand SKUs sharing the same active, "Anti-Seborrheic Dermatitis," "Anti-Psoriasis").

## Mechanism
Reduces yeast (*Malassezia*/*Pityrosporum*) growth on the scalp and has a cytostatic effect that reduces epidermal cell turnover, together reducing flaking and associated inflammation. Mechanistically parallel to, but chemically distinct from, [[pyrithione-zinc]] and [[ketoconazole]] — all three target the same underlying dandruff biology via different chemical routes.

## Dosing
OTC concentrations found in this pass: **1%** (most Selsun Blue SKUs — Normal to Oily, Moisturizing, 2-in-1, Medicated) and **2.5%** (a higher-strength tier referenced in the independent clinical-study literature and in some store-brand "Maximum Strength" products, e.g., Walgreens/CVS Health/GoodSense/Family Wellness Maximum Strength Dandruff shampoos found alongside the Selsun Blue DailyMed listing). Directions: use at least twice weekly or as directed by a doctor.

## Efficacy Evidence

**2.5% single-center study (Cureus 2024, search-summarized, not full-text pulled):** found a favorable safety profile and effective reduction of dandruff, itching, oiliness, and greasiness in Indian adult subjects using 2.5% selenium sulfide shampoo every three days for four weeks, with adverse-event incidence as the primary safety endpoint.

**Comparative RCT vs. ketoconazole 2% (Karger, Skin Appendage Disorders, search-summarized, not full-text pulled):** compared a 1% selenium-disulfide-based shampoo against 2% ketoconazole shampoo in moderate-to-severe scalp seborrheic dermatitis — this pass located the study's existence and title but did not independently pull its quantitative results; flagged as a genuine gap rather than assumed to favor either arm.

**Combination-shampoo study (search-summarized):** a shampoo combining selenium sulfide with salicylic acid produced significant improvements (p<0.001) in dandruff, scalp redness, itching, and greasiness in seborrheic dermatitis patients — evidence for a combination product, not selenium sulfide as monotherapy specifically, so should not be over-attributed to the single-ingredient Selsun Blue product line documented on this page.

## Contraindications
Known hypersensitivity to selenium sulfide or shampoo components.

## Key Safety Data
- If swallowed, per the Selsun Blue label: get medical help or contact Poison Control right away — a more explicit ingestion warning than seen on the [[pyrithione-zinc]] labels checked this pass
- No serious systemic toxicity signal was located for topical/shampoo use in the sources gathered this pass
- Cosmetic effects (not health risks) reported anecdotally in broader consumer literature (not independently verified here): possible discoloration of light/gray/tinted hair, similar to the caution seen on [[coal-tar]] labels

## Drug Interactions
Not addressed with specific interaction data in the sources gathered this pass.

## Special Population Flags
No specific pregnancy, breastfeeding, or pediatric restriction was located on the label checked this pass.

## Overdose / Toxicity
If swallowed, contact Poison Control immediately, per label. No further overdose-management detail was located in this pass.
