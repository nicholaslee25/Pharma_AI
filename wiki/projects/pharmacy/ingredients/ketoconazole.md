# Ketoconazole (Topical/Shampoo)

**Type:** Active Ingredient
**Drug Class:** Imidazole antifungal
**Verification Status:** AI-compiled from primary sources, unverified — not yet reviewed by a pharmacist/physician
**Evidence Grade:** High — multiple RCTs and a documented dose-response relationship (2% > 1% > 0.5% > placebo), though the important caveat is that the OTC US product is fixed at the lowest of the three actively-studied concentrations.
**Last updated:** 2026-08-27
**Sources:** DailyMed (Nizoral 2% Rx label, Nizoral A-D 1% OTC label search), PubMed search-summarized (Pierard-Franchimont et al. 2002, PMID 12476017; comparative dose-response study PMID 11306850), general web search for OTC/Rx status. Brand-page research 2026-08-27.

---

## Indication
OTC (1%, US): dandruff (flaking/itching) — "Nizoral A-D." Prescription (2%, US): tinea (pityriasis) versicolor specifically, per the Nizoral 2% label pulled this pass — notably, the 2% Rx label's own on-label indication is tinea versicolor, NOT dandruff/seborrheic dermatitis, even though 2% is also the concentration used in most of the seborrheic-dermatitis efficacy literature discussed below (an off-label-vs-studied distinction worth being precise about).

## Mechanism
Imidazole antifungal — inhibits fungal cytochrome P450-dependent synthesis of ergosterol, disrupting fungal cell membrane integrity. Active against *Malassezia* (formerly *Pityrosporum*) species, the yeast implicated in dandruff and seborrheic dermatitis pathogenesis.

## Dosing
Per Nizoral 2% (Rx) label: apply to damp affected area, lather, leave in place 5 minutes, rinse; one application typically sufficient for tinea versicolor. Per OTC Nizoral A-D 1% product information (search-summarized, not independently pulled as a full label this pass): use twice weekly, allowing at least 3 days between applications, not for daily use, ages 12+.

## Efficacy Evidence

**Dose-response relationship (search-summarized from a comparative study, PMID 11306850):** ketoconazole 2% shampoo was significantly superior to 1% for reducing both flakiness and *Malassezia* density from baseline at 2 and 4 weeks — a real, direct concentration-efficacy relationship, not just a theoretical assumption. Broader pooled literature (search-summarized) places the potency order as **2% > 1% > 0.5% > placebo/vehicle**.

**Head-to-head vs. zinc pyrithione (Pierard-Franchimont et al. 2002, multicenter RCT, PMID 12476017):** ketoconazole 2% achieved **73% improvement** in total dandruff severity score at week 4, vs. **67% for zinc pyrithione 1%** — a real but modest edge for ketoconazole at its higher (Rx-only in the US) concentration. Ketoconazole 2% also showed a significantly lower disease-recurrence rate than zinc pyrithione 1% in the same trial.

**Critical concentration caveat for this database's brand pages:** the OTC US product ([[Nizoral]] A-D) is fixed at **1%**, not the 2% concentration used in the dose-response and head-to-head studies above showing ketoconazole's strongest results. The 1%-vs-1% comparison (ketoconazole 1% vs. zinc pyrithione 1%) was not separately quantified in the sources located this pass — meaning the actual real-world OTC comparison a US consumer faces (Nizoral A-D 1% vs. [[pyrithione-zinc]]-based Head & Shoulders 1%) is NOT the same comparison as the widely-cited "ketoconazole beats zinc pyrithione" 2%-vs-1% trial result. This is a genuine, easily-missed distinction — flagged rather than assumed to transfer.

## Contraindications
Known hypersensitivity to ketoconazole or shampoo components. Per the 2% label: severe hypersensitivity reactions including anaphylaxis have been reported.

## Key Safety Data
- Adverse effects per label: hair discoloration, abnormal hair texture, itching, burning, contact dermatitis, rash, urticaria
- Discontinue and seek medical care if rash, swelling, angioedema, or breathing difficulty occur
- Eye irritation risk — avoid contact with mucous membranes
- Pediatric safety not established (2% Rx label); OTC 1% product labeled for ages 12+ per manufacturer information (not independently confirmed against a full pulled label this pass)
- Systemic absorption from topical/shampoo use is minimal given short contact time and rinse-off use, though this was not separately quantified with pharmacokinetic data in the sources located this pass — unlike oral ketoconazole, which carries well-known (and separately regulated) hepatotoxicity boxed-warning history not applicable to this topical/shampoo formulation

## Drug Interactions
Not addressed with specific topical/shampoo interaction data in the sources gathered this pass.

## Special Population Flags
Pediatric use under 12 not established per OTC product information; 2% Rx label states pediatric safety/effectiveness not established at all. No specific pregnancy/breastfeeding topical-shampoo data located this pass.

## Overdose / Toxicity
Not intended for oral ingestion or internal use; no specific overdose data located for the topical/shampoo formulation in this pass.
