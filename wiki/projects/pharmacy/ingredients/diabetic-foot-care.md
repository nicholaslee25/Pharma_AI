## Indication
Prevention of diabetic foot complications (ulceration, infection, amputation) in patients with diabetes, particularly those with peripheral neuropathy, peripheral arterial disease, prior ulceration/amputation, structural deformities, or Charcot neuroarthropathy.

## Risk Data (real, quantified — StatPearls)
- Patients with diabetes AND neuropathy: **7–10% annual risk** of foot ulceration, rising to **25–30%** with additional complications (e.g., added vascular disease or deformity).
- **~85% of diabetes-related lower-extremity amputations result from complications of foot ulceration** — i.e., ulcer prevention is directly amputation prevention, not a separate concern.
- **Non-compliant patients are 54x more likely to develop pedal ulcerations and 20x more likely to undergo amputation.**
- Five-year mortality after amputation: **40–82%** (below-knee), **40–90%** (above-knee) — a strikingly high mortality burden that underscores why prevention matters more than treatment-after-the-fact.
- Age >65: 12-fold increased amputation risk; ages 65–74 specifically: 23-fold risk.
- Charcot neuroarthropathy: 17% annual ulceration risk; 12x higher amputation risk once ulceration is present.
- Each 1% rise in HbA1c increases peripheral vascular disease risk by 25–28%.
- **Interprofessional (team-based) care reduced major limb amputations by 75%** — the single largest quantified protective effect in the sources gathered, and it is a care-model effect, not a product effect.

## The Prevention Protocol (StatPearls)
- **Screening:** annual comprehensive foot assessment for all diabetic patients; every clinical encounter for those with sensory deficits, prior ulceration, or amputation history; regular podiatry follow-up for high-risk individuals.
- **Daily self-care:** visual foot inspection (mirrors if needed), skin/nail monitoring, proper hygiene and thorough drying.
- **Footwear:** therapeutic/protective footwear for anyone with loss of protective sensation, foot deformities, current/prior ulcers, poor circulation, or amputation history; proper fit is emphasized as ulcer-preventive in itself.
- **Skin care:** autonomic dysfunction reduces perspiration, causing dryness (xerosis) and fissuring — StatPearls does not endorse any specific moisturizer ingredient or brand, keeping the emphasis on footwear and monitoring instead.
- **Avoid self-treatment of ulcers/calluses** in high-risk patients — this requires professional evaluation, not home remedies.

## Efficacy Evidence — patient education interventions (the actual evidence base, not a cream)

**Drovandi et al. 2024 (PMID 37926437, Diabetes Metab Res Rev)** — 29 RCTs, n=3,891:
- Education programs reduced foot ulcer risk by roughly half: **OR 0.54 (95% CI 0.29–1.00, I²=65%)** — note the CI's upper bound reaches exactly 1.00, meaning this is a borderline, not fully decisive, result despite the favorable point estimate
- Reduced risk of any amputation: **OR 0.34 (95% CI 0.13–0.88)**
- Reduced HbA1c: SMD -0.73 (95% CI -1.26 to -0.20, but I²=93% — very high heterogeneity, interpret cautiously)
- No effect on all-cause mortality: OR 1.09 (95% CI 0.57–2.07)
- Improved DFD knowledge in 13/16 trials and self-care behavior in 19/20 trials
- Only 1 of 29 trials was low risk of bias overall — a real quality limitation on this evidence base

**Adiewere et al. 2018 (PMID 29872752, Heliyon)** — smaller, earlier review, only 6 RCTs met inclusion:
- General/combined educational approaches: no significant advantage, RR 0.50 (95% CI 0.21–1.17, P=0.11)
- Foot-care education alone vs. usual care: non-significant (P=0.57), high heterogeneity
- **Intensive vs. brief education specifically: RR 0.37 (95% CI 0.14–1.01, P=0.05)** — borderline-significant, but the more intensive approach was the one that showed a signal

**Bottom line:** neither review evaluated any specific OTC topical cream or moisturizer — the real, quantified evidence base here is about education intensity and care-team structure, not about product choice. This directly supports the page's original framing: "a cream cannot compensate for neuropathy, vascular disease, infection, or poor wound care."

## Special Population Flags
High-risk diabetic patients (existing neuropathy, vascular disease, or prior ulceration): self-treatment of ulcers/calluses should be avoided — professional evaluation is required given the amputation-risk data above.

## Formulation Caveat
"Diabetic" foot creams are evaluated on the same general moisturizing/prevention principle as any foot moisturizer — no source gathered in this pass shows any specific diabetic-labeled cream formulation outperforming general skin care and the footwear/screening protocol above. The demonstrated protective effects in the literature (ulcer/amputation risk reduction) come from education and care-team interventions, not from any cream ingredient.
