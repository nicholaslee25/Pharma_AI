## Indication
Sore throat pain, swollen throat, and difficulty swallowing from acute pharyngitis/upper respiratory tract infection. Does not treat the underlying (typically viral) infection.

## Mechanism
Local NSAID anti-inflammatory/analgesic action at the oropharyngeal mucosa — mechanistically distinct from AMC/DCBA lozenges, which act via local antiseptic/anesthetic effect rather than anti-inflammatory action (see [[sore-throat-lozenges-amc-dcba]]). Some rapid-onset relief (within 15 minutes) is also attributed partly to the lozenge's physical demulcent/soothing effect rather than the drug alone (Benrimoj et al. 2001).

## Dosing
Studied dose: flurbiprofen 8.75mg per lozenge. Schachtel et al. 2016: up to 5 lozenges every 3–6h for 7 days. Benrimoj et al. 2001 additionally tested 12.5mg and found no added benefit over 8.75mg — i.e., 8.75mg appears to be the effective dose without further gain from going higher.

## Efficacy Evidence

**Schachtel et al. 2016 RCT (PMID 27734772, DOI 10.2217/pmt-2015-0001)** — 204 patients (102/group) with confirmed pharyngitis (onset ≤4 days), flurbiprofen 8.75mg vs. placebo, up to 5 lozenges q3–6h for 7 days:
- Over first 24h vs. placebo: sore throat pain reduced 47% more, difficulty swallowing 66% more, swollen throat 40% more (all p<0.05)

**Benrimoj et al. 2001 RCT (PMID 27517548)** — 320 patients with objective/subjective sore throat from URI, randomized to flurbiprofen 8.75mg (n=128), 12.5mg (n=64), or placebo (n=128):
- 8.75mg significantly superior to placebo on primary endpoint (total pain relief 15–120min) and for throat soreness (2h) and swollen throat (2h and 6h), all p<0.05
- **12.5mg was NOT significantly better than 8.75mg**
- No significant AE difference between groups once taste perversion (a palatability complaint, not a safety signal) was excluded (p=0.776)
- Onset within 15 minutes, statistically significant separation from placebo by 30 minutes, sustained over 4 hours

**Synthesis:** two independently-conducted RCTs (15 years apart) show a consistent, statistically significant effect across pain, swallowing difficulty, and throat swelling — a notably more robust and consistent pattern than seen for some other OTC ingredient classes in this database (e.g. guaifenesin, cerumenolytics).

## Contraindications
Not established with lozenge-specific data in the sources reviewed. General NSAID contraindications (aspirin/NSAID hypersensitivity, aspirin-triad asthma) likely apply by drug class but were not independently confirmed for this specific lozenge formulation in this pass — see [[ibuprofen]] for detailed same-class NSAID contraindication data.

## Key Safety Data
No significant difference in adverse events between flurbiprofen and placebo groups once taste perversion (palatability, not safety) was excluded (Benrimoj 2001, p=0.776). No serious adverse events reported in either trial reviewed.
