## Indication
OTC relief of occasional constipation.

## Mechanism
Stimulant laxative — stimulates the myenteric (Auerbach) plexus, increasing intestinal secretions and motility while decreasing water absorption from the bowel. Osmotic or stimulant laxatives (including senna) are typically first-line treatment for constipation, which affects an estimated 8–12% of the US population (chronic idiopathic constipation).

## Dosing (per Senokot FDA OTC label and StatPearls)

| Population | Dose |
|---|---|
| Adults/children ≥12y | 1 tablet (17.2mg sennosides) daily, max 2 tablets twice daily |
| Children 6–11y | ½ tablet daily, max 1 tablet twice daily |
| Children <6y | Consult a doctor |
| StatPearls general senna dosing | 8.6mg tablet, 1–2 tablets, 1–2x/day |

Take preferably at bedtime. Expected onset: bowel movement in 6–12 hours (label).

## Contraindications
Not itemized senna-specifically beyond general laxative-class hypersensitivity in the sources reviewed; OTC label directs against use beyond 1 week without medical direction.

## Key Safety Data
- Stimulant laxatives like senna are known to cause abdominal pain
- In vitro research has suggested potential neoplastic effects, but **human data are lacking** — this vault does not claim a proven cancer risk, only that the question has been raised in vitro and not resolved in humans
- Long-term use correlates with loss of haustral folds in the colon, potentially indicating neuronal or muscular injury — a real structural finding associated with chronic stimulant-laxative use
- OTC label: stop use and see a doctor if rectal bleeding occurs or no bowel movement results after use
