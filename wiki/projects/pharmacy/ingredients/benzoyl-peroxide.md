# Benzoyl Peroxide

**Type:** Active Ingredient
**Drug Class:** Topical antimicrobial/keratolytic (organic peroxide)
**Verification Status:** AI-compiled from primary sources, unverified — not yet reviewed by a pharmacist/physician
**Evidence Grade:** Moderate — Cochrane rates the evidence for participant-reported improvement as low-certainty despite the large trial base (120 RCTs, ~29,600 participants), largely due to inconsistent risk of bias and outcome reporting across trials

## Indication
FDA-approved: acne vulgaris (primary OTC use, 2.5–10% strengths); rosacea (microencapsulated 5% cream, moderate-severe papulopustular, prescription). Off-label: folliculitis variants, pseudofolliculitis barbae, progressive macular hypomelanosis, pressure ulcers, perforating diseases, pitted keratolysis.

## Mechanism
Bactericidal against *Cutibacterium acnes* — absorbed through skin and converted to benzoic acid, releasing free-radical oxygen species that oxidize bacterial proteins. Also has mild sebostatic and keratolytic effects. StatPearls cites a study finding 10% benzoyl peroxide applied daily for two weeks reduced *P. acnes* counts by 98% and skin free fatty acids by 50%. ~5% of applied dose is absorbed systemically and renally excreted.

## Dosing
Available OTC strengths: 2.5%, 5%, 10%, across washes, gels, creams, cleansing bars, and masks.

| Format | Regimen |
|---|---|
| Cleansing bar/wash (e.g., PanOxyl) | Apply thin lather to affected areas, 1–3x/day; start once daily and increase as tolerated; rinse and pat dry |
| Leave-on mask | Thin layer, 15–25 min, weekly |
| Combined with topical antibiotic | Typically twice daily |
| Combined with adapalene | Typically once daily |

Per label: reduce frequency if excessive dryness occurs; apply sunscreen when going outside due to increased UV sensitivity.

## Efficacy Evidence

**Cochrane review (Yang et al. 2020, PMID 32175593)** — 120 RCTs, 29,592 participants (72 trials mild-moderate acne, 26 severe; mean age 18–30; treatment mostly 10–12 weeks):
- Participant-reported acne improvement, BPO vs. placebo/no treatment: **RR 1.27** (95% CI 1.12–1.45; 3 RCTs, 2,234 participants; low-certainty evidence)
- BPO vs. adapalene: little to no difference, RR 0.99 (95% CI 0.90–1.10; 5 RCTs, 1,472 participants)
- BPO vs. clindamycin: RR 0.95 (95% CI 0.68–1.34; 1 RCT, 240 participants)
- **Withdrawal due to adverse effects was HIGHER with BPO** vs. placebo/no treatment: RR **2.13** (95% CI 1.55–2.93; 24 RCTs, 13,744 participants; low-certainty) — most common causes erythema, pruritus, skin burning
- Cochrane's own framing: despite the large trial base, most comparisons are low- or very-low-certainty evidence due to bias and reporting issues across trials — a genuinely mixed verdict, not a clean "strong evidence" one

**Vehicle-controlled trial-design review (Lamel et al. 2015, PMID 26048131)** — 12 RCTs, 2,818 patients on BPO monotherapy vs. 2,004 on vehicle:
- Total lesion reduction: **44.3%** (SD 9.2) active vs. **27.8%** (SD 21.0) vehicle
- Inflammatory lesion reduction: **52.1%** (SD 10.4) active vs. **34.7%** (SD 22.7) vehicle
- Study-defined "success": 28.6% (SD 17.3) active vs. 15.2% (SD 9.5) vehicle
- Authors' caution: vehicle (placebo) response rates are large and variable across trials, meaning individual-trial efficacy numbers should be read against that noisy baseline, not treated as precise

## Pharmacokinetics
Topical application; ~5% systemic absorption, renally excreted as benzoic acid metabolite. No accumulation data reported in sources gathered.

## Contraindications
Known hypersensitivity to benzoyl peroxide. Caution in patients with cinnamon or other benzoic-acid-derivative sensitivity (cross-reactivity risk). Avoid contact with eyes, lips, mouth, and mucous membranes.

## Key Safety Data
- Most common effect: bleaching of colored fabric and hair (cosmetic, not a health risk, but a well-documented practical downside)
- At higher concentrations: mild dryness, erythema, scaling
- Contact dermatitis reported in "a small percentage" of patients (StatPearls; no precise incidence given)
- Cochrane: withdrawal due to adverse effects roughly doubled with BPO vs. placebo/no treatment (RR 2.13, 95% CI 1.55–2.93)
- Some combination/vehicle formulations contain propylene glycol or dimethyl sulfoxide, each associated with their own toxicity considerations (StatPearls flags this without further incidence detail)

## Drug Interactions
- **Topical dapsone (sulfone):** can cause skin/facial hair yellowing when combined
- **Topical hydroquinone:** increased irritation, transient epidermal staining
- **Oral isotretinoin:** increased skin irritation/dryness (additive drying effect)
- **Topical tretinoin:** benzoyl peroxide can oxidize and reduce tretinoin's efficacy (adapalene, by contrast, is chemically compatible with BPO)
- **Topical anesthetics:** decreased anesthetic efficacy when combined

## Special Population Flags
- **Pregnancy:** former FDA Category C; StatPearls states topical use is "generally considered safe" given minimal systemic absorption, and that 5% BPO twice daily is considered safe throughout all stages of pregnancy — but the OTC label itself directs patients to consult a health professional before use.
- **Breastfeeding:** insufficient formal data; topical use generally considered safe given low systemic absorption; avoid applying to areas with direct infant skin contact.
- **Pediatric:** little formal safety/efficacy data in children specifically, though generally considered safe given minimal systemic absorption; OTC label does not specify a minimum age restriction in the text extracted.
- **Hepatic/renal impairment, elderly:** no dosage adjustments specified in either source.

## Overdose / Toxicity
Not intended for oral ingestion. If swallowed: contact Poison Control (1-800-222-1222), call 911, or go to the nearest emergency department. No known antidote (StatPearls).
