# Melatonin

**Type:** Active Ingredient
**Drug Class:** Endogenous hormone / dietary supplement (not FDA-approved as a drug in the US; related synthetic agonists ramelteon and tasimelteon ARE FDA-approved)
**Verification Status:** AI-compiled from primary sources, unverified — not yet reviewed by a pharmacist/physician
**Evidence Grade:** Moderate — a large dose-response meta-analysis (26 RCTs, 1689 observations) shows a real, dose-dependent sleep-onset/duration effect, but common real-world dosing (2mg at bedtime) may be suboptimal versus the evidence-derived ideal

## Indication
Not FDA-approved for any indication in the US (sold as an unregulated dietary supplement). Clinically used for: primary insomnia (first-line per AAFP), REM sleep behavior disorder, circadian rhythm disorders (delayed sleep-wake phase disorder, non-24-hour sleep-wake rhythm disorder), jet lag, shift work sleep disorder, and adjunctively for post-TBI sleep issues and migraine prophylaxis.

## Mechanism
Endogenous hormone synthesized in the pineal gland from tryptophan via serotonin, light-dependent and regulated by the suprachiasmatic nucleus. Promotes sleep via MT1/MT2 receptor binding in the hypothalamus and retina, inhibiting wake-promoting signals.

## Dosing
StatPearls: studied range 0.1–10mg, typically dosed up to 2h before bedtime (timing varies by indication); no defined maximum dose. **Cruz-Sanabria et al. 2024 meta-analysis finding: effect peaks at 4 mg/day, and administration 3 hours before desired bedtime outperforms the common real-world practice of 2mg taken just 30 minutes before bed** — i.e., typical consumer use (low dose, dosed very close to bedtime) may be a suboptimal protocol relative to the pooled trial evidence.

## Efficacy Evidence — Cruz-Sanabria et al. 2024 dose-response meta-analysis (PMID 38888087)
26 RCTs (1987–2020), 1689 total observations, in insomnia patients and healthy volunteers, sleep onset latency (SOL) and total sleep time (TST) vs. placebo as outcomes:
- Melatonin gradually and dose-dependently reduces SOL and increases TST, **peaking at 4 mg/day**
- Insomnia status significantly predicted SOL benefit (β=0.50, p<0.001) — i.e., people with diagnosed insomnia benefit more than healthy volunteers
- Time between dosing and sleep episode significantly predicted SOL benefit (β=−0.16, p=0.023)
- Time of day was the only significant predictor of TST benefit (β=−0.086, p<0.01)
- **Authors' conclusion:** advancing dose timing to 3h before bedtime and using 4mg/day may optimize efficacy vs. the common 2mg/30-min-before-bed pattern

## Pharmacokinetics
Bioavailability highly variable, 1–74% depending on formulation. ~90% hepatic metabolism via CYP1A2 (minor CYP2C19). 61–78% albumin-bound. Elimination half-life 1–2 hours (notably short — relevant to why timing matters as much as dose per the meta-analysis above). Primarily renal excretion.

## Contraindications
Rarely associated with angioedema despite general good tolerability. Caution in autoimmune conditions due to potential immune stimulation via interleukin production (StatPearls).

## Key Safety Data
- Mild adverse effects: drowsiness, daytime sedation, nausea, headache
- Impaired glucose tolerance reported in some cases
- No documented tolerance development with use
- **Toxicity:** remarkably low acute toxicity — no established LD50 in animal studies even at 800 mg/kg
- Long-term human exposure may reduce semen quality (limited evidence, per StatPearls)
- **Quality control problem:** a cited study of 31 melatonin supplements found actual content ranged from −83% to +478% of the labeled dose — a substantial real-world concern specific to this unregulated supplement class; USP-verified products offer some quality assurance
- **Pediatric overdose trend:** US poison control cases rose from ~8,000 (2012) to >52,000 (2021); ~15% of these required hospitalization

## Drug Interactions
- **CYP1A2 inhibitors** (e.g. fluvoxamine): caution — can raise melatonin blood levels
- **CNS depressants** (benzodiazepines, zolpidem, eszopiclone): avoid combining — additive sedation risk

## Special Population Flags
- **Hepatic impairment:** reduced metabolism/slower clearance; melatonin itself does not appear to cause hepatotoxicity
- **Renal impairment:** caution in dialysis patients due to impaired elimination
- **Pregnancy/breastfeeding:** insufficient safety evidence — avoidance recommended
- **Pediatric:** supplementation risks exceeding natural physiologic hormone levels; StatPearls notes concerns raised in postmortem case reports; see also the sharp overdose-case increase noted above
- **Elderly:** potential fall and fracture risk reported

## Overdose / Toxicity
Very low acute toxicity in animal models (no LD50 established up to 800 mg/kg). No specific antidote needed for typical exposures; management is supportive. The steep rise in pediatric exposure cases (8,000→52,000+/year, 2012–2021, ~15% hospitalized) reflects accessibility/appealing formulations (e.g. gummies) more than acute lethality — worth stating plainly rather than implying high toxicity that the animal data don't support.
