# Dextromethorphan

**Type:** Active Ingredient
**Drug Class:** Antitussive (centrally acting; NMDA receptor antagonist + sigma-1 receptor agonist)
**Verification Status:** AI-compiled from primary sources, unverified — not yet reviewed by a pharmacist/physician
**Evidence Grade:** Low-to-moderate — objectively reduces cough reflex sensitivity in a validated challenge model, but the best-designed placebo-controlled trial located found no subjective symptom benefit over placebo
**Last updated:** 2026-08-25
**Sources:** raw/primary-sources/dextromethorphan/statpearls-dm-dextromethorphan-excerpt.md, raw/primary-sources/dextromethorphan/dailymed-delsym.md, raw/primary-sources/dextromethorphan/pubmed-ramsay-2008-rct.md

## Indication
FDA-approved: productive cough from the common cold, chest congestion, productive cough from allergies. Off-label: acute URTIs, chronic bronchitis, productive cough in pneumonia. OTC framing (Delsym label): temporarily relieves cough from minor throat/bronchial irritation; helps control cough impulse to aid sleep.

## Mechanism
NMDA receptor antagonist that nonselectively inhibits reuptake of serotonin and catecholamines; also a sigma-1 receptor agonist in the medullary cough center, suppressing cough impulse transmission. Structurally similar to alkaloid opioids but does not interact with the μ-opioid receptor (relevant to why it's not scheduled as an opioid despite dissociative abuse potential at high doses).

## Dosing (per Delsym FDA OTC label)

| Population | Dose | Max |
|---|---|---|
| Adults/children ≥12y | 10 mL (30mg) q12h | 20 mL/24h |
| Children 6–11y | 5 mL (15mg) q12h | 10 mL/24h |
| Children 4–5y | 2.5 mL (7.5mg) q12h | 5 mL/24h |
| Children <4y | Do not use | — |

Note: this is the extended-release polistirex formulation (Delsym, q12h dosing). Immediate-release combination syrups (e.g. with guaifenesin) commonly dose dextromethorphan 20mg q4h — see [[guaifenesin]] for that combination's own label data.

## Efficacy Evidence — Ramsay et al. 2008 RCT (PMID 18279476)
Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, crossover trial in adults with smoker's cough (an established antitussive research model), dextromethorphan 22mg vs. placebo:
- **Objective (citric acid cough-challenge threshold):** dextromethorphan significantly raised the cough threshold vs. placebo at both 1h (p<0.05) and 2h (p<0.05) post-dose — a real, measurable reduction in cough reflex sensitivity
- **Subjective (symptom diary, Leicester Cough Questionnaire):** highly significant improvement vs. baseline in BOTH dextromethorphan and placebo arms, but **no significant difference between them**
- No correlation between the objective cough-sensitivity threshold and actual recorded cough counts/symptoms
- Authors explicitly state prior literature shows the same pattern: efficacy on cough reflex sensitivity is established in healthy volunteers, but "evidence for an effect on clinically important cough is poor" — and question whether current objective antitussive tests actually predict real-world benefit

**Practical implication:** dextromethorphan has a real, reproducible pharmacologic effect on the cough reflex, but this trial (and the literature it cites) found that effect does not reliably outperform placebo on what patients actually experience. This parallels similar objective/subjective mismatches already documented for pseudoephedrine and guaifenesin in this database.

## Pharmacokinetics
Well absorbed in the small intestine; volume of distribution 5–6.7 L/kg. Hepatic metabolism via CYP2D6 to dextrorphan (active metabolite), also N-demethylated by CYP3A4. Dextrorphan and metabolites eliminated renally.

## Contraindications
- Concomitant MAOI use, or within 2 weeks of stopping one (serotonin syndrome risk)
- Phenylketonuria (some formulations contain phenylalanine)
- Age <4 years — StatPearls describes this combination as potentially fatal in this age group
- Severe hepatic/renal disease
- Known hypersensitivity

## Key Safety Data
- **Neuropsychiatric:** at supratherapeutic/abuse doses, can cause PCP-like dissociative psychosis (paranoid feelings, hallucinations, delusions), insomnia, dissociation
- **Cardiovascular:** tachycardia, hypertension
- **Other:** dizziness, drowsiness, blurred vision, altered mental status; severe type I hypersensitivity (rash, dyspnea, oral/facial swelling)
- **Abuse potential:** high despite OTC status, due to dissociative/euphoric effects — documented "plateau" dose thresholds: 1.5 mg/kg mild stimulation; 2.5–7.5 mg/kg mild-moderate euphoria/hallucinations; 7.5–15 mg/kg dissociation ("out-of-body" state); >15 mg/kg complete dissociation/nonresponsiveness

## Drug Interactions
- **MAOIs:** contraindicated — major serotonin syndrome risk
- **Serotonergic agents (SSRIs, SNRIs, TCAs):** increased serotonin syndrome risk
- **Respiratory depressants (benzodiazepines, opioids, alcohol):** enhanced CNS/respiratory depression
- **Antiarrhythmics (amiodarone, procainamide, quinidine):** affect heart rhythm
- **CYP2D6 inhibitors (e.g. rolapitant):** can triple dextromethorphan exposure; CYP2D6 "poor metabolizers" require close monitoring given narrow therapeutic index at high doses

## Special Population Flags
- **Pediatric:** not for <4y (contraindicated per StatPearls' own combination-product wording); dosing exists down to age 4 for extended-release Delsym specifically (see table above)
- **Pregnancy:** CYP2D6/CYP3A activity is elevated during pregnancy — individualized consideration warranted per StatPearls (no blanket contraindication given)
- **Breastfeeding:** dextromethorphan/dextrorphan minimally present in breast milk, reported not to affect the infant
- **Hepatic/renal impairment:** no dose adjustment advised per manufacturer label, though severe hepatic/renal disease is a contraindication
- **Elderly:** no specific dose adjustment needed

## Overdose / Toxicity
No specific antidote. Overdose symptoms: dyspnea, seizures, vomiting, drowsiness, tachycardia, hyperthermia, anxiety, altered mental status. Management is symptomatic — activated charcoal may help, supplemental oxygen/assisted ventilation if needed. Naloxone can reverse coma/respiratory depression in overdose but should not replace supportive care (dextromethorphan is not an opioid-receptor agonist, so naloxone's benefit here is more limited/adjunctive than in true opioid overdose).

## Pharmacy Times Context (recommendation share — NOT efficacy)
Cough Suppressants (Pharmacy Times 2026 OTC Guide, ~1,700 pharmacists surveyed): Delsym 54%, Robitussin 15%. Children's Cough: Children's Delsym 33%, Children's Robitussin 12%. These are pharmacist recommendation shares, not response/cure rates — and per the Ramsay 2008 findings above, do not imply strong subjective-symptom efficacy data. Note "Robitussin" as a brand name spans both plain dextromethorphan and guaifenesin+dextromethorphan (Robitussin DM) formulations — see Brands section below.
