## What This Page Is
Lancets are not drugs — the relevant evidence concerns device usability and infection-control safety, not efficacy. The single most important safety issue, backed by real CDC outbreak data below, is behavioral (reuse/shared use across patients), not a property of any specific device's gauge or spring mechanism.

## What to Measure and Why
Pain, puncture depth, bleeding/sample adequacy, tissue trauma, infection risk, user preference, reuse practices, gauge, spring mechanism, and sampling location.

## Key Safety Data — infection risk from reuse/sharing (real incidence data)
**CDC MMWR (2025;74(29):463–464, public domain U.S. government report):**
- During 2008–2019, **15 hepatitis B outbreaks** in U.S. long-term care facilities were tied to assisted blood glucose monitoring (shared glucometers/lancet-adjacent equipment between residents).
- A 2024 North Carolina case: a 69-year-old skilled-nursing-facility resident developed acute hepatitis B (confirmed HBsAg+, IgM anti-HBc+, HBV DNA+) after 10 months of assisted glucose monitoring with a glucometer **shared** among several residents. Investigation traced the source to another resident with previously undiagnosed chronic HBV whose monitoring period overlapped.
- Critically: the shared glucometer **was disinfected per manufacturer instructions between uses** — disinfection alone did not prevent transmission. Sharing the device/process itself was the exposure route, not a lapse in cleaning protocol.
- The first documented U.S. outbreak of this kind (26 cases) was reported in 1990, caused by reused lancet endcaps physically transferring blood from a used lancet to a supposedly-unused one, later used on a different patient.
- **CDC recommendation:** assign one glucometer (and, by extension, one lancet device) to one person — never share, regardless of cleaning between uses. HBV vaccination of persons with diabetes further reduces risk.

## Efficacy/Comfort Evidence — puncture site and pain
**Loveland et al. 1999 RCT (PMID 10489959, published in the journal *Lancet*):** lancet puncture to the **side of the thumb caused less pain** than puncture to the finger or venepuncture at the elbow, with equivalent success rates (sample adequacy) across all three methods. This is one of the few hard RCT data points located for lancet use specifically, and it concerns puncture *site* technique rather than any specific device/brand.

## Special Notes
- The single most important safety point is behavioral (reuse/shared use), not a property of any specific device design — this should not be lost by focusing only on gauge or spring-mechanism marketing claims.
- Disinfecting a shared device between uses is NOT sufficient to prevent bloodborne pathogen transmission (per the 2024 CDC case above) — the device itself must not be shared between people at all.
