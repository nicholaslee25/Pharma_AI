# Ibuprofen

**Type:** Active Ingredient
**Drug Class:** NSAID (propionic acid derivative), non-selective COX-1/COX-2 inhibitor
**Verification Status:** AI-compiled from primary sources, unverified — not yet reviewed by a pharmacist/physician
**Evidence Grade:** High — large-scale meta-analytic evidence (9186 participants) for acute pain; FDA-labeled boxed warnings for CV/GI risk
**Last updated:** 2026-08-24
**Sources:** raw/primary-sources/ibuprofen/statpearls-ibuprofen.md, raw/primary-sources/ibuprofen/fda-label-dailymed.md, raw/primary-sources/ibuprofen/cochrane-postop-pain-summary.md

## Indication
FDA-approved: rheumatoid arthritis, osteoarthritis, mild-to-moderate pain, primary dysmenorrhea, fever (IV, pediatric ≥3mo), opioid-adjunct pain (IV). OTC: fever, musculoskeletal pain, dysmenorrhea, headache/migraine, sore throat, cold/flu, arthralgia, odontalgia. IV ibuprofen lysine: patent ductus arteriosus (PDA) closure in premature infants.

## Mechanism
Non-selective COX inhibitor — blocks COX-1 and COX-2, reducing prostaglandin synthesis. COX-1 inhibited ~2.5x more strongly than COX-2. Produces analgesic, antipyretic, anti-inflammatory effects.

## Dosing

| Population | Dose | Max |
|---|---|---|
| Adult, OTC/unsupervised pain relief | 200–400 mg PO q4–6h | 1200 mg/day |
| Adult, physician-directed | 400–800 mg PO q6h | 3200 mg/day |
| Adult, RA/OA/ankylosing spondylitis | — | 1800–2400 mg/day |
| Adult, acute pericarditis (ACC) | 600–800 mg PO q8h | — |
| Pediatric | 5–10 mg/kg PO q6–8h | 400 mg/dose or 40 mg/kg/day |
| PDA closure (ibuprofen lysine, IV) | 10 mg/kg initial, then 5 mg/kg at 24h and 48h | — |

**Renal dose adjustment (elderly, by eGFR):** ≥60 → max 1600 mg/day · 30–59 → max 1200 mg/day · ≤29 → contraindicated. GFR <30 mL/min/1.73m²: avoid entirely (KDIGO 2012). GFR <60: prolonged therapy not recommended.

## Efficacy Evidence — Cochrane, single-dose acute postoperative pain (Derry et al. 2009, PMID 19588326)
72 RCTs, 9186 participants, ibuprofen vs. placebo, ≥50% pain relief over 4–6h as primary outcome:
- **200 mg** (n=2690): NNT **2.7** (95% CI 2.5–3.0); 46% achieved ≥50% relief
- **400 mg** (n=6475): NNT **2.5** (95% CI 2.4–2.6); 54% achieved ≥50% relief
- Remedication within 6h: 48% (200mg) vs. 42% (400mg); median time to remedication 4.7h vs. 5.4h
- Dental-impaction pain models and soluble ibuprofen salts produced better efficacy estimates than other models/formulations
- Adverse events: uncommon, not different from placebo
- Note: 2 of 4 review authors disclosed pharma industry consulting/lecture-fee ties; funding was academic/public (Oxford Pain Research, NHS, NIHR)

## Pharmacokinetics
Rapid, complete oral absorption; peak serum 1–2h. Food reduces Cmax 30–50%, delays Tmax 30–60min (extent of absorption not appreciably reduced). ~99% protein-bound. Linear dose-response up to 800 mg single dose; above that, AUC increases less than proportionally. Metabolized via CYP2C9/CYP2C8 to two major metabolites (~25% and ~37% of 24h urinary recovery respectively), ~1% excreted unchanged, ~14% as conjugate. No accumulation/enzyme induction. Serum half-life 1.8–2.0h. Excretion essentially complete within 24h.

## Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to ibuprofen, other NSAIDs, or aspirin
- History of asthma/urticaria/allergic reaction to aspirin or NSAIDs (aspirin-triad patients: asthma + nasal polyps/rhinitis + NSAID-induced bronchospasm — risk of severe, potentially fatal bronchospasm)
- CABG (coronary artery bypass graft) surgery setting
- IV ibuprofen lysine specifically: congenital heart disease requiring PDA patency, active bleeding, thrombocytopenia, renal impairment, coagulation defects, necrotizing enterocolitis

## Boxed Warnings (FDA label)
1. **Cardiovascular thrombotic events** — increased MI/stroke risk, can be fatal, risk may occur early and rises with duration; ~2x increase in heart-failure hospitalization vs. placebo
2. **Contraindicated perioperatively for CABG**
3. **GI bleeding/ulceration/perforation** — can occur anytime without warning; elderly at greater risk

## Key Safety Data (with real incidence numbers)
- **GI ulcer/bleed/perforation:** ~1% of patients at 3–6 months of use, ~2–4% at one year; >10-fold increased risk with prior ulcer/GI-bleed history
- **Hepatic:** borderline LFT elevation in ~15% of NSAID users; ALT/AST rise to ~3x ULN in ~1% of trial patients; rare severe/fatal hepatic reactions reported
- **Hematologic:** ≥1 g/dL hemoglobin drop in 17.1% (1600 mg/day, OA) and 22.8% (2400 mg/day, RA) of patients in postmarketing data
- **Renal:** NNH 5.5 for AKI in dehydrated ultramarathon runners (StatPearls-cited figure) — illustrates dehydration as a major renal-risk multiplier, not just chronic disease
- **Serious skin reactions:** Stevens-Johnson syndrome, toxic epidermal necrolysis, exfoliative dermatitis, fixed drug eruption (can be life-threatening) — discontinue at first rash
- **DRESS syndrome:** fever + rash + lymphadenopathy + facial swelling, may include hepatitis/nephritis/myocarditis — some fatal

## Drug Interactions
- **Aspirin (cardioprotective, low-dose):** ibuprofen blocks aspirin's antiplatelet effect if dosed too close together. Precise timing data (thromboxane B2 inhibition): ibuprofen 2h *before* aspirin → 53% inhibition (interaction present); 8h before → 90.7%; 2h *after* aspirin → 99.2% (no interaction); ibuprofen 400mg TID timed 1h/7h/13h post-aspirin → 98.3% mean inhibition (no interaction). **Practical rule: dose immediate-release aspirin ≥2h before ibuprofen, or consider an alternative analgesic for patients relying on aspirin cardioprotection.**
- **ACE inhibitors:** reduced antihypertensive effect
- **Diuretics (furosemide, thiazides):** reduced natriuretic effect; increased AKI risk
- **Lithium:** plasma lithium ↑15%, renal clearance ↓19% in one cited study — monitor for toxicity
- **Methotrexate:** competitive inhibition of MTX renal clearance → can enhance MTX toxicity
- **Warfarin:** no significant effect on PT/clotting factors in controlled studies, but combined GI bleeding risk is synergistic (higher than either drug alone)
- **H2 antagonists (cimetidine, ranitidine):** no significant effect on ibuprofen levels

## Special Population Flags
- **Pregnancy:** avoid ≥30 weeks gestation (premature ductus arteriosus closure risk); ≥20 weeks risk of fetal renal dysfunction/oligohydramnios; if used 20–30 weeks, lowest dose/shortest duration, consider ultrasound monitoring if treatment >48h
- **Breastfeeding:** StatPearls (2024) calls ibuprofen a preferred NSAID for nursing mothers — relative infant dose <0.38% of maternal dose (up to 0.6% during colostral phase). Note: the FDA label text (older, more conservative) instead says it's unknown if excreted in milk and to weigh discontinuing nursing vs. drug — both are preserved here since they're genuinely different-vintage sources; StatPearls reflects more current pharmacokinetic data.
- **Pediatric:** this specific FDA label states safety/effectiveness not established and no controlled pediatric trials were conducted under it — but StatPearls gives an actual pediatric dosing regimen (5–10 mg/kg q6–8h) reflecting real-world/other-label pediatric use (e.g. Children's Advil/Motrin). Both facts are true simultaneously: this label is conservative, other ibuprofen-specific pediatric products/labels exist.
- **Elderly:** AGS 2023 guidance — long-term use only when necessary, with gastroprotection (PPI or misoprostol); avoid short-term combination with corticosteroids/anticoagulants/antiplatelets unless no effective alternative exists and gastroprotection is feasible. General caution ≥65 years per FDA label.
- **Renal impairment:** see Dosing table above for eGFR-based limits.
- **Hepatic impairment:** no specific FDA dose modification; use caution in preexisting liver disease (drug-induced liver injury risk).

## Overdose / Toxicity
- Max labeled daily dose: 3200 mg
- Severe pediatric toxicity threshold: ≥400 mg/kg
- Case reports on this FDA label: 19-month-old (12kg) ingesting 2800–4000mg → apnea/cyanosis, blood level 102.9 mcg/mL at 8.5h, full recovery by 12h. 19-year-old ingesting 8000mg → dizziness/nystagmus, recovered with supportive care.
- No antidote. Management: gastric emptying if <1h post-ingestion, activated charcoal, supportive care. CRRT/hemodialysis may help stabilize severe cases despite limited acute drug removal; Selective Plasma Adsorption (SPAD) noted as promising for severe multi-organ-failure overdose (StatPearls, 2024 — an emerging technique, not standard of care).
