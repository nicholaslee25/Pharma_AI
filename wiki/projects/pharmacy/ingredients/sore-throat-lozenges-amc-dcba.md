## Indication
Acute uncomplicated sore throat pain relief. Does not treat the underlying (typically viral) infection.

## Mechanism
Local antiseptic and local anesthetic action at the oropharyngeal mucosa — mechanistically distinct from flurbiprofen lozenges, which act via local NSAID-mediated anti-inflammatory/analgesic action (see [[sore-throat-lozenges-flurbiprofen]]).

## Dosing
Formulation studied: amylmetacresol 0.6mg + 2,4-dichlorobenzyl alcohol 1.2mg per lozenge (Weckmann et al. 2017). Specific frequency/max-dose label directions were not independently pulled from a US product label in this pass (see Sourcing Note above).

## Efficacy Evidence

**Weckmann et al. 2017 systematic review/meta-analysis (PMID 28869700, DOI 10.1111/ijcp.13002, PROSPERO CRD42015008826)** — 77 citations screened, 3 RCTs met inclusion, 660 adults with acute uncomplicated sore throat:
- **Primary outcome** (pain intensity reduction at ~2h post-dose vs. placebo): SMD **−0.60** (95% CI −0.75 to −0.45) on an 11-point scale, favoring AMC/DCBA
- **Adverse events:** no serious AEs; mild effects (headache, cough, nasal congestion, oral irritation) in up to 16% of subjects in BOTH active and placebo groups — not clearly drug-attributable
- **Major limitation: all 3 included trials were sponsored by a manufacturer of AMC/DCBA-containing lozenges**
- Authors' own conclusion calls the additional effect vs. non-medicated lozenges "modest"

**McNally et al. 2009 RCT (PMID 19849767)** — N=310 (155 active/155 placebo); pain reduction detectable as early as 5 minutes, persisting ~2 hours; also improved swallowing difficulty and functional impairment; no AE difference between groups. *(PMID existence/attribution confirmed via PubMed's related-article linking from the directly-verified Weckmann review; the specific numbers here were not independently re-read from the full abstract this pass — see raw file for this caveat.)*

**McNally et al. 2012 AMC/DCBA+lidocaine RCT (PMID 22579007)** — N=190, randomized to AMC/DCBA+lidocaine, hexylresorcinol, or placebo; onset ~1–10min (AMC/DCBA+lidocaine) vs. ~1–5min (hexylresorcinol); **the AMC/DCBA+lidocaine arm did NOT reach significance on its primary endpoint**, though several secondary endpoints favored active treatment. Retained deliberately as an example of a partially negative trial within this evidence base, rather than cherry-picking only positive results. *(Same verification caveat as the 2009 RCT above.)*

## Key Safety Data
No serious adverse events across any of the three trials reviewed. Mild effects (headache, cough, nasal congestion, oral irritation) occurred at similar rates (up to 16%) in both active and placebo groups in the Weckmann meta-analysis, suggesting these may reflect the underlying URI/sore-throat illness itself rather than a drug effect.

## Drug Interactions

## Special Population Flags
