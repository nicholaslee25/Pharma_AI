# Second-Generation Antihistamines

**Type:** Active Ingredient (class)
**Drug Class:** Peripheral H1-receptor antagonists (examples: cetirizine, levocetirizine, loratadine, desloratadine, fexofenadine)
**Verification Status:** AI-compiled from primary sources, unverified — not yet reviewed by a pharmacist/physician
**Evidence Grade:** Strong for allergic rhinitis symptom relief as a class; between-individual-drug differences are mostly small/clinically trivial per a 2026 network meta-analysis, with mostly low-to-very-low certainty evidence
**Last updated:** 2026-08-25
**Sources:** raw/primary-sources/second-generation-antihistamines/statpearls-cetirizine.md, raw/primary-sources/second-generation-antihistamines/dailymed-zyrtec.md, raw/primary-sources/second-generation-antihistamines/pubmed-vieira-2026-network-meta-analysis.md

**Coverage note:** This page uses cetirizine (Zyrtec) as the representative agent for StatPearls/FDA-label data, since it has the largest single Pharmacy Times recommendation share among this class in this database. Loratadine and fexofenadine have their own separate StatPearls entries (not independently pulled in this pass) and may differ in specific PK/interaction details.

---

## Indication
Allergic rhinitis (seasonal/perennial): runny nose, sneezing, itchy/watery eyes, itching of nose/throat. Also FDA-approved (cetirizine specifically) for chronic and acute urticaria and allergic conjunctivitis.

## Mechanism
Selective peripheral H1-receptor antagonism. Unlike first-generation antihistamines (e.g. diphenhydramine), second-generation agents cross the blood-brain barrier minimally, producing substantially less sedation. Cetirizine specifically also reduces vascular permeability and has anti-inflammatory effects (reduced neutrophil/eosinophil migration) beyond pure H1 blockade.

## Dosing (cetirizine, per Zyrtec FDA OTC label and StatPearls)

| Population | Dose |
|---|---|
| Adults/children ≥12y | 5–10mg once daily |
| Children 6–11y | 5–10mg once daily (syrup) |
| Children 2–5y | 2.5mg once daily (syrup) |
| Infants 6–23mo | 2.5mg once daily (syrup) — non-OTC/physician-directed use |

OTC label: adults/children ≥6y, one 10mg tablet once daily, max 1/24h; consult a doctor if ≥65y, <6y, or with liver/kidney disease.

## Efficacy Evidence — Vieira et al. 2026 network meta-analysis (PMID 41707943)
74 RCTs (21 perennial AR, 53 seasonal AR) comparing oral H1-antihistamines vs. placebo and vs. each other:
- **Cetirizine, ebastine, bilastine, and rupatadine** were among the individual agents with the highest efficacy for improving nasal symptoms specifically
- Most-efficacious agent varied for other outcomes (ocular symptoms, quality of life)
- Similar adverse-event frequency across second-generation agents; serious AEs rare
- **Certainty of evidence for most comparisons was rated "low" or "very low"**
- **Authors' conclusion:** differences between individual second-generation antihistamines are mostly trivial or small, with no relevant safety-profile differences found — i.e., brand/molecule choice within this class matters less than commonly assumed, though cetirizine trends slightly favorably for nasal symptoms specifically

## Pharmacokinetics (cetirizine)
Rapid GI absorption, peak plasma ~1h; onset 20–60min, persists ≥24h (supports once-daily dosing). 93% protein-bound. Oxidative O-dealkylation metabolism; NOT a CYP450 substrate (lower drug-interaction potential than many drugs), but is a P-glycoprotein substrate. Half-life 8.3h. Primarily renal excretion.

## Contraindications
Known hypersensitivity to cetirizine or its ingredients; known hypersensitivity to hydroxyzine (cetirizine's parent compound, relevant given cross-reactivity).

## Key Safety Data
- **Adults, most common AEs:** somnolence (dose-related), fatigue, pharyngitis, dizziness, dry mouth
- **Pediatric:** similar profile; more likely than adults to report headache; mostly mild/moderate AEs in ages 2–11
- **Rare/severe (postmarketing):** severe hypotension, anaphylaxis, hemolytic anemia, cholestasis, orofacial dyskinesia, glomerulonephritis, hepatitis, stillbirth, thrombocytopenia
- **Overdose case data:** adult 150mg — somnolence only; infant 180mg — restlessness/irritability/drowsiness; 6-year-old accidental ingestion — fixed/dilated pupils, tachycardia, agitation, hyperthermia, hallucinations (anticholinergic toxicity pattern)

## Drug Interactions (cetirizine)
- **CNS depressants** (alcohol, benzodiazepines, opioids): dose-related sedation risk
- **Pitolisant:** cetirizine may reduce its therapeutic efficacy
- **Gabapentin:** cetirizine decreases gabapentin plasma levels; combined CNS depression possible
- **Verapamil, erdafitinib** (P-gp inhibitors): increase cetirizine CNS penetration; avoid concurrent use with erdafitinib specifically

## Special Population Flags
- **Hepatic impairment:** reduce cetirizine to 5mg/day (≥12y); lower pediatric doses; monitor LFTs
- **Renal impairment:** reduce to 5mg/day for CrCl 11–31 mL/min or hemodialysis (≥12y)
- **Pregnancy:** ACOG/ACAAI support cetirizine when antihistamine treatment is needed, used only when necessary; some guidance still considers first-generation antihistamines the historically "safest" option — a nuance worth preserving rather than flattening into a single recommendation
- **Breastfeeding:** occasional/small doses acceptable; prolonged larger doses risk decreased milk supply and infant drowsiness; OTC label specifically says "not recommended" while breastfeeding — the two sources differ in strictness, both preserved here
- **Elderly (≥65y):** inadequate efficacy-specific data; safety profile not significantly different from younger adults, though individual ADR sensitivity remains possible

## Overdose / Toxicity
Animal data: minimal lethal dose in rats ~460x max recommended adult daily dose. No known specific antidote; cetirizine is not effectively removed by dialysis. Management is supportive/symptomatic.

## Pharmacy Times Context (recommendation share — NOT efficacy)
Antihistamines, Oral (Pharmacy Times 2026 OTC Guide, ~1,700 pharmacists surveyed, 2,399,370 monthly recommendations): Zyrtec 34%, Claritin 30%, Allegra 17%, Benadryl 8%, Xyzal 6%, Alavert 5%. Note Benadryl (diphenhydramine) is a **first-generation** antihistamine and does not belong to this ingredient class despite appearing in the same Pharmacy Times category — see a first-generation-antihistamine-specific page if one exists in this database, do not conflate the two. Zyrtec-D (cetirizine + pseudoephedrine) separately holds 5% of the Decongestants, Oral category. These are pharmacist recommendation shares, not response/cure rates.
