# Fiber

**Type:** Active Ingredient
**Drug Class:** Dietary fiber supplement (types include psyllium, methylcellulose, wheat dextrin, inulin, calcium polycarbophil)
**Verification Status:** AI-compiled from primary sources, unverified — not yet reviewed by a pharmacist/physician
**Evidence Grade:** Better-supported than many supplements owing to a direct physiological mechanism and a real, quantified 2022 meta-analysis — but the effect is specific to certain fiber types (psyllium, pectin), doses (>10g/day), and durations (≥4 weeks), not a blanket "fiber helps constipation" claim
**Last updated:** 2026-08-25
**Sources:** raw/primary-sources/fiber/pubmed-vanderschoot-2022-constipation-meta-analysis.md

## Indication
Chronic constipation in adults (best-evidenced use); also marketed for cholesterol management and general digestive health (not covered in depth by the source reviewed here).

## Mechanism
Soluble fibers like psyllium absorb water and form a gel in the GI tract, increasing stool bulk/water content and softening consistency; this mechanical bulking effect stimulates peristalsis and reduces transit time. Not all fiber types work the same way or with the same evidence strength — see Efficacy Evidence.

## Dosing
Per van der Schoot et al. 2022: benefit was seen specifically at **doses >10 g/day**, sustained for **at least 4 weeks** — lower doses or shorter durations did not show the same improvement in stool frequency specifically. This is more specific, actionable guidance than a generic "increase fiber intake" recommendation.

## Efficacy Evidence — van der Schoot et al. 2022 meta-analysis (PMID 35816465)
16 RCTs, 1,251 adults with chronic constipation:
- **Overall treatment response:** 66% (fiber) vs. 41% (control) — **RR 1.48** (95% CI 1.17–1.88, p=0.001)
- **Which fiber types worked:** specifically **psyllium and pectin** showed significant effects — not a universal fiber-class effect
- **Dose-dependent:** higher response only at fiber doses **>10 g/day**
- **Duration-dependent (for stool frequency specifically):** improvement only with **≥4 weeks** of treatment at the higher dose
- **Stool consistency:** improved (SMD 0.32, 95% CI 0.18–0.46), particularly at higher doses
- **Real tradeoff:** flatulence was significantly HIGHER in fiber groups than control (SMD 0.80, 95% CI 0.47–1.13) — a common, quantified side effect worth setting expectations around
- **Authors' conclusion:** fiber supplementation is effective for constipation, with **psyllium >10g/day for ≥4 weeks** appearing optimal — though considerable heterogeneity across trials warrants some caution

## Pharmacokinetics
Not applicable in the conventional sense — fiber acts locally/mechanically in the GI tract rather than being systemically absorbed and metabolized.

## Contraindications
Not itemized in the source reviewed — general fiber-supplement caution around adequate fluid intake (to avoid worsening obstruction risk) is common general medical knowledge but was not independently sourced in this specific pass.

## Key Safety Data
Flatulence was the one specifically quantified adverse effect in the meta-analysis reviewed (significantly more common with fiber supplementation than control) — otherwise, no other adverse-event data was detailed in the abstract reviewed.

## Drug Interactions
Not addressed in the source reviewed in this pass — flagged as a gap. (Fiber supplements are commonly advised to be taken apart from other oral medications to avoid absorption interference, per general pharmacy practice, but this specific claim was not independently sourced here.)

## Special Population Flags
Not addressed with population-specific data in the source reviewed — the meta-analysis population was adults with chronic constipation generally, without reported subgroup breakdowns.

## Overdose / Toxicity

## Brands Using This Ingredient (in this database — formulation not individually verified per SKU)
Metamucil — confirmed psyllium husk per widely-known brand identity (the specific fiber type shown effective in the meta-analysis above), though not independently re-verified against a label in this pass. Benefiber (wheat dextrin), FiberCon (calcium polycarbophil), and Citrucel (methylcellulose) are different fiber types by brand identity — none of these specific types were shown significantly effective in the van der Schoot 2022 meta-analysis, which only found significant effects for psyllium and pectin specifically. This is a meaningful distinction: not every product in this category has the same evidence behind it.
