# Ammonium Lactate

**Type:** Active Ingredient
**Drug Class:** Alpha-hydroxy acid (AHA) derivative — lactic acid neutralized with ammonium hydroxide; keratolytic/humectant
**Verification Status:** AI-compiled from primary sources, unverified — not yet reviewed by a pharmacist/physician
**Evidence Grade:** Moderate — real RCT evidence for xerosis, generally found less rapid/effective than higher-concentration urea in head-to-head comparison, but superior to lower-concentration lactic acid alone
**Last updated:** 2026-08-27
**Sources:** DailyMed (Ammonium Lactate Lotion 12% label), PubMed (Rogers 1989 comparative lactic-acid-vs-ammonium-lactate RCT; Lin 2002 urea-vs-ammonium-lactate xerosis RCT), ClinicalTrials.gov (halobetasol combination trial for psoriasis)

---

## Indication
FDA-approved (prescription-strength 12%, also sold OTC by some brands at this and adjacent concentrations): treatment of dry, scaly skin (xerosis) and ichthyosis vulgaris. This is the confirmed active ingredient in [[AmLactin]], distinguishing it as one of the few genuine FDA-regulated drugs in this database's Therapeutic Skin Care, Moisturizers subcategory, most of which are cosmetics.

## Mechanism
Lactic acid (an alpha-hydroxy acid) neutralized with ammonium hydroxide to form ammonium lactate, formulated at a lotion pH of 4.5-5.5. Acts as both a **humectant** (hydrates the outermost skin layer/stratum corneum, reducing transepidermal water loss) and a mild **keratolytic** (breaks up abnormal scale/hyperkeratosis by reducing corneocyte cohesion) — a dual mechanism distinct from the pure-occlusive mechanism of petrolatum-based moisturizers (see [[petrolatum]], [[moisturizers-ceramides]]).

## Efficacy Evidence
- **Rogers et al. 1989 (double-blind RCT):** 12% ammonium lactate lotion significantly more effective than 5% lactic acid lotion alone for reducing xerosis severity, both during a 3-week treatment period and during a subsequent 3-week no-treatment regression period — supporting the neutralized/higher-concentration formulation over plain lactic acid.
- **Head-to-head vs. urea (double-blind, bilateral paired-comparison RCT, n=25, moderate-to-severe xerosis):** **40% urea cream achieved improvement faster than 12% ammonium lactate lotion**, and was superior on skin roughness, fissuring, thickness, and dryness measures at day 14 — a real, if modest, comparative efficacy disadvantage for ammonium lactate relative to high-concentration urea specifically.
- **Combination-therapy case evidence:** sequential use of 12% ammonium lactate followed by a ceramide/cholesterol/free-fatty-acid barrier-repair cream produced near-complete resolution of ichthyotic scaling in a reported case — suggesting a genuine complementary role alongside (not necessarily instead of) ceramide-based moisturizers like those covered on [[moisturizers-ceramides]].
- A separate clinical trial (NCT01111123) evaluated ammonium lactate 12% combined with halobetasol propionate (a topical corticosteroid) for psoriasis treatment/maintenance — a different indication from the OTC xerosis use case, not independently assessed for outcome in this pass.

## Key Safety Data
Generally well-tolerated; the main reported adverse effect across the AHA class is a burning/stinging sensation on application, particularly on broken or irritated skin, and increased sun sensitivity common to AHAs generally.

## Contraindications / Warnings
External use only; avoid eyes and mucous membranes; may cause irritation on broken skin; increased photosensitivity possible (sunscreen/protective measures advisable with regular AHA use, consistent with general AHA-class labeling conventions documented elsewhere in this database, e.g., [[CeraVe]]'s diabetic foot cream AHA warning).
