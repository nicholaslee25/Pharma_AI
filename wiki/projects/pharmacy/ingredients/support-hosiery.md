## What This Page Is
Support hosiery (graduated compression socks/stockings) is a **device, not a drug** — evaluated on circulatory/physiologic outcomes (edema reduction, venous symptom relief, ulcer healing) rather than drug pharmacology. Compression works via external gradient pressure (highest at the ankle, decreasing up the leg) that assists venous return and reduces capillary filtration/edema.

## Compression Class Terminology — a real, non-contradictory labeling gap
Two different mmHg systems are in common use and are frequently confused:
- **US retail/OTC labeling** (seen on Dr. Scholl's, Truform, generic drugstore hosiery): "mild" 8–15 mmHg, "moderate" 15–20 mmHg, "firm" 20–30 mmHg, "extra-firm" 30–40 mmHg.
- **European medical compression classes** (per the Rabe/Partsch 2017 consensus statement, PMID 28549402): Class 1 = 18–21 mmHg, Class 2 = 23–32 mmHg, Class 3 = 30–35 mmHg, with some systems placing high-pressure hosiery at 34–46 mmHg.
These bands overlap but are not the same scale — a US-labeled "firm, 20–30 mmHg" product sits across the European Class 1/Class 2 boundary. This is a labeling-convention difference between markets, not a discrepancy between sources.

## Efficacy Evidence

**Consensus statement (Rabe, Partsch, Hafner et al. 2017, PMID 28549402, DOI 10.1177/0268355516689631)** — graded (Grade 1 = strong recommendation) evidence-based indications:
- Chronic venous disorder symptom relief — Grade 1B
- Improved quality of life in chronic venous disease — Grade 1B
- Prevention of leg edema — Grade 1B
- Reduction of existing leg edema — Grade 1B
- Venous leg ulcer healing ("ulcer kits") — **Grade 1A**
- Reduction of venous leg ulcer recurrence — **Grade 1A**
- Immediate compression in acute DVT to reduce pain/swelling — Grade 1B
- Lymphedema maintenance therapy — **Grade 1A**
- Characterized as "robust evidence" specifically for venous ulcer care and lymphedema maintenance; "moderately robust" for general symptom relief and edema.

**Cochrane review (graduated compression stockings for varicose veins, PMID 34271595, DOI 10.1002/14651858.CD008819.pub4)** — 13 RCTs, 1,021 participants, primary varicose veins (CEAP C2–C4) without ulceration: mixed, low-to-very-low certainty results. One trial found a significant pain reduction with active stockings (mean VAS −2.8, p=0.02); another found no clear difference in ankle circumference; a third found 84.6% oedema resolution by day 3 with compression. Compliance was highly variable (33–86.7% across trials).

**Bottom line — these two reviews are not contradictory, they answer different questions.** The strong graded consensus recommendations apply to venous ulcer care, lymphedema, and edema/symptom management broadly (where compression is genuinely a first-line, guideline-backed standard of care). The weaker Cochrane evidence applies specifically to the narrower question of RCT-proven benefit for uncomplicated varicose veins without ulceration — a real, well-documented gap between long-standing clinical practice and the RCT evidence base for that specific indication. A support-hosiery product recommended for "tired, achy legs" or general varicose-vein comfort is on much thinner evidence ground than the same product recommended for a physician-diagnosed venous ulcer or lymphedema.

## FDA Classification
Medical/gradient support stockings ("to prevent pooling of blood in legs"): 21 CFR 880.5780, **Class II**, product code ODWL — a real step up in regulatory tier from the Class I joint-support-braces category above, subject to premarket notification (510(k)) requirements, though many individual OTC compression-sock SKUs at the lower end (8–20 mmHg) are marketed as general-wellness/comfort products rather than as cleared medical devices; the Class II pathway applies more clearly to prescription-strength (20+ mmHg) medical-grade hosiery. **A brand's own marketing claim of "Class II medical device" status is accurate for the product category generally, not a special distinction earned only by that brand** — see [[Truform]] for a case where this exact framing appeared in marketing copy.

## Special Notes
- The APMA (American Podiatric Medical Association) Seal of Acceptance, seen on some Dr. Scholl's packaging, is an organizational endorsement/marketing program, not an independent clinical trial or FDA finding — worth distinguishing from actual trial evidence.
- No brand-specific RCT was found for any individual brand in this category (ACE, Futuro, Dr. Scholl's, Sigvaris, Truform) beyond the class-level evidence above — expected for this device type, not a research gap.

## Brands Using This Category (in this database)
**Futuro** (38%, 3M) and **ACE** (21%, 3M) — mass-market OTC compression, typically in the "mild-to-firm" US retail band. **Dr. Scholl's** (31%) — OTC graduated compression in 8–15/15–20/20–30 mmHg tiers, APMA-accepted, positioned for travel/prolonged standing comfort. **Sigvaris** (7%) — a specialist medical-compression manufacturer offering the full European class range (Class 1–3+), positioned toward physician-directed/prescription-strength use. **Truform** (3%) — offers both OTC comfort tiers (15-20, 20-30 mmHg) and higher medical-grade tiers (30-40 mmHg, anti-embolism 18 mmHg), manufactured at an FDA-registered facility.
