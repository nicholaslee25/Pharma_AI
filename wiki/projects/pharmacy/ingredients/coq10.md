# CoQ10 (Coenzyme Q10)

**Type:** Active Ingredient
**Drug Class:** Antioxidant/mitochondrial cofactor supplement
**Verification Status:** AI-compiled from primary sources, unverified — not yet reviewed by a pharmacist/physician
**Evidence Grade:** Sharply indication-dependent — a well-powered RCT (Q-SYMBIO) found a real, significant mortality/hospitalization benefit as adjunctive chronic heart failure therapy, while a separate meta-analysis found NO benefit for the far more commonly marketed use case of statin-associated muscle symptoms
**Last updated:** 2026-08-25
**Sources:** raw/primary-sources/coq10/pubmed-mortensen-2014-qsymbio-heart-failure.md, raw/primary-sources/coq10/pubmed-wei-2022-statin-myopathy-meta-analysis.md

## Indication
Adjunctive therapy in chronic heart failure (strongest evidence — see below). Widely marketed (with much weaker evidence) for statin-associated muscle symptoms/myopathy, and generally for antioxidant/energy support.

## Mechanism
Essential cofactor for mitochondrial electron transport (energy production) and a lipid-soluble antioxidant. Relevant to statins specifically: statins inhibit HMG-CoA reductase, which sits upstream in the same mevalonate pathway that also produces CoQ10 — so statins reliably LOWER serum (and sometimes muscle-tissue) CoQ10 levels. This is the mechanistic rationale for CoQ10 supplementation alongside statins, though (see Efficacy Evidence) the mechanistic plausibility has not translated into a demonstrated clinical benefit for muscle symptoms specifically.

## Efficacy Evidence

**Mortensen et al. 2014 Q-SYMBIO trial (PMID 25282031)** — chronic heart failure, adjunctive therapy:
- Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, 420 patients with moderate-severe HF, CoQ10 100mg three times daily (300mg/day) vs. placebo added to standard therapy, 2-year follow-up
- **Primary composite cardiovascular endpoint (2 years):** reached by 15% (CoQ10) vs. 26% (placebo) — HR **0.50** (95% CI 0.32–0.80, p=0.003)
- **Cardiovascular mortality:** 9% vs. 16% (p=0.026); **all-cause mortality:** 10% vs. 18% (p=0.018)
- Significantly lower HF hospitalization incidence (p=0.033) and significant NYHA class improvement at 2 years (p=0.028)
- No significant short-term (16-week) changes in NYHA class, 6-min walk, or NT-proBNP
- **Authors' conclusion:** long-term CoQ10 (300mg/day) is safe, improves symptoms, and reduces major adverse cardiovascular events in chronic HF — a genuinely strong, well-powered positive result (the authors themselves note prior CoQ10-in-HF trials were underpowered for major clinical endpoints)

**Wei et al. 2022 meta-analysis (PMID 33999383)** — statin-induced myopathy, 8 studies, 472 patients:
- **Creatine kinase (objective marker):** mean difference +3.29 U/L (95% CI −29.58 to 36.17, p=0.84) — **no significant effect**
- **Muscle pain (subjective):** SMD −0.59 (95% CI −1.54 to 0.36, p=0.22) — **no significant effect**
- **Authors' conclusion:** CoQ10 supplementation showed NO significant benefit for statin-induced myopathy on either objective or subjective measures

**This is a deliberately preserved two-sided finding:** the SAME supplement has real, well-powered positive trial evidence for one specific indication (chronic heart failure, at a specific 300mg/day dose) and negative trial evidence (across 8 pooled RCTs) for the far more commonly marketed consumer use case (statin muscle symptoms). A consumer taking CoQ10 "for statin side effects" is using it for the weaker-evidenced indication, not the stronger one.

## Key Safety Data
Q-SYMBIO (2-year, 420 patients, 300mg/day) found long-term CoQ10 to be safe with no notable adverse-event signal reported in the abstract reviewed. No specific safety concerns were raised in the statin-myopathy meta-analysis either — the negative finding there was about lack of efficacy, not safety.

## Drug Interactions
Not itemized in the sources reviewed — flagged as a gap. (General pharmacology knowledge suggests CoQ10 may theoretically interact with warfarin via vitamin-K-like structural similarity, but this was not independently confirmed via a primary source in this pass and is therefore not asserted here as established.)

## Special Population Flags
- **Chronic heart failure patients on standard therapy:** the specific, well-evidenced population for the 300mg/day adjunctive-therapy benefit shown in Q-SYMBIO
- **Statin users seeking myopathy relief:** the common real-world use case, but NOT the one with positive trial support per Wei et al. 2022

## Overdose / Toxicity
Not addressed with specific toxicity data in the sources reviewed; Q-SYMBIO's 2-year safety data at 300mg/day showed no notable adverse-event signal.
