## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed OTC label: Benadryl Allergy's active ingredient is **diphenhydramine HCl 25mg** (oral). This is a **first-generation** antihistamine, distinct from the second-generation agents (Zyrtec, Claritin, Allegra, Xyzal, Alavert) it's ranked alongside in the Antihistamines, Oral category — this database has no dedicated ingredient page for diphenhydramine/first-generation antihistamines (the [[second-generation-antihistamines]] page explicitly excludes it), so this page stands on its own DailyMed/search-derived data rather than pointing to an ingredients/ page. ## Pros
- Fast onset, effective for acute allergic reactions and hives; also FDA-labeled as a cough suppressant and sleep aid ingredient in other diphenhydramine products
- Long, well-established safety record for short-term allergic-reaction use
- Widely available, inexpensive, no prescription needed

## Cons
- Significant sedation is the norm, not a side effect to be minimized — this is a first-generation antihistamine that crosses the blood-brain barrier freely
- A meta-analysis of 18 RCTs found diphenhydramine produces significantly worse cognitive performance (attention, working memory, processing speed, vigilance) than second-generation antihistamines or placebo
- **The TIRED study** (randomized, placebo-controlled, PMID 16818836, JAMA Pediatrics) found diphenhydramine was **no more effective than placebo** for infant nighttime awakening (1/22 improved on drug vs. 3/22 on placebo) — the trial's data safety monitoring board stopped it early for futility. This directly undercuts diphenhydramine's common off-label reputation as a sleep aid.
- Anticholinergic side effects (dry mouth, urinary retention risk, confusion in elderly) — the American Geriatrics Society Beers Criteria flags first-generation antihistamines as generally inappropriate for older adults
- Do not combine with other diphenhydramine-containing products (including topical) — additive overdose risk

## Use Cases
Allergic rhinitis and hives/urticaria (oral); allergic reactions; occasional sleep aid (though evidence for this specific use is weak per the TIRED study above); cough suppression (some diphenhydramine-containing cough formulations); topical anti-itch (separate topical products, not the oral tablet covered here).

## Allergies / Warnings
Contraindicated in known diphenhydramine hypersensitivity. Ask a doctor before use with glaucoma, breathing problems (emphysema/chronic bronchitis), or enlarged prostate/trouble urinating. May cause marked drowsiness — avoid alcohol and other CNS depressants. Overdose can cause hallucinations, inability to be awakened, seizures, trouble breathing, or collapse — contact Poison Control (1-800-222-1222) or seek emergency care immediately.

## Symptoms Treated
Runny nose, sneezing, itchy/watery eyes, hives, allergic reactions, occasional sleeplessness (weak evidence), cough (in combination products).
