## Appears In

## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed: plain "Phillips'" is Milk of Magnesia (magnesium hydroxide 1200mg/15mL) — a genuinely different mechanism (saline/osmotic laxative) than every fiber-based product in this database's Laxatives Bulk/Fiber category, and distinct from the same brand family's own Phillips' Stool Softener (docusate, researched separately) and Phillips' Fiber Good (inulin dietary-supplement gummy, researched separately) — a real three-way mechanism split under one "Phillips'" family name. **Missing-ingredient-page finding resolved:** this database previously had no dedicated page for magnesium hydroxide's laxative use (distinct from [[magnesium]]'s dietary-supplement framing); a new [[magnesium-hydroxide]] page was created in this pass specifically because the laxative dose (up to 4,800mg/day at label maximum) vastly exceeds [[magnesium]]'s cited supplemental Tolerable Upper Intake Level (350mg), a genuinely different safety context.

## Pros
- Confirmed FDA OTC label; fast onset (bowel movement in ½-6 hours per label) — meaningfully faster than PEG-3350 (MiraLAX, 1-3 days) or bulk fiber (12-72 hours), making this a better fit for a consumer wanting same-day relief
- Long-standing, well-established OTC product with extensive real-world use history at normal renal function

## Cons
- **Real, documented hypermagnesemia/toxicity risk in renal impairment** per the new [[magnesium-hydroxide]] page: because magnesium is renally cleared, patients with kidney disease cannot adequately eliminate the large magnesium load a laxative dose delivers, and published case reports document severe, sometimes fatal hypermagnesemia from Milk-of-Magnesia use in this population — a materially more serious risk profile than fiber-based, PEG-3350-based, or stimulant laxatives for this specific patient population
- Elderly patients using this unsupervised/chronically carry elevated hypermagnesemia risk even without diagnosed renal failure, per case-report literature reviewed for [[magnesium-hydroxide]]
- Real brand-family confusion risk: "Phillips'" spans three mechanistically distinct products (Milk of Magnesia here, docusate stool softener, inulin fiber-supplement gummy) — a consumer assuming brand-name consistency implies mechanistic consistency would be mistaken
- No Phillips'-brand-specific RCT beyond general magnesium-hydroxide laxative literature was independently located in this pass

## Use Cases
Fast relief of occasional constipation via a saline/osmotic mechanism — a same-day-relief use case genuinely distinct from PEG-3350's slower onset and fiber's preventive/maintenance use case.

## Allergies / Warnings
**Real, brand-specific warning: patients with kidney disease should avoid or use only under physician guidance, given documented hypermagnesemia/toxicity risk with impaired renal clearance.** Per label: do not exceed maximum recommended daily dose; ask a doctor if on a magnesium-restricted diet.

## Symptoms Treated
Occasional constipation — via the saline/osmotic mechanism detailed in the new [[magnesium-hydroxide]] page, genuinely faster-onset than most other laxative mechanisms in this database.
