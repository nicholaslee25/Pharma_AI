## Overview
Estroven's core formula is confirmed as black cohosh + soy isoflavones + rhapontic rhubarb extract (a third phytoestrogen-family ingredient) — see [[menopause-herbal-supplements]] for the full evidence base (mixed/low-moderate: modest hot-flash reduction shown in some trials, inconsistent across others, with black cohosh's mechanism itself not definitively established as phytoestrogenic despite common marketing framing). **Real, confirmed one-brand-many-formulations finding:** Estroven sells multiple sub-SKUs under variant names — "Complete Menopause Relief" is stated to contain NO soy (a genuine formulation difference from the core product), while other variants add melatonin (Sleep Cool), magnolia bark (Stress Relief & Energy Boost), or green tea/yerba mate (energy-focused variants) — meaning "Estroven" as tracked in this database's 50% category share cannot be assumed to refer to one fixed ingredient list.

## Pros
- Uses named, real, at-least-partially-evidenced phytoestrogen ingredients (black cohosh, soy isoflavones) rather than an ingredient class with no plausible mechanism at all
- Product-line variety lets users match a specific sub-formulation to co-occurring symptoms (sleep, stress, energy) beyond hot flashes alone
- Category-leading share (50%) reflects long market history and consumer trust, though this database's standing caution applies: market share is not an efficacy measure

## Cons
- **No Cochrane-level, high-certainty efficacy evidence exists for black cohosh or soy isoflavones** — per [[menopause-herbal-supplements]], NCCIH describes black cohosh evidence as mixed, and black cohosh's very mechanism (long assumed to be phytoestrogenic) is now understood by NCCIH to possibly work through non-estrogenic pathways instead — a real gap between common marketing framing and current science
- Black cohosh carries a real, if uncommon, hepatotoxicity signal (rare case reports of liver injury) per [[menopause-herbal-supplements]] — not established as causal, but a genuine regulatory-flagged caution
- **"Estroven" is not one fixed formula** — the brand spans soy-containing and soy-free variants, plus melatonin-, magnolia-bark-, and caffeine-source-added versions, a real navigation burden for a consumer comparing products by brand name alone rather than checking the specific SKU's ingredient panel
- No brand-specific (Estroven-specific) RCT distinct from the general black-cohosh/soy-isoflavone literature was located in this pass

## Use Cases
Relief of menopausal hot flashes, night sweats, and (per specific sub-SKU) mood, sleep, stress, or energy support.

## Allergies / Warnings
Discontinue and seek care for signs of liver injury (jaundice, dark urine, abdominal pain) given black cohosh's hepatotoxicity signal. Soy isoflavones carry a theoretical caution for those with a personal/family history of hormone-sensitive (estrogen-receptor-positive) cancers — discuss with a physician before use. Soy-containing variants are obviously unsuitable for soy allergy; check the specific SKU since not all Estroven variants contain soy.

## Symptoms Treated
Hot flashes, night sweats (core evidence-adjacent claim); sleep, stress, energy, mood (sub-SKU-specific, additional ingredients).
