## Appears In

## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed: Neosporin Original's own FDA OTC Drug Facts label lists **bacitracin zinc, neomycin sulfate, and polymyxin B sulfate** — the classic "triple antibiotic" combination — as its active ingredients. It is the category leader in both places it appears (57% of Topical Antibiotics/Antiseptics, the largest share by far, and 32% of Burn Treatments). A brand-family nuance found in this pass: Johnson & Johnson/Kenvue also sells **Simply Neosporin**, a neomycin-free 2-ingredient version (bacitracin zinc + polymyxin B sulfate only, per its own DailyMed label) explicitly marketed for people with a neomycin allergy — meaning "Neosporin" is not a single fixed formulation across the full product line, though the flagship "Neosporin Original" product driving these two category shares is the triple-antibiotic version. Per [[topical-antibiotics]], neomycin carries its own well-documented contact-allergy and rare-anaphylaxis risk (NACDG data: ~7.0% patch-test prevalence) distinct from and in addition to bacitracin's — meaning Neosporin Original's triple-antibiotic formula carries a broader allergy-risk surface than the neomycin-free Bacitraycin Plus/Polysporin products it competes against in the same category. Per the pivotal Smack et al. 1996 RCT already documented on [[petrolatum]]/[[topical-antibiotics]], plain petrolatum performed comparably to bacitracin alone on overall infection rate with a cleaner allergy profile — that finding applies most directly to single-agent bacitracin, not independently re-tested for the triple-antibiotic combination, though the underlying allergy-risk logic (more active ingredients = more allergen exposure) extends naturally to Neosporin's 3-ingredient formula.

## Pros
- Confirmed FDA Drug Facts label with a well-established, decades-old active-ingredient combination (triple antibiotic)
- By far the largest share in Topical Antibiotics/Antiseptics (57%) and the largest in Burn Treatments (32%), reflecting broad consumer/pharmacist familiarity
- A neomycin-free "Simply Neosporin" variant exists under the same parent brand for neomycin-allergic users, without requiring a switch to a different brand name entirely

## Cons
- Triple-antibiotic formula carries allergy-risk exposure from THREE potential contact allergens (bacitracin, neomycin, polymyxin B) rather than one or two — neomycin specifically is well-documented as a common and sometimes severe contact/anaphylaxis allergen per [[topical-antibiotics]]
- Per the pivotal RCT evidence on [[petrolatum]], antibiotic ointment (at least the single-agent bacitracin form directly tested) is not proven to reduce OVERALL infection risk vs. plain petrolatum for uncomplicated minor wounds — it does specifically prevent staph infections, a real but narrower benefit
- "Neosporin" as a brand name spans at least two materially different formulations (Original triple-antibiotic vs. Simply Neosporin's 2-ingredient neomycin-free version) — a consumer should check the specific product, not assume "Neosporin" means one fixed ingredient list
- No brand-specific (as opposed to ingredient-class) clinical trial for Neosporin's specific triple-combination product was identified beyond the general topical-antibiotic evidence

## Use Cases
First aid to help prevent infection in minor cuts, scrapes, and burns.

## Allergies / Warnings
Per label: for external use only; stop use and ask a doctor if the condition persists/worsens, or if a rash or other allergic reaction develops. Neomycin-containing formula (Original) carries a materially higher contact-allergy/rare-anaphylaxis risk than the neomycin-free Simply Neosporin variant — see [[topical-antibiotics]] for the quantified NACDG patch-test data.

## Symptoms Treated
Prevention/treatment of minor localized skin infection risk in cuts, scrapes, and burns; does not relieve pain (no analgesic ingredient in the Original formula, unlike Bacitraycin Plus or Bactine MAX's combination products).
