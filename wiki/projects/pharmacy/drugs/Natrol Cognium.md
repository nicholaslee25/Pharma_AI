## Overview
**Real, serious discrepancy found here, directly confirming the pattern already flagged on [[Natrol]]'s own brand page.** Natrol Cognium's active ingredient is silk protein hydrolysate ("Cera-Q," derived from silkworm cocoons), packaged with claims of improving "Memory Recall Efficiency" by 90% after four weeks of twice-daily use. A class-action lawsuit alleges: (1) **2 of the 9 clinical studies cited on the product's own packaging have been retracted, including one specifically for data fabrication and falsification**; (2) the studies actually supporting the ingredient used doses of **400-600mg/day — 2 to 6 times the amount actually contained in two Cognium tablets**, meaning even if the underlying research were sound, the commercial product's dose does not match the tested dose; and (3) independent experts retained in the litigation concluded the ingredient likely works no better than a placebo at the labeled dose. This is a real, serious, multi-part evidence failure — not merely "insufficient evidence" but active data-integrity problems (a retracted fabrication study) combined with a confirmed dose mismatch. See [[brain-health-nootropics]] for the broader cross-brand pattern this fits.
