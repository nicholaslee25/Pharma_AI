## Appears In

## Overview
CranRx (a Nature's Way product line — see [[Nature's Way]] for that brand's own separate quality/litigation profile) is confirmed via manufacturer/retailer product listings to use a **100% whole cranberry fruit concentrate** (juice, flesh, skin, and seeds, not an isolated PAC extract), standardized to proanthocyanidins (PACs) in its capsule form; the liquid formulation adds D-mannose, vitamin C, and hibiscus extract. This "whole fruit" positioning is a genuine formulation detail distinguishing it from isolated-PAC-extract competitors, though this pass found no evidence that whole-fruit-vs-isolated-extract formulation changes clinical outcomes specifically — [[cranberry]]'s underlying Cochrane review notes dose/formulation comparisons remain unclear/very-low-certainty even within its own analysis. No CranRx-brand-specific clinical trial was located. Cranberry Supplements (7% share) is covered by [[cranberry]] — see that page for the population-specific efficacy breakdown (strong evidence in children and intervention-susceptible patients, modest in women with recurrent UTIs, absent in elderly institutionalized/pregnant/neuromuscular-bladder-dysfunction populations).

## Pros
- Real, confirmed formulation transparency: whole-fruit cranberry concentrate (not merely an isolated extract), free of common allergens/additives (no dairy, corn, soy, wheat, artificial colors/flavors per the manufacturer's own ingredient statement) — more disclosed formulation detail than several competitors in this pass
- Liquid formulation adds D-mannose and vitamin C, both plausible complementary urinary-tract-health ingredients (though not independently evidence-graded on their own dedicated pages in this database)

## Cons
- No CranRx-brand-specific clinical trial was located — efficacy evidence is at the general cranberry-ingredient level only
- Whole-fruit-vs-isolated-PAC-extract formulation was not shown in this pass to have a different clinical effect than other cranberry formulations — a real, unresolved formulation-vs-outcome gap
- Same population-specific efficacy caveat applies as for every cranberry product in this category: real benefit shown for children/intervention-susceptible patients, modest for women with recurrent UTIs, little-to-none for elderly institutionalized, pregnant, or neuromuscular-bladder-dysfunction populations — not a blanket "cures/prevents UTIs" product regardless of who takes it

## Use Cases
Prevention (not treatment) of recurrent UTIs — see [[cranberry]] for the population-specific evidence breakdown.

Manufacturer states the product is free of dairy, corn, soy, wheat, artificial colors/flavors, and preservatives (liquid form uses citric acid and mixed tocopherols as natural preservatives) — a real positive for allergy-sensitive users, though the specific SKU's full label should still be checked. See [[cranberry]] for general ingredient-level cautions; not a treatment for an active infection.
