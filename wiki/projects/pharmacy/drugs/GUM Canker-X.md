## Overview
GUM Canker-X (manufactured by Sunstar, marketed as based on the internationally-sold "Aloclair" mouth-ulcer gel) is explicitly marketed as containing **no alcohol and no benzocaine**. Its key ingredients are **polyvinylpyrrolidone (PVP), sodium hyaluronate, and aloe vera** — a bioadhesive-film/barrier mechanism rather than a true local anesthetic: the PVP forms a "micro-thin, invisible protective coating" over the sore intended to shield exposed nerve endings and promote healing, mechanistically closer to DenTek Canker Relief's patch-barrier approach than to benzocaine's sodium-channel-blocking anesthesia. No DailyMed listing under the "GUM Canker-X" name was located in this pass — its regulatory filing status (OTC monograph drug vs. medical device) was not independently confirmed against a DailyMed label, though third-party sourcing indicates an FDA filing exists for a related "GUM Canker-X Oral Gel" product.

## Pros
- No benzocaine, no alcohol — avoids the methemoglobinemia risk documented on [[topical-anesthetics]] for the category's dominant competitors, and avoids the stinging/drying effect alcohol-based gels can cause on an already-irritated sore
- Barrier-film mechanism may provide longer-lasting physical protection against further mechanical irritation than a gel that dissolves/rinses away
- Contains aloe vera, commonly associated with soothing/anti-inflammatory topical use (a general property, not independently trial-verified for this specific product)

## Cons
- No clinical trial evidence specific to GUM Canker-X itself was identified in this pass; its "clinically tested" marketing claim was not independently verified against a located published trial
- Regulatory status (drug vs. device, and the specific DailyMed label) was not independently confirmed in this pass — a genuine sourcing gap, flagged rather than guessed at
- A barrier-film/PVP mechanism provides no true local anesthesia the way benzocaine does — pain relief is likely more indirect (via physical protection) than a direct numbing effect, a plausible but unconfirmed tradeoff

## Use Cases
Marketed for temporary protection and pain relief of canker sores and mouth ulcers via a bioadhesive barrier film.
