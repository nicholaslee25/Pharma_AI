## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed: Tylenol Extra Strength's FDA Drug Facts label lists **acetaminophen 500 mg** as the sole active ingredient, purpose "pain reliever/fever reducer" — matching [[acetaminophen]]'s existing "Tylenol → acetaminophen" worked example. Directions: adults/children 12+, 2 caplets every 6 hours while symptoms last, max 6 caplets (3000 mg)/24h unless directed by a doctor.

**Significant, currently unresolved regulatory/legal development (2025–2026), directly relevant to this brand specifically:** on 2025-09-22 the FDA announced it had initiated a label-change process for acetaminophen regarding pregnancy use, based on large observational studies (Nurses' Health Study II, Boston Birth Cohort, and the Alemany et al. cohort already documented on [[acetaminophen]]) associating prenatal acetaminophen exposure with later autism/ADHD diagnoses — while explicitly stating in its own announcement that "a causal relationship has not been established" and noting "contrary studies in the scientific literature." Separately, a federal MDL (No. 3043, S.D.N.Y.) consolidating Tylenol-specific autism/ADHD injury lawsuits, dismissed in August 2024, was **revived 2026-07-13** when the U.S. Second Circuit vacated that dismissal and reinstated ~550 cases. Tylenol's manufacturer, Kenvue, has publicly urged the FDA to reject the label change, and medical societies including ACOG maintain the evidence doesn't support causation and that acetaminophen remains the only OTC antipyretic/analgesic considered appropriate for pregnancy (NSAIDs/aspirin carry well-documented fetal risks instead — see [[ibuprofen]], [[aspirin]]). This is flagged here as a live, contested matter, not a settled fact in either direction — full detail on [[acetaminophen]].

## Pros
- Confirmed FDA Drug Facts label with a well-established analgesic/antipyretic active ingredient, the largest single-brand share of Headache Products (25%) in this database
- Acetaminophen's non-NSAID mechanism avoids the GI-bleed/cardiovascular/antiplatelet risks documented for ibuprofen/naproxen/aspirin, making it the preferred OTC option for many patients on blood thinners or with GI ulcer history, per [[acetaminophen]]'s own evidence summary
- Remains the medical-society-recommended OTC option for pregnancy despite the ongoing FDA label-change controversy, since the NSAID/aspirin alternatives carry their own well-documented fetal risks

## Cons
- **Historical manufacturing-quality issue (resolved):** Tylenol's manufacturer, McNeil (Johnson & Johnson), was subject to a 2010 multistate consumer-fraud settlement (~$33M to ~40 states + DC) tied to manufacturing quality-control lapses at McNeil plants that triggered recalls across several McNeil brands (Tylenol, Motrin, Benadryl, Sudafed) — see `drugs/Motrin.md` for detail. This is a resolved historical event, not an indicator of current product risk.
- Carries a well-documented, real hepatotoxicity risk distinct from any NSAID's risk profile — ~500 US deaths/year and ~50,000 ED admissions/year linked to acetaminophen per [[acetaminophen]]'s sourced StatPearls data; combination-product overlap (taking multiple acetaminophen-containing products simultaneously) is a leading real-world cause of unintentional overdose
- **The pregnancy/autism-ADHD association is a live, unresolved regulatory and legal question** — an FDA label-change process is underway, contested by the manufacturer and major medical societies, with parallel federal litigation freshly revived on appeal; this should not be presented to a family member as either a proven risk or a fully debunked non-issue
- Cochrane review evidence (Li et al. 2013, documented on [[acetaminophen]]) found acetaminophen's benefit for common-cold-specific symptoms (as opposed to general pain/fever) insufficiently supported — relevant since acetaminophen-based cold combination products market broader symptom relief than this specific review could confirm

## Use Cases
General analgesia and fever reduction — headache, minor arthritis pain, muscular aches, menstrual cramps, common cold-associated aches (though not confirmed to relieve cold-specific symptoms like congestion per Cochrane evidence).

## Allergies / Warnings
Per label: severe liver damage may occur with >4000 mg/24h, use with other acetaminophen-containing products, or ≥3 alcoholic drinks/day; severe skin reactions possible (stop use if rash/blistering occurs); ask a doctor before use if pregnant or breastfeeding; keep out of reach of children; contact Poison Control immediately in case of overdose. See Overview above for the additional, currently-unresolved pregnancy-specific regulatory question.

## Symptoms Treated
Headache, migraine-associated pain (adjunctive), menstrual cramps, general pain, and fever.
