## Overview
Confirmed: Claritin's own DailyMed OTC label lists **loratadine 10mg** (tablet/RediTab; 5mg chewable/syrup formulations also exist) as active ingredient. This replaces the earlier hedged "widely known as loratadine... not independently confirmed" pointer. See [[second-generation-antihistamines]] for ingredient-level evidence. Its 3% share of the Cough/Cold/Flu Combinations Daytime category likely reflects Claritin being recommended alongside/instead of combination products for daytime allergy-type symptoms rather than an actual combination SKU — the source data doesn't specify a distinct multi-ingredient Claritin product in that category, so this is not treated as a confirmed combination formulation. Label saved at `raw/primary-sources/brands/claritin/dailymed-claritin.md`.

## Pros
- Labeled non-drowsy at OTC dosing — loratadine crosses the blood-brain barrier minimally, similar rationale to cetirizine but generally rated even less sedating in comparative reviews
- Once-daily dosing, 24-hour labeled duration
- Available in tablet, RediTab (dissolving), chewable, and syrup forms — flexible for different ages/swallowing ability

## Cons
- A published pair of RCTs (Kaptchuk et al., PNAS/PMC3740832, "Advertisements impact the physiological efficacy of a branded drug") found that direct-to-consumer brand advertising exposure measurably changed subjects' objective physiological response to Claritin compared to the same molecule presented as unbranded/generic — i.e., some of Claritin's real-world effect may be a genuine brand-perception/placebo-amplification effect layered on top of the pharmacology, not purely the drug itself. This is a legitimate brand-specific finding worth flagging rather than a criticism of loratadine's efficacy.
- Per the 2026 network meta-analysis on the ingredient page, loratadine was not among the individual agents rated highest for nasal-symptom efficacy specifically (cetirizine, ebastine, bilastine, rupatadine ranked higher there) — though overall class differences were called mostly small/trivial
- Liver/kidney disease patients should consult a doctor before use (dose adjustment may be needed)

## Use Cases
Seasonal/perennial allergic rhinitis symptoms: sneezing, runny nose, itchy watery eyes, itchy throat/nose.

## Allergies / Warnings
Ask a doctor before use with liver or kidney disease. Contact Poison Control for overdose. No known Claritin-specific hypersensitivity warning beyond standard loratadine allergy.

## Symptoms Treated
Sneezing, runny nose, itchy/watery eyes, itchy nose or throat (indoor/outdoor allergies).
