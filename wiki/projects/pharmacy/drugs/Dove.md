## Overview
Dove (Unilever) is confirmed via DailyMed as a case where **only a specific sub-line, not the flagship brand, is actually antibacterial**:

- **Antibacterial Soaps (7%):** the "Care & Protect" line (Antibacterial Beauty Bar, Moisture Renew Antibacterial Body Wash) is confirmed **benzalkonium chloride 0.13%**, per [[benzalkonium-chloride]] — purpose "antibacterial," use "for washing to decrease bacteria on the skin."
- **Therapeutic Skin Care, Cleansers (3%):** the standard Dove Beauty Bar and Sensitive Skin Bar — the products most consumers associate with the "Dove" name — carry **no antibacterial active ingredient and no Drug Facts panel at all**. These are cosmetic cleansing bars, not FDA-monograph OTC drugs.

This is a real, consumer-relevant split: a shopper reaching for "Dove" soap gets a plain, non-medicated cosmetic bar unless they specifically select the "Care & Protect" sub-line, which is a small fraction of the overall Dove product range.

**Third Dove product family (separate brand page):** [[Dove DermaCare Scalp]] is a third, unrelated Dove formulation — pyrithione zinc 1.0% dandruff shampoo, tracked in this database's Dandruff Shampoo category (6% share) — chemically identical to [[Head & Shoulders]]. "Dove" as a name thus spans three pharmacologically distinct product families (antibacterial body wash, cosmetic body wash, dandruff shampoo) across this database.

## Pros
- The antibacterial-specific "Care & Protect" line is clearly sub-branded, giving a consumer who wants an actual antibacterial soap a real, identifiable option within the family
- Confirmed 0.13% benzalkonium chloride concentration matches other brands in this category (Softsoap, Cetaphil)
- The plain (non-antibacterial) Dove Beauty Bar's long-standing "1/4 moisturizing cream" cosmetic positioning is consistent with its actual formulation — it does not falsely claim antibacterial action

## Cons
- **Benzalkonium chloride carries no final FDA GRASE determination** for antiseptic-wash use, per [[benzalkonium-chloride]] — the one Dove product family that IS medicated shares this database-wide unresolved-regulatory-status finding
- A consumer unfamiliar with the "Care & Protect" sub-branding could easily assume any bar labeled "Dove" is the antibacterial one, given the brand's category placement here — the opposite is closer to true (most Dove SKUs are plain cosmetic bars)
- No Dove-brand-specific clinical trial was located in this pass for either the antibacterial or cosmetic line
- The cosmetic Dove Beauty Bar's cross-category appearance (Therapeutic Skin Care, Cleansers — 3%) alongside the medicated Care & Protect line (Antibacterial Soaps — 7%) under one brand page makes it easy to blur the two into a single "Dove" identity when they are formulation-wise unrelated

## Use Cases
Care & Protect line: daily hand/body washing intended to reduce surface bacteria. Standard Dove Beauty Bar/Sensitive Skin Bar: general cosmetic cleansing and moisturizing, no medicinal claim.

## Allergies / Warnings
Care & Protect (benzalkonium chloride): for external use only; do not use in or near the eyes (rinse thoroughly with water if contact occurs); stop use and ask a doctor if irritation or redness develops; keep out of reach of children except under adult supervision; contact Poison Control if swallowed. Standard Dove Beauty Bar/Sensitive Skin Bar: no FDA Drug Facts warnings (no active ingredient); general cosmetic-allergen/fragrance-sensitivity caution applies.

## Symptoms Treated
Care & Protect line: not a treatment for an existing condition — preventive hygiene, reduces bacterial load during washing. Standard Dove bars: none — cosmetic cleansing/moisturizing only.
