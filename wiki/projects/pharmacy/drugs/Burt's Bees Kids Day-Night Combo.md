## Overview
**Non-drug category-placement discrepancy, consistent with the pattern already flagged for Zarbee's Naturals:** Burt's Bees Kids Cough Syrup is a **dietary supplement, not an FDA-regulated OTC drug** — Daytime formula: Manuka honey + slippery elm bark extract + acerola cherry, in a tapioca syrup base. Nighttime formula: Manuka honey + **valerian root extract** + slippery elm bark extract + **lemon balm leaf extract**. No conventional drug active ingredient in either SKU; both carry the standard supplement disclaimer. Contains honey, so [[honey-pediatric-cough]]'s under-12-months botulism contraindication applies (label states not for use under 12 months). The Nighttime formula's use of **valerian root and lemon balm — herbal sedatives** — as the sleep-support mechanism (rather than a diphenhydramine-type antihistamine as competitors like NyQuil Kids use) is a genuinely distinct approach not otherwise seen in this Children's Cough batch, and neither herb has a dedicated ingredient page in this database.

## Pros
- No dextromethorphan, antihistamine, or decongestant — avoids those ingredient classes' specific side-effect profiles
- Manuka honey base draws on the same evidence class as [[honey-pediatric-cough]]'s Cochrane-reviewed benefit, though Manuka specifically (vs. generic honey) was not separately evaluated in that review
- Herbal-sedative nighttime approach (valerian/lemon balm) avoids diphenhydramine's anticholinergic/CNS-depressant profile documented on [[oral-diphenhydramine-sleep-aid]]

## Cons
- **Not an FDA-regulated drug** — filed under this database's drug-focused Children's Cough category despite making no FDA-reviewed efficacy claim, the same real category-placement issue already documented for Zarbee's Naturals
- **No dedicated ingredient page exists in this database for valerian root or lemon balm** — a genuine evidence gap; neither herb's pediatric safety/efficacy data for sleep-support use in a cough-syrup context was independently verified in this pass. Valerian in particular has known but incompletely characterized sedative/CNS effects in adults; pediatric-specific dosing and safety data for a proprietary low-dose extract blend like this one is not established with the same rigor as an FDA-monograph antihistamine
- **Under-12-months honey contraindication applies** — same real, physiologically-grounded restriction as every other honey-based product in this batch
- No brand-specific clinical trial located for either formulation as a combination product
- Slippery elm bark — a demulcent with a long folk-medicine history — was not independently evidence-reviewed in this pass; its inclusion is not backed by a Cochrane-level or RCT source located here

## Use Cases
Daytime: soothing relief of cough/throat irritation via a demulcent (honey/slippery elm) mechanism, without sedation. Nighttime: same demulcent effect plus herbal sedative support (valerian/lemon balm) intended to aid sleep during a cold, ages 12 months+.

## Allergies / Warnings
Not for infants under 12 months (honey-botulism risk). Not FDA-evaluated for safety or efficacy as a drug. General allergy caution for honey, valerian, or lemon balm sensitivity — not separately quantified in sources reviewed.

## Symptoms Treated
Cough, throat irritation (daytime); cough, throat irritation, plus sleep support via herbal sedatives (nighttime) — unproven as an FDA-reviewed drug claim.
