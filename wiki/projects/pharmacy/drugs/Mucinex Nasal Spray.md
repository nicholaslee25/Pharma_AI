## Appears In
Decongestants, Nasal Spray - 4%

## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed: Mucinex Sinus-Max nasal spray line = **oxymetazoline HCl 0.05%**, the same active ingredient as Afrin, Dristan, and Vicks Sinex — this is a genuinely different active ingredient from plain oral Mucinex (guaifenesin), despite sharing the Mucinex brand name; a consumer should not assume any cross-effect or shared mechanism between "Mucinex" oral products and this nasal spray line. This is a new confirmation touching [[oxymetazoline]] directly. Label saved at `raw/primary-sources/brands/mucinex-nasal-spray/dailymed-mucinex-nasal-spray.md`.

## Pros
- Also labeled to promote nasal/sinus drainage and relieve sinus congestion/pressure specifically, slightly broader labeled use than a plain decongestant claim
- Same well-established oxymetazoline pharmacology (10-12h duration) as the category leader

## Cons
- Full oxymetazoline rebound-congestion risk profile applies identically — see [[oxymetazoline]] for the 3-day limit and rhinitis medicamentosa mechanism
- Brand-name overlap with oral Mucinex (guaifenesin) products creates real potential for consumer confusion about what active ingredient they're actually using — worth flagging explicitly since the two "Mucinex" products share no active ingredient at all

## Use Cases
Short-term (≤3 days) relief of nasal/sinus congestion, promotion of nasal/sinus drainage.

## Allergies / Warnings
Do not exceed 3 consecutive days of use. Ask a doctor before use with heart disease, high blood pressure, diabetes, thyroid disease, or enlarged prostate.

## Symptoms Treated
Nasal congestion, sinus congestion/pressure, sinus drainage.

## Brand-Specific Alignment Check
Full alignment with [[oxymetazoline]] — identical active ingredient/concentration to the ingredient page's representative product. The main point worth surfacing is the brand-name/ingredient mismatch with oral Mucinex products, which share nothing pharmacologically.
