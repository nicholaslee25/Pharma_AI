## Pros
- Once-daily dosing (10mg), effective ≥24h per label and PK data (half-life 8.3h, onset 20-60min)
- Minimal sedation relative to first-generation antihistamines (e.g. Benadryl) — crosses blood-brain barrier minimally
- Not a CYP450 substrate, so lower drug-interaction potential than many drugs (though it is a P-glycoprotein substrate)
- Also FDA-approved for chronic/acute urticaria and allergic conjunctivitis, beyond just hay fever symptoms

## Cons
- Still the most sedating of the second-generation antihistamines in head-to-head comparisons (somnolence is dose-related and the most common adverse effect)
- Not recommended by the OTC label while breastfeeding (a stricter position than StatPearls' more recent PK-based guidance — see ingredient page for that discrepancy)
- Dose must be reduced to 5mg/day in hepatic or significant renal impairment
- Rare but serious postmarketing effects: severe hypotension, anaphylaxis, hemolytic anemia

## Use Cases
Seasonal/perennial allergic rhinitis (runny nose, sneezing, itchy/watery eyes, nasal/throat itching); chronic idiopathic urticaria; allergic conjunctivitis.

## Allergies / Warnings
Contraindicated with known hypersensitivity to cetirizine or hydroxyzine (cross-reactivity). Ask a doctor first if ≥65y, <6y, or with liver/kidney disease. Avoid alcohol and other CNS depressants (additive sedation).

## Symptoms Treated
Runny nose, sneezing, itchy/watery eyes, itchy nose or throat, hives/skin itching.
