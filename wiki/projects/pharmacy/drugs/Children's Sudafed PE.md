## Appears In

## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed: the "Sudafed PE" children's line spans at least two distinct products — **"Children's Sudafed PE Nasal Decongestant"** (phenylephrine HCl 2.5mg/5mL, single-ingredient) and **"Children's Sudafed PE Cold Plus Cough"** (dextromethorphan HBr + phenylephrine HCl, a genuine combination). Since this brand is filed under **Children's Cough/Cold Combinations** specifically (a category name implying a multi-ingredient product), the Cold Plus Cough SKU is the more plausible match — but as with Children's Delsym and Children's Robitussin above, this database's category-mapping data does not specify which SKU underlies the 4% share, so this is flagged rather than silently assumed. Both SKUs' phenylephrine component inherits [[oral-phenylephrine]]'s FDA-flagged efficacy problem — including the newly-documented (this pass) November 2024 FDA proposed order to remove oral phenylephrine from the OTC monograph entirely on GRASE grounds, still pending as of this research date.

## Pros
- If the Cold Plus Cough SKU applies: pairs a decongestant with dextromethorphan, giving broader symptom coverage than the single-ingredient Nasal Decongestant SKU
- Manufactured under a long-established brand family, distinguishing itself from plain pseudoephedrine-based Sudafed by an accessible (non-behind-the-counter) purchase pathway, per [[oral-phenylephrine]]'s existing note that pseudoephedrine's methamphetamine-precursor restrictions likely affect real-world OTC purchasing patterns independent of efficacy

## Cons
- **Inherits oral phenylephrine's core efficacy problem directly**: the Hatton et al. 2007 meta-analysis and subsequent FDA reviews found the standard 10mg adult OTC dose produced no significant objective decongestant effect vs. placebo, leading to the FDA's 2023 unanimous advisory-committee "not GRASE" finding and, now, a formal November 2024 proposed order to remove oral phenylephrine from the OTC monograph — a pediatric dose (2.5mg/5mL) is proportionally lower still, so this efficacy concern applies at least as strongly here as to the adult product
- If the Cold Plus Cough combination SKU applies: the **2008 FDA/CHPA pediatric cough-cold labeling history** (detailed on `drugs/Children's Dimetapp Cold and Allergy.md`) applies directly — combination products are subject to the industry's "not under 4" voluntary labeling floor
- Same brand-name/SKU ambiguity already flagged for Children's Delsym and Children's Robitussin: this database cannot confirm with certainty which Sudafed PE children's SKU the 4% share reflects

## Use Cases
Nasal/sinus congestion relief (Nasal Decongestant SKU); congestion plus cough relief (Cold Plus Cough SKU) associated with the common cold or allergies.

## Allergies / Warnings
Per label: ask a doctor before use if the child has heart disease, high blood pressure, thyroid disease, diabetes, or trouble urinating due to an enlarged prostate; do not use with an MAOI; do not use with any other decongestant- or cough-suppressant-containing product.

## Symptoms Treated
Nasal/sinus congestion; cough (Cold Plus Cough SKU only).
