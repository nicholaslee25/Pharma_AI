# Mentholatum Children's Nighttime Vaporizing Rub

## Appears In
Children's Topical Cough Suppressants, Ointments - 12%

## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed: **camphor 5.3%, eucalyptus oil 1.3%, menthol 2.8%** ointment — the same three-active combination as category leader Vicks VapoRub, full detail on [[camphor-menthol-eucalyptus]]. **Notable finding:** this product's camphor concentration (5.3%) is HIGHER than Vicks VapoRub's (4.8%), despite being the brand specifically marketed as the "Children's"/nighttime formula — a real, counterintuitive potency difference given camphor's documented pediatric toxicity risk (see [[camphor-menthol-eucalyptus]]'s Key Safety Data). Both products carry the identical "children under 2: ask a doctor" age restriction regardless of this concentration difference.

## Pros
- Confirmed FDA Drug Facts label with the same active-ingredient class as category leader Vicks VapoRub, backed by the same general mechanism evidence on [[camphor-menthol-eucalyptus]]
- Genuine FDA-regulated drug, unlike three of the other four brands in this same category (Zarbee's Naturals Soothing Baby Chest Rub, Maty's All Natural Baby Chest Rub, Frida Baby No-Mess Vapor Rub — see [[camphor-menthol-eucalyptus]]'s Formulation Caveat)
- Also labeled for minor muscle/joint ache relief, a secondary use case beyond cough

## Cons
- **Higher camphor concentration (5.3%) than Vicks VapoRub (4.8%)** despite the "Children's"/nighttime branding — a caregiver assuming a children's-branded product is formulated more conservatively than the general-audience market leader would be incorrect; both carry the same under-2 age restriction and the same real camphor-toxicity risk profile documented on [[camphor-menthol-eucalyptus]]
- No dedicated brand-specific pediatric RCT located for this specific formulation — this product relies on the general ingredient-class evidence (Paul et al. 2010 tested Vicks VapoRub specifically, not this brand)
- Same camphor/menthol/eucalyptus toxicity and application-site warnings apply as for Vicks VapoRub (see [[camphor-menthol-eucalyptus]])

## Use Cases
Topical relief of cough due to minor throat/bronchial irritation associated with the common cold, particularly framed around nighttime/sleep disruption; secondary use for minor muscle/joint aches.

## Allergies / Warnings
Children under 2 years: ask a doctor. External use only; do not heat/microwave or add to hot water; do not take by mouth or place in nostrils; do not apply to wounds or damaged skin. Keep out of reach of children; contact Poison Control if swallowed.

## Symptoms Treated
Cough, chest congestion sensation, nighttime cough-related sleep disruption, minor muscle/joint aches (via topical/sensory action, not a true expectorant or decongestant mechanism — see [[camphor-menthol-eucalyptus]]).

## Brand-Specific Alignment Check
**New finding, added to both this page and [[camphor-menthol-eucalyptus]]:** this "Children's" nighttime product actually carries a higher camphor concentration than the general-market-leading Vicks VapoRub — a real, quantified, counterintuitive potency difference that a caregiver choosing between the two based on "children's" branding alone would not expect, flagged explicitly rather than left unexamined.
