## Overview
**Major real finding, confirmed across all six category appearances (this pass, 2026-08-27, completes the 2026-08-26 partial pass): "Neutrogena" is a house brand spanning at least six chemically unrelated mechanisms, several with internal multi-mechanism splits of their own.**

- **Sun Burn Relief (16%):** camphor 0.45% (Sun Rescue After Sun Medicated Relief Gel), an external analgesic — see the 2026-08-26 finding preserved below, unchanged this pass.
- **Acne Products (7%):** confirmed via DailyMed to span **three separate acne mechanisms** under the Neutrogena name — benzoyl peroxide (e.g., Rapid Clear Stubborn Acne Spot, On-The-Spot Vanishing Formula), salicylic acid (Oil-Free Acne Wash line), and **sulfur 10%** (Stubborn Acne Spot Drying Lotion) — the same multi-mechanism-under-one-brand pattern increasingly common across this database's acne-brand research (see [[Clearasil]], [[AcneFree]] elsewhere in this pass).
- **Lip Balms (4%):** Neutrogena's lip-balm SKUs are sunscreen products, not moisturizer-only balms — Revitalizing Lip Balm SPF 20 uses **octinoxate + oxybenzone**, and MoistureShine Lip Soother SPF 20 is a separate SPF lip product. No non-SPF "plain" Neutrogena lip balm was located in this pass, meaning the brand's Lip Balm category entry is functionally a sunscreen product wearing a lip-balm name.
- **Sunscreen (27%, Neutrogena's largest single share in this database):** spans BOTH chemical filters (avobenzone/homosalate/octisalate/octocrylene, +/- oxybenzone or bemotrizinol depending on SKU, e.g. Ultra Sheer, Invisible Daily Defense) AND mineral filters (zinc oxide alone, e.g. Sheer Zinc Mineral Sunscreen) — a real active-ingredient-class split (organic chemical UV absorbers vs. inorganic mineral blockers) that changes photostability, application feel, and environmental-marketing considerations, all under one "Neutrogena Sunscreen" umbrella.
- **Therapeutic Skin Care, Cleansers (8%) and Moisturizers (4%):** this pair spans a **cosmetic/drug split** — some SKUs (e.g., Hydro Boost Water Gel/Gel Crème without SPF) carry no Drug Facts panel at all (cosmetic, no FDA-recognized active ingredient), while others in the same product families add sunscreen actives (Hydro Boost with SPF 50: avobenzone/homosalate/octisalate/octocrylene) or acne-adjacent actives (sulfur-based cleansers). A consumer cannot assume any given "Neutrogena" cleanser or moisturizer contains an active drug ingredient at all without checking the specific SKU's label.

**Net finding:** across its six category appearances, Neutrogena's product lines use camphor, benzoyl peroxide, salicylic acid, sulfur, octinoxate/oxybenzone, avobenzone/homosalate/octisalate/octocrylene/bemotrizinol, zinc oxide, and (for several SKUs) no active ingredient at all — at least eight distinct pharmacological categories under one house-brand name. This is the single most mechanism-diverse brand identified in this database's Topicals research to date, exceeding even [[CeraVe]] and [[Aveeno]] in the number of distinct actives spanned.

## Pros
- Confirmed FDA Drug Facts labels located for the great majority of drug-containing SKUs across all six categories; ingredient concentrations match this database's existing ingredient pages ([[benzoyl-peroxide]], [[salicylic-acid]]) where checked
- Genuine breadth of choice within each category — e.g., a consumer can pick mineral or chemical sunscreen, or BPO/salicylic-acid/sulfur acne treatment, all under one trusted retail name
- Sun Burn Relief line offers a real medicated-vs-moisturizing-only choice (see 2026-08-26 finding below)

## Cons
- **The breadth itself is the risk:** with at least eight distinct active-ingredient classes spanning six unrelated product categories, brand-name recognition alone tells a consumer almost nothing about what a specific "Neutrogena" product contains — more extreme than the same pattern already flagged on [[CeraVe]] and [[Aveeno]]
- "Neutrogena" Lip Balm SKUs are SPF sunscreen products (octinoxate/oxybenzone) rather than plain moisturizing balms — a consumer wanting simple lip moisturizing (as most competitors in the Lip Balms category, e.g. [[Carmex]]/[[Blistex]], offer as an option) may not realize they're applying a chemical sunscreen to their lips
- No dedicated ingredient page exists in this database for sulfur as an acne active, despite its confirmed use in Neutrogena's Stubborn Acne Spot Drying Lotion (10% sulfur) — a gap, not fabricated
- Sunscreen line's chemical-filter formulations (avobenzone/oxybenzone-containing) carry the same photostability and systemic-absorption open questions as any oxybenzone/avobenzone sunscreen; brand-specific data beyond the general chemical-filter evidence was not independently located
- No Neutrogena-brand-specific clinical trial beyond general ingredient-level evidence was located for any of the six category appearances in this pass

## Use Cases
Sunburn pain/itch relief (Medicated Relief Gel, camphor); acne treatment via consumer's choice of BPO, salicylic acid, or sulfur mechanism; sun protection via consumer's choice of chemical or mineral filter; lip sun protection (SPF lip products); general facial cleansing/moisturizing (cosmetic-only SKUs) or, in specific SKUs, combined with an added drug active (SPF or acne treatment).

## Allergies / Warnings
Varies by SKU and active ingredient — see [[benzoyl-peroxide]] and [[salicylic-acid]] for acne-line warnings (bleaching of fabric/hair for BPO; do not use with other topical acne products without doctor's advice for either). Sunscreen-line SKUs: same-active warnings apply across brands using avobenzone/oxybenzone/homosalate/octisalate/octocrylene or zinc oxide — external use only, avoid eyes, discontinue if rash occurs. Sun Burn Relief Medicated Relief Gel: flammable, external use only (see 2026-08-26 finding below). No overarching brand-wide warning applies across all six categories given the mechanism diversity documented above.

## Symptoms Treated
Sunburn pain/itching (Sun Burn Relief); acne (Acne Products, via three possible mechanisms); sun protection (Sunscreen, Lip Balms); general dry/sensitive skin (Therapeutic Skin Care lines, cosmetic SKUs) or acne/sun protection layered onto those same product families in specific SKUs.

## Brand-Specific Alignment Check
**Confirmed, database-wide-relevant finding:** Neutrogena is not a single-mechanism brand in any of its six category appearances — three of the six categories (Acne, Sunscreen, Therapeutic Skin Care) individually contain internal multi-mechanism splits (BPO/salicylic acid/sulfur; chemical/mineral filters; cosmetic/drug SKUs), on top of the six categories already being mechanistically unrelated to each other. Flagged here; no single ingredient page can represent "Neutrogena" as a brand-name link, consistent with this database's practice of leaving genuinely non-single-ingredient brands unlinked from any one ingredient page's "brand" cross-references.

## Overview (Sun Burn Relief appearance — 2026-08-26 finding, unchanged)
Confirmed via DailyMed: **Neutrogena Sun Rescue After Sun Medicated Relief Gel** uses **camphor 0.45%** (external analgesic) as its active ingredient — a lower concentration than [[BiteMD]]'s 3% camphor. Camphor has no dedicated ingredient evidence page in this database yet (same gap flagged on BiteMD); a broader counterirritant page is anticipated when this database's research reaches Thermal Relief Products. **A separate, non-drug SKU exists under nearly identical branding:** "Neutrogena Sun Rescue After Sun Replenishing Lotion" contains no active drug ingredient at all (per third-party ingredient listings — water, mineral oil, glycerin, shea butter, aloe vera, mint extract; no camphor or other Drug-Facts-listed analgesic) — it is a moisturizer, not a treatment. A consumer must check for the word "Medicated" to know which "Sun Rescue After Sun" product actually contains pain-relief medication.
