## Overview
Nexcare (3M) is the #2 brand in Bandages, Covers, and Gauze (19%) and also carries a Liquid Bandages share (16%, a mechanically distinct product — see [[liquid-bandages]] for that category's separate cyanoacrylate-based chemistry, not covered by this adhesive-strip page). No FDA drug label exists for the adhesive-strip line — regulated as a **Class I device** under 21 CFR 880.5240 (FDA product code KGX), 510(k)-exempt. Full evidence and regulatory detail is on [[bandages-covers-and-gauze]]. In the 2024 Mamavation/EHN independent PFAS lab study (40 bandage products, 18 brands), at least one 3M product tested came back non-detect for organic fluorine (<10 ppm), placing 3M/Nexcare among the brands with a clean result for the specific SKU(s) tested — no 3M/Nexcare SKU was identified among the higher-PFAS results reported in that study's coverage.

## Pros
- #2 recommendation share in Bandages, Covers, and Gauze (19%) plus a separate, substantial share in the mechanically distinct Liquid Bandages category (16%)
- The specific 3M SKU(s) tested in the 2024 independent PFAS lab study came back non-detect, a favorable result relative to the highest-PFAS results found elsewhere in that same study

## Cons
- No brand-specific clinical or comparative-performance trial exists (expected for this Class I device category)
- The PFAS testing referenced above covered only specific tested SKUs, not 3M/Nexcare's full product lineup — a clean result for one product should not be generalized to every Nexcare SKU
- "Nexcare" spans two mechanically unrelated first-aid product types under one brand name (adhesive-strip bandages vs. liquid/brush-on skin sealant) — a consumer should not assume identical material composition or use-case across the two

## Use Cases
Covering and protecting minor cuts, scrapes, and abrasions (adhesive-strip line); see [[liquid-bandages]] separately for the liquid-bandage product line's distinct use case (flexible, waterproof liquid seal, often for small/awkward-to-bandage areas).
