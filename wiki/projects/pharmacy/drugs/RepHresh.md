## Overview
RepHresh Vaginal Gel is confirmed via manufacturer ingredient disclosure to be a polycarbophil/carbomer-based bioadhesive gel (purified water, glycerin, polycarbophil, carbomer homopolymer type B, among other listed ingredients) — **not lactic-acid-based**, despite "pH balancing" marketing language that could suggest an acid-based mechanism to some consumers. This is the same underlying polycarbophil bioadhesive mechanism confirmed for [[Replens]] elsewhere in this database (see [[vaginal-lubricants-moisturizers]]) — RepHresh and Replens are functionally similar products, not meaningfully differentiated mechanisms, despite being tracked as separate brands with separate market shares in both of this database's categories. RepHresh's own label explicitly states the product **"is not intended to prevent or treat vaginal infections."**

## Pros
- Shares the real, FDA-cleared-device-class bioadhesive mechanism already documented for Replens — the polycarbophil/carbomer gel adheres to the vaginal wall and releases moisture over an extended period, a genuine, functioning mechanism for its stated pH-balancing/moisturizing purpose
- Explicit label disclaimer ("not intended to prevent or treat vaginal infections") is a real point of label transparency, correctly scoping the product's actual claims rather than overstating them

## Cons
- **Real tension between marketing and label disclaimer:** RepHresh is marketed as "odor eliminating" and for balancing vaginal pH — claims a consumer could reasonably associate with addressing an infection-related cause of odor — while the label simultaneously states it is not intended to treat or prevent vaginal infections; both statements are literally true (the gel can reduce odor via pH/moisture effects without treating an underlying infection), but the juxtaposition is a real source of potential consumer misunderstanding
- Functionally near-identical to [[Replens]] (both polycarbophil/carbomer-based) despite being tracked and marketed as distinct competing brands — no meaningful mechanism differentiation was located in this pass
- No RepHresh-specific RCT distinct from the general polycarbophil-gel mechanism literature was located in this pass

## Use Cases
Vaginal pH balancing and odor reduction (per label) — NOT a treatment or prevention for vaginal infections, per the product's own explicit disclaimer.

## Allergies / Warnings
No brand-specific recall or lawsuit located in this pass. Standard vaginal-product caution: discontinue and consult a doctor if irritation occurs; not a substitute for medical evaluation of unusual discharge, odor, or irritation that could indicate an actual infection requiring treatment.

## Symptoms Treated
