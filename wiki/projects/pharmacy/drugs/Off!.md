## Overview
Same brand as [[Off]] (already researched under Insect Bite and Sting Management, 2% share, where its category placement was flagged as a mismatch). **This "Insect Repellants" category is OFF!'s correct regulatory home** — OFF! is not an FDA-regulated drug but an **EPA-regulated pesticide/repellent under FIFRA**; no DailyMed/FDA Drug Facts label exists for any OFF! product. Confirmed via existing research: OFF! spans multiple active-ingredient classes depending on product line — **DEET** (5-99% depending on SKU, e.g. Deep Woods/FamilyCare lines), **picaridin** (DEET-free, e.g. Clean Feel/Defense lines), **PMD** (plant-derived, ~10%, "Botanicals" line), and various **essential oils** (rosemary, citronella, geranium, cedarwood, peppermint, lemongrass, cornmint, clove — "Kids" line). See [[insect-repellents-deet-picaridin]] (new ingredient page built this pass) for the comparative DEET/picaridin efficacy and safety evidence, including a genuinely contested finding: real documented DEET pediatric seizure/encephalopathy case reports exist in the peer-reviewed literature, while EPA's own 1998/2014 re-registration reviews could not confirm DEET caused them and concluded labeled use "does not present a health concern."

Category-leading 58% share reflects OFF!'s long-standing market dominance and broad SKU range across all major EPA-registered active-ingredient classes, giving consumers a single trusted brand to choose from regardless of active-ingredient preference (DEET, picaridin, or plant-derived).

## Pros
- EPA has reviewed DEET (the most common OFF! active ingredient) twice (1998, 2014) and concluded DEET-containing repellents "do not present a health concern" when used as directed, with no age restriction for children
- Offers genuine choice across active-ingredient classes (DEET, picaridin, PMD, essential oils) for users with different sensitivities or preferences, all under one recognizable brand
- Per [[insect-repellents-deet-picaridin]]'s comparative evidence review (140+ studies): DEET and picaridin perform comparably at equivalent concentrations, so OFF!'s DEET-free picaridin lines are not a meaningfully weaker choice for users avoiding DEET specifically
- Category-leading market share and long product history provide broad real-world usage data

## Cons
- **Real, documented DEET pediatric neurotoxicity case reports exist** (17 reports of toxic encephalopathy in children, seizures in 72% of cases, one at a concentration as low as 17.6%) — per [[insect-repellents-deet-picaridin]], this is a genuine safety signal in the peer-reviewed/CDC literature that EPA's own review could not rule DEET in or out of causing, and should not be dismissed simply because EPA's overall conclusion favors safety when used as directed
- Spans at least four different active-ingredient classes under one brand name, each with its own EPA-reviewed safety/efficacy profile and protection-duration claims not independently verified against primary EPA registration data for each specific SKU in this pass (manufacturer-stated durations only)
- No brand-specific clinical trial was sought, since this is a pesticide-efficacy question (bite prevention rates) rather than a drug-efficacy question — outside the scope of this database's FDA-label-centric methodology
- A consumer selecting "OFF!" without checking the specific SKU could end up with a much higher or lower DEET concentration than intended (range spans 5-99%)

## Use Cases
Prevention of insect bites/stings (mosquitoes, ticks, and other biting insects) by repelling insects from skin/clothing before contact occurs — via DEET, picaridin, PMD, or essential-oil mechanisms depending on the specific SKU chosen.

## Allergies / Warnings
Varies by active ingredient/SKU; DEET-specific EPA guidance: do not apply over cuts, wounds, or irritated skin; avoid application near children's eyes, mouth, and hands; use only enough to cover exposed skin/clothing; wash treated skin with soap and water after returning indoors; for sprays, apply to hands first then rub onto face rather than spraying directly on the face. See [[insect-repellents-deet-picaridin]] for the fuller safety picture, including the documented pediatric seizure case-report history.

## Symptoms Treated
None — this is a preventive repellent, not a symptom-treatment product. It does not relieve pain, itching, or any symptom of an existing bite or sting.
