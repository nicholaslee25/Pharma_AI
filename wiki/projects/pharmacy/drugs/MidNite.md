## Appears In
Melatonin Sleep Aids - 3%

## Overview
MidNite is confirmed to use a **notably low melatonin dose (1.5mg)** combined with a botanical blend of chamomile, lavender, and lemon balm (no valerian, despite some competitor/generic-category confusion on this point) — positioned specifically as a "low dose" sleep aid. This is a real, meaningful contrast to [[melatonin]]'s own dose-response meta-analysis finding that effect **peaks at 4mg/day**, dosed ~3 hours before bedtime — MidNite's dose is well below that evidence-derived optimum, and its "low dose" marketing framing is at least internally honest about this (rather than claiming maximal efficacy at a low dose). No MidNite-brand-specific RCT for the complete formulation was located in this pass; the chamomile/lavender/lemon balm botanical blend has its own separate, generally modest evidence base in the broader sleep-aid literature not independently evidence-graded in this database. Melatonin Sleep Aids (3% share) is covered by [[melatonin]].

## Pros
- Transparent, disclosed formulation (melatonin dose plus three named botanicals) rather than an undisclosed proprietary blend
- "Low dose" positioning is at least honestly framed rather than claiming maximal efficacy from a sub-optimal dose — appropriate for users specifically seeking a gentler effect or lower nightly melatonin exposure
- No lawsuit or recall history specific to this brand was located in this pass

## Cons
- **Real, quantified dose gap**: 1.5mg is well below the 4mg/day dose [[melatonin]]'s own meta-analysis found to be the effect-maximizing dose — appropriate framing as "low dose," but real evidence that this product is not optimized for maximum sleep-onset/duration benefit per the cited research
- No brand-specific RCT was located testing the complete melatonin + chamomile/lavender/lemon balm combination as a unit
- The individual botanical components (chamomile, lavender, lemon balm) have their own separate, generally more modest sleep-evidence bases not independently evidence-graded in this database

## Use Cases
Sleep-onset support at a lower melatonin dose than the meta-analysis-identified optimum — appropriate for users specifically seeking a gentler/lower-dose option, per [[melatonin]]'s dose-response evidence.

## Allergies / Warnings
See [[melatonin]] for general warnings. Chamomile carries a real ragweed/Asteraceae-family cross-allergy consideration not independently evidence-graded on a dedicated page in this database.

## Symptoms Treated
Sleep-onset difficulty, at a lower melatonin dose than the evidence-optimized amount.
