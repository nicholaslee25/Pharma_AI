## Overview
No FDA drug label exists — Emergen-C is a **dietary supplement**, not an FDA-approved OTC drug, so there is no DailyMed Drug Facts panel to pull. Manufacturer Supplement Facts labels (pulled from emergenc.com product PDFs) confirm the core formula: **1,000mg vitamin C** (as ascorbic acid and zinc ascorbate) per packet, ~1,111% daily value, plus B vitamins and electrolytes depending on SKU; the "Immune+" line adds vitamin D3; gummies use 750mg ascorbic acid per 3-gummy serving. This confirms the earlier hedged pointer to [[vitamin-c]] and adds that some formulations also deliver a meaningful zinc dose (via zinc ascorbate) relevant to [[zinc]] as well — not previously noted in the hedged version. Not saved to raw/ since this is a standard manufacturer ingredient list, not a distinct regulatory document (no FDA label exists to preserve).

## Pros
- Very high vitamin C dose (1,000mg, ~11x adult RDA) delivered in a palatable effervescent/gummy format that may improve compliance vs. plain tablets
- Some formulations add zinc (via zinc ascorbate) and B vitamins/electrolytes, which plain ascorbic acid supplements don't provide
- Widely available, no prescription needed

## Cons
- No brand-specific clinical trial exists for Emergen-C — see [[vitamin-c]] for the actual efficacy evidence (Hemilä & Chalker 2013 Cochrane review), which found regular vitamin C supplementation does not prevent colds in the general population and produces only a modest (~8% adult, ~14% children) reduction in cold duration — a much weaker effect than the "52%/43% recommendation share" figures might imply to a reader unfamiliar with the two-layer rule
- At 1,000mg/packet, a user taking more than 2/day risks exceeding the adult Tolerable Upper Intake Level (2,000mg) from vitamin C alone, before counting other dietary sources
- High sugar content in original-formula packets (a Cons point specific to the product formulation, not the vitamin C itself)

## Use Cases
General immune/nutritional support; adjunct during a cold to modestly shorten its duration (per ingredient-level evidence, not a brand-specific claim); vitamin C supplementation for smokers or others with increased need.

## Allergies / Warnings
No FDA-mandated warnings panel exists (dietary supplement). General vitamin C cautions apply: GI upset at high doses; those with a history of kidney stones (oxalate) should discuss high-dose vitamin C with a doctor per general nutrition guidance (not itself sourced to a specific citation in this pass).

## Symptoms Treated
Not a symptom-treating drug in the FDA sense — marketed for general immune support and modest reduction in common-cold duration/severity per the ingredient-level Cochrane evidence.
