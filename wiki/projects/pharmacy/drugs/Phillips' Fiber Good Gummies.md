## Overview
Confirmed via manufacturer product information: Phillips' Fiber Good Gummies use **inulin** (4g soluble prebiotic fiber per 2-gummy serving) as their fiber source — the same non-significant-for-constipation fiber type (per [[fiber]]'s cited 2022 meta-analysis, which found only psyllium and pectin statistically significant) that this database's research has already flagged for [[Nature Made Fiber Gummies]], Benefiber, Citrucel, FiberCon, Mirafiber, and Vitafusion Fiber Well. This confirms and cross-references [[fiber]]'s own prior note that Phillips'-brand products span "a brand family that also spans genuinely different-mechanism FDA drug products" — Phillips' also markets [[magnesium-hydroxide]]-based Milk of Magnesia and bisacodyl-based laxative tablets under the same "Phillips'" name, meaning the brand name alone does not indicate which of several distinct mechanisms a given "Phillips'" product uses. Fiber Supplements (2%, smallest tracked share in this category) is covered by [[fiber]].

## Pros
- Inulin has its own separate, real prebiotic evidence base (colonic fermentation feeding beneficial bacteria, short-chain fatty acid production) distinct from the bulk-laxative mechanism [[fiber]]'s meta-analysis tested
- Gummy format may improve adherence for users who dislike fiber powders

## Cons
- **Confirmed to use inulin, not psyllium or pectin — the two fiber types shown to have a statistically significant constipation-relief effect in [[fiber]]'s cited meta-analysis** — same non-significant-type finding as the majority of this category's brands
- **Real, brand-name-scope caution**: "Phillips'" spans multiple, mechanistically unrelated product lines (magnesium hydroxide-based Milk of Magnesia, bisacodyl stimulant tablets, and this inulin-based fiber gummy) — a consumer should not assume a "Phillips'" product works the way another same-brand-name product does
- Smallest tracked share (2%) in this category
- No Phillips'-Fiber-Good-Gummies-specific clinical trial was located — efficacy evidence is at the general-ingredient level, which specifically does not favor inulin for constipation relief per the cited meta-analysis

## Use Cases
Prebiotic fiber/gut-microbiome support (inulin's separate evidence base) — not confirmed effective for constipation relief specifically per [[fiber]]'s meta-analysis.

## Allergies / Warnings
Inulin/prebiotic fibers commonly cause gas, bloating, and cramping, especially at higher doses. Do not confuse with other "Phillips'"-branded products (Milk of Magnesia, stimulant laxative tablets) which use entirely different active ingredients and mechanisms — see [[magnesium-hydroxide]] and [[bisacodyl]].

## Symptoms Treated
Marketed for digestive/fiber-intake support; not confirmed effective for constipation relief specifically per [[fiber]]'s meta-analysis (significant only for psyllium and pectin).
