## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed: Anbesol Maximum Strength (gel and liquid) lists **benzocaine 20%** as its sole active ingredient — a single-agent local anesthetic, mechanistically identical to Orajel's core benzocaine SKUs and covered by the same [[topical-anesthetics]] evidence/safety page. Labeled uses span toothache, sore gums, canker sores, braces irritation, minor dental procedures, and dentures — consistent with its three category appearances here. A separate sibling product, **Anbesol Cold Sore Therapy** (allantoin/benzocaine/camphor/petrolatum ointment), is a materially different multi-ingredient formulation filed as its own brand entry in this database (see `drugs/Anbesol Cold Sore Therapy.md`) — the "Anbesol" name is not a single fixed formulation across its full product line, mirroring Orajel's own multi-SKU spread.

## Pros
- Confirmed single-active-ingredient FDA Drug Facts label (benzocaine 20%), simpler than Orajel's multi-ingredient "All Mouth Sores" gel
- Second-largest share in both Canker Sore Treatments (22%) and Toothache Products (16%), reflecting broad familiarity
- Labeled for a wide range of mouth-pain triggers (braces, dentures, minor dental procedures) beyond just canker sores/toothache

## Cons
- Full benzocaine methemoglobinemia risk profile applies per [[topical-anesthetics]] — same disproportionate risk (Guay 2009 breakdown) as every other benzocaine-based competitor in this category
- Contraindicated in children under 2, capped at 7 days of use per label, and contraindicated with "caine"-family anesthetic allergy
- The "Anbesol" brand name spans two materially different formulations (Maximum Strength's single-agent benzocaine vs. Cold Sore Therapy's four-ingredient allantoin/benzocaine/camphor/petrolatum combination) — do not assume one fixed formula from the name alone
- No brand-specific (vs. ingredient-class) clinical trial identified for Anbesol specifically

## Use Cases
Temporary relief of pain from toothaches, sore gums, canker sores, minor dental procedures, braces, and dentures.

## Allergies / Warnings
Per label: methemoglobinemia risk (stop use, seek immediate medical attention for pale/gray/blue skin, headache, rapid heart rate, shortness of breath, dizziness, fatigue); do not use in children under 2; do not use for more than 7 days unless directed by a dentist/doctor; do not use if allergic to procaine, butacaine, benzocaine, or other "caine" anesthetics; avoid contact with eyes.

## Symptoms Treated
Temporary numbing/pain relief for toothaches, canker sores, sore gums, and mouth irritation from dental appliances.

## Brand-Specific Alignment Check
Consistent with [[topical-anesthetics]]'s existing benzocaine framing; the ingredient page previously listed Anbesol as merely "plausibly benzocaine-based by real-world market identity, not individually confirmed" — now upgraded to independently confirmed via its own DailyMed label, and the Cold Sore Therapy sub-line's distinct four-ingredient formulation is newly documented.
