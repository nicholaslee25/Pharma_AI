## Overview
Ginkgo biloba has a real, current, and nuanced evidence base — a 2026 Cochrane review (82 RCTs, 10,613 participants) found ginkgo has **little to no benefit for mild cognitive impairment (MCI) or subjective memory complaints**, but **may offer modest short-term benefit specifically for people with diagnosed dementia** (global status, cognition, activities of daily living at 6 months). This is a real, important distinction: most consumers buying a "Memory Support" ginkgo product are not diagnosed dementia patients but are exactly the MCI/subjective-memory-complaint population the same review found little-to-no-benefit for. No serious safety signal was found across conditions. See [[Nature's Bounty]] for the parent brand's own broader profile (real recalls, slack-fill/mislabeling litigation, 2024 FTC review-manipulation settlement) — none confirmed specific to this ginkgo product. Memory Support (17% share) has no other dedicated ingredient page in this database for ginkgo specifically.

## Pros

- Real, modest, statistically-supported short-term benefit demonstrated specifically in diagnosed dementia patients
- No increased risk of serious harm versus placebo across the conditions studied

## Cons
- **The population most likely to buy an OTC "Memory Support" ginkgo product (people with subjective memory complaints or mild cognitive impairment, not diagnosed dementia) is exactly the population the same Cochrane review found little-to-no benefit for** — a real, significant use-case mismatch between the evidence and the likely real-world buyer
