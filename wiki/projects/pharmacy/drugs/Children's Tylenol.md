## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed: Children's Tylenol is **acetaminophen 160 mg per 5 mL** oral suspension, for ages 2-11, matching [[acetaminophen]]. This is the current, standardized concentration — see [[acetaminophen]]'s newly documented Special Population Flags entry on the real 2011 concentration-standardization history: prior to June 2011, "infant" drops (80mg/0.8mL) and "children's" liquid (160mg/5mL) were sold as two different concentrations, a real, quantified source of caregiver dosing errors (up to 3-fold overdose or ~66% underdose from mix-ups) that led to a voluntary industry-wide standardization to the single 160mg/5mL concentration used today across Children's Tylenol, Genexa, DrKids, and Little Remedies.

## Pros
- Confirmed FDA Drug Facts label at the current standardized concentration, backed by [[acetaminophen]]'s general analgesic/antipyretic evidence base
- By far the dominant brand in Children's Analgesics (57% share) in this database, reflecting broad caregiver/pharmacist familiarity
- Comes with an enclosed dosing cup/syringe specifically calibrated to this product's concentration, a real safety feature directly addressing the concentration-mixup history documented on [[acetaminophen]]

## Cons
- Carries the full pediatric-relevant risk profile documented on [[acetaminophen]]: hepatotoxicity risk if the labeled maximum (5 doses/24h) is exceeded or combined with other acetaminophen-containing products (a genuine risk for children also receiving a combination cold/flu product containing acetaminophen)
- The currently-unresolved FDA pregnancy/autism-ADHD label-change controversy documented on `drugs/Tylenol.md` and [[acetaminophen]] concerns prenatal exposure specifically, not this pediatric product's own direct use in children — should not be conflated with a safety concern about giving this product to an already-born child
- Label explicitly warns to use ONLY the enclosed dosing device, not a household spoon — a real, well-documented source of dosing error when caregivers substitute kitchen spoons
- No independent brand-specific RCT (beyond the general acetaminophen ingredient-class evidence) was identified for this specific pediatric formulation in this pass

## Use Cases
Pain relief and fever reduction in children ages 2-11.

## Allergies / Warnings
Per label: use only the enclosed dosing cup; severe liver damage may occur if a child takes more than 5 doses in 24 hours or combines with other acetaminophen-containing drugs; contact Poison Control immediately (1-800-222-1222) in case of overdose. Dose every 4 hours while symptoms last, max 5 doses/24h.
