## Overview
Zicam is a multi-SKU brand line spanning several genuinely different formulations, confirmed via DailyMed. **Zicam Cold Remedy RapidMelts** (Citrus): zinc acetate anhydrous + zinc gluconate tablet — a real zinc dose, same active-ingredient class as [[zinc]] (Cold-EEZE). **Elderberry Citrus RapidMelts** additionally adds Sambucus nigra (elderberry) — relevant to [[elderberry]] if that combination product needs cross-referencing there too. **Zicam Cold Remedy Nasal Swabs** notably do **NOT** contain zinc at all — they use different homeopathic ingredients, meaning the "Decongestants, Nasal Spray - 3%" line item for Zicam is very likely this non-zinc swab/spray product, not a zinc formulation, which is an important distinction the earlier hedged Overview did not make (it treated all Zicam SKUs as ambiguously zinc-related). This directly confirms and refines the earlier "not verified here" hedge for the zinc lozenge lines specifically, while flagging the nasal product as a separate, non-zinc formulation. ## Pros
- RapidMelts/lozenge zinc formulations: see [[zinc]] for real efficacy evidence — zinc gluconate/acetate lozenges started within 24-48h of symptom onset show a meaningful reduction in cold duration per the Cochrane review cited there
- Multiple delivery formats (RapidMelts, lozenges, soft chews) may improve compliance
- Elderberry Citrus variant combines two ingredients (zinc + elderberry) each independently supported for cold-symptom reduction

## Cons
- **Zicam intranasal zinc products (a different, discontinued/reformulated line historically marketed as "Zicam Nasal Gel/Swabs" with zinc) were linked to permanent loss of smell (anosmia)** — the [[zinc]] ingredient page explicitly flags "intranasal zinc specifically: anosmia... a distinct, formulation-specific risk not shared by lozenges/oral forms." This is a serious, well-documented, brand-relevant history (FDA warning, product reformulation/litigation in the mid-2010s) that a reader should see directly on this page, not only buried in the ingredient page. Current Zicam Nasal Swabs formulations found in this search do NOT list zinc as an active ingredient — consistent with the brand having reformulated away from intranasal zinc after the anosmia findings, though this history/timeline was not independently re-verified against a specific FDA safety communication in this pass.
- Homeopathic-labeled SKUs (2X potency zinc products) carry the standard homeopathic disclaimer: "not evaluated by the FDA for safety or efficacy," even though — per the [[zinc]] page's regulatory note on Cold-EEZE — a "2X" dilution still delivers a pharmacologically real zinc dose (this nuance likely also applies to Zicam's zinc formulations, though Zicam's own specific dilution label text wasn't independently pulled in this pass)
- Avoid citrus fruit/juice around dosing time for zinc lozenges (may reduce effectiveness, per the general zinc-lozenge class warning also seen on Cold-EEZE's label)

## Use Cases
Zinc RapidMelts/lozenges: reducing duration/severity of the common cold if started early. Elderberry Citrus variant: combined zinc + elderberry cold-symptom support. Nasal Swabs: marketed for cold symptom relief via a non-zinc (likely homeopathic botanical) formulation not independently characterized here.

## Allergies / Warnings
Do not use zinc lozenges for influenza or allergies (per label). Ask a doctor/pharmacist before use if taking minocycline, doxycycline, tetracycline, or warfarin (Coumadin) — consistent with the zinc-class interaction warning on [[zinc]]. Discontinue any intranasal zinc product immediately if smell/taste changes occur.

## Symptoms Treated
Cough, sore throat, nasal congestion, post-nasal drip, hoarseness (zinc lozenge lines, per label); general cold symptoms (other lines).
