## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed: Sting-Kill (single-use crack-vial swabs) uses **benzocaine USP 20%** (topical anesthetic) + **menthol USP 1%** (antipruritic) — the same benzocaine + menthol combination pattern seen in [[Dermoplast]]'s Pain Relieving line, though delivered via a single-use disposable-swab format rather than a spray/cream. Per [[topical-anesthetics]], benzocaine carries a disproportionate, well-documented methemoglobinemia risk relative to lidocaine (~66% vs. ~5% of 242 reviewed case-report episodes), which applies to this product like every other benzocaine-based brand in this category. The single-swab, single-application delivery format is a real practical mitigation against the repeated/large-area misuse pattern implicated in methemoglobinemia case reports, though it does not eliminate the underlying benzocaine risk for a given application.

## Pros
- Confirmed FDA Drug Facts label; simple two-ingredient combination (anesthetic + antipruritic)
- Single-use, pre-measured swab format is a practical dosing-control feature — limits the risk of repeated over-application to one body site compared with a multi-use spray/tube
- Explicit label warning to seek immediate medical attention for insect-venom sensitivity signs, a real and appropriately prominent safety instruction for this specific use case (stings, not just bites)

## Cons
- Benzocaine 20% carries the disproportionate methemoglobinemia risk documented on [[topical-anesthetics]] — same class-level concern as Dermoplast, BiteMD, and the "Wipe Insect Sting Relief" SKU of After Bite
- No brand-specific clinical trial for Sting-Kill was identified beyond the general benzocaine/menthol ingredient-level evidence
- Single-swab format, while limiting over-application per use, does not prevent a user from applying multiple swabs in quick succession if pain/itch persists

## Use Cases
Temporary relief of pain and itching from bee stings, insect bites, and minor skin irritations.

## Allergies / Warnings
Per label: external use only; avoid eyes; not for children under 2 without a doctor; seek immediate medical attention if experiencing signs of insect-venom sensitivity (serious allergic reaction risk); discontinue and consult a doctor if condition worsens or persists beyond 1 week; if swallowed, contact Poison Control immediately. See [[topical-anesthetics]] for the quantified benzocaine methemoglobinemia risk data.
