## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed: "Dr. Teal's" (manufactured by Advanced Beauty Systems, Inc. / PDC Brands) spans **two genuinely different active-ingredient mechanisms** relevant to this category — a real "one brand, multiple mechanisms" case:

- **Epsom Salt Soaking Solution — magnesium sulfate USP**, an actual FDA OTC drug with a dual-labeled purpose: external "relieves pain from minor sprains and bruises" (soak) AND, on the identical product, an internal saline-laxative use ("relieves occasional constipation") — the same active ingredient, same package, serving two completely different indications depending on how it's used. The foot-soak use case relevant to this category is the external soaking-solution indication only.
- **Pre and Post Workout Pain Relief Cream — menthol 10%**, a standard topical-analgesic counterirritant (see [[topical-counterirritants]]), labeler PDC Brands, consistent with the concentration used by several other menthol-based brands in this database (Biofreeze Cream, Biofreeze Foot Cream).

**Real, contested evidence finding on the Epsom-salt mechanism itself:** the primary evidence often cited for Epsom-salt-bath efficacy is Waring 2004 (University of Birmingham) — 19 subjects, daily 12-minute magnesium sulfate baths for 7 days, reporting increased plasma/urinary magnesium and sulfate. However, this study was **small, had no control group, and was never peer-reviewed** — and it is hosted/promoted by the Epsom Salt Council, a real conflict-of-interest concern. A separate, peer-reviewed 2017 review (published in Nutrients) **concluded that current evidence does not support transdermal magnesium absorption claims** generally. This is a genuinely unresolved, contested area of the science, not a settled question — flagged plainly here rather than asserting the soak "works" or "doesn't work" for pain relief via magnesium absorption specifically.

No Dr.-Teal's-brand-specific clinical trial was located for either SKU beyond the general (and contested) magnesium-sulfate-absorption literature above and the standard [[topical-counterirritants]] menthol evidence base.

## Pros
- Magnesium sulfate soaking solution is a genuine FDA-monograph OTC drug (not merely a cosmetic bath product) with a real, if narrow, labeled external indication (minor sprains/bruises)
- Menthol 10% Pain Relief Cream has a confirmed FDA Drug Facts label and a well-characterized counterirritant mechanism (see [[topical-counterirritants]])
- Warm-water soaking itself (independent of any magnesium absorption claim) has plausible general comfort/circulation benefits for tired feet, even setting aside the contested transdermal-magnesium science

## Cons
- **The core "magnesium is absorbed through the skin and relieves pain" claim underlying the Epsom-salt soak's popular appeal rests on contested science** — the most commonly cited supporting study (Waring 2004) was small, uncontrolled, and never peer-reviewed, while a 2017 peer-reviewed review found current evidence does not support the transdermal-absorption claim generally. This gap between popular belief and the actual evidence base is flagged explicitly rather than glossed over
- No Dr.-Teal's-brand-specific clinical trial exists for either SKU
- The same magnesium-sulfate active ingredient carries a dual external-pain-relief/internal-laxative labeled use on a single product — a real risk of consumer confusion about appropriate use/dosing if the internal-use directions were followed for what a consumer intended only as a foot soak, or vice versa
- Per [[diabetic-foot-care]] and the product's own label: hot/warm soaks are contraindicated for individuals with diabetes except on physician advice — a genuinely relevant caution given this category's overlap with diabetic foot-care needs, since reduced sensation from neuropathy could mask a thermal burn during a hot soak
- Menthol cream concentration (10%) exceeds the FDA's 2012 burn-risk 3% threshold per [[topical-counterirritants]], consistent with several other menthol-based brands in this database

## Use Cases
Epsom Salt Soaking Solution: temporary relief of minor sprains and bruises via a warm-water foot/body soak (external use); separately, occasional constipation relief if used as an oral laxative per label (not the foot-care use case). Pre and Post Workout Pain Relief Cream: temporary relief of minor muscle/joint aches via menthol's cooling counterirritant mechanism.

## Allergies / Warnings
Epsom Salt Soaking Solution: keep away from children, contact Poison Control if ingested outside labeled laxative directions; avoid if kidney disease, on prescription medications, or magnesium-restricted diet; stop use and consult a doctor if redness/irritation/swelling occurs (external) or rectal bleeding occurs (internal); pregnant/nursing individuals should consult a physician; hot/warm soaks not recommended for diabetics without physician guidance. Pain Relief Cream: external use only; avoid wounds/damaged skin, eyes, mucous membranes; do not use with a heating pad or bandage tightly; discontinue if condition worsens or irritation occurs; consult a doctor if pregnant/nursing; not for children under 12 with arthritis-like conditions without doctor approval.

## Symptoms Treated
Minor sprains, bruises, and tired/sore feet via warm-water magnesium sulfate soak (external, mechanism contested per above); minor muscle/joint aches via menthol counterirritant cream.

This is preserved as an open evidence gap rather than either validated or dismissed. No formulation-vs-label discrepancy was found for either SKU.
