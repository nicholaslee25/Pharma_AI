## Appears In
Topical Analgesics - 3%

## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed (AleveX Lotion, manufacturer Bayer — same parent company as oral Aleve): **camphor 5.5% + menthol 16%**, purpose "pain relieving lotion," for temporary relief of minor muscle/joint aches from arthritis, backache, strains, sprains, and bruises. **This is a pure counterirritant formula — it contains NO naproxen sodium, the NSAID active ingredient that made "Aleve" a household name**, and no methyl salicylate either. The manufacturer's own FAQ page explicitly confirms this ("AleveX does not contain naproxen sodium"), a genuinely notable brand-name association risk: a consumer familiar with oral Aleve's NSAID mechanism could reasonably but incorrectly assume "AleveX" delivers a related or complementary anti-inflammatory effect, when the actual mechanism (menthol/camphor sensory counterirritant) is completely different and does not address inflammation at all. This is a milder version of the Differin Resurfacing Scar Gel-style brand-name/active-ingredient mismatch already documented in this database — milder because the manufacturer proactively discloses it, but still a real point of consumer confusion given how strongly "Aleve" itself connotes NSAID pain relief.

**Real, quantified burn-risk finding:** at **menthol 16%**, AleveX carries a substantially higher menthol concentration than any other brand yet confirmed in this database's Thermal Relief Products/Topical Analgesics research — more than 5x the FDA's 3% menthol burn-risk threshold documented on [[topical-counterirritants]] (per the FDA's September 2012 Drug Safety Communication reviewing 43 burn cases, most severe burns involved menthol >3% or methyl salicylate >10%). AleveX's own label directly reflects this risk, explicitly warning to stop use if "pain/swelling/blistering" or "severe burning" develops — language notably more specific/alarming than most other counterirritant labels reviewed in this database. The label also flags the product as flammable, an additional handling caution not seen on most other topical analgesics reviewed.

## Pros
- Confirmed, single, consistent active-ingredient formula (camphor 5.5%/menthol 16%) across the lotion, spray, and roll-on formats
- No methyl salicylate, meaning AleveX avoids the NSAID-class systemic salicylate risk (aspirin-allergy cross-reactivity, pregnancy warning) that applies to Icy Hot, BENGAY, Tiger Balm, and Salonpas
- Manufacturer transparently discloses the no-naproxen distinction in its own consumer FAQ, rather than leaving the Aleve-brand association ambiguous

## Cons
- **Highest menthol concentration (16%) confirmed in this database's counterirritant research to date — over 5x the FDA's own 3% burn-risk threshold** — and the label's explicit "severe burning"/"blistering" stop-use warning reflects a real, non-hypothetical risk
- **Brand-name association risk:** "AleveX" trades on the Aleve name's NSAID reputation while containing no naproxen or any NSAID — a consumer could reasonably misunderstand what mechanism they're purchasing
- Flammable — an additional handling/storage caution (keep away from open flame) not found on most competing counterirritant labels reviewed in this database
- No AleveX-brand-specific clinical trial (as distinct from general menthol/camphor counterirritant evidence) was located in this pass

## Use Cases
Temporary relief of minor muscle and joint aches associated with arthritis, backache, strains, sprains, and bruises via a cooling/warming sensory counterirritant effect — not an anti-inflammatory or NSAID-based treatment despite the brand name.

## Allergies / Warnings
Per label: for external use only; do not use if allergic to any ingredient; avoid eyes and mucous membranes; do not use with tight bandages or heat sources (heating pads); avoid wounds/irritated skin; stop use and consult a doctor if condition worsens, irritation/pain/swelling/blistering/severe burning develops, or symptoms persist beyond 7 days; consult a doctor if pregnant/breastfeeding; flammable — keep away from open flame; not for children under 12 without a doctor's direction.

## Symptoms Treated
Minor muscle and joint aches from arthritis, backache, strains, sprains, and bruises (sensory/cooling-warming symptom relief, not anti-inflammatory).

## Brand-Specific Alignment Check
**Real brand-name/mechanism discrepancy flagged:** "AleveX" shares its name with oral Aleve (naproxen sodium, an NSAID) but contains no naproxen and no NSAID-class ingredient at all — a pure menthol/camphor counterirritant. The manufacturer's own FAQ proactively addresses this, which is a mitigating factor, but the association risk remains real given how strongly the "Aleve" name signals NSAID pain relief to consumers. Separately, this product's 16% menthol concentration is the highest confirmed in this database's counterirritant coverage, directly implicating the FDA's 2012 burn-risk communication threshold documented on [[topical-counterirritants]].
