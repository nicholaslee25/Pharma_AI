## Appears In
High-Potency Vitamin C Supplements - 6%

## Overview
Ester-C is confirmed to be a combination of **calcium ascorbate and vitamin C metabolites (including L-threonate)**, marketed as a neutral-pH (non-acidic) vitamin C form with a specific brand-level bioavailability claim: longer retention (up to 24 hours) in immune cells (leukocytes) versus standard ascorbic acid. This pass found a genuinely mixed, honestly-reported evidence picture rather than a clean confirmation: a small published independent study (8 women, 1 man) found **no difference** between Ester-C and standard ascorbic acid tablets in absorption/urinary excretion; separately, the manufacturer claims an unpublished (non-peer-reviewed) human study showing increased bioavailability; other studies found Ester-C associated with higher leukocyte vitamin C concentrations specifically, while some found more favorable plasma concentrations with alternative forms. One study did find an association between calcium ascorbate EC and fewer colds/shorter severe-symptom duration versus placebo, and calcium ascorbate specifically showed better GI tolerability (fewer epigastric adverse events) than standard ascorbic acid in comparative sourcing. High-Potency Vitamin C Supplements (6%) is covered by [[vitamin-c]].

## Pros
- Confirmed better GI tolerability (fewer epigastric/stomach adverse events) than standard ascorbic acid — a real, useful practical advantage for vitamin-C-sensitive users, since standard ascorbic acid's acidity can cause GI upset at high doses
- Neutral pH formulation is a genuine chemical property, not merely a marketing claim
- At least one independent study found a real association with fewer colds and shorter severe-symptom duration versus placebo, and some (not all) studies found higher leukocyte vitamin C retention specifically with this form
- The added calcium (as calcium ascorbate) provides incidental calcium intake alongside the vitamin C

## Cons
- **The manufacturer's own key bioavailability claim rests on an unpublished, non-peer-reviewed human study** — a real, confirmed evidence-transparency gap; the independently published comparative literature this pass found is genuinely mixed, not a clean confirmation of superior bioavailability
- A small independent published study found **no difference** in absorption/urinary excretion between Ester-C and standard ascorbic acid — directly contradicting the brand's core marketing differentiator in at least one controlled comparison
- Findings on leukocyte vs. plasma vitamin C concentrations are mixed across different studies (some favor Ester-C on leukocyte measures, more studies found standard/alternative forms favorable on plasma measures) — not a one-sided result
- No Ester-C-brand-specific large-scale RCT was located; see [[vitamin-c]] for the general ingredient evidence (modest ~8% cold-duration reduction in adults)

## Use Cases
Vitamin C supplementation for users seeking better GI tolerability than standard ascorbic acid — see [[vitamin-c]] for the general evidence base.

## Allergies / Warnings
Contains calcium (as calcium ascorbate) — relevant for users tracking total calcium intake across supplements (see [[calcium]] for the Tolerable Upper Intake Level and drug-interaction timing). See [[vitamin-c]] for general vitamin-C warnings.

## Symptoms Treated
Marketed for immune/cold support with a specific enhanced-bioavailability/retention claim — see [[vitamin-c]] for the general evidence-supported use; the bioavailability-superiority claim itself is evidentially mixed (see Cons).

## Brand-Specific Alignment Check
**Real, honestly-mixed discrepancy — flag explicitly:** Ester-C's headline bioavailability/retention claim is not uniformly supported across the independent literature found in this pass — one small independent study found no absorption difference from standard ascorbic acid, while the manufacturer's own stronger bioavailability claim rests on an unpublished study. The confirmed GI-tolerability advantage and the leukocyte-retention findings in some studies are real positives that should not be conflated with a fully proven overall bioavailability superiority.
