## Overview
Already fully confirmed on [[oral-phenylephrine]] — that page's dosing table and sourcing are built directly from Sudafed PE's own FDA OTC label: **phenylephrine HCl 10mg every 4 hours, max 60mg/24h**. This upgrades the earlier bare "name-confirmed" note to a full confirmation. The Sudafed PE line also spans numerous combination SKUs (Sudafed PE Head Congestion Plus Flu Severe, Plus Pain with ibuprofen or acetaminophen) beyond the plain phenylephrine-only tablet tracked in this database's category entry.

## Pros
- No behind-the-counter purchase restriction, unlike its pseudoephedrine-based sibling product Sudafed — freely available on open shelves
- Multiple combination SKUs exist for users wanting added pain relief or multi-symptom cold/flu coverage under the same trusted brand name

## Cons
- **This is the ingredient page's own flagged negative-evidence example**: per [[oral-phenylephrine]], the Hatton et al. 2007 meta-analysis found the standard 10mg oral dose produced **no significant objective decongestant effect** vs. placebo, and this evidence base directly led to the FDA's 2023 Nonprescription Drug Advisory Committee unanimously concluding oral phenylephrine is **not GRASE** (Generally Recognized As Safe and Effective) as a decongestant at any tested OTC dose
- Despite this, the product remains on shelves as of this research pass — an advisory committee vote is a recommendation, not an automatic market withdrawal, so consumers may not be aware of the efficacy controversy when purchasing
- Only 4% recommendation share vs. Sudafed's 45% in the same category — directionally consistent with pharmacists' apparent awareness of the weaker evidence, though purchase-restriction convenience for pseudoephedrine-avoidant buyers cuts the other way

## Use Cases
Labeled for temporary relief of sinus congestion/pressure and nasal congestion — though see Cons above regarding the evidence base for this claim at the oral 10mg dose.

## Allergies / Warnings
Do not use with MAOIs. Ask a doctor before use with heart disease, high blood pressure, thyroid disease, diabetes, or enlarged prostate. Stop use if nervousness, dizziness, or sleeplessness occur.

## Symptoms Treated
Labeled for sinus/nasal congestion, though the evidence for actual decongestant effect at this dose is now formally contested (see Overview).
