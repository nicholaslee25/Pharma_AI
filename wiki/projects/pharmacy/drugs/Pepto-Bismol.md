## Pros
- Confirmed FDA label matches [[bismuth-subsalicylate]]'s existing sourcing exactly — no discrepancy found
- Genuinely broad, multi-symptom evidence base: relieves diarrhea, nausea, and upset stomach via the same active ingredient, unlike single-symptom competitors
- Real off-label H. pylori quadruple-therapy role with up to 90% reported success rate per [[bismuth-subsalicylate]]

## Cons
- **Same salicylate as aspirin** — carries the same Reye syndrome risk in children/teens with viral illness, per [[bismuth-subsalicylate]]'s Contraindications; a caregiver may not immediately recognize "Pepto-Bismol" as a salicylate-class product the way they would recognize aspirin by name
- Real bismuth-toxicity risk with extended overconsumption (rare but serious neurotoxicity) and salicylate-toxicity risk with excessive/repeated dosing (>150mg/kg threshold) per [[bismuth-subsalicylate]]
- Harmless-but-alarming temporary black-tongue/black-stool discoloration — worth proactively counseling on since it can otherwise look like a medical emergency
- No Pepto-Bismol-brand-specific RCT beyond the general bismuth-subsalicylate evidence base was independently located in this pass
- CDC recommends AGAINST use for traveler's diarrhea prevention/treatment in pregnancy specifically, per [[bismuth-subsalicylate]]

## Use Cases
Relief of diarrhea, nausea, and upset stomach (gas, belching, fullness, overindulgence) — via the multi-pathway mechanism (antisecretory, anti-inflammatory, mild antimicrobial) detailed in [[bismuth-subsalicylate]].

## Allergies / Warnings
Per [[bismuth-subsalicylate]]: avoid with anticoagulants, other salicylate products, or in children/teens with flu-like/chickenpox symptoms (Reye syndrome risk); salicylate/aspirin allergy is a contraindication; use caution with concurrent doxycycline (reduced absorption). Pregnant/breastfeeding: CDC/manufacturer guidance leans toward alternative treatments.

## Symptoms Treated
Diarrhea, nausea, upset stomach (gas, belching, fullness), traveler's diarrhea — via the mechanism described in [[bismuth-subsalicylate]].
