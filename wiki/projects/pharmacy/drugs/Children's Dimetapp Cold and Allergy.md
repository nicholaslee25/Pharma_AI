## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed: Children's Dimetapp Cold and Allergy is a **combination product — brompheniramine maleate (first-generation antihistamine) + phenylephrine HCl (nasal decongestant)** — labeled for ages 6 and up, relieving stuffy/runny nose, itchy/watery eyes, and sneezing. This is the only multi-ingredient combination product in the Children's Allergy category researched in this pass (all other brands — Claritin, Zyrtec, Allegra, Xyzal, Benadryl — are single-ingredient antihistamines).

**Real, important pediatric safety history directly relevant to this product category:** following an FDA nationwide Public Health Advisory in January 2008 (recommending against use in children under 2, citing reports of convulsions, rapid heart rate, and death associated with OTC pediatric cough/cold products) and a 2007 FDA advisory-committee review that surfaced many reports of harm and death, the **Consumer Healthcare Products Association (CHPA) formally adopted a voluntary industry-wide labeling change in November 2008**: OTC oral pediatric cough/cold medicine labels were modified to state "do not use" in children under 4 years of age. This history is the direct regulatory backdrop for why Children's Dimetapp Cold and Allergy (and combination cold/allergy products generally) carry an "ages 6 and up" label rather than a lower age cutoff — combination products with multiple active ingredients are treated more conservatively for young children than single-ingredient antihistamines.

## Pros
- Addresses both allergy symptoms (via brompheniramine) and nasal congestion (via phenylephrine) in one product, useful for a child with mixed cold/allergy symptoms rather than needing two separate medications
- The "ages 6 and up" labeling reflects real, documented post-2008 regulatory caution specifically calibrated to combination pediatric cough/cold products, not an arbitrary cutoff

## Cons
- **Combines a first-generation antihistamine's sedation/anticholinergic risk (per the mechanism already documented for diphenhydramine on [[oral-diphenhydramine-sleep-aid]] and `drugs/Children's Benadryl.md` — brompheniramine is a related first-generation agent) with a decongestant's own stimulant/cardiovascular-adjacent risk profile** — a more complex combined risk than any single-ingredient competitor in this category
- **Real, documented history of serious adverse events (convulsions, rapid heart rate, death) associated with this general product category** (OTC pediatric cough/cold combination medicines) drove the 2008 FDA advisory and industry-wide relabeling — this is not a hypothetical caution, it reflects actual reported harm that led to a regulatory response
- No independent brand-specific RCT (as opposed to the general ingredient-class safety history) was identified for this specific combination in this pass
- Smallest share (6%) of the researched Children's Allergy brands in this database, consistent with more conservative pharmacist recommendation for a combination product versus single-ingredient alternatives

## Use Cases
Combined relief of stuffy/runny nose, itchy/watery eyes, and sneezing due to allergies or the common cold, in children ages 6+.

## Allergies / Warnings
Per label and the 2008 regulatory history above: not for children under 6 (per current label; the broader industry standard from 2008 sets a "not under 4" floor for this product category generally); do not exceed labeled dose; consult a doctor before combining with any other antihistamine or decongestant-containing product, since the same child could be given a duplicate cold-symptom active ingredient inadvertently across products.

## Symptoms Treated
Stuffy/runny nose, itchy/watery eyes, sneezing.
