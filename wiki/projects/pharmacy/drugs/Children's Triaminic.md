## Overview
**Significant discrepancy found:** multiple secondary sources (Drugs.com) state **"the Triaminic brand name and all generics (if any) have been discontinued in the United States,"** including the Triaminic Thin Strips Cough & Runny Nose line specifically. This directly conflicts with this database's Pharmacy Times-sourced category files, which list Triaminic as an actively pharmacist-recommended brand at 4% share (Children's Cough/Cold Combinations) and 2% (Children's Cough, as "Children's Triaminic"). This vault could not independently confirm current retail availability, and Drugs.com discontinuation notices sometimes reflect a specific formulation or manufacturer-SKU discontinuation rather than a full brand exit — but if accurate, this would mean this database (and/or the underlying Pharmacy Times 2026 OTC Guide survey) is citing pharmacist recommendation share for a brand no longer sold, which would itself be a notable data-currency problem worth surfacing rather than silently resolving. Confirmed historical formulation (per Day Time Cold & Cough labeling, while the brand was active): dextromethorphan 3.67mg (≈5mg DXM HBr equivalent) + phenylephrine HCl 2.5mg.

## Pros
- If still available: combines dextromethorphan and phenylephrine, giving cough-plus-congestion coverage in one product
- Historically one of the most recognized pediatric cough/cold brand names in the US market, which may explain a lingering pharmacist-recommendation-share entry even if current retail availability has changed

## Cons
- **Real historical safety issue, resolved by ingredient removal, not by continued monitoring:** older Triaminic formulations contained **phenylpropanolamine (PPA)**, a decongestant later found to be associated with an increased risk of hemorrhagic stroke in women, per an FDA-driven withdrawal of PPA from all OTC products in the early 2000s. Modern Triaminic (Day Time Cold & Cough) formulations replaced PPA with phenylephrine — the safety issue is historical and resolved via reformulation, not an active current risk, but it's a genuine documented episode this database had not previously captured for this brand.
- **Separate 2013 recall:** Triaminic Syrups (along with Theraflu Warming Relief Syrups, same manufacturer family) were recalled due to child-resistant caps failing to function properly, risking unintentional pediatric ingestion/poisoning — a real, CPSC-documented packaging-defect recall, distinct from any ingredient-safety issue.
- **Current-availability discrepancy** (see Overview) — if the brand is genuinely discontinued, this database's recommendation-share data for it may be stale; flagged explicitly rather than treated as settled either way
- If the phenylephrine-containing formulation is what's being recommended: inherits [[oral-phenylephrine]]'s FDA-flagged efficacy problem and the pending November 2024 proposed removal order
- **Standing 2008 FDA/CHPA pediatric cough-cold labeling history applies** to this combination-product category generally

## Use Cases
Historically: relief of cough and nasal/sinus congestion due to the common cold, in the Day Time Cold & Cough formulation.

## Allergies / Warnings
Per the last-confirmed label: not for children under the labeled age threshold; do not combine with other decongestant- or cough-suppressant-containing products; the historical PPA-containing formulation is no longer marketed and should not be confused with current (if any) phenylephrine-based products.

## Symptoms Treated
Cough, nasal/sinus congestion (historical formulation).
