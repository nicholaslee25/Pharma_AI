## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed: **ammonium lactate 12-15%** (varies by specific line — e.g., "Daily Nourish Lotion" markets 12% lactic acid equivalent), a genuine FDA-regulated alpha-hydroxy-acid keratolytic/humectant drug. See new ingredient page [[ammonium-lactate]] (built this pass — no dedicated page previously existed despite this being one of the only confirmed genuine OTC drugs, as opposed to cosmetics, found across this database's Therapeutic Skin Care, Cleansers/Moisturizers research). This stands in real contrast to several higher-share competitors in this same subcategory found to be cosmetics with no FDA drug status (see [[Lubriderm]], [[BIODERMA]], [[Albolene]]).

## Pros
- **Confirmed genuine FDA-regulated drug** (ammonium lactate 12-15%), a real distinction from several cosmetic-only competitors tracked in the same Pharmacy Times category
- Real RCT evidence: significantly outperforms plain 5% lactic acid lotion for xerosis (Rogers et al. 1989); genuine dual keratolytic + humectant mechanism suited to scaling/rough-skin conditions beyond simple moisturization
- A reported case series shows benefit combining ammonium lactate with ceramide-based barrier-repair creams for ichthyosis — suggesting a complementary, not merely competing, role alongside products like [[CeraVe]]

## Cons
- **Head-to-head RCT evidence shows 40% urea cream outperforms 12% ammonium lactate lotion** on speed of improvement and several severity measures (roughness, fissuring, thickness, dryness) at day 14 in moderate-to-severe xerosis — a real comparative efficacy disadvantage against a specific competing keratolytic (though urea-based competitors were not identified as major brands in this specific Pharmacy Times subcategory)
- AHA mechanism carries a real risk of stinging/burning on application, particularly on already-irritated or fissured skin, plus general AHA-class photosensitivity
- Smallest tied share in the Moisturizers subcategory pass so far (3%) despite the strongest confirmed regulatory/evidentiary footing of any brand examined in this pass

## Use Cases
Treatment of dry, scaly, rough skin (xerosis) and ichthyosis vulgaris via ammonium lactate's keratolytic/humectant mechanism.

## Allergies / Warnings
Per general ammonium lactate/AHA-class conventions (see [[ammonium-lactate]]): external use only; avoid eyes/mucous membranes; may sting on broken/irritated skin; increased sun sensitivity possible with regular use.

## Symptoms Treated
Dry, scaly, rough, or fissured skin; ichthyosis vulgaris scaling.
