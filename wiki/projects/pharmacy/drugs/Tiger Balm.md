## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed: Tiger Balm spans **three genuinely different formulation patterns** across its SKU lineup — (1) **triple-ingredient** (methyl salicylate 15% + menthol 5% + camphor 3%): Active Muscle Rub, Muscle Rub, Pain Relieving Muscle Spray; (2) **methyl salicylate + menthol, no camphor:** Active Muscle Gel; (3) **camphor + menthol only, NO methyl salicylate:** Neck and Shoulder Rub, Ultra Strength. This third group is meaningfully different from the first two: without methyl salicylate, it does NOT carry the NSAID-class systemic risk profile (stomach bleeding risk factors, aspirin/salicylate allergy contraindication, pregnancy warning) documented on [[topical-counterirritants]] — a real, brand-internal safety distinction a consumer should know about. The Active Muscle Rub's methyl salicylate (15%) and menthol (5%) concentrations both exceed the FDA's 2012 burn-risk thresholds (>10% methyl salicylate, >3% menthol — see [[topical-counterirritants]]), though Tiger Balm was not one of the specific brands named in that communication's press coverage.

## Pros
- Confirmed FDA Drug Facts labels for multiple SKUs, with the aspirin/salicylate allergy alert explicitly present on the methyl-salicylate-containing formula
- Offers a genuine choice between methyl-salicylate-containing (NSAID-class risk) and methyl-salicylate-free (camphor+menthol only) formulations under the same brand — a real option for salicylate-sensitive users who still want a Tiger Balm product
- Long-established international brand (Haw Par, Singapore) with broad recognition

## Cons
- Active Muscle Rub's concentrations (15% methyl salicylate, 5% menthol) both exceed the FDA's identified burn-risk thresholds, per [[topical-counterirritants]]
- The methyl-salicylate-containing SKUs carry the full NSAID-class risk profile (stomach bleeding, salicylate allergy, pregnancy warning) — the label's own "consult doctor" language for pregnancy/breastfeeding and sensitive skin is a real, specific caution
- Three distinct formulation patterns under one brand name means a consumer cannot assume "Tiger Balm" implies a fixed ingredient set — must check the specific product (Active Muscle Rub vs. Neck and Shoulder Rub vs. Active Muscle Gel, etc.)
- No brand-specific clinical trial for Tiger Balm specifically was identified beyond the general counterirritant-class evidence, which itself concerns a different product's formulation (Salonpas's 10%/3%, not Tiger Balm's 15%/5%/3%)

## Use Cases
Temporary relief of minor aches and pains of muscles and joints — simple backache, arthritis, bruises, strains, sprains — via counterirritant mechanisms that vary by specific SKU (with or without methyl salicylate).

## Allergies / Warnings
Per Active Muscle Rub label: allergy alert for aspirin/salicylate sensitivity (methyl-salicylate SKUs only); ask a healthcare professional before use if pregnant, breastfeeding, or if you have sensitive skin; do not apply to wounds/irritated skin; avoid eyes/mucous membranes; do not bandage or use heating pads; stop use if condition worsens, severe skin irritation develops, pain persists beyond 7 days, or recurs; adults/children over 12 only. The camphor+menthol-only SKUs (Neck and Shoulder Rub, Ultra Strength) do not carry the salicylate-specific warnings.

## Symptoms Treated
Minor musculoskeletal aches and pains via counterirritant sensory masking; mechanism varies by SKU (methyl salicylate + menthol + camphor vs. camphor + menthol only).
