## Appears In
Children's Motion Sickness Products - 71%

## Overview
Per [[dimenhydrinate]]'s existing, detailed DailyMed-confirmed research: Dramamine For Kids = **dimenhydrinate 25mg chewable**, ages 2+ (do not give under 2 without doctor approval), dosed 1/2-1 tablet q6-8h (2-6y) or 1-2 tablets q6-8h (6-12y). This is the largest single-brand share (71%) in this entire Pediatrics batch so far. **Standing evidence gap already documented on [[dimenhydrinate]]**: the strongest pediatric RCT located (Uhlig 2009) studied gastroenteritis-related vomiting, NOT motion sickness specifically — no dedicated pediatric motion-sickness RCT with hard prevention/cure-rate numbers was located for this or any other product in this category.

## Pros
- Confirmed FDA-registered OTC label with explicit pediatric age-banded dosing, unlike several homeopathic/device competitors in the same category (Nauzene Kids, Sea-Band) that either lack an FDA-reviewed dose or aren't a drug at all
- Clear mechanism (H1/muscarinic receptor blockade at the vestibular nucleus and area postrema) with StatPearls-documented plausibility for motion-sickness-type nausea specifically, even though the hard efficacy numbers available (Uhlig 2009) come from a different vomiting etiology
- By far the largest recommendation share in this category (71%, vs. 18% for the non-drug Sea-Band device and 11% for the homeopathic Nauzene Kids) — the most-recommended pediatric motion-sickness option pharmacists surveyed chose

## Cons
- **No dedicated pediatric motion-sickness RCT with hard numbers exists for this or any ingredient in this category** — the Uhlig 2009 trial [[dimenhydrinate]] cites studied gastroenteritis-related vomiting, and its own authors judged the overall clinical benefit LOW despite reduced vomiting frequency, since it didn't improve rehydration success or overall outcome
- **Paradoxical excitation** is a specifically documented pediatric risk for dimenhydrinate per [[dimenhydrinate]] — the opposite of the expected sedation, an important counseling point parents may not anticipate
- Anticholinergic/sedation side-effect profile common to first-generation antihistamines; caution warranted in children with asthma/respiratory disease under age 2 per StatPearls
- Do not give under age 2 without doctor approval

## Use Cases
Prevention and treatment of motion sickness symptoms (nausea, dizziness, vomiting) in children ages 2+; first dose recommended 30-60 minutes before the anticipated motion-sickness trigger.

## Allergies / Warnings
Per [[dimenhydrinate]]'s label data: do not give under 2 without doctor approval; ask a doctor before use if the child has a breathing problem (asthma, emphysema, chronic bronchitis) or glaucoma; avoid combining with sedatives/tranquilizers/alcohol (synergistic CNS depression); contact Poison Control (1-800-222-1222) for suspected overdose.

## Symptoms Treated
Motion-sickness-associated nausea, dizziness, and vomiting.
