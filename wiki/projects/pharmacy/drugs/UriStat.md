## Overview
**Confirmed 2026-08-28 via DailyMed:** UriStat Ultra is phenazopyridine hydrochloride 99.5mg — an identical active-ingredient dose to AZO Urinary Pain Relief Maximum Strength, resolving this database's prior hedge on [[phenazopyridine]]. UriStat is a direct generic-equivalent to AZO's phenazopyridine product, not a mechanistically distinct alternative. Its label, like Uricalm's, carries the FDA's standard monograph-pending disclaimer: "This drug has not been found by FDA to be safe and effective, and this labeling has not been approved by FDA" — a real, if not brand-specific, regulatory-status note shared by phenazopyridine products generally.

## Pros
- Confirmed identical active ingredient/dose to the category-leading AZO product, giving UriStat the same real symptomatic-relief evidence base described on [[phenazopyridine]]

## Cons
- Shares every safety limitation already documented on [[phenazopyridine]]: symptom relief only, does NOT treat or cure the underlying UTI; G6PD-deficiency hemolysis risk; contraindicated at GFR <50; urine/contact-lens staining
- Carries the FDA's "not been found by FDA to be safe and effective" monograph-status disclaimer on its own label — worth a user's awareness, though this reflects phenazopyridine's general regulatory history rather than a UriStat-specific manufacturing or safety defect
- No UriStat-specific clinical trial exists distinct from the general phenazopyridine literature already covered on [[phenazopyridine]]

## Use Cases
Fast relief from urinary pain, burning, urgency, and frequency associated with urinary tract infections (per label) — symptom relief only.

## Allergies / Warnings
Do not use with liver or kidney problems; consult a doctor if allergic to foods/preservatives/dyes or previously hypersensitive to phenazopyridine. Same [[phenazopyridine]]-class cautions apply in full (G6PD deficiency, renal impairment, pregnancy).
