## Appears In

## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed: Aquaphor Baby Healing Ointment is **petrolatum 41%**, labeled as a skin protectant explicitly indicated to help treat and prevent diaper rash — a genuinely correct category-label match, unlike Calmoseptine's anorectal-labeled product also appearing in this category. Confirms [[zinc-oxide]]'s earlier hedge that Aquaphor is "more likely petrolatum-primary" rather than zinc-oxide-based; this is now independently verified. Aquaphor's petrolatum concentration (41%) is meaningfully higher than the ~53% figure documented for A&D/A+D Original Ointment's petrolatum-plus-lanolin combination on [[petrolatum]] — though the two products differ in that A+D also contains lanolin as a second active, while Aquaphor Baby's only active ingredient is petrolatum itself.

## Pros
- Confirmed FDA Drug Facts label with a correctly-matched, diaper-rash-specific indication (unlike Calmoseptine, also in this category)
- Petrolatum's occlusive barrier mechanism has real supporting RCT evidence for uncomplicated wound care (Smack et al. 1996, on [[petrolatum]]) — though that trial did not test diaper rash specifically
- Single-active-ingredient formulation (unlike A+D's lanolin+petrolatum combination) simplifies the allergen profile — no lanolin-contact-allergy risk, a real point of contrast with A&D/A+D Original Ointment/Calmoseptine, all of which contain lanolin

## Cons
- Per [[zinc-oxide]]'s Alkhamis et al. 2026 systematic review: petroleum jelly/Vaseline specifically showed NO efficacy for reducing diaper-rash severity/incidence in at least one included study — a real negative finding for the petrolatum mechanism generally (though that specific study tested plain petroleum jelly, not necessarily this exact 41% Aquaphor formulation, and the review's overall evidence quality is graded low, Grade C)
- Zinc oxide's barrier-plus-mild-astringent mechanism (used by most of this category's other brands — Desitin, Boudreaux's, Balmex, Triple Paste, Calmoseptine) is mechanistically distinct from Aquaphor's purely occlusive petrolatum approach; comparative efficacy between the two approaches for diaper rash specifically was not independently confirmed in this pass
- No Aquaphor-Baby-specific RCT for diaper rash was independently located in this pass

## Use Cases
Diaper rash prevention/treatment via occlusive moisture-barrier protection; general dry/chapped/irritated infant skin care.

## Allergies / Warnings
Per label: for external use only; discontinue and consult a doctor if condition worsens or does not improve. No lanolin (unlike A&D/A+D/Calmoseptine), reducing one specific known contact-allergy risk present in several category-mates.

## Symptoms Treated
Diaper rash / irritant diaper dermatitis, general dry/irritated skin — via petrolatum's occlusive moisture-barrier mechanism, per [[petrolatum]].
