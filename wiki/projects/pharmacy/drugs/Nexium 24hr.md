## Cons
- **No clinically meaningful efficacy advantage over omeprazole (Prilosec OTC) was identified in this pass** — esomeprazole and omeprazole are closely related molecules (S-isomer vs. racemic mixture) within the same drug class, and this database's [[proton-pump-inhibitors]] page does not distinguish a superior efficacy profile for esomeprazole specifically; a consumer paying a brand premium for Nexium over generic omeprazole should know the two share the same core mechanism and the same ~50%-non-complete-resolution real-world efficacy gap noted on that page
- Same full PPI-class safety-signal list applies (hypomagnesemia, C. diff/infection risk, rebound acid hypersecretion) — no Nexium-specific mitigation identified
- Not for immediate relief (1–4 days for full effect), same as every other PPI in this category
- No Nexium-24HR-specific RCT beyond the general PPI-class evidence base was independently located in this pass

## Use Cases
Treatment of frequent heartburn (≥2 days/week) via a full 14-day course — same use pattern and limitations as [[Prilosec OTC]] and the rest of the PPI class.

## Allergies / Warnings
Per label: do not use with trouble/pain swallowing food, vomiting blood, bloody/black stools, or chest pain with shortness of breath. Children under 18: ask a doctor first (pediatric heartburn can signal a more serious underlying condition). Pregnant/breastfeeding: ask a health professional. Esomeprazole may cause severe skin reactions.
