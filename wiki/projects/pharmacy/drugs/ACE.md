## Overview
**ACE** (3M) is the leading brand in Joint Support/Braces (53% share, by far the largest in the category) and also carries a meaningful Support Hosiery share (21%). No FDA drug label exists — ACE makes elastic bandages, neoprene/adjustable braces, and compression hosiery, all regulated as devices, not drugs. The elastic bandage line is regulated as a Class I device under 21 CFR 880.5075; the brace line falls under the Class I limb-orthosis regulation (21 CFR 890.3475), which explicitly names "elastic stocking" and "knee cage" as covered examples — the same regulatory tier as [[Futuro]] and [[Mueller]]. No brand-specific clinical trial for any ACE product was found; the applicable class-level evidence is on [[joint-support-braces]] and [[support-hosiery]]. One real FDA MAUDE adverse-event record exists for an ACE self-adhering elastic bandage, consistent with normal post-market surveillance for a widely-sold Class I device rather than a signal of unusual risk.

## Pros
- Largest recommendation share in Joint Support/Braces (53%) and a substantial Support Hosiery share (21%) — reflects broad retail availability and pharmacist familiarity, not a demonstrated efficacy edge over competitors
- Class I device status means minimal regulatory risk profile; elastic bandages and basic braces have a long, well-established safety track record
- Product breadth (elastic wraps, neoprene sleeves, hinged supports, compression socks) covers most common OTC joint-support use cases under one recognizable brand

## Cons
- Per the class-level evidence on [[joint-support-braces]], the best-available systematic reviews found no clearly demonstrated clinical-outcome benefit (pain, function, swelling, recovery time) for either knee bracing or elastic-wrap compression — this applies to the whole device class, ACE included, not a defect specific to this brand
- No brand-specific trial exists to show ACE's specific products perform differently from the class-level evidence
- Elastic wraps carry a real if uncommon injury risk if applied too tightly (numbness, tingling, impaired circulation) — a technique-dependent risk inherent to the product type

## Use Cases
Mild joint strain/sprain support and compression (wrist, ankle, knee, elbow); post-minor-injury swelling control; general venous/leg-comfort compression via the hosiery line. Not a substitute for medical evaluation of significant joint injury or diagnosed venous disease.

## Allergies / Warnings
Not a drug — no allergy/ingredient profile. Standard device cautions: do not wrap so tightly as to cause numbness, tingling, or color change in the extremity; discontinue and seek care if pain worsens or swelling doesn't improve; some users may have contact sensitivity to neoprene or latex components in specific SKUs (check individual product labeling).

## Symptoms Treated
