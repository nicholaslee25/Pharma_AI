## Overview
Confirmed via manufacturer labeling: SmartyPants is a large multi-SKU pediatric gummy vitamin line — Kids Complete (with omega-3 DHA/EPA fish oil + algal oil), Kids Formula, Kids Plus Multi & Fiber, Kids Plus Multi & Omegas, Toddler Multi & Omegas, and Toddler Organic Multi & Omegas among the variants found — the same one-brand-spans-multiple-formulations pattern already confirmed for Flintstones, Enfamil Poly-Vi-Sol, One A Day Kids, and OLLY Kid's Multi in this same category (see consolidated note on [[multivitamins]]). This database cannot confirm which specific SKU the 5% pharmacist-recommendation share refers to. **Allergen nuance worth flagging explicitly:** the omega-3-containing SKUs (Kids Complete and both Plus/Omegas variants) derive fish oil from actual fish, yet the brand markets ALL its formulas — including the fish-oil-containing ones — as "Certified Free From the 9 Major US Allergens" (including fish). This is plausible (highly refined/purified fish oil typically has allergenic fish protein removed, unlike whole fish), but it is a genuine tension worth noting rather than accepting uncritically, especially set against Nature Made Kids First Multivitamin's confirmed fish allergen finding elsewhere in this exact category — two brands in the same category both containing fish oil, reaching opposite allergen-labeling conclusions.

## Pros
- Omega-3-containing SKUs combine multivitamin and fish-oil supplementation in one product, potentially reducing the number of separate supplements a caregiver needs to give
- Manufacturer claims Certified Free From the 9 Major US Allergens and Certified Gluten Free across all formulas, including the fish-oil-containing ones (refined fish oil is plausibly allergen-protein-free, though this database did not independently verify the specific refining/testing standard used)
- Gelatin used is bovine-sourced (not porcine), relevant for some dietary/religious restrictions, though most formulas are also available as pectin-based

## Cons
- **Same brand-name/SKU ambiguity flagged repeatedly in this category**: this database's category-mapping data ("SmartyPants," 5% share) does not specify which of at least six identified SKUs applies, and their ingredient panels differ meaningfully (presence/absence of fish oil, fiber, organic certification)
- **Allergen-labeling tension** (see Overview): fish-oil-containing SKUs are marketed as fish-allergen-free — plausible given refining, but this database could not independently verify the specific manufacturer testing standard behind that claim, and at least one third-party ingredient-scan source flagged possible fish-derived ingredients despite the certification
- Per [[multivitamins]]'s general evidence review: broad disease-prevention claims for multivitamin use are not established in a large modern adult RCT; no pediatric-specific outcome trial exists for any SmartyPants formulation
- No brand-specific pediatric RCT located for any SmartyPants SKU beyond per-nutrient ingredient-page evidence

## Use Cases
General pediatric nutrient-gap supplementation; omega-3-containing SKUs add fish-oil/DHA-EPA supplementation framing.

## Allergies / Warnings
Marketed as free of the 9 major FDA-recognized food allergens across all formulas per manufacturer labeling, including fish-oil-containing SKUs — caregivers of fish-allergic children should independently verify this claim with the manufacturer given the genuine tension noted above. Contains bovine gelatin in gelatin-based SKUs. Keep out of reach of children (fat-soluble vitamin overconsumption risk in gummy format).

## Symptoms Treated
General nutrient-gap correction; omega-3 SKUs add general DHA/EPA supplementation framing (see [[omega-3-fish-oil]] for the underlying evidence).

## Brand-Specific Alignment Check
**New finding, added to both this page and [[multivitamins]]:** SmartyPants is the fifth brand in this Children's Multivitamins category confirmed to span multiple formulations under one umbrella name, and introduces a new allergen-labeling tension not previously seen in this category — a brand claiming fish-allergen-free status on SKUs that actually contain fish oil, set directly against Nature Made Kids First Multivitamin's confirmed fish allergen finding in the same category. Neither claim is resolved here; both are flagged for a caregiver's independent verification.
