## Appears In
Eczema Care/Relief Products - 5%

## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed (labeler: Gold Bond Co LLC): **colloidal oatmeal 2%** as the sole active ingredient (purpose: skin protectant), consistent across every identified SKU (Medicated Eczema Relief Cream, Eczema Relief Medicated Skin Protectant Cream, Eczema Relief Medicated Hand Cream, Ultimate Eczema Relief Cream, Ultimate Eczema Relief Hand). This is **double the concentration** of [[Aveeno Eczema Therapy]] and [[Eczema Honey]] (both colloidal oatmeal 1%) — a real, confirmed formulation difference within the same category and active ingredient, though this pass found no head-to-head trial establishing whether 2% outperforms 1% clinically (see [[colloidal-oatmeal]], whose trial base uses 1% cream in its pivotal non-inferiority RCT). The "Ultimate" line adds ceramides, aloe vera, shea butter, oat kernel extract, ginger root extract, and panthenol as inactive ingredients — bridging with [[moisturizers-ceramides]]'s emollient mechanisms alongside the skin-protectant active.

**Manufacturer marketing claim flagged, not independently verifiable:** Gold Bond's own product page states "In a clinical study, 9 out of 10 eczema sufferers also experienced significant relief of redness and irritation in just four weeks" and "8 of 10 eczema sufferers saw and felt significant skin improvement in just two weeks." No sample size, methodology, control group, or citation/footnote is provided anywhere on the manufacturer's page — this is a manufacturer-sponsored claim without any accessible peer-reviewed backing, structurally similar to unsupported claims flagged elsewhere in this database, and should not be treated as equivalent to the independently peer-reviewed [[colloidal-oatmeal]] ingredient-level evidence (Lisante 2017 RCT, etc.).

## Pros
- Confirmed consistent FDA Drug Facts label (colloidal oatmeal 2%) across all identified SKUs, independently verified via DailyMed
- Higher colloidal-oatmeal concentration (2%) than several category competitors (Aveeno Eczema Therapy, Eczema Honey at 1% each), though clinical superiority at 2% is not independently established in the trial literature reviewed
- "Ultimate" line's added ceramides align with a second, independently evidenced eczema-relevant mechanism (barrier lipid replacement, see [[moisturizers-ceramides]]) alongside the skin-protectant active
- National Eczema Association product listing found for multiple SKUs, suggesting some degree of independent organizational review (not independently verified against the seal's specific criteria in this pass)

## Cons
- **Manufacturer's "9 out of 10" / "8 of 10" clinical-improvement claims carry no disclosed methodology, sample size, or citation** — a real transparency gap between the marketing claim and what can be independently verified, flagged explicitly rather than repeated as established evidence
- No Gold-Bond-brand-specific peer-reviewed clinical trial was located; evidence is at the [[colloidal-oatmeal]] ingredient level, and even that pivotal trial (Lisante 2017) tested a 1% formulation, not this brand's 2%
- No Cochrane systematic review exists for colloidal oatmeal generally (per [[colloidal-oatmeal]]), so the evidence base underlying this brand's active ingredient is thinner than several other ingredients in this database
- Multiple SKUs (cream, hand cream, lotion, "Ultimate" variants) with differing inactive-ingredient formulations beyond the shared 2% colloidal oatmeal were not individually itemized in this pass

## Use Cases
Temporary relief of minor skin irritation and itching due to eczema and rashes, via colloidal oatmeal's skin-protectant mechanism (occlusive barrier, avenanthramide anti-inflammatory activity — see [[colloidal-oatmeal]]), with added moisturizing/ceramide support in the "Ultimate" line.

## Allergies / Warnings
Per label: external use only; avoid eye contact; discontinue and consult a doctor if condition worsens or symptoms persist beyond 7 days or recur within a few days; keep away from children, seek immediate medical attention if ingested. Per [[colloidal-oatmeal]]: oat/cereal-grain allergy carries a theoretical (uncommon) contact-allergy risk. Manufacturer states the line is hypoallergenic and free of fragrance, steroids, and dyes (not independently re-verified against a full ingredient panel in this pass).

## Symptoms Treated
Itching and minor skin irritation from eczema and rashes, via colloidal oatmeal's skin-protectant mechanism.
