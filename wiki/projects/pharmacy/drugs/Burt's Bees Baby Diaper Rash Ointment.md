## Appears In

## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed: Burt's Bees Baby Bee Diaper Rash Ointment is **40% zinc oxide** ("Maximum Strength"), sold at this single concentration rather than spanning a standard/maximum-strength split like Desitin, Boudreaux's, and Triple Paste. **New, significant allergen finding:** the inactive-ingredient list includes **sweet almond oil (a tree-nut allergen under US labeling law) and soybean oil (one of the 9 major FDA-recognized food allergens)** — both present in a "100% Natural Origin" marketed infant diaper-rash product, alongside lanolin (the recurring contact-allergen already flagged for A&D, A+D Original Ointment, Calmoseptine, and Boogie Diaper Rash Spray) and a broad range of essential oils/extracts (lavender, chamomile/anthemis nobilis, calendula, rosemary) with their own individual contact-sensitization profiles. This is the SECOND real tree-nut allergen finding in this database's broader pediatric research (after L'il Critters Gummy Vites' coconut finding in Children's Multivitamins), and the first instance of BOTH a tree-nut allergen AND a top-9 soy allergen appearing together as undisclosed "natural" inactive ingredients in a single product.

## Pros
- Confirmed FDA Drug Facts label at a single, unambiguous 40% zinc-oxide concentration — no brand-name/SKU concentration ambiguity, a positive contrast with Desitin/Boudreaux's/Triple Paste's multi-tier splits
- Zinc oxide's barrier-protectant mechanism matches [[zinc-oxide]]'s general evidence base
- "100% Natural Origin" formulation may appeal to caregivers specifically seeking to avoid petroleum-derived or synthetic ingredients — though see Cons for why "natural" does not mean allergen-free

## Cons
- **Contains sweet almond oil (tree-nut allergen) and soybean oil (top-9 major allergen) as inactive ingredients** — neither disclosed as an allergen concern in the product's "100% Natural Origin"/pediatrician-tested marketing, and both genuinely relevant for tree-nut- or soy-allergic infants and their caregivers. This is a real, actionable finding, not a theoretical risk: both ingredients are recognized allergens under US food-allergen labeling law even though this is a topical (not oral) product, and topical exposure can still provoke contact reactions in sensitized individuals.
- Also contains lanolin — the same recurring contact-allergy risk flagged across multiple other brands in this category
- Contains multiple essential oils/botanical extracts (lavender, chamomile, calendula, rosemary) each carrying their own uncommon but real contact-sensitization risk, a broader allergen-exposure profile than the single-ingredient zinc-oxide-only competitors (Balmex, standard Desitin, standard Triple Paste) in this category
- Per [[zinc-oxide]]'s own evidence review: overall evidence quality for zinc-oxide barrier preparations is graded low-level (Grade C), applying here regardless of the "natural" positioning
- No Burt's-Bees-specific RCT was independently located in this pass

## Use Cases
Prevention and treatment of diaper rash (irritant diaper dermatitis) via 40% zinc-oxide barrier protection, in a "natural origin" oil/beeswax-based formulation.

## Allergies / Warnings
**Contains sweet almond oil (tree-nut allergen) and soybean oil (top-9 major FDA-recognized allergen) — caregivers of tree-nut- or soy-allergic infants should check this product's ingredient list before use, despite "100% Natural Origin" marketing.** Also contains lanolin (known, if uncommon, contact allergen) and multiple essential oils/botanical extracts with individual contact-sensitization risk. Per label: for external use only; discontinue and consult a doctor if condition worsens or does not improve.

## Symptoms Treated
Diaper rash / irritant diaper dermatitis — via zinc oxide's moisture-barrier mechanism, per [[zinc-oxide]].

## Brand-Specific Alignment Check
**Significant new finding, added to both this page and [[zinc-oxide]]:** Burt's Bees Baby Diaper Rash Ointment is the first product in this database's Diaper Rash Products category confirmed to contain BOTH a tree-nut allergen (sweet almond oil) and a top-9 major allergen (soybean oil) as undisclosed "natural" inactive ingredients — reinforcing this database's recurring finding (previously seen with L'il Critters Gummy Vites' coconut oil, and Maty's All Natural Baby Chest Rub's coconut oil) that "natural"/"all natural" branding does not imply allergen-free, and in fact often correlates with MORE plant-derived allergen exposure than a simpler synthetic/petroleum-based formulation would carry.
