## Appears In
Homeopathic Topical Analgesics - 31%

## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed (labeler: Boiron, Inc., Newtown Square, PA; made in France): both the Gel and Cream formats use **Arnica montana 1X HPUS 7%** as the active ingredient, purpose "temporarily relieves muscle pain, stiffness, and bruise-related symptoms (pain, swelling, discoloration)." See [[homeopathic-arnica]] (new ingredient page built this pass) for the full evidence picture. This is the **category leader (31% share)** in Homeopathic Topical Analgesics — correctly filed as homeopathic, unlike [[Blue-Emu Original Cream]] elsewhere in the same category.

**Real, dose-dependent evidence nuance, per [[homeopathic-arnica]]'s dedicated research:** a systematic review of topical arnica trials found "the efficacy of Arnica in doses of 10% and below is not supported by the available evidence," while the one positive controlled trial located (Knuesel et al., knee osteoarthritis) used a fresh-plant (non-diluted, higher-concentration) preparation, not a 1X HPUS homeopathic dilution like this product. Boiron's own label carries the standard homeopathic disclaimer: "Claims based on traditional homeopathic practice not accepted medical evidence. Not FDA evaluated" (as documented on retail sourcing) alongside DailyMed's standard "FDA has not evaluated this homeopathic product for safety or efficacy" language.

Boiron also sells related but distinct Arnicare sub-lines not driving this specific brand-file row: **Arnicare Arthritis** (adds harpagophytum procumbens/zeyheri root — devil's claw — to the arnica base, a genuinely different combination formula) and **Arnicare Foot Care** cream — both separate SKUs from the plain Gel/Cream tracked here.

## Pros
- Confirmed consistent active ingredient/concentration (Arnica montana 1X HPUS 7%) across both Gel and Cream formats, independently verified via DailyMed
- 1X dilution is far less extreme than classical high-potency homeopathy (e.g., [[oscillococcinum]]'s 200CK) — some actual arnica plant material is plausibly present, a genuinely different (if still contested) evidence category
- Long-standing brand recognition and category-leading recommendation share (31%), suggesting broad pharmacist/consumer familiarity
- Short, relatively simple inactive-ingredient list (shea butter, coconut oil, glycerin) for the cream format
- Approved for use starting at age 1 per label directions — a notably young pediatric threshold relative to many other topical analgesics in this database

## Cons
- **Per [[homeopathic-arnica]]'s dedicated evidence review: topical arnica efficacy at 10% concentration and below is not supported by the available literature** — and this product's 1X HPUS 7% dilution falls within that unsupported range, a real and direct evidence gap for this category's largest-share brand
- The one positive controlled trial located for arnica used a fresh-plant, non-homeopathic-dilution preparation — not directly applicable evidence for this specific diluted product
- Product's own FDA label carries the standard "not evaluated by the FDA for safety or efficacy" homeopathic disclaimer
- No Boiron-Arnicare-brand-specific clinical trial (as opposed to general arnica-ingredient trials, none of which used this exact dilution) was located

## Use Cases
Temporary relief of muscle pain, stiffness, and discomfort from minor injuries, overexertion, or falls; reduces bruising-related symptoms (pain, swelling, discoloration).

## Allergies / Warnings
Per label: external use only; avoid contact with eyes, mucous membranes, and damaged skin; do not wrap tightly or apply heat/ice immediately before or after use; discontinue if condition persists beyond 3 days or worsens.

## Symptoms Treated
Muscle pain, stiffness, and bruise-related symptoms (pain, swelling, discoloration) from minor injuries.
