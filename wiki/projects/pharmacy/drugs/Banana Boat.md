## Overview
**Major discrepancy flagged:** Direct DailyMed search for "Banana Boat" returns ONLY sunscreen products — chemical-filter combinations (avobenzone, octocrylene, oxybenzone, homosalate, octisalate) and mineral-filter combinations (zinc oxide, titanium dioxide) across numerous SKUs (Kids Max Protect & Play SPF 100, Baby Mineral SPF 50+, Kids Mineral SPF 50+, Sheer Sensitive SPF 50, Kids Sport SPF 50, Simply Protect Kids SPF 50+, Light as Air Face SPF 50+, Protection Plus Vitamins Face SPF 50+). **No after-sun/aloe product appears in DailyMed at all.** Per multiple third-party ingredient-listing sources, Banana Boat's after-sun "Soothing After Sun Gel with Aloe," "Aloe After Sun Spray," and "Aloe After Sun Lotion" — the products plausibly driving the 13% Sun Burn Relief share, given "Sun Burn Relief" is a treatment category and sunscreen is prevention — contain **no FDA-recognized active drug ingredient at all** (no camphor, menthol, lidocaine, benzocaine, or hydrocortisone), despite marketing language including "Sunburn Relief" directly in product names. These are cosmetics/moisturizers, not drugs.

## Pros
- Sunscreen line: confirmed FDA Drug Facts labels across a wide SKU range, both chemical- and mineral-filter options, including oxybenzone/paraben-free mineral options for sensitive users
- Sunscreen line offers genuine broad-spectrum UVA/UVB protection (a real prevention benefit) across a wide range of SPF levels and formats (lotion, spray)
- After-sun aloe products, while non-drug, use a genuinely studied natural ingredient (aloe vera) with some supporting evidence for burn-wound healing, even if not sunburn-specific or high-quality

## Cons
- **Discrepancy flagged:** the after-sun products most plausibly responsible for Banana Boat's "Sun Burn Relief" category share contain no active drug ingredient and no FDA Drug Facts panel at all — a consumer expecting a medicated pain-relief product based on "Sunburn Relief" language on the packaging is not getting one
- The only aloe vera evidence located (a systematic review of 4 controlled trials, 371 patients) concerns general burn-wound healing, not sunburn specifically, and the two RCTs in that review scored only 2/5 on the Jadad quality scale — real but weak evidence, and not tested on this brand's specific product
- Sunscreen SKUs use oxybenzone in several chemical-filter formulations — oxybenzone carries its own separate environmental/reef-safety and systemic-absorption discussion in the broader sunscreen literature, not independently investigated in this pass
- No brand-specific clinical trial for either the sunscreen or after-sun product lines was identified

## Use Cases
Sunscreen line: broad-spectrum UV protection to PREVENT sunburn. After-sun aloe line: post-sun-exposure skin moisturizing and soothing — marketed for sunburn relief but without any medically active pain-relief ingredient.

## Allergies / Warnings
Sunscreen: standard chemical-filter sunscreen warnings apply (per DailyMed labels, not itemized brand-by-SKU in this pass); mineral-filter SKUs marketed as free of oxybenzone/parabens for sensitive-skin users. After-sun aloe products: no drug-specific warnings apply since no active ingredient is present; standard cosmetic-allergen considerations (fragrance, preservatives) may apply per their ingredient lists.

## Symptoms Treated
Sunscreen: none directly — a prevention product, not a treatment. After-sun aloe products: general skin moisturizing/soothing sensation; no confirmed pharmacological pain or itch relief.
