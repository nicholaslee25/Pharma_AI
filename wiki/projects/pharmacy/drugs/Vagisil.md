## Overview
Vagisil (Combe Inc.) is a large brand family spanning multiple, genuinely different product types across all three categories it appears in — confirmed via DailyMed and manufacturer data: **Vagisil Anti-Itch Creme** (external analgesic) is benzocaine 5% + resorcinol 2% (Regular Strength) or benzocaine 20% + resorcinol 3% (Maximum Strength) — a topical local-anesthetic/keratolytic combination, distinct from the miconazole/tioconazole-class antifungals ([[miconazole-nitrate-topical]]) used elsewhere in this database's Yeast Infection category. Separately, a **Vagisil-branded "Vagistat"** product is confirmed as miconazole nitrate cream — a real, potentially confusing case where the Vagisil brand ALSO sells a genuinely different, antifungal-class product under a name ("Vagistat") that is easily conflated with the unrelated, tioconazole-based "Vagistat-1" product from a different manufacturer (see `drugs/Vagistat.md`). Vagisil's lubricant-category products (14% share of Vaginal Moisturizers and Lubricants) were not independently osmolality-tested in the source reviewed for [[vaginal-lubricants-moisturizers]] in this pass — filed there by real-world market identity, not independently confirmed.

## Pros
- Anti-Itch Creme's benzocaine component is a well-established, FDA-monograph local anesthetic with a long safety record for external itch relief
- Real product-line breadth lets Vagisil address itch relief (Anti-Itch Creme), antifungal treatment (Vagisil-brand Vagistat, miconazole), and lubrication (Vaginal Moisturizers category) under one recognized name
- Wide market availability and long-established brand recognition

## Cons
- **A real, current class-action lawsuit was found**: a filing alleges certain Vagisil hygiene washes are falsely advertised as "hypoallergenic" and "gentle enough for daily use" when they contain known allergens/irritants — a genuine, ongoing legal allegation distinct from the antifungal/anti-itch products discussed above, worth flagging on the brand's cosmetic-wash sub-line specifically
- Resorcinol (the Anti-Itch Creme's second active ingredient) is a keratolytic/antiseptic with a real, if less commonly discussed, systemic-absorption profile at higher chronic/whole-body exposure levels in some contexts (thyroid-function and dermal-sensitization literature exists for resorcinol generally) — this database did not independently verify a Vagisil-specific safety signal here, but flags the ingredient class for completeness given how little brand-specific safety literature was located for this particular component
- **Real brand-name-collision risk:** a Vagisil-branded "Vagistat" (miconazole) product sits alongside an entirely different, differently-manufactured "Vagistat-1" (tioconazole) product in the broader market — a consumer searching by the name "Vagistat" cannot assume a single active ingredient across all products carrying that name; flagged on both this page and `drugs/Vagistat.md`
- Its lubricant products' osmolality was not independently tested in the source reviewed for [[vaginal-lubricants-moisturizers]] — unlike K-Y, Replens, and Astroglide (all independently confirmed hyperosmolar in that same source), Vagisil's specific lubricant formulation's tissue-safety profile remains an open question in this pass, neither confirmed safe nor confirmed unsafe

## Use Cases
External vulvar/vaginal itch relief (Anti-Itch Creme, benzocaine/resorcinol); vaginal antifungal treatment (Vagisil-brand Vagistat, miconazole); vaginal lubrication/moisturizing (Vaginal Moisturizers category placement).

## Allergies / Warnings
Anti-Itch Creme: do not use with a history of allergy to local anesthetics (procaine, butacaine, benzocaine, other "-caine" anesthetics); for external use only, avoid eye contact. The Vagisil-brand Vagistat (miconazole) carries the standard azole-antifungal cautions per [[miconazole-nitrate-topical]]. The class-action allegation above specifically concerns hygiene-wash allergen/irritant content, not the Anti-Itch or antifungal lines.

## Symptoms Treated
External itching/irritation (Anti-Itch Creme); vaginal yeast infection (Vagisil-brand Vagistat, miconazole); vaginal dryness (Vaginal Moisturizers line).
