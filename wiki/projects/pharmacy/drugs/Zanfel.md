## Overview
**Category-placement discrepancy, confirmed via ingredient sourcing and manufacturer description:** Zanfel Poison Ivy, Oak & Sumac Wash has **no FDA drug active ingredient**. Its formula (water, synthetic wax, sodium lauroyl sarcosinate, carbomer, a nonylphenoxy-based surfactant, C12-15 pareth-9, disodium EDTA, quaternium-15) is a **surfactant/detergent-based mechanical wash**, not a chemical drug. The mechanism, per the manufacturer, is that the product's surfactants surround and bind urushiol (poison ivy/oak/sumac's allergenic oil) so it can be rinsed away with water — a physical/chemical binding-and-removal action rather than a pharmacological anesthetic, anti-inflammatory, or antihistamine effect. The manufacturer claims itch relief within 30 seconds of application, attributed to urushiol removal itself rather than any active drug ingredient. This places Zanfel alongside Tecnu Original and (in other categories already documented in this database) products like Frida Baby No-Mess Vapor Rub as a **cosmetic/mechanical product filed in a drug-recommendation category** — a real, recurring pattern in this database where "remedy" categories mix genuine FDA drugs with non-drug mechanical/cosmetic products.

Unlike Tecnu Original, Zanfel is explicitly marketed for use **after a rash has already developed** (unlike Tecnu's pre-rash decontamination framing), which arguably fits the "Remedies" category label better on a functional basis even though it still carries no FDA-recognized active ingredient. No Zanfel-brand-specific clinical trial (e.g., a controlled comparison against plain soap-and-water washing) was located in this pass — the 30-second itch-relief claim is a manufacturer statement, not independently verified against a published trial in this search.

## Cons

## Use Cases
Mechanical removal of residual urushiol oil from skin after poison ivy/oak/sumac exposure, marketed for use on an already-developing or established rash to reduce itching and prevent further oil spread.
