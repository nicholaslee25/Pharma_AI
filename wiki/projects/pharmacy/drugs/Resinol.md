## Appears In

## Overview
**Real mismatched-ingredient finding.** [[zinc-oxide]]'s Pharmacy Times Context section listed Resinol among this category's brands without confirming its formulation, and a caregiver could reasonably assume (as this database's older Calmoseptine reference nearby illustrates) that a diaper-rash-category product here is zinc-oxide-based by default. Confirmed via DailyMed/manufacturer (ResiCal, Inc.) labeling, this is **not the case for Resinol**: its actual FDA-labeled ACTIVE ingredients are **petrolatum 55% (skin protectant) and resorcinol 2% (topical analgesic, with mild antiseptic/antibacterial properties)** — zinc oxide, calamine, corn starch, and lanolin appear only as INACTIVE ingredients. This makes Resinol mechanistically distinct from every other brand in this category checked so far (all zinc-oxide-primary except Aquaphor Baby, which is petrolatum-only with no resorcinol). **Separate, more important safety finding: resorcinol carries a real, documented methemoglobinemia risk** in infants/children with excessive or prolonged use, especially on broken/irritated skin or large body-surface areas — a genuinely serious safety consideration given this product's marketed use on diaper-area skin that is, by definition, often irritated (potentially broken) and covers a relatively large fraction of an infant's total body-surface area.

## Pros
- Confirmed FDA Drug Facts label; the resorcinol component adds a topical-analgesic (itch/pain relief) mechanism beyond the pure barrier-protectant action of zinc-oxide-only competitors in this category
- Petrolatum's occlusive barrier mechanism has real supporting RCT evidence for uncomplicated wound care (Smack et al. 1996, on [[petrolatum]])
- Manufacturer markets the product toward urine/diarrhea/incontinence-related skin irritation specifically, a use case consistent with (if not identically worded as) diaper rash

## Cons
- **No dedicated resorcinol ingredient page exists in this database** — a genuine data gap, since resorcinol's own efficacy and safety evidence base has not been independently reviewed here beyond this brand-specific safety search
- **Resorcinol carries a real, documented methemoglobinemia risk with excessive or prolonged topical use, particularly in children** — resorcinol can be absorbed through intact skin and more readily through broken/irritated skin, with reported effects including severe dermatitis, methemoglobinemia, cyanosis, convulsions, tachycardia, dyspnea, and (in extreme cases) death from overuse; general drug-safety guidance specifically warns against use on wounds and over large skin areas in children. This is a meaningfully more serious safety profile than the zinc-oxide-only competitors in this category, and caregivers should follow labeled directions strictly (not more than 3-4 times daily; children under 2, ask a doctor) rather than treating this as an equivalent, interchangeable "diaper rash cream"
- **Zinc oxide is only an inactive ingredient here, not the active mechanism** — worth correcting explicitly since this product sits in a category dominated by genuinely zinc-oxide-primary competitors
- Labeled ages 2+ generally, with "children under 2: ask a doctor" — a real age restriction not shared by most single-ingredient zinc-oxide diaper-rash products in this category (which are broadly used from birth)
- No Resinol-specific RCT for diaper rash was independently located in this pass

## Use Cases
Per manufacturer marketing: relief of skin irritation from urine, diarrhea, and incontinence — a diaper-rash-adjacent use case, though "diaper rash" is not the product's literal labeled term. Also generally labeled for minor cuts, scrapes, burns, and minor skin irritation pain/itching relief.

## Allergies / Warnings
**Resorcinol should not be applied to broken skin, wounds, or large body-surface areas, and use should not exceed labeled frequency/duration, given a real methemoglobinemia risk with excessive absorption — a meaningfully more serious safety consideration than most other products in this category.** Children under 2: ask a doctor before use. Contains lanolin (inactive ingredient) — the same recurring contact-allergy risk flagged for several other brands in this category.

## Symptoms Treated
Skin irritation/itching/pain from urine, diarrhea, incontinence, minor cuts/scrapes/burns — via petrolatum's occlusive barrier plus resorcinol's topical-analgesic/mild-antiseptic action, distinct from the zinc-oxide-primary mechanism of most other brands in this category.

## Brand-Specific Alignment Check
**Real finding, added to both this page and [[zinc-oxide]]:** Resinol is NOT zinc-oxide-primary despite sitting in a category dominated by zinc-oxide-based competitors — its actual active ingredients are petrolatum and resorcinol, and resorcinol carries a genuine, non-trivial methemoglobinemia safety risk in infants with excessive/improper use that is meaningfully different from (and more serious than) the safety profile of every zinc-oxide-only competitor in this category. Flagged explicitly given the vulnerable population (infants with irritated diaper-area skin) this product is marketed toward.
