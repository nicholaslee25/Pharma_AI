## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed: "Orajel" spans at least two materially different mechanisms across its own product line, not a single fixed active ingredient. The flagship mouth-pain SKU driving its Canker Sore Treatments (43%, category leader) and part of its Cold Sore Treatments (4%) shares — **Orajel 3X Medicated For All Mouth Sores gel** — is confirmed **benzocaine 20% + menthol 0.1% + zinc chloride 0.15%** (oral pain reliever/astringent combination). Its Toothache Products (68%, category leader) and Oral Pain Relievers, Adult (52%, category leader) shares are driven by the plain benzocaine/menthol liquid/gel toothache SKUs (2X/3X/4X Medicated for Toothache and Gum). Separately, **Orajel Antiseptic Rinse** — filed as its own brand entry in this database — uses **hydrogen peroxide and menthol**, an oxidizing antiseptic with NO anesthetic ingredient at all, mechanistically unrelated to the benzocaine gels. This brand-name-spans-multiple-mechanisms pattern matches this database's repeated finding (Neosporin/Simply Neosporin, Salonpas's five SKUs) that one brand name cannot be assumed to carry one fixed formulation.

## Pros
- Confirmed FDA OTC Drug Facts label for its core benzocaine SKU, a well-established local-anesthetic mechanism for temporary mouth/tooth pain relief
- By far the largest share in three of its four categories (68% Toothache, 52% Oral Pain Relievers Adult, 43% Canker Sore) — reflects strong consumer/pharmacist familiarity
- A non-benzocaine antiseptic-rinse line exists under the same brand for users who want an oxidizing rinse rather than a numbing gel

## Cons
- Benzocaine 20% carries the well-documented, disproportionate methemoglobinemia risk detailed on [[topical-anesthetics]] (Guay 2009: ~66% of 242 reviewed methemoglobinemia cases involved benzocaine)
- "Orajel" as a brand spans at least two mechanistically unrelated product lines (benzocaine anesthetic gels vs. hydrogen-peroxide antiseptic rinse) — a consumer should check the specific SKU rather than assume one active ingredient
- No brand-specific (as opposed to ingredient-class) clinical trial for Orajel's own benzocaine formulation was identified beyond the general topical-anesthetic evidence
- Not for teething use per label — despite historical consumer association of "Orajel" with teething relief, current benzocaine labels explicitly warn against pediatric teething use

## Use Cases
Temporary relief of pain from toothaches, canker sores, cold sores, and minor mouth/gum irritation or injury (benzocaine gel/liquid SKUs, adults and children 2+); the separate Antiseptic Rinse line targets mouth sores via an oxidizing/cleansing mechanism rather than numbing.

## Allergies / Warnings
Per label (benzocaine SKUs): do not use in children under 2; do not use for teething; do not use for more than 7 days unless directed by a dentist/doctor; methemoglobinemia risk — stop and seek immediate medical attention for pale/gray/blue skin, headache, rapid heart rate, shortness of breath, dizziness, or fatigue; do not use if allergic to "caine" anesthetics (procaine, butacaine, etc.).

## Symptoms Treated
Temporary numbing/pain relief for toothaches, canker sores, cold sores, and minor mouth/gum irritation (benzocaine line); antiseptic cleansing/pain relief for mouth sores (hydrogen peroxide rinse line, separate SKU — see `drugs/Orajel Antiseptic Rinse.md`).
