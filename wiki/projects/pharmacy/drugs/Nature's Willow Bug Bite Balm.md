## Appears In

## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed: the ONLY FDA-recognized active ingredient in Nature's Willow Bug Bite Balm is **menthol 1%** (External Analgesic). **Discrepancy flagged:** despite the brand name "Nature's Willow" and its marketing as a "natural" bug-bite remedy, **white willow bark extract — the namesake ingredient — is listed as an INACTIVE ingredient** on the FDA label, meaning the FDA does not recognize it as providing the product's pain/itch relief. Camphor is also present but likewise classified as inactive here (contrast with [[BiteMD]], where camphor 3% is an active ingredient at a higher, therapeutically-labeled concentration). A search for willow-bark-extract clinical evidence found real dermatologic research (a topical salicin serum study on skin-aging signs — wrinkles, radiance) but this concerns an entirely different use case (anti-aging/exfoliation) than insect-bite itch relief, and is unrelated to this specific product regardless of the inactive-ingredient status.

## Pros
- Confirmed FDA Drug Facts label; simple single-active-ingredient formulation (menthol 1%)
- Menthol provides a genuine, well-recognized cooling/counterirritant antipruritic effect
- "Kid-friendly" marketing and pregnancy/nursing advisory (ask a doctor first) suggest attention to gentler use cases, though these are marketing/label framing rather than independently verified safety claims beyond the standard menthol warnings

## Cons
- **Unsupported marketing/naming discrepancy:** the brand name centers on "willow" and white willow bark extract, but that ingredient is FDA-labeled as INACTIVE — it is not credited with any of the product's therapeutic effect. A consumer choosing this product for a perceived "natural salicylate" bite treatment is relying on an ingredient the FDA does not recognize as active in this formulation.
- "Natural" marketing framing is a vague, not independently verified claim — the product also contains synthetic-sounding inactive ingredients (e.g., Ceteareth-20, Phenoxyethanol, Triethanolamine) alongside the botanical extracts
- Only a single, mild-strength active ingredient (menthol 1%) — no anesthetic (benzocaine/lidocaine) or antihistamine component, a narrower mechanism than competitors like [[Sting-Kill]] or [[Benadryl Topical]] in the same category
- No brand-specific clinical trial for Nature's Willow Bug Bite Balm was identified; the willow-bark clinical literature located concerns an unrelated use case (skin aging, not bite/itch relief)

## Use Cases
Temporary relief of pain and itching from insect bites and minor skin irritations.

## Allergies / Warnings
Per label: external use only; avoid eyes; discontinue and consult a doctor if condition worsens or symptoms persist beyond 7 days or recur shortly after clearing; pregnant/nursing individuals should seek professional medical advice before use; keep out of reach of children, contact Poison Control if swallowed.
