## Appears In
Lice Treatments - 71%

## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed (NIX Lice Killing Creme Rinse, labeler Insight Pharmaceuticals/Prestige Brands): **permethrin 1% (280 mg per fl oz)**, a single-application cream rinse — 10-minute dwell time after shampooing (without conditioner) and towel-drying to damp. See [[permethrin-pyrethrin-lice]] for the full ingredient evidence page. Nix is this category's runaway leader at 71% pharmacist-recommendation share, more than 5x the next brand (RID, 13%).

**Critical finding carried over from the ingredient page — flagged here prominently because it bears directly on this brand's 71% share:** classic clinical trials found permethrin achieved 97-99% cure rates, but US surveillance data (138 sites, 48 states, 2013-2015) found the kdr resistance-mutation allele frequency averaging **98.3%** in real-world head lice populations, with 132 of 138 sites at 100% resistance-allele frequency. **The product formulation has not changed since those original trials, but the target organism largely has** — meaning Nix's real-world cure rate for a typical US infestation today is very likely well below the label's implied 97-99%, even though no re-formulation, recall, or label change has occurred to reflect this. This is a genuine, well-documented efficacy concern that a 71% recommendation share does not on its own reflect.

No Nix-brand-specific clinical trial (as opposed to generic permethrin trials) was located in this pass. No FDA recall was found for the current creme rinse formulation (one 2018 MAUDE adverse-event report describes accidental inhalation by a user, not a product defect).

## Pros
- Confirmed, consistent FDA Drug Facts label (permethrin 1%) across the product line
- Single 10-minute application is markedly more convenient than RID's two-applications-7-days-apart regimen
- Long clinical trial history (97-99% cure in classic trials) and Cochrane-indexed superiority over lindane
- Approved down to 2 months of age, a lower age floor than several competitors in this category

## Cons
- **Same near-universal kdr-mutation resistance problem documented on [[permethrin-pyrethrin-lice]] applies fully to Nix** — the classic 97-99% cure-rate evidence this brand's reputation rests on predates the resistance data and likely no longer reflects real-world outcomes for most US infestations
- Not labeled for use on eyebrows or eyelashes; ragweed-allergic individuals should consult a doctor first (permethrin is chrysanthemum-derived)
- No Nix-brand-specific trial exists distinguishing its real-world performance from the generic permethrin literature
- A 71% recommendation share for a mechanism with well-documented, well-quantified resistance is a genuine tension this database flags but cannot resolve — pharmacist recommendation habits do not yet appear to reflect the resistance literature

## Use Cases
Treatment of active head lice (Pediculus humanus capitis) infestation, including nits, via a single 10-minute permethrin cream-rinse application, with a repeat application after 7+ days if live lice persist.

## Allergies / Warnings
Per label: external use only; avoid eyes, nose, ears, mouth, and vagina; not for use on eyebrows/eyelashes (see a doctor); consult a doctor first if allergic to ragweed; not for children under 2 months.

## Symptoms Treated
Active head lice infestation and nits; itching/scalp irritation from infestation.

## Brand-Specific Alignment Check
Formulation is consistent with the source data (permethrin 1%, single application). **The alignment issue here is not about mislabeling — it's the resistance/efficacy gap documented on [[permethrin-pyrethrin-lice]]:** this brand carries the largest share of any product in this category while using a mechanism with the most extensively documented real-world resistance problem in the entire pharmacy database to date.
