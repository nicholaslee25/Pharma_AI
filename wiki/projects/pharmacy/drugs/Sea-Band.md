## Overview
**Non-drug category-placement note, consistent with this database's recurring pattern:** Sea-Band is an **acupressure wristband device, not a drug** — it applies continuous physical pressure to the Nei-Kuan (P6) acupressure point on each wrist, with no active pharmaceutical ingredient at all. Marketed for ages 3+ (children's variant), latex-free, reusable. Filed under both "Children's Motion Sickness Products" (18% share, second-largest after Dramamine for Kids) and "Nausea Remedies" (4%), both drug-focused category names in this database. **Evidence is genuinely mixed and weaker than manufacturer marketing implies**: published independent evidence review found most trials do not support acupressure wristbands for motion-sickness prevention, citing low-quality studies with inconsistent results; at least one study found neither the acupressure band NOR a placebo band prevented motion sickness development, regardless of correct or incorrect band placement — a genuinely unflattering finding for the product's proposed mechanism specifically, since it suggests even the manufacturer's own claimed physiological mechanism (P6 pressure point stimulation) may not be doing the work.

## Pros
- Zero drug-interaction risk and no anticholinergic/sedation side effects, unlike every dimenhydrinate- or antihistamine-based competitor in adjacent motion-sickness categories — a real, meaningful safety differentiator for children on other medications or where sedation is specifically undesired (e.g., needing the child alert during travel)
- Reusable, latex-free, no age-based dosing math required — simpler logistics than a syrup or chewable requiring correct weight/age-based dosing
- No overdose, allergic reaction, or drug-interaction risk profile to counsel on, since there is no active ingredient

## Cons
- **The independent evidence base is weak and inconsistent, and at least one study found the band performed no better than a placebo band at preventing motion sickness regardless of correct placement** — this directly undercuts the plausibility of the manufacturer's own claimed P6-acupressure mechanism, not just the product's real-world effectiveness in general. Manufacturer marketing describing the product as "clinically tested" should be weighed against this more skeptical independent evidence.
- **Filed under drug-focused "Children's Motion Sickness Products" and "Nausea Remedies" categories despite being a device with no active ingredient** — the same real category-placement pattern already flagged for non-drug supplements (Zarbee's Naturals, Burt's Bees, HoneyWorks Kids) and homeopathic products elsewhere in this database, here extended to a physical device rather than an ingested product
- Second-largest share (18%) in Children's Motion Sickness Products despite this comparatively weak independent evidence base — a reminder that a high pharmacist-recommendation share should not be read as strong efficacy evidence, this database's standing rule
- No pediatric-specific trial (as opposed to general adult motion-sickness acupressure trials) was located in this pass

## Use Cases
Marketed for prevention/relief of nausea associated with motion sickness, morning sickness, chemotherapy, and anesthesia, via acupressure rather than a pharmacologic mechanism, ages 3+.

## Allergies / Warnings
No pharmacologic contraindications (no active ingredient). Latex-free per label — relevant for latex-sensitive individuals as a genuine positive rather than a warning. No drug-interaction profile.

## Symptoms Treated
Motion-sickness-associated nausea (per manufacturer claim; independent evidence for actual efficacy is weak and inconsistent per the studies reviewed above).
