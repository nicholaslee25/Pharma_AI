## Appears In

## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed ("Mederma Stretch Marks Therapy — dimethicone cream"): **dimethicone 2.0%** is the declared active ingredient, purpose "skin protectant" — a genuine FDA OTC drug product. **This is a different active ingredient from [[Mederma]]'s Scar Treatments product (allantoin 0.5%)** — the same "Mederma" brand name spans at least two chemically distinct FDA-monograph actives depending on which product line is meant, the same "one brand, multiple mechanisms" pattern already documented for [[Blistex]], [[Carmex]], and RID elsewhere in this database, though here (unlike the Mederma cold-sore-patch discrepancy already flagged on [[Mederma]]'s own page) both SKUs are at least genuine FDA drug products. The formulation also includes **Cepalin® botanical extract** (Merz's own trademarked onion-extract-derived ingredient, related to the mechanism in the company's original scar gel) and hyaluronic acid as supporting, non-declared-active ingredients; centella asiatica extract appears in the inactive-ingredient list, not as a declared active.

Marketing cites a "clinically shown to produce noticeable improvement in 4 weeks" claim; the specific underlying trial was not independently verified in this pass beyond confirming the dimethicone active-ingredient/skin-protectant drug status.

No independently-published (non-company) Mederma Stretch Marks-brand-specific clinical trial was located in this pass.

## Pros
- Confirmed, genuine FDA OTC drug product (dimethicone 2.0%, skin protectant) — unlike several competitors in the adjacent Scar Treatments subcategory, and unlike two of its two direct competitors here ([[Palmer's Cocoa Butter Formula]] and [[Bio-Oil]], both cosmetics — see their pages)
- Category leader (48% share) in Stretch Mark Treatments, nearly double the next brand
- Labeled safe for use during pregnancy and breastfeeding per manufacturer marketing (not independently verified against the FDA label's specific pregnancy language in this pass)
- Cepalin/hyaluronic acid supporting ingredients add hydration alongside the core dimethicone occlusive mechanism

## Cons
- **Uses a different active ingredient (dimethicone) than the flagship Mederma Advanced Scar product (allantoin)** — a consumer assuming "Mederma" means one consistent formulation across its scar and stretch-mark lines would be incorrect
- The "clinically shown" 4-week improvement marketing claim's underlying study was not independently verified in this pass
- No independently-published (non-company-sponsored) clinical trial was located specific to this product
- Dimethicone's general evidence base is strongest as an occlusive/moisture-barrier agent; no dedicated trial evaluating dimethicone specifically for stretch-mark appearance (as opposed to general skin protection) was independently verified here

## Use Cases
Skin protectant marketed to help prevent and improve the appearance of stretch marks via a dimethicone-based occlusive/hydrating formulation, including during pregnancy.

## Allergies / Warnings
Per general OTC dimethicone skin-protectant conventions: external use only; avoid eye contact; discontinue if irritation develops. Specific FDA label pregnancy/breastfeeding language was not independently itemized in this pass.

## Symptoms Treated
Prevention and appearance improvement of stretch marks (striae).
