## Overview
The classic O'Keeffe's for Healthy Feet jar/tube cream itself appears to be a cosmetic product, not an FDA-registered OTC drug — its ingredient list (water, glycerin, stearic acid, sodium hydroxide, allantoin, paraffin, hydroxypropyl methylcellulose, octyldodecyl stearate, diazolidinyl urea, iodopropynyl butylcarbamate, acrylates/acrylamide copolymer, mineral oil, polysorbate 85) carries no "Active Ingredient" percentage claim in the sources checked. **Important brand-line distinction found:** O'Keeffe's separately sells **"O'Keeffe's Healthy Feet Pain Relief,"** a genuinely different SKU with its own DailyMed label — Allantoin 0.5% (skin protectant) + Lidocaine HCl 4% (topical analgesic) — see [[topical-anesthetics]] for lidocaine's safety profile relative to benzocaine. This is NOT the same product as the plain "for Healthy Feet" cream in the Diabetic Foot Cream category table, and should not be assumed to share its (absent) ingredient profile. No individual clinical trial for either SKU was found beyond this labeling distinction.

## Pros
- Glycerin/allantoin-based cosmetic formula, consistent with basic humectant/emollient moisturizing mechanisms (see [[moisturizers-ceramides]] for the general moisturizer evidence base, though O'Keeffe's itself is not individually listed there)
- The separate Pain Relief SKU offers an actual FDA-registered lidocaine option within the same brand family for patients who need analgesic as well as moisturizing action
- Marketed specifically for "extremely dry, cracked feet," a targeted use case

## Cons
- The flagship product in this category has no confirmed active ingredient or independently verified clinical claim
- Real risk of consumer confusion between the plain cream (no analgesic) and the "Pain Relief" variant (lidocaine 4%) given the near-identical branding — a genuine labeling-clarity concern worth flagging explicitly
- No diabetic-population-specific outcome data exists — see [[diabetic-foot-care]]

## Use Cases
Extremely dry, cracked feet (plain cream); temporary pain/itch relief from minor skin irritation plus skin protection (Pain Relief SKU specifically, not the plain cream).

## Allergies / Warnings
Plain cream: no drug-facts warnings identified (cosmetic classification). Pain Relief SKU: external use only; avoid eyes; do not use on deep wounds, animal bites, serious burns, or raw surfaces; ask a doctor if pregnant/breastfeeding; consult a doctor for children under 12.

## Symptoms Treated
Dry, cracked feet (plain cream); pain and itching from minor skin irritation plus chapped/cracked skin (Pain Relief SKU).

## Brand-Specific Alignment Check
No ingredient page link applies to the plain cream (no confirmed active ingredient). The Pain Relief SKU's lidocaine content is consistent with [[topical-anesthetics]]'s safety data for lidocaine (materially safer methemoglobinemia profile than benzocaine per the Guay 2009 review already cited there) — no discrepancy, but this is a different product from the one actually ranked in the Diabetic Foot Cream category, a distinction worth preserving rather than collapsing.
