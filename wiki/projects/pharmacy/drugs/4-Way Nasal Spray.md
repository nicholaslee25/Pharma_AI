## Appears In
Decongestants, Nasal Spray - 4%

## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed: 4 Way Fast Acting = **phenylephrine hydrochloride 1%** nasal spray — the same active ingredient class as Neo-Synephrine (topical phenylephrine), not oxymetazoline as a brand named "4-Way" might otherwise suggest by analogy to multi-symptom combination products. This is a new confirmation. See `drugs/Neo-Synephrine.md` for the important distinction between topical and oral phenylephrine efficacy — that same distinction applies here. Label saved at `raw/primary-sources/brands/4-way-nasal-spray/dailymed-4-way.md`.

## Pros
- Same topical-application advantage discussed on Neo-Synephrine's page — avoids the first-pass metabolism problem that undermines oral phenylephrine's efficacy
- Dosed every 4 hours, giving more frequent relief opportunities than 12-hour oxymetazoline products for a user who prefers more granular dosing control

## Cons
- Same rebound-congestion class risk as any topical decongestant with prolonged/frequent use
- Shorter dosing interval (every 4h) means more chances to lose track of cumulative daily use or the 3-day limit than once/twice-daily oxymetazoline products
- No dedicated ingredient page exists for nasal phenylephrine, same gap noted on Neo-Synephrine's page

## Use Cases
Short-term (≤3 days) relief of nasal congestion from the common cold, hay fever, or upper respiratory allergies.

## Allergies / Warnings
Adults/children ≥12y: 2-3 sprays per nostril, not more often than every 4 hours. Do not exceed 3 consecutive days. Ask a doctor before use with heart disease, high blood pressure, diabetes, thyroid disease, or enlarged prostate.

## Symptoms Treated
Nasal congestion.
