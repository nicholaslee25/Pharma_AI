## Appears In

## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed: Safetussin (Kramer Laboratories) is a multi-SKU line. Core **Safetussin DM** = dextromethorphan HBr 10mg + guaifenesin 100mg per 5mL (touches [[dextromethorphan]] and [[guaifenesin]]) — identical dose profile to [[Diabetic Tussin]] DM and close to [[Robitussin]]'s DM combination. Other confirmed line SKUs: Non-Drowsy Cough Plus Chest Congestion (same dextromethorphan/guaifenesin combination), High Blood Pressure and Diabetes Cough, Cold and Flu (adds acetaminophen), and Max Strength MS Cough, Cold and Flu (acetaminophen + higher-dose dextromethorphan/guaifenesin). Explicitly marketed as containing no decongestant, no sugar/alcohol/sorbitol/fructose/gluten/dyes — a formulation claim aimed at diabetic and hypertensive patients avoiding pseudoephedrine (a genuine clinical rationale, since pseudoephedrine can raise blood pressure) and sugar, not a pharmacological difference in the active ingredients themselves. Label saved at `raw/primary-sources/brands/safetussin/dailymed-safetussin-dm.md`.

## Pros
- No decongestant in the core line — a real, clinically relevant design choice for the hypertensive patients this brand targets, since pseudoephedrine can raise blood pressure (unlike, say, Robitussin CF which does add one)
- Sugar/alcohol/dye-free, matching its "safe for diabetics" positioning
- DM formulation pairs a cough suppressant with an expectorant at standard OTC monograph doses

## Cons
- Smallest recommendation share (9%) of the three brands in this category, well behind Diabetic Tussin (63%) and Robitussin (28%)
- Same multi-SKU ambiguity as its category-mates: the "Safetussin" name alone does not specify which combination (plain DM vs. the acetaminophen-containing variants) a given box contains
- The High Blood Pressure and Diabetes SKU adds acetaminophen — a real added liver-toxicity consideration at high doses or with other acetaminophen-containing products, worth checking before combining with anything else
- Guaifenesin's own evidence page notes a negative objective RCT result (no significant mucociliary/cough clearance benefit vs. placebo) — applies here as to any guaifenesin product

## Use Cases
Cough suppression and chest congestion relief for patients who specifically want to avoid decongestants (hypertension) and sugar (diabetes); combined pain/fever/cough/congestion relief (acetaminophen-containing SKUs).

## Allergies / Warnings
Do not use with MAOIs or within 2 weeks of stopping one. Ask a doctor before use with excessive mucus production or chronic cough. Stop and seek medical advice if cough persists >7 days, returns, or occurs with fever/rash/headache. Not for children under 12. Avoid if pregnant/nursing without professional consultation. Acetaminophen-containing SKUs carry the standard liver-toxicity caution against exceeding total daily acetaminophen from all sources.

## Symptoms Treated
Cough (dry and productive), chest congestion, and (acetaminophen SKUs) fever/minor aches associated with cold/flu.

## Brand-Specific Alignment Check
No discrepancy found with [[dextromethorphan]] or [[guaifenesin]] — doses match standard OTC monograph amounts already reflected on those pages, and are identical to Diabetic Tussin DM's dose profile. The no-decongestant design choice is a genuine, clinically sensible differentiator from Robitussin CF within this same broad product family, not an error.
