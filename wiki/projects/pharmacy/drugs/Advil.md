## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed: Advil's core tablet/capsule lines list **ibuprofen 200 mg** as the active ingredient (pain reliever/fever reducer) — matching [[ibuprofen]]'s existing "confirmed ibuprofen per prior source mapping" entry. **Formulation nuance, directly relevant to existing ingredient-page evidence:** Advil Film-Coated tablets use **ibuprofen SODIUM** (200 mg ibuprofen provided as ibuprofen sodium 256 mg) rather than plain ibuprofen acid, and Advil Liqui-Gels use solubilized ibuprofen (free acid + potassium salt). This is a real-world confirmation of the exact formulation type — [[ibuprofen]]'s own Cochrane efficacy section (Derry et al. 2009) notes "soluble ibuprofen salts produced better efficacy estimates than other models/formulations" — Advil's sodium/solubilized product lines are a direct, named example of the formulation category that Cochrane review found favorable, not merely a marketing claim.

## Pros
- Confirmed FDA Drug Facts label with a well-established NSAID active ingredient, backed by [[ibuprofen]]'s large-scale Cochrane meta-analytic evidence (72 RCTs, 9186 participants, NNT 2.5–2.7 for acute pain)
- Advil's sodium-salt and solubilized-gel formulations are a concrete brand example of the specific formulation type Cochrane's own review flagged as producing better efficacy estimates — a genuine, non-marketing-only distinction from plain ibuprofen acid tablets
- By far the largest share of Oral Anti-Inflammatory Products (45%) and a leading share of Menstrual Pain Relief (24%) in this database, reflecting broad consumer/pharmacist familiarity

## Cons
- Carries the full ibuprofen risk profile documented on [[ibuprofen]]: boxed warnings for cardiovascular thrombotic events and GI bleeding/ulceration/perforation, contraindicated perioperatively for CABG, and the aspirin-interaction timing issue (ibuprofen can block low-dose aspirin's cardioprotective antiplatelet effect if dosed too close together)
- **Real FDA Class I recall (the most serious FDA recall category):** GSK Consumer Healthcare/Haleon voluntarily recalled several lots of Advil tablets/capsules on 2022-12-06 (FDA designated it Class I on 2023-01-25) due to a label-adhesive defect causing product labels to tear/become illegible when peeled back — a labeling-legibility issue (consumers might not see dosing/warning information), not a contamination or formulation defect
- Separately, Pfizer Consumer Healthcare (a former Advil distributor) voluntarily recalled one lot of Children's Advil Suspension Bubble Gum due to a dosage-cup unit mismatch (teaspoon markings vs. milliliter instructions) that could lead to inadvertent overdose — a genuine pediatric dosing-safety event, tied to the Children's Advil product specifically rather than adult Advil
- No independent, non-manufacturer brand-specific efficacy trial (as opposed to the ingredient-class Cochrane evidence) was identified for standard Advil tablets in this pass

## Use Cases
General analgesia, fever reduction, and anti-inflammatory relief — headache, toothache, backache, menstrual cramps, common cold aches, muscular aches, minor arthritis pain.

## Allergies / Warnings
Per label: NSAID warning — may cause severe stomach bleeding; may cause severe allergic reaction, especially in people allergic to aspirin. Full boxed-warning and contraindication detail per [[ibuprofen]] applies (CV risk, GI bleed risk, CABG contraindication, aspirin-timing interaction).
