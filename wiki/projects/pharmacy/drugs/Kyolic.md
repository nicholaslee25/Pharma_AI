## Overview
Kyolic (Aged Garlic Extract) is genuinely one of the better brand-specifically-studied supplements found across this entire vitamins/supplements research pass — unlike most brands in this database, Kyolic has **real, published, brand-named clinical trials**, not just general-ingredient evidence: (1) the **GarGIC Trial**, a 12-week double-blind RCT (n=49) assessing Kyolic's effect on blood pressure, pulse wave velocity, and arterial stiffness in hypertensive patients; (2) a randomized double-blind placebo-controlled 3-month trial (aerobic-fitness outcome in middle-aged recreational endurance athletes); (3) a 1997 experimental-atherosclerosis study; and (4) a 1-year trial in 80 diabetic patients (2400mg/day Kyolic) that found **no significant effect** on total plaque volume, dense calcium, or fibrous plaque volume — a real null finding worth preserving alongside the positive studies. Kyolic's manufacturer states its Cholesterol Health Formula 104 (600mg aged garlic extract + 380mg lecithin) has been the subject of "over 1,000 published scientific papers" — a claim this pass did not independently verify in full, though the several named trials above confirm genuine brand-specific research activity beyond marketing language. Garlic Supplements (7%) is covered by [[garlic]] for the general ingredient evidence.

## Pros
- **Genuinely brand-specific clinical trial evidence exists** (GarGIC Trial, aerobic-fitness RCT, atherosclerosis study, 1-year diabetic-plaque trial) — a real positive differentiator versus the great majority of brands in this pass, which have no clinical evidence beyond the general ingredient page
- "Aged garlic extract" processing is a specific, named preparation method distinct from raw garlic powder or garlic oil — allows more precise evidence attribution than generic "garlic supplement" claims
- The located evidence set honestly includes a null finding (the 1-year diabetic-plaque trial), suggesting this is not simply cherry-picked positive marketing literature

## Cons
- The GarGIC Trial (n=49) and aerobic-fitness trial are modestly sized — real RCTs, but not large enough alone to be considered definitive; should be read alongside (not instead of) [[garlic]]'s broader meta-analysis evidence
- The 1-year diabetic-plaque trial found **no significant benefit** on any of its three imaging endpoints — a real negative finding for that specific outcome (atherosclerotic plaque progression) even though other Kyolic trials found positive results on different outcomes (blood pressure, arterial stiffness, fitness)
- The company's "1,000+ published papers" claim was not independently itemized/verified in this pass — treat as a company claim pending closer verification
- No independent third-party quality certification (USP Verified, NSF) was located for Kyolic specifically in this pass

## Use Cases
Blood pressure/arterial-stiffness support and general cardiovascular health, per its own named trials, alongside the general garlic evidence in [[garlic]] (cholesterol, blood pressure, immune support surrogate-marker effects).

## Allergies / Warnings
See [[garlic]] — garlic supplements generally "highly tolerated," mild GI effects (burping, flatulence, reflux) in about a third of users. Kyolic's aged-garlic-extract processing is specifically marketed as odorless/reduced-GI-irritation relative to raw garlic, though this pass did not independently verify a head-to-head GI-tolerability comparison.

## Symptoms Treated
Blood pressure and arterial-stiffness support (Kyolic-specific trial evidence); general cholesterol/cardiovascular surrogate-marker support (see [[garlic]]). Not confirmed to reduce atherosclerotic plaque progression specifically — a real null finding from Kyolic's own 1-year diabetic trial.
