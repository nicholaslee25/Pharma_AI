## Overview
No individual FDA/DailyMed label was found for Eucerin's diabetic-skin line in this pass — like [[CeraVe]], this appears to be a cosmetic product, not an OTC monograph drug. The relevant product for the Diabetic Foot Cream category, **Eucerin Diabetics' Dry Skin Relief** (sold as body lotion, body creme, and foot creme SKUs), is formulated around **alpha hydroxy acid (AHA) + ceramide-3** rather than a labeled "active ingredient." Marketing material carries the same AHA sun-sensitivity caution seen on [[CeraVe]]'s equivalent product ("may increase your skin's sensitivity to the sun... use sunscreen, protective clothing, limit sun exposure during use and for a week afterward") — a genuinely useful, non-obvious warning for a foot-care product. The ceramide-3 component is mechanistically consistent with [[moisturizers-ceramides]] (already lists Eucerin among its brands). No brand-specific clinical trial for the diabetic-line SKUs was found.

## Pros
- Ceramide-3-containing formulation, mechanistically consistent with the real (if modest) flare-reduction evidence for moisturizers on existing eczematous/dry skin — see [[moisturizers-ceramides]]
- Marketed across multiple formats (lotion, creme, foot creme) allowing different application preferences
- Clearly labeled AHA sun-sensitivity warning, more explicit than some competitors

## Cons
- Not an FDA-regulated drug for this SKU — no active ingredient, no independently verified clinical claim
- The AHA sun-sensitivity warning is a real, easy-to-overlook caution specific to this exfoliating-acid-containing line, not shared by plain occlusive competitors like [[Aquaphor]] or [[Gold Bond Ultimate]]
- No diabetic-population-specific outcome data exists for this product — see [[diabetic-foot-care]]
- House-brand name spans five very different Pharmacy Times categories; ingredient identity should not be assumed to carry across SKUs

## Use Cases
Dry, flaky skin relief for diabetic patients, particularly on the feet; general therapeutic moisturizing and cleansing.

## Allergies / Warnings
Contains an Alpha Hydroxy Acid — increases sun sensitivity; use sunscreen and protective clothing during use and for about a week after stopping. For external use only; discontinue if irritation occurs. No diabetes-specific contraindication identified.

## Symptoms Treated
Dry, flaky, rough skin, particularly on feet; general xerosis.
