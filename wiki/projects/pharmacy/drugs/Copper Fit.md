## Overview
**Copper Fit** (13% share) is a neoprene/fabric compression-sleeve and brace line marketed on a copper-infusion claim — the brand's distinguishing feature relative to [[ACE]], [[Futuro]], and [[Mueller]]. No FDA drug label exists (device, not drug), same Class I limb-orthosis framework (21 CFR 890.3475) as the rest of [[joint-support-braces]]. On the copper claim specifically: copper has documented antibacterial/antifungal properties relevant to hospital-surface infection control (2014-era research), which is a real but different claim from "copper relieves joint pain" — no evidence located in this pass supports a therapeutic (pain, inflammation, recovery) benefit of copper infusion itself in a wearable garment. Independent product-review sources reviewed here (MedicalNewsToday, HighYa) explicitly attribute any real benefit of Copper Fit products to the **compression component**, not the copper, and describe the copper content as primarily an odor-resistance/marketing feature rather than a demonstrated therapeutic mechanism.

## Pros
- Compression mechanism is the same as competing neoprene sleeves — general compression-garment benefits (mild support, reduced-odor moisture-wicking fabric) are real, independent of the copper claim
- Lower price point than competing copper-infused compression brands per market-comparison sources
- Widely available and easy to size/fit (sleeve-style, no adjustable straps needed on most SKUs)

## Cons
- **The "copper" marketing claim is not supported by therapeutic evidence found in this pass** — copper's documented antimicrobial-surface benefits do not translate into a demonstrated joint-pain or inflammation benefit when woven into fabric, and no clinical trial isolating copper's contribution (vs. compression alone) was located; this is the kind of "active ingredient" claim gap this project's ingredient-page discipline is designed to flag, applied here to a device marketing claim rather than a drug claim
- Per the class-level evidence on [[joint-support-braces]], generic compression/bracing itself has inconclusive-to-low-certainty outcome-trial support — so Copper Fit inherits that same weak evidence base even before considering the copper claim separately
- User reviews cited in research (HighYa, product reviews) report inconsistent build quality/durability (e.g. stitching failure) on some SKUs — a real-world quality-control concern distinct from the efficacy question

## Use Cases
Mild joint compression/support (knee, shoulder, back, elbow) marketed with an added copper/odor-control claim; general comfort/compression use, not a substitute for medical evaluation of significant joint injury.

## Allergies / Warnings
Not a drug — no allergy/ingredient profile in the pharmacological sense. Standard compression-garment cautions apply (should not cause numbness/tingling/circulation impairment); check for individual sensitivity to synthetic/copper-infused fabric components.

## Symptoms Treated
Not applicable in the drug sense — provides mechanical compression only; no independently verified copper-specific therapeutic effect.
