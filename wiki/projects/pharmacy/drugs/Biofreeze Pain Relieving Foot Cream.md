## Appears In

## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed ("Biofreeze Foot Cream," menthol cream): **menthol USP 10%**, the same concentration as the standard Biofreeze Cream format already documented on [[Biofreeze]]'s brand page (spray 10.5%, cream 10%, gel 4%, roll-on 4%) — no formulation discrepancy found for this specific foot-labeled SKU relative to the brand's existing regular-cream concentration. This is the same counterirritant mechanism (menthol activating cold-sensing TRPM8 receptors, see [[topical-counterirritants]]) applied to a foot-specific marketing/packaging variant rather than a distinct chemistry. No Biofreeze-Foot-Cream-specific clinical trial was located, consistent with [[Biofreeze]]'s existing finding that no brand-specific trial exists for any format.

## Pros
- Confirmed consistent menthol 10% concentration, matching the brand's standard cream — no foot-specific under- or over-dosing concern relative to the rest of the product line
- Genuine FDA Drug Facts label with clear directions (thin film, up to 4 times daily, ages 12+)
- Counterirritant mechanism is well suited to generalized foot ache/soreness relief, distinct from the antifungal, keratolytic, or hydrocortisone mechanisms used by other Foot Care Products brands in this category

## Cons
- No brand/SKU-specific clinical trial exists; evidence is at the [[topical-counterirritants]] ingredient-class level
- Per [[topical-counterirritants]]: FDA's 2012 burn-risk communication flagged menthol above 3% as a risk factor for more severe burns — this product's 10% concentration exceeds that threshold (consistent with the standard Biofreeze Cream already flagged on [[Biofreeze]]'s page), though Biofreeze itself was not named in that communication's press coverage
- Treats general musculoskeletal foot soreness only — does not address structural foot-care needs (calluses, cracked heels, fungal infection) that many competing Foot Care Products brands in this category (Dr. Scholl's, AmLactin Foot Repair, Kerasal) specifically target
- Not for children under 12 without physician consultation

## Use Cases
Temporary relief of minor aches and pains of sore muscles and joints in the feet, via menthol's cooling counterirritant mechanism.

## Allergies / Warnings
Per label: for external use only; avoid contact with eyes or mucous membranes; do not apply to wounds or damaged skin; do not use with heating pads or other ointments/creams/sprays/liniments; discontinue if condition worsens or symptoms persist beyond 7 days. See [[topical-counterirritants]] for the FDA's 2012 burn-risk data at menthol concentrations above 3%.
