## Appears In

## Overview
Citracal is confirmed to use **calcium citrate** (200–250mg elemental calcium per serving depending on formulation), distinguishing it from category-rival [[Caltrate]]'s calcium carbonate. This pass located a real, brand-specific comparative pharmacokinetic study: in 18 postmenopausal women given a single 500mg oral calcium dose, Citracal (calcium citrate) produced a **2.5-fold greater change in serum calcium AUC** and a **76% higher peak-basal variation** than Os-Cal (calcium carbonate) — a genuine, quantified brand-relevant absorption advantage, though the comparator was Os-Cal specifically, not [[Caltrate]] by name (though Caltrate uses the same carbonate salt class). Calcium citrate's absorption advantage is also clinically relevant because, unlike carbonate, it does not require stomach acid for dissolution — a real, mechanistically meaningful difference for older adults or anyone on acid-reducing medication (e.g., PPIs, H2 blockers — see [[proton-pump-inhibitors]]), who may absorb carbonate-based calcium less reliably. Calcium Supplements (36%, category-leading share) is covered by [[calcium]].

## Pros
- **Real, brand-relevant comparative absorption data**: a controlled PK study found calcium citrate (Citracal's salt form) produced meaningfully higher serum calcium AUC than calcium carbonate (Os-Cal) in postmenopausal women — a genuine, quantified pharmacological advantage, not merely a marketing claim
- Calcium citrate does not require stomach acid for absorption, unlike calcium carbonate — a real practical advantage for older adults (who often have reduced stomach acid) or anyone taking acid-suppressing medication
- Category-leading share (36%) in this database's Calcium Supplements listing

## Cons
- The located comparative study used Os-Cal as the carbonate comparator, not [[Caltrate]] specifically by brand name — the calcium-citrate-vs-carbonate absorption advantage is a real, sourced ingredient-class finding, but this pass did not locate a Citracal-vs-Caltrate head-to-head trial by brand name
- Calcium citrate formulations are typically lower in elemental calcium per tablet (200–250mg) than carbonate formulations, meaning more tablets/servings may be needed to reach an equivalent total daily elemental-calcium target
- No independent third-party quality certification (USP Verified, NSF) was located specifically for Citracal in this pass
- See [[calcium]] for the ingredient's own general cautions (drug interactions with dolutegravir, levothyroxine, lithium, quinolone antibiotics via chelation)

## Use Cases
Calcium supplementation, particularly favorable for populations with reduced stomach acid (older adults, PPI/H2-blocker users) given calcium citrate's acid-independent absorption — see [[calcium]] for the general evidence base.

## Allergies / Warnings
See [[calcium]] — take calcium supplements 2+ hours apart from dolutegravir, levothyroxine, lithium, or quinolone antibiotics to avoid absorption interference (chelation).
