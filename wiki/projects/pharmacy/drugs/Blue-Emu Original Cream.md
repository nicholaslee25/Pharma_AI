## Overview
**Real, significant category-placement mismatch, actively flagged.** "Blue-Emu Original Cream" (also marketed as "Blue-Emu Original Super Strength Cream" / "Blue-Emu Maximum Pain Relief Cream," same underlying formulation family per [[Blue-Emu]]'s existing brand-page research) is confirmed via DailyMed to contain **trolamine salicylate 10%** as its active ingredient — a genuine, FDA-recognized OTC monograph topical analgesic drug (the same salicylate-class active used by non-lidocaine Aspercreme products), **not a homeopathic preparation of any kind**. This product carries none of the HPUS labeling, decimal/centesimal dilution notation, or "not evaluated by the FDA for homeopathic safety/efficacy" disclaimer that genuinely characterizes every other brand in this "Homeopathic Topical Analgesics" category ([[Boiron Arnicare Gel-Cream]], [[Theraworx Relief]], [[MagniLife Arnica Gel]]).

This is the same brand-identity discrepancy already documented on [[Blue-Emu]]'s existing page: the brand markets itself around emu oil (an inactive, non-therapeutic carrier ingredient in every confirmed SKU), and separately, this specific "Original/Maximum" cream formulation's actual mechanism (trolamine salicylate, a conventional pharmaceutical salicylate) has nothing to do with homeopathy at all — meaning this database's Pharmacy Times source data places a genuine, standard-strength FDA monograph drug in a category built around dilute, FDA-unevaluated homeopathic remedies. A consumer comparing "recommendation share" across this category's four brands would have no way to know, from the category listing alone, that three brands are homeopathic dilutions while the fourth (this one, at a substantial 25% share) is a conventional salicylate analgesic with real, if unremarkable, FDA monograph status.

No Blue-Emu-Original-Cream-specific clinical trial was located; see [[topical-counterirritants]] for the general salicylate-class evidence context (methyl salicylate specifically, not trolamine salicylate, is the better-studied salicylate in this database) and [[Blue-Emu]]'s existing page for the emu-oil-naming discrepancy.

## Use Cases
Temporary relief of minor aches and pains of muscles and joints associated with arthritis, backache, strains, sprains, and bruises — via trolamine salicylate's conventional salicylate-class analgesic mechanism, not any homeopathic dilution.

## Allergies / Warnings
Per label: allergy alert for aspirin/salicylate sensitivity — consult a doctor before use; do not bandage tightly, use with heating pads, or apply to wounds/damaged skin; avoid eyes/mucous membranes; ask a health professional before use if pregnant/nursing; discontinue if condition worsens, symptoms persist beyond 7 days, or redness/irritation develops. A California Proposition 65-style warning (per [[Blue-Emu]]'s existing research) also notes possible trace diethanolamine exposure.
