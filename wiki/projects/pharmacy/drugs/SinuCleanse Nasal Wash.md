## Appears In
Saline Nasal Moisturizers - 2%

## Overview
Confirmed: SinuCleanse's own FDA/DailyMed label lists **sodium bicarbonate USP 703mg + sodium chloride USP 2300mg per packet**, dissolved in water and used with a neti pot — a buffered saline **irrigation** format, mechanistically similar to the saline sprays elsewhere in this category but delivered as a larger-volume rinse rather than a mist. Per [[saline-nasal-sprays]], Cochrane reviews support saline irrigation specifically (not just sprays) for allergic rhinitis symptom-severity benefit and some acute-URI symptom benefit. No SinuCleanse-brand-specific clinical trial was identified beyond the general saline-irrigation evidence, which this delivery format is actually a closer match to than the mist-spray products elsewhere in this category.

## Pros
- Irrigation (neti pot) format is the delivery method most directly studied in the Cochrane reviews cited on [[saline-nasal-sprays]], arguably giving it the most direct evidentiary support among this category's formats
- Buffered (sodium bicarbonate + sodium chloride) formulation is designed not to sting or burn, per manufacturer
- Pre-measured, preservative-free packets simplify correct concentration mixing vs. a home-mixed solution

## Cons
- Requires a neti pot and correct water preparation (should use distilled/sterile/previously-boiled water per general nasal-irrigation safety practice) — a real usability/compliance barrier vs. a ready-to-use spray bottle
- Smallest share in the category tied with Xlear (2%) — likely reflects the extra preparation effort vs. spray convenience, not an evidence gap
- No brand-specific outcome trial exists beyond the general saline-irrigation class evidence

## Use Cases
Temporary relief of sinusitis/cold/flu/allergy symptoms (sneezing, runny nose, nasal stuffiness, post-nasal drip); removes inhaled irritants; promotes nasal/sinus drainage.

## Allergies / Warnings
Per label: adults/children 4+, 1-2 packets per neti-pot use up to every 2 hours as needed; under 4, consult a physician; stop use and ask a doctor if washing is uncomfortable or symptoms aren't relieved. General nasal-irrigation safety practice (not specific to this label) calls for distilled or previously-boiled/cooled water to avoid rare waterborne-organism risk.

## Symptoms Treated
Sinus congestion, post-nasal drip, nasal stuffiness from sinusitis, cold, flu, or allergies.
