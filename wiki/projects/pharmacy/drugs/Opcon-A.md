## Appears In
Ophthalmic Antihistamines/Decongestants - 3%

## Overview
Confirmed: Opcon-A's (Bausch & Lomb) own FDA/DailyMed label lists **pheniramine maleate 0.315% + naphazoline hydrochloride 0.02675%** — a near-identical combination formulation to [[Naphcon-A]] (naphazoline 0.025% + pheniramine 0.3%), just marginally different concentrations of the same two actives from a different manufacturer. Per [[ophthalmic-decongestants]], naphazoline carries the same well-documented rebound-hyperemia risk regardless of which brand delivers it. Smallest share in the category (3%). No Opcon-A-brand-specific clinical trial beyond general naphazoline/pheniramine pharmacology was identified.

## Pros
- Combination formulation treats both redness and itching in one product
- Essentially interchangeable with [[Naphcon-A]] at the ingredient level, giving consumers a backup option if one brand is unavailable
- Established, decades-long market history for both actives

## Cons
- **Rebound hyperemia risk** with regular/prolonged naphazoline use, per [[ophthalmic-decongestants]] — identical risk profile to [[Naphcon-A]], [[Clear Eyes]], and [[Visine]]
- Smallest recommendation share in the category (3%) despite being functionally near-identical to the third-largest brand ([[Naphcon-A]], 13%) — suggests brand recognition/marketing, not formulation difference, drives most of the share gap
- No brand-specific outcome trial exists beyond general class pharmacology

## Use Cases
Temporary relief of redness and itching due to pollen, ragweed, grass, animal hair/dander. Not intended for prolonged daily use.

## Allergies / Warnings
Per label: do not use for more than 72 hours without consulting a doctor; avoid in narrow-angle glaucoma or with MAOI use; discontinue if pain, vision changes, or symptoms worsen; rebound redness risk with overuse.

## Symptoms Treated
Eye redness (vasoconstrictor mechanism) and itching (antihistamine mechanism) from allergic conjunctivitis.
