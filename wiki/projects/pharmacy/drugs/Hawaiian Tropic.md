## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed: Hawaiian Tropic's FDA-registered product line is entirely **sunscreen** — chemical-filter combinations (avobenzone, homosalate, octisalate, octocrylene) across lotion/spray/aerosol/stick formats, plus a separate octocrylene + oxybenzone oil SKU and a "Sensitive Skin" avobenzone/octocrylene/oxybenzone spray. **No after-sun or lidocaine-containing Hawaiian Tropic product appears in DailyMed at all.** Retail listings advertise a "Burn Relief Gel with Lidocaine, Cool Aloe I.C.E." product with a secondary source claiming lidocaine 0.8% as the active ingredient (alongside aloe vera, allantoin/comfrey-root derivative, vitamins A/E, and menthol listed as inactive) — but **this could not be independently confirmed against an actual DailyMed/FDA Drug Facts label in this pass.** This is flagged honestly as unresolved: it may be a real product simply not indexed in DailyMed (which isn't fully comprehensive of every regional/private-label SKU), or a marketing claim not matching an actual FDA-registered listing — the same category-placement pattern seen with [[Banana Boat]] (a sunscreen-identity brand appearing in a treatment category via an after-sun line), but here the after-sun product's exact ingredient content remains genuinely unverified rather than confirmed-absent.

## Pros
- Sunscreen line: confirmed FDA Drug Facts labels across multiple formats and both single- and combination-filter formulations
- If the retail-claimed lidocaine-based Burn Relief Gel formulation is accurate, it would offer genuine analgesic action (lidocaine, the lower-methemoglobinemia-risk anesthetic per [[topical-anesthetics]]) beyond aloe alone — a meaningfully different and more substantiated product than [[Banana Boat]]'s confirmed-non-drug after-sun line, if the claim holds up

## Cons
- The specific after-sun product driving the 5% Sun Burn Relief share could not be confirmed against a primary FDA source — neither its existence as a genuine FDA-registered drug nor its exact active-ingredient content is settled in this pass
- If the lidocaine claim is accurate, menthol is reportedly present but classified as inactive — a similar "camphor/menthol below active-ingredient threshold or reclassified as inactive" pattern seen with [[Nature's Willow Bug Bite Balm]]'s camphor
- No brand-specific clinical trial for either the sunscreen or after-sun product lines was identified
- Comfrey-root-derived allantoin, per the retail ingredient list, is worth noting: comfrey extracts have a separate, real safety literature around pyrrolizidine alkaloid hepatotoxicity in other contexts (oral/large-scale topical comfrey use) — not independently investigated here, and allantoin itself (as opposed to whole comfrey extract) is generally regarded as a benign skin-conditioning agent, but the ingredient's botanical sourcing is worth flagging for completeness

## Use Cases
Sunscreen line: broad-spectrum UV protection to prevent sunburn. After-sun line (unconfirmed formulation): marketed for post-sun-exposure pain/itch relief and moisturizing.
