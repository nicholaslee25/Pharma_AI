## Overview
Exergen is the company most directly associated with **temporal artery thermometry** as a technology (its "TemporalScanner" product line) — meaning [[digital-thermometers]]'s existing Geijer et al. 2016 meta-analysis (37 articles, 5,026 participants, PMID 27033957), already cited on that page for temporal artery thermometers generally, is directly and specifically relevant to this brand rather than merely analogous. That review found: pooled mean difference -0.19°C vs. core-temperature reference (wide 95% limits of agreement, -1.16 to +0.77°C), pooled sensitivity for fever detection 0.72 (95% CI 0.61-0.81), specificity 0.94 (95% CI 0.87-0.97), with a trend toward underestimating temperature specifically in febrile patients — the population where accuracy matters most. The review's own authors concluded temporal artery thermometers are "not sufficiently accurate to replace rectal/bladder/invasive reference methods" and perform similarly to (not better than) tympanic thermometers, explicitly calling both methods "inaccurate" in their own words. No separate, additional Exergen-brand-specific study beyond this technology-level evidence was located in this pass — but because Exergen is essentially synonymous with commercial temporal artery thermometry, this is a closer brand-to-evidence match than most other brands in this database receive.

## Pros
- High specificity (0.94, 95% CI 0.87-0.97) per the Geijer 2016 review — a temporal artery reading that comes back normal/afebrile is relatively trustworthy
- Non-invasive, fast, forehead-swipe measurement technique, well suited to home use across ages including infants where rectal/oral methods are less practical
- Directly and specifically covered by name-brand-level evidence (rather than only generically similar technology), a genuinely closer evidence match than for most brands in this category

## Cons
- **Real, quantified accuracy limitation directly applicable to this brand:** moderate sensitivity (0.72) means roughly 1 in 4 true fevers may be missed, with a documented tendency to specifically underestimate temperature in febrile patients — the exact population where a missed fever matters most clinically
- The review authors' own conclusion states temporal artery thermometers are not accurate enough to replace rectal/bladder reference methods, and are no better than tympanic thermometers like [[Braun ThermoScan]] despite different technology — this is the review's explicit finding, not an inference
- Smallest share (9%) among the top four thermometer brands, though whether this reflects the accuracy limitations above or other market factors was not established in this pass

## Use Cases
Fast, non-contact-adjacent (forehead-swipe) temperature measurement for fever screening across all ages including infants.

## Allergies / Warnings
Not a drug — no allergy profile. Given the documented tendency to underestimate fever, a normal/afebrile temporal artery reading in a clinically ill-appearing patient (especially an infant) should be confirmed with a more accurate reference method (e.g. rectal) rather than assumed definitive, per the Geijer 2016 findings on [[digital-thermometers]].
