## Overview
**Major mismatched-category finding.** Beano's active ingredient is **alpha-galactosidase**, a dietary-supplement enzyme (not an FDA-approved drug — filed on DailyMed as a dietary supplement listing, delivering 600 FCC GalU per serving) derived from Aspergillus niger. It breaks down **galactooligosaccharides (GOS)** — the complex sugars in beans, cruciferous vegetables, and whole grains that cause gas because humans universally, naturally lack this enzyme. **This is mechanistically unrelated to lactose intolerance**: lactose is broken down by lactase (see [[lactase]]), a completely different enzyme targeting a completely different sugar. Beano's own confirmed mechanism gives it **no plausible pathway to relieve lactose-intolerance symptoms**, yet this database files it under "Lactose Intolerance Products" at a real, non-trivial 13% recommendation share — a genuine mismatch a pharmacist recommending Beano for lactose intolerance specifically would be recommending a product with no mechanistic basis for that indication.

## Pros
- Genuinely addresses its own actual target (GOS-related gas from beans/vegetables) via a real, if dated, evidence base: two small double-blind controlled trials (n=39 total, mid-1990s) found significantly reduced gas symptoms with a bean meal
- Non-systemic, enzyme-based mechanism with a favorable general safety profile (comparable in principle to lactase — see [[lactase]])
- Taken at the start of a meal, offering genuine preventive (not just after-the-fact) gas relief for its actual target foods

## Cons
- **No mechanistic basis for treating lactose intolerance specifically** — alpha-galactosidase targets galactooligosaccharides, not lactose; a consumer using Beano for dairy-related gas/bloating (as this database's "Lactose Intolerance Products" 13% share implies real-world pharmacist recommendations for that exact use) would need lactase (e.g. [[lactase]]/Lactaid) instead, not this product
- **Not an FDA-approved OTC drug** — filed as a dietary supplement, same regulatory-pathway distinction already flagged for DiaResQ elsewhere in this database's Gastrointestinal section
- Evidence base is genuinely thin: only two small trials (n=39 combined) from the mid-1990s, with no larger/more rigorous confirmatory studies located in this pass, despite a real 7-13% real-world recommendation share
- No dedicated alpha-galactosidase ingredient page exists in this database — a genuine gap, since this ingredient's evidence/mechanism is distinct enough from both [[simethicone]] and [[lactase]] to warrant separate treatment rather than folding into either

## Use Cases
Prevention of gas/bloating specifically from beans, cruciferous vegetables (broccoli, cabbage), and whole grains — via breakdown of galactooligosaccharides before they reach colonic bacteria. **NOT an effective treatment for lactose-intolerance-related gas/bloating from dairy** despite this database's category placement implying otherwise.

## Allergies / Warnings
No FDA-mandated Drug Facts warnings located (dietary supplement, not an FDA-monograph drug). General enzyme-supplement caution applies; no significant safety signal identified in this pass, consistent with [[lactase]]'s similarly benign supplement-enzyme profile.

## Symptoms Treated
Gas, bloating from bean/vegetable/whole-grain galactooligosaccharides — genuinely distinct from, and not effective for, lactose-intolerance-related symptoms despite this database's dual-category placement.
