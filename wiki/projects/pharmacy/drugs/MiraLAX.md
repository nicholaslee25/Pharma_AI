## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed: MiraLAX (PEG-3350, 17g/dose) matches [[peg-3350]]'s existing representative-agent data exactly — that ingredient page was already built directly around this brand's own DailyMed label. Category-dominant (63% share) in Laxatives, Nonfiber; second-largest in Stool Softeners (18%), which [[peg-3350]] already notes likely reflects PEG-3350 being loosely grouped with "stool softening" in consumer/pharmacist framing despite being mechanistically an osmotic laxative, not a surfactant stool softener like docusate. **Real brand-family finding (added this pass):** MiraFIBER, sold under this same brand family (researched separately in this database's Laxatives Bulk/Fiber category), is inulin-based — mechanistically and chemically unrelated to PEG-3350 despite the shared "Mira-" brand prefix, a real brand-confusion risk for a consumer assuming the two share potency/evidence.

## Pros
- Confirmed FDA label matches [[peg-3350]]'s existing sourcing exactly — no discrepancy found
- Category-dominant share (63%) in Laxatives, Nonfiber
- Per [[peg-3350]]'s own Efficacy Evidence: a randomized, placebo-controlled, double-blind trial (n=203) found significantly (p<0.0001) less hardened stool and reduced exertion — among the better brand-specific evidence bases in this database's Gastrointestinal section
- Minimal systemic absorption supports a favorable safety profile even in pregnancy (AAFP considers it drug-of-choice for chronic constipation in pregnancy per [[peg-3350]])

## Cons
- Per [[peg-3350]]'s own Key Safety Data: a real, FDA-reported pediatric neuropsychiatric signal exists with prolonged use (seizures, tremors, obsessive-compulsive behaviors, mood swings, tics) — directly relevant given how commonly PEG-3350 is used off-label in children despite lacking FDA approval below age 17
- Onset is slower (1-3 days) than saline laxatives like Phillips' Milk of Magnesia (½-6 hours) or Fleet enemas (1-5 minutes) — not a same-day-relief option, a real use-case distinction from other Laxatives, Nonfiber category-mates
- Should not be combined with starch-based thickeners (used for dysphagia) per [[peg-3350]]'s own label warning — a specific interaction not shared by most other laxatives in this database
- No MiraLAX-brand-specific RCT beyond the trial already cited on [[peg-3350]] was independently located in this pass
- Real, separate brand-confusion risk: MiraFIBER (same brand family, inulin-based) is mechanistically unrelated despite the shared brand name — see [[Mirafiber]]

## Use Cases
Relief of occasional/chronic constipation via a slower-onset, gentler osmotic mechanism than saline laxatives or enemas — per [[peg-3350]], the preferred agent for chronic constipation/disimpaction given its favorable adverse-effect profile.

## Allergies / Warnings
Per [[peg-3350]]: contraindicated in known/suspected bowel obstruction, appendicitis, inflammatory bowel disease, perforated bowel; do not combine with starch-based thickeners; real pediatric neuropsychiatric signal with prolonged off-label use in children warrants monitoring.

## Symptoms Treated
Occasional and chronic constipation — via the osmotic mechanism detailed in [[peg-3350]], genuinely slower-onset but with a favorable overall safety profile relative to saline laxatives/enemas.
