## Appears In
Magnesium Supplements - 13%

## Overview
SlowMag Mg is confirmed to use **magnesium chloride** (combined with calcium carbonate) in an enteric-coated tablet — a formulation specifically designed around gastrointestinal tolerability: the enteric coating allows the tablet to bypass the stomach and dissolve gradually in the intestine (the "Slow" in the brand name), which independent sourcing confirms reduces nausea and cramping relative to formulations that dissolve immediately in the stomach. Magnesium chloride is explicitly named on this database's own [[magnesium]] evidence page as one of the higher-bioavailability forms (alongside aspartate, citrate, and lactate), a real, meaningful contrast to category-leading [[Mag-Ox 400]] (46% share), which uses magnesium oxide — independently documented as poorly absorbed at typical supplemental doses (15–30%) and "primarily useful as a laxative" rather than for efficient systemic magnesium repletion. This makes SlowMag Mg, despite its smaller 13% share, arguably the better-absorbed choice within this specific category by this database's own ingredient-page criteria.

## Pros
- **Confirmed use of magnesium chloride, one of the higher-bioavailability forms named on this database's own [[magnesium]] evidence page** — a real, meaningful contrast to category-leading [[Mag-Ox 400]]'s magnesium oxide
- Enteric-coated, slow-dissolving tablet design specifically engineered to reduce GI side effects (nausea, cramping) relative to standard-release magnesium tablets — a real, sourced formulation rationale, not just a brand name
- Co-formulated with calcium, addressing two mineral needs in one product (though this also means users should track total calcium intake across all supplements to avoid exceeding the Tolerable Upper Intake Level)

## Cons
- Smaller share (13%) than the category-leading but comparatively less-absorbable [[Mag-Ox 400]] (46%) — pharmacist recommendation volume does not track absorption quality in this category, per this database's two-layer rule
- No SlowMag-brand-specific clinical trial was located — the absorption-advantage claim is supported by general magnesium-chloride-vs-oxide bioavailability sourcing, not a SlowMag-named RCT
- The added calcium carbonate component carries its own absorption caveat (requires stomach acid for dissolution — see [[Caltrate]]/[[calcium]]) and its own drug-interaction profile (dolutegravir, levothyroxine, lithium, quinolone antibiotics) that magnesium-only products don't carry
- No independent third-party quality certification (USP Verified, NSF) was located for SlowMag Mg in this pass
