## Appears In
Therapeutic Mouthwashes/Oral Rinses - 4%

## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed: Cepacol Antibacterial Multi-Protection Mouthwash (Reckitt Benckiser LLC) lists **cetylpyridinium chloride (CPC) 0.05%** as its active ingredient, labeled antigingivitis/antiplaque — kills germs that cause bad breath, reduces plaque, and freshens breath. This is a different mechanism from the plain "Cepacol" lozenge entries elsewhere in this database (benzocaine-based, per [[topical-anesthetics]]'s existing "Cepacol — confirmed benzocaine 7.5-15mg per lozenge" note) — meaning "Cepacol" as a brand spans both a topical-anesthetic lozenge line and a CPC-based antigingivitis mouthwash line depending on product format.

## Pros
- CPC is a well-studied antiplaque/antigingivitis quaternary ammonium compound; per general dental literature (search-located systematic reviews), CPC-based rinses show measurable plaque/gingivitis reduction, generally positioned as intermediate in strength between essential-oil rinses and chlorhexidine
- Same active-ingredient class and concentration range as Crest Scope Original (0.07%) and SmartMouth Clinical DDS (0.05%), giving a consumer multiple brand options for the same evidence-backed mechanism
- Clear labeled purpose (antigingivitis/antiplaque) distinct from Cepacol's separate benzocaine lozenge line, avoiding the anesthetic/antigingivitis mechanism confusion

## Cons
- No brand-specific (as opposed to general CPC ingredient-class) clinical trial evidence was identified for this specific Cepacol formulation
- "Cepacol" spans a benzocaine-based oral-anesthetic lozenge line (see [[topical-anesthetics]]) and this CPC-based antigingivitis mouthwash line — materially different mechanisms and purposes under one brand name, a pattern now well-documented across this database
- Restricted to ages 12+ with supervision for ages 6-11, not for use under 6 — consistent with other CPC-based rinses in this category

## Use Cases
Daily antigingivitis/antiplaque maintenance and bad-breath control via cetylpyridinium chloride.

## Allergies / Warnings
Per label: adults/children 12+, swish 20mL twice daily for 30 seconds, spit out; keep out of reach of children under 6; seek medical help/Poison Control if accidentally swallowed beyond rinsing use.

## Symptoms Treated
Plaque accumulation, gingivitis, and oral malodor via cetylpyridinium chloride's antibacterial action — no anesthetic/pain-relief effect (unlike Cepacol's separate lozenge line).
