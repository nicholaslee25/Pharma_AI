## Appears In
Children's Multivitamins - 10%

## Overview
Confirmed via manufacturer labeling: "One A Day Kids" spans **at least three distinct gummy formulations** — the base "Multivitamin" (Vitamin C, D, E, Niacin, B6, Folate, B12, Biotin, Iodine, Zinc — no Vitamin A, no iron), "Multivitamin with Iron" (adds ferrous fumarate to the same base panel), and "Multivitamin with Probiotic" (adds Vitamin A, pantothenic acid, and Bacillus subtilis DE111 probiotic strain, but drops the iron). This is the same one-brand-multiple-formulations pattern already flagged for Flintstones and Enfamil Poly-Vi-Sol in this same category — here with three distinct SKUs rather than two, and this database's category-mapping data ("One A Day Kids," 10% share) does not specify which formulation underlies it.

## Pros
- Broad product-line coverage allows targeting a specific nutritional gap (iron-deficient diets, or added probiotic/digestive-health support) if the correct SKU is selected
- The Probiotic variant's Bacillus subtilis DE111 strain draws on a different probiotic species than the Lactobacillus rhamnosus GG used by Culturelle Kids elsewhere in this category, though this database's [[probiotics]] evidence page notes species/strain matters significantly for actual efficacy and no dedicated Bacillus subtilis DE111 evidence was reviewed in this pass

## Cons
- **Three distinct formulations under one "One A Day Kids" umbrella name, with no SKU-level specificity in this database's category-mapping data** — the same brand-name/SKU ambiguity problem recurring throughout this Pediatrics research pass, now confirmed for a third children's-multivitamin brand (after Flintstones and Enfamil)
- If the iron-free base or Probiotic SKU applies: a caregiver assuming "One A Day Kids" provides iron across the whole product line (a reasonable assumption from the shared name) would be incorrect for two of the three confirmed formulations
- Per [[multivitamins]]'s general evidence review: broad disease-prevention claims for multivitamin use are not established in a large modern adult RCT; no pediatric-specific outcome trial exists for any One A Day Kids formulation
- The Probiotic variant's specific strain (Bacillus subtilis DE111) was not independently evidence-reviewed against [[probiotics]]'s AAD-prevention-focused evidence base in this pass — a real gap, since that page explicitly cautions that probiotic efficacy is species/strain-dependent and does not transfer automatically across products

## Use Cases
General pediatric nutrient-gap supplementation (base formulation); dietary iron supplementation (With Iron variant); combined vitamin and digestive/immune probiotic support (With Probiotic variant).

## Allergies / Warnings
Keep out of reach of children — iron-containing gummies (With Iron variant) carry the same fatal-overdose-risk-if-ingested-in-bulk concern as any pediatric iron supplement. Verify which specific SKU matches the intended nutritional need.

## Symptoms Treated
General nutrient-gap correction; not a treatment for any specific disease or symptom.

## Brand-Specific Alignment Check
**New finding, added to both this page and [[multivitamins]]:** One A Day Kids is the third pediatric multivitamin brand in this single category (after Flintstones and Enfamil Poly-Vi-Sol) confirmed to span multiple, materially different formulations under one umbrella name — reinforcing that this specific data-quality pattern (brand-name tracking without SKU specificity) is now a category-wide phenomenon in Children's Multivitamins, not isolated to one or two brands.
