## Overview
**Note on brand scope:** this page covers Vicks-branded digital thermometers specifically (this database's Digital Thermometers category entry) — a separate, unrelated Vicks product line (VapoRub, VapoCOOL, cough/cold products) is covered elsewhere in this database under its own brand pages and is not addressed here. Confirmed via an FDA 510(k) clearance document (K180131, Kaz USA Inc., a Helen of Troy company — Vicks' manufacturer/parent): the **Vicks VDT972 RapidRead Digital Thermometer** underwent a prospective, multicenter, non-inferiority pivotal clinical study (165 subjects, 36% febrile, comparing device readings to the Welch Allyn SureTemp Plus 690 reference "gold standard" thermometer). The study concluded the device was **equivalent to the predicate thermometer for Clinical Accuracy across all age groups combined, each age group individually, and specifically among febrile subjects**, meeting its accuracy endpoints (Clinical Bias/Limits of Agreement and Clinical Repeatability) at the required 95% confidence level. Second-largest share (22%) of this category. No brand-specific data was found for other Vicks thermometer models beyond the VDT972.

## Pros
- Real FDA 510(k) clearance documentation with a pivotal clinical non-inferiority study specifically including febrile subjects (the population where fever-detection accuracy matters most) — among the more rigorously documented brand-specific clinical evidence found in this category
- Compared directly against a well-established reference device (Welch Allyn SureTemp Plus 690), a legitimate gold-standard comparator
- Second-largest market share (22%), reflecting broad consumer/pharmacist familiarity

## Cons
- The pivotal study evaluated a specific model (VDT972 RapidRead) — this data should not be assumed to apply identically to every Vicks thermometer SKU without independent confirmation
- Sample size (165 subjects) is modest relative to some other diagnostic-device evidence in this database (e.g. the Cochrane COVID-antigen reviews' 100,000+ sample sizes on [[at-home-covid-tests]])
- No information on the specific measurement site/technology (oral, forehead/temporal, or other) for the VDT972 was confirmed in the sources found — a real gap given [[digital-thermometers]]'s emphasis that measurement site materially affects accuracy and normal-range interpretation

## Use Cases
At-home body temperature measurement for fever screening in adults and children (age ranges tested per the pivotal study not further broken out in sources found).
