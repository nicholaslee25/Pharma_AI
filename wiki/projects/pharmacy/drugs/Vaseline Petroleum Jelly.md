## Appears In
Incontinence-Related Skin Protectants - 3%

## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed (labeler: Conopco Inc. d/b/a Unilever): "Vaseline Pure Petroleum Jelly" lists **white petrolatum USP 100%** as its sole active ingredient, purpose "skin protectant" — the classic, plain occlusive formulation, and (per [[petrolatum]]'s existing brand entry) the product in this database most directly plausible as matching the exact formulation tested in petrolatum's pivotal RCT (Smack et al. 1996, JAMA, 922 patients, plain white petrolatum vs. bacitracin for postprocedure wound care). This is a materially different, much higher-concentration formulation than [[Vaseline]]'s bare brand-file entry (Vaseline Intensive Care lotion, white petrolatum 30% blended with water/glycerin/emulsifiers) — the two "Vaseline" entries in this database should not be treated as interchangeable despite the shared brand name.

Correctly filed in this "Incontinence-Related Skin Protectants" category — plain petrolatum's occlusive moisture-barrier mechanism is exactly the intended use case, no category-placement discrepancy found (a contrast to some of this database's other petrolatum-containing products, e.g. [[A&D]]'s mismatched "Topical Antibiotics/Antiseptics" filing).

## Pros
- Confirmed 100% white petrolatum — the simplest, most concentrated, and (per [[petrolatum]]) most directly RCT-supported formulation of any petrolatum-based product in this database
- Petrolatum's occlusive mechanism reduces transepidermal water loss by ~99%, a strong, well-characterized effect directly relevant to protecting incontinence-irritated skin from further moisture/friction damage
- Very clean allergy profile per the Smack 1996 trial (0% allergic contact dermatitis in the petrolatum arm vs. 0.9% in the bacitracin comparator arm) — a meaningful safety advantage for a product intended for frequent, prolonged use on already-compromised skin
- Correctly filed category placement — no mechanism/category mismatch

## Cons
- No brand-specific (Vaseline-branded) clinical trial exists; the Smack 1996 RCT, while plausibly the closest real-world match, tested generic "white petrolatum," not independently confirmed to be this exact Unilever product
- Purely occlusive/barrier mechanism — provides no antimicrobial, anti-inflammatory, or antifungal action, so it does not address secondary infection or candidiasis that can complicate incontinence-associated dermatitis if it progresses
- Lowest recommendation share (3%) in this category, behind more heavily marketed combination products (Calmoseptine, A+D, Desitin) that add zinc oxide/lanolin/menthol to the same basic occlusive principle

## Use Cases
Temporary protection of minor cuts, scrapes, and burns; temporary protection and relief of chapped or cracked skin; protection from drying effects of wind/cold weather — applied here as a moisture barrier for incontinence-associated skin irritation.

## Allergies / Warnings
Per label: external use only; discontinue if condition worsens or does not improve. Per [[petrolatum]]: generally very clean allergy profile, among the safest options in this category from an allergy-risk standpoint.

## Symptoms Treated
Skin irritation and breakdown associated with incontinence, via petrolatum's occlusive protective-barrier mechanism.
