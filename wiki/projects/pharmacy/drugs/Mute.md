## Overview
Confirmed: Mute (Rhinomed) is an **FDA-cleared internal nasal dilator** — a pair of flexible inserts that fit inside the nostrils to dilate the nasal valve from within, mechanically distinct from an external adhesive strip like [[Breathe Right]]. Per [[snore-aid-devices]]'s cited 2016 meta-analysis, internal nasal dilators showed a slight apnea-index reduction (−4.87 events/hr) in a subgroup analysis, compared to essentially no benefit for external nasal dilators (+0.64 events/hr) — a real, if modest, mechanistic edge for this device subtype. Manufacturer-sponsored trial data (Royal Victorian Eye and Ear Hospital) reports ~38% more nasal airflow vs. nasal strips, and a company white paper claims 75% of users experienced reduced snoring; these manufacturer-reported figures are a lower-confidence evidence tier than the independent meta-analysis, though directionally consistent with it. Smallest share in the category (2%) despite this comparatively favorable evidence position relative to the category-leading external strips.

## Pros
- Belongs to the internal-dilator subtype that outperformed external strips in the cited meta-analysis's apnea-index subgroup comparison
- FDA-cleared OTC device with a straightforward, non-adhesive mechanism (no skin contact/irritation risk like adhesive strips)
- Reusable per unit (each dilator usable for a period before replacement, per manufacturer)

## Cons
- The manufacturer's specific efficacy claims (75% reduced snoring, 73% bed-partner-reported improvement) are self-reported/company-sponsored, not independently replicated in the peer-reviewed literature reviewed in this pass
- Smallest recommendation share in the category (2%) despite a comparatively favorable evidence position — market awareness, not evidence, appears to drive [[Breathe Right]]'s dominant share instead
- No large independent RCT specific to this exact product (beyond the cited meta-analysis's internal-dilator subgroup, which pools multiple internal-dilator products, not Mute specifically) was identified

## Use Cases
Snoring reduction and nasal airflow improvement via internal nasal dilation; not a substitute for diagnosed-OSA treatment.

## Allergies / Warnings
Minor nasal discomfort possible on initial use (general internal-nasal-dilator consideration); no major contraindications identified. Not a substitute for medical evaluation of suspected sleep apnea.

## Symptoms Treated
Snoring, reduced nasal airflow/congestion contributing to snoring.
