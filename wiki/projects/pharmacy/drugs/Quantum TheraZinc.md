## Appears In
Zinc Lozenges - 8%

## Overview
No FDA drug label was located — Quantum TheraZinc is marketed as a **dietary supplement**. Per manufacturer data, formulations vary: **zinc gluconate 14mg or 7mg per lozenge**, some variants adding a "Proprietary Premium Herbal Blend" (slippery elm bark, eastern larch arabinogalactan, marshmallow root, 13.5mg) and/or elderberry. This confirms the earlier "see zinc.md" pointer with a concrete dose range. The 14mg variant is close to Cold-EEZE's confirmed 13.3mg/lozenge, making it a reasonably direct comparator for [[zinc]]'s Cochrane evidence; the 7mg variant delivers roughly half that dose.

## Pros
- The higher-dose (14mg) variant is dosed close to Cold-EEZE's confirmed 13.3mg zinc/lozenge, making [[zinc]]'s Cochrane review evidence a reasonably direct comparison for that specific SKU
- Manufacturer directions specify a clear dosing schedule (every 2-4 hours, max 6 lozenges/day adults, 3/day ages 12-17) with an explicit pediatric consult recommendation <12y — more detailed self-imposed dosing guidance than some competitors provide despite lacking an FDA label
- Herbal blend (slippery elm, marshmallow root) ingredients have some traditional demulcent/soothing use, similar in spirit to Luden's pectin, though not independently evidence-checked here

## Cons
- Two different zinc doses (7mg vs. 14mg) exist across variants under the same brand name — a consumer must check the specific product to know their actual zinc intake, similar to the multi-SKU ambiguity already flagged for other brands in this batch (Zicam, Alka-Seltzer Plus)
- As a dietary supplement, no FDA drug label exists to independently verify these manufacturer-reported doses against a regulatory document
- The proprietary herbal blend's specific ingredient doses and evidence base were not independently verified in this pass

## Use Cases
Zinc-based cold-symptom support, potentially combined with soothing herbal ingredients and/or elderberry depending on the specific variant.

## Allergies / Warnings
Manufacturer directions: max 6 lozenges/day (adults), 3/day (ages 12-17); consult a health professional before use in children <12y. No FDA-mandated warnings panel since this is a dietary supplement.

## Symptoms Treated
Cold symptoms, sore throat soothing (via herbal blend in some variants) — not FDA-labeled claims.

## Brand-Specific Alignment Check
No discrepancy with [[zinc]] — the 14mg variant's dose is close to the ingredient page's Cold-EEZE reference dose; the 7mg variant is meaningfully lower and shouldn't be assumed equivalent.
