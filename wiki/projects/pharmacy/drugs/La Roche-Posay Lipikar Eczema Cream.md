## Appears In
Eczema Care/Relief Products - 3%

## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed (labeler: Cosmetique Active Production, part of L'Oréal's dermo-cosmetics division): **colloidal oatmeal 1%** as the sole active ingredient (purpose: skin protectant), consistent with the exact same active ingredient/concentration as [[Aveeno Eczema Therapy]] and [[Eczema Honey]]. Two closely-named DailyMed SKUs exist — "Lipikar Eczema Relief" and "Lipikar Eczema Soothing Relief" — both colloidal oatmeal 1% oatmeal cream, OTC Monograph Drug status approved 2017-11-21.

**Real regional clinical-claims mismatch, actively flagged.** Searching for La Roche-Posay Lipikar clinical evidence surfaces prominent claims — "82% of patients... don't relapse vs. only 26% with standard care," "tested on 14,180 patients in 29 clinical studies" — but these figures belong specifically to **"Lipikar Baume AP+M,"** a related but different product marketed by La Roche-Posay's non-US (EU/Middle East) product line, not to the US "Lipikar Eczema Relief Cream" actually sold domestically and tracked by Pharmacy Times in this category. The US product's own marketing makes only general, uncited claims ("clinically shown to relieve itchy, irritated skin") without referencing that 29-study/14,180-patient dataset. A consumer or pharmacist encountering the "14,180 patients, 29 studies" statistic online could easily attribute it to the US product actually being recommended here, when it in fact describes a different SKU in a different regulatory market with an unconfirmed relationship (shared "Lipikar" family branding, not confirmed identical formulation) to the US Eczema Relief Cream.

No La-Roche-Posay-Lipikar-Eczema-Relief-brand-specific (US SKU) clinical trial was located in this pass beyond the general marketing claims above and the ingredient-level evidence on [[colloidal-oatmeal]].

## Pros
- Confirmed FDA OTC skin-protectant active ingredient (colloidal oatmeal 1%) matching the category leader's ([[Aveeno Eczema Therapy]]) concentration, backed by [[colloidal-oatmeal]]'s ingredient-level evidence (Lisante 2017 non-inferiority RCT)
- National Eczema Association acceptance listed for this product
- Backed by a large dermo-cosmetics manufacturer (L'Oréal/Cosmetique Active) with substantial general dermatological-testing infrastructure across its product family, even though the specific large-scale study numbers circulating do not belong to this exact US SKU

## Cons
- **The widely-cited "82% non-relapse" / "14,180 patients, 29 clinical studies" figures belong to "Lipikar Baume AP+M," a different, non-US product** — not to the actual US "Lipikar Eczema Relief Cream" tracked in this database, a genuine brand-family evidence-attribution risk flagged here rather than silently resolved
- No US-SKU-specific peer-reviewed clinical trial was located; the only concrete study-level evidence available is the general [[colloidal-oatmeal]] ingredient page's data (which used a different manufacturer's 1% formulation in its pivotal RCT)
- Smallest recommendation share (3%) among the colloidal-oatmeal-based competitors in this category, despite prominent brand-family marketing claims
- Two near-identically-named SKUs ("Eczema Relief" vs. "Eczema Soothing Relief") both exist on DailyMed with the same active ingredient — a minor but real source of naming ambiguity for consumers comparing products

## Use Cases
Temporary relief of minor skin irritation and itching due to eczema and rashes, via colloidal oatmeal's skin-protectant mechanism (see [[colloidal-oatmeal]]).

## Allergies / Warnings
Per label: external use only; avoid eyes; discontinue use if condition worsens or symptoms persist beyond 7 days; keep out of reach of children, contact Poison Control if swallowed. Per [[colloidal-oatmeal]]: oat/cereal-grain allergy carries a theoretical (uncommon) contact-allergy risk. Manufacturer states the product is steroid- and antibiotic-free and suitable for children 3 years and up (not independently re-verified against a full pediatric-safety data set in this pass).

## Symptoms Treated
Itching and minor skin irritation from eczema and rashes, via colloidal oatmeal's skin-protectant mechanism.
