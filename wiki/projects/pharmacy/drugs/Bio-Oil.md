## Overview
**No FDA Drug Facts panel or FDA-monograph active ingredient was found for Bio-Oil.** Its marketed "active ingredient" is **"PurCellin Oil"** — a trademarked proprietary oil-blend name (not a single FDA-recognized chemical entity) — alongside a mix of plant oils and vitamins (calendula, lavender, rosemary, chamomile oils; vitamins A and E). Bio-Oil is marketed and regulated as a **cosmetic**, not an OTC drug, consistent with no DailyMed listing being found. This places it in the same cosmetic-not-drug category already documented for several brands in the adjacent Lip Balms subcategory ([[e.l.f.]], [[Evolution of Smooth (eos)]]).

This pass covers **Scar Treatments only** (10% share); Bio-Oil's much larger 24% share in Stretch Mark Treatments is deferred to that subcategory's dedicated pass.

No Bio-Oil-brand-specific clinical trial with a rigorous, independent design was located in this pass (the company cites its own consumer-perception studies in marketing materials, not evaluated here as independent clinical evidence).

## Pros
- Long-standing, globally recognized brand (originally South African, "Bio-Oil" trademark dates to the 1980s) with broad retail availability
- Plant-oil-based formulation avoids the drug-active-ingredient irritation risks (chemical sunscreen filters, camphor, silicone-specific reactions) found elsewhere in adjacent subcategories
- No known major safety signal identified in this pass

## Cons
- **No FDA-regulated active ingredient or Drug Facts panel found** — "PurCellin Oil" is proprietary marketing terminology, not a verifiable single active compound with independent evidence; this brand's presence in an OTC "Scar Treatments" drug category is not backed, on the evidence located here, by any FDA drug-monograph claim
- No independent, non-company-sponsored clinical trial was located evaluating Bio-Oil's efficacy for scar appearance
- Category leader Mederma (56% share) is a confirmed OTC drug (allantoin); Bio-Oil's second-place 10% share rests on comparatively weaker regulatory/evidentiary footing
- Fragrance/essential-oil content (lavender, rosemary, chamomile) carries a real, if generally mild, contact-sensitization risk not present in fragrance-free competitors

## Use Cases
Cosmetic moisturizing/appearance product marketed for improving the look of scars (and separately, stretch marks) — no confirmed FDA drug claim.

## Allergies / Warnings
No FDA Drug Facts label exists to cite. Standard cosmetic-ingredient precautions apply: possible fragrance/essential-oil contact sensitization (lavender, rosemary, chamomile); patch-testing is reasonable for sensitive-skin users.

## Symptoms Treated
None confirmed via independent clinical evidence — marketed cosmetic claim of improved scar appearance, not a treated medical condition.
