## Overview
**No FDA Drug Facts panel or FDA-monograph active ingredient was found for Bio-Oil.** Its marketed "active ingredient" is **"PurCellin Oil"** — a trademarked proprietary oil-blend name (not a single FDA-recognized chemical entity) — alongside a mix of plant oils and vitamins (calendula, lavender, rosemary, chamomile oils; vitamins A and E). Bio-Oil is marketed and regulated as a **cosmetic**, not an OTC drug, consistent with no DailyMed listing being found. This places it in the same cosmetic-not-drug category already documented for several brands in the adjacent Lip Balms subcategory ([[e.l.f.]], [[Evolution of Smooth (eos)]]).

**Stretch Mark Treatments (24% share, this brand's larger of its two category appearances) — clinical evidence picture, researched 2026-08-27:** a company-commissioned, double-blind, placebo-controlled study (proDERM Institute for Applied Dermatological Research, 2010, n=38) reported 95% of participants showing a "positive outcome" after 15 days — but this is a contract-research-organization study commissioned by the manufacturer, not an independently initiated academic trial, and "positive outcome" is a vague, not clearly pre-specified endpoint. A genuinely independent, peer-reviewed study exists in the adjacent literature — Bielfeldt et al. 2018 (*International Journal of Cosmetic Science*, PMID 29094366), an observer-blind RCT (n=80) of "a cosmetic blend of safflower, olive and other plant oils" for scar/striae appearance — but this trial's exact brand identity was not confirmed to be Bio-Oil specifically in this pass, only that the tested formulation profile (plant-oil blend) resembles Bio-Oil's own composition. **A separate systematic review search reportedly found zero RCTs meeting rigorous inclusion criteria specifically naming the Bio-Oil brand** — a genuine, notable evidence-quality gap for a product holding a substantial 24% share of this subcategory's recommendations.

No independent (non-company-sponsored), Bio-Oil-brand-confirmed clinical trial was located in this pass for either the Scar Treatments or Stretch Mark Treatments use case.

- Long-standing, globally recognized brand (originally South African, "Bio-Oil" trademark dates to the 1980s) with broad retail availability
- Plant-oil-based formulation avoids the drug-active-ingredient irritation risks (chemical sunscreen filters, camphor, silicone-specific reactions) found elsewhere in adjacent subcategories
- No known major safety signal identified in this pass

## Cons
- **No FDA-regulated active ingredient or Drug Facts panel found** — "PurCellin Oil" is proprietary marketing terminology, not a verifiable single active compound with independent evidence; this brand's presence in an OTC "Scar Treatments" drug category is not backed, on the evidence located here, by any FDA drug-monograph claim
- No independent, non-company-sponsored clinical trial confirmed to be Bio-Oil-brand-specific was located evaluating its efficacy for scar or stretch-mark appearance
- Category leaders in both subcategories where Bio-Oil appears ([[Mederma]], 56% Scar Treatments; [[Mederma Stretch Marks]], 48% Stretch Mark Treatments) are confirmed genuine OTC drugs; Bio-Oil's shares in both (10% and 24% respectively) rest on comparatively weaker regulatory/evidentiary footing

- A separate systematic review reportedly found zero RCTs meeting rigorous inclusion criteria specifically naming the Bio-Oil brand, despite its substantial 24% share of Stretch Mark Treatments recommendations
- Fragrance/essential-oil content (lavender, rosemary, chamomile) carries a real, if generally mild, contact-sensitization risk not present in fragrance-free competitors

## Use Cases
Cosmetic moisturizing/appearance product marketed for improving the look of scars (and separately, stretch marks) — no confirmed FDA drug claim.

## Allergies / Warnings
No FDA Drug Facts label exists to cite. Standard cosmetic-ingredient precautions apply: possible fragrance/essential-oil contact sensitization (lavender, rosemary, chamomile); patch-testing is reasonable for sensitive-skin users.

## Symptoms Treated
