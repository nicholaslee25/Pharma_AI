## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed: Tylenol PM is **acetaminophen 500 mg + diphenhydramine HCl 25 mg** per caplet — resolving this page's prior hedge into a confirmed finding, matching [[acetaminophen]] and the newly built [[oral-diphenhydramine-sleep-aid]] (built in this pass, since no oral/sleep-aid diphenhydramine page previously existed in this database — only [[topical-diphenhydramine]] did). Use: temporary relief of occasional headaches and minor aches/pains WITH accompanying sleeplessness — a genuinely dual-symptom indication, not simply "pain reliever plus sedative" marketing. Category-dominant share (65%).

## Pros
- Confirmed FDA Drug Facts label combining two individually well-evidenced ingredients for their respective primary uses (acetaminophen for pain/fever per [[acetaminophen]])
- Addresses a real, common combined complaint (pain with sleeplessness) in a single product, avoiding the need to take two separate medications
- Acetaminophen's non-NSAID mechanism avoids the GI-bleed/cardiovascular risk that NSAID-based PM competitors (Advil PM, Aleve PM) carry

## Cons
- **The diphenhydramine sleep-aid component carries a real, guideline-level "avoid in older adults" flag** (AGS Beers Criteria, per [[oral-diphenhydramine-sleep-aid]]) — directly relevant since sleep-and-pain combination products are commonly reached for by older adults, exactly the population this guideline recommends avoid diphenhydramine due to anticholinergic burden and its association with falls, delirium, and dementia risk
- Independent systematic-review evidence describes diphenhydramine's OTC sleep-aid efficacy generally as lacking robust support — the sleep-aid half of this combination is less well-evidenced than the acetaminophen half
- Carries acetaminophen's full hepatotoxicity risk profile and the currently-unresolved FDA pregnancy/autism-ADHD label-change controversy documented on `drugs/Tylenol.md` — same active ingredient, same open regulatory question
- Combination-product acetaminophen overlap risk (per [[acetaminophen]]) is especially relevant here — this database's own continuation-source material specifically named Tylenol PM as an example to watch for overlapping acetaminophen exposure across the Tylenol brand family
- Label explicitly warns against combining with other diphenhydramine-containing products, warfarin, or sedatives without medical guidance, and warns drowsiness will occur (avoid driving/alcohol)

## Use Cases
Combined temporary relief of headache/minor aches and pains WITH accompanying occasional sleeplessness.

## Allergies / Warnings
Per label: liver damage risk if exceeding 4,000mg acetaminophen/24h or combined with alcohol/other acetaminophen products; acetaminophen may cause severe skin reactions; not for children under 12; avoid with other diphenhydramine products; do not use with warfarin or sedatives without doctor consultation; drowsiness will occur — avoid driving and alcohol; discontinue if sleeplessness persists beyond 2 weeks, pain exceeds 10 days, fever lasts over 3 days, or new symptoms develop.

## Symptoms Treated
Headache, minor aches and pains, and occasional sleeplessness accompanying pain.
