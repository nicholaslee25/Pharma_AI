## Overview
**Confirmed 2026-08-28 via DailyMed: Cystex is genuinely NOT phenazopyridine-based**, resolving this database's prior hedge on [[phenazopyridine]]. Its actual active ingredients are methenamine 162mg and sodium salicylate 162.5mg — methenamine is a urinary antibacterial (converts to formaldehyde in acidic urine, inhibiting bacterial growth) and sodium salicylate is a salicylate-class NSAID analgesic (same drug family as [[aspirin]], though a different specific salt). This is a genuinely different mechanism from every phenazopyridine-based competitor in this category (AZO, UriStat, Uricalm) — Cystex combines a mild antibacterial action with a salicylate pain reliever, rather than the azo-dye local-anesthetic mechanism those products use.

## Pros
- Methenamine's antibacterial mechanism is at least directionally aimed at bacterial growth itself, a mechanistic difference from phenazopyridine's purely symptomatic azo-dye analgesia — though this database did not independently verify a dedicated Cystex clinical trial demonstrating actual infection-clearance outcomes in this pass
- Does not carry phenazopyridine's specific urine-staining/methemoglobinemia/G6PD-hemolysis risk profile, since it uses a different active-ingredient class entirely

## Cons
- **No dedicated Cystex-brand RCT was located in this pass** — methenamine/sodium salicylate combination efficacy rests on the individual components' general pharmacology, not a Cystex-specific trial
- Sodium salicylate carries the same salicylate-class safety considerations as [[aspirin]] in principle (GI irritation, Reye syndrome risk in children/teens with viral illness, bleeding-risk interaction with anticoagulants) — this database did not find Cystex's own label independently re-stating these salicylate-class cautions in this pass, a real information-completeness gap worth a user's awareness given the shared drug class with aspirin
- Methenamine requires acidic urine to convert to its active antibacterial form (formaldehyde) — its effectiveness can be reduced by concurrent alkalinizing substances/foods, a real mechanism-dependent limitation not obviously conveyed by simple "urinary pain relief" branding
- **Real brand-identity risk:** since Cystex sits in the same "Urinary Health" category and serves the same general dysuria/burning-urination complaint as phenazopyridine-based competitors, a consumer comparing products by category/symptom rather than active ingredient could reasonably (but incorrectly) assume interchangeability with AZO/UriStat/Uricalm

## Use Cases
Relief of symptoms of bladder infection or irritation — painful/burning urination, urgency, frequency (per label).

## Allergies / Warnings
Salicylate-class caution applies given the sodium salicylate component: avoid in children/teenagers with chickenpox or flu-like symptoms (Reye syndrome risk, the same [[aspirin]]-class caution); caution with anticoagulants/other salicylates/NSAIDs given additive bleeding/GI-irritation risk; salicylate hypersensitivity is a contraindication.

## Symptoms Treated
Painful/burning urination, urinary urgency, and increased frequency associated with bladder infection or irritation.
