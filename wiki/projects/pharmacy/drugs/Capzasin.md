## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed (already pulled during ingredient-page research): **Capzasin HP Arthritis Pain Relief** carries **Capsaicin 0.1%** as its active ingredient. Full evidence — mechanism (TRPV1 agonism, substance P depletion), efficacy across neuropathic pain/osteoarthritis, safety data, and label directions — lives on [[capsaicin]]. That page also already notes that other Capzasin-line products (e.g. Capzasin Quick Relief Gel) combine capsaicin with menthol at a reportedly lower concentration (~0.025%, not independently confirmed via a full label pull), so the exact SKU matters for concentration even within this one brand. Capsaicin's own OTC label restricts use to adults/children over 18 — notably more restrictive than most other OTC topical analgesics in this database, a genuinely relevant fact for a product marketed broadly for "Diabetic Neuropathy Products." No brand-specific clinical trial beyond the general capsaicin evidence was found.

## Pros
- Confirmed active ingredient and mechanism directly relevant to neuropathic pain — capsaicin has systematic-review-level support for neuropathic pain (nondiabetic and postherpetic neuralgia specifically cited on [[capsaicin]]), a more direct mechanistic fit for the Diabetic Neuropathy Products category than the counterirritant menthol products it competes with (e.g. [[Biofreeze]])
- Long clinical track record and well-characterized mechanism (TRPV1 agonism/substance P depletion)
- No absolute contraindications identified beyond broken/damaged skin and eye/mucous-membrane contact

## Cons
- Classic capsaicin adherence problem: initial burning sensation from acute substance-P release before the longer-term desensitization benefit sets in — many users discontinue before benefit is reached
- OTC label restricts use to ages >18, more restrictive than many competing topical analgesics
- Serious burns have been reported with this class of product specifically when combined with tight bandaging or local heat (both explicitly warned against on label) — a real risk relevant to patients with diminished sensation from diabetic neuropathy itself, who may not notice overheating or excessive occlusion
- No brand-specific (as opposed to molecule-level) trial exists for diabetic neuropathy specifically

## Use Cases
Minor muscle/joint pain from arthritis, simple backache, strains, sprains, bruises; per the broader capsaicin evidence base, also plausible (though not FDA-labeled at the OTC 0.1% concentration specifically) for neuropathic pain symptom relief, which is the basis for its Diabetic Neuropathy Products category placement.

## Allergies / Warnings
Adults/children >18 only per label. Do not apply to wounds or damaged/broken/irritated skin; avoid eyes and mucous membranes; do not bandage tightly or apply local heat (serious burn risk). Patients with asthma show heightened sensitivity. Ask a doctor before use if pregnant/breastfeeding. **Diabetic-neuropathy-specific consideration:** patients with reduced sensation in the treated area may not perceive early signs of burn injury as readily — see [[capsaicin]]'s burn-risk data and [[diabetic-foot-care]] for the broader context on impaired healing/sensation risk in this population.

## Symptoms Treated
Minor muscle/joint pain, arthritis discomfort, backache, strains/sprains/bruises; neuropathic burning/tingling sensations (symptom relief, category placement rather than an FDA-labeled indication at this concentration).
