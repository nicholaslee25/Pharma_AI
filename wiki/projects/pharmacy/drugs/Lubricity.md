## Overview
**Discrepancy flagged:** Lubricity Dry Mouth Oral Spray is **FDA-cleared as a medical device**, not an OTC drug — the manufacturer's own marketing states it is "FDA cleared and is a medical device registered with the United States Food and Drug Administration," manufactured under current Good Manufacturing Practices. Its formulation is deliberately minimal: **sodium hyaluronate (hyaluronic acid), xylitol, sodium benzoate, and potassium sorbate** in water — just five ingredients, with sodium hyaluronate forming a flavorless gel film that coats oral mucosal tissue for tissue-on-tissue lubrication lasting up to 4 hours. This places Lubricity in the same "not actually a drug" category as several other products this database has flagged (e.g., Mederma's Cold Sore Discreet Patch, Off's EPA regulation) — filed under `drugs/` here for database consistency, but regulatorily a device.

## Pros
- Device-level FDA clearance (a 510(k)-type pathway, though the specific clearance number was not independently verified in this pass) provides a real, if different-from-drug-monograph, form of regulatory oversight
- Extremely simple, minimal ingredient list (5 total) may appeal to users sensitive to the preservatives, flavors, or dyes present in some rinse-based competitors
- Sodium hyaluronate's tissue-coating/lubricating mechanism is physically direct (a film barrier) rather than relying on osmotic or enzymatic action — a plausible mechanism for symptomatic relief even without a drug-level efficacy claim

## Cons
- As a device rather than a drug, Lubricity is not required to (and does not appear to) carry an FDA Drug Facts panel or make an FDA-reviewed therapeutic efficacy claim — any relief is a device-performance claim, a different regulatory standard than proven drug efficacy
- No independently located peer-reviewed clinical trial was found for this specific product's dry-mouth relief claims, though the manufacturer describes internal testing supporting "significant improvement in quality of life"
- Small brand with limited independent (non-manufacturer) sourcing available for verification in this pass

## Use Cases
Symptomatic relief of dry mouth/xerostomia via a lubricating, film-forming oral spray — device-based tissue coating rather than a saliva-substitute or salivary-stimulant drug mechanism.

## Allergies / Warnings
No FDA drug-specific warnings identified, consistent with device (not drug) status. General precaution: consult a doctor/dentist for persistent or unexplained dry mouth given the range of possible underlying causes (medications, Sjögren syndrome, radiation — see [[saliva-substitutes]]).

## Symptoms Treated
Dry mouth/xerostomia discomfort via physical tissue lubrication (device mechanism), not saliva substitution or stimulation.
