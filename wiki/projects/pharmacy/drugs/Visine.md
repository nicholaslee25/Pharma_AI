## Overview
**Discrepancy flagged — same category-placement pattern as [[Clear Eyes]]:** Visine's (Kenvue) core products are built around **tetrahydrozoline hydrochloride**, a vasoconstrictor redness reliever, not a pure lubricant — confirmed via multiple FDA labels (DailyMed): Visine Red Eye Comfort (tetrahydrozoline HCl alone), Visine Red Eye Total Comfort Multi-Symptom (tetrahydrozoline HCl 0.05% + PEG 400 + zinc sulfate), Visine A.C. Itchy Eye Relief (tetrahydrozoline HCl + zinc sulfate). One specific kit product, "Visine Red Eye Total Comfort and Tears Dry Eye Relief," bundles a tetrahydrozoline/PEG/zinc-sulfate redness-and-comfort drop together with a separate pure-lubricant component — reflecting the brand's split identity across the Artificial Tears (3%) and Ophthalmic Antihistamines/Decongestants (8%, larger share) categories it appears in. Tetrahydrozoline works identically in mechanism to [[Clear Eyes]]'s naphazoline (alpha-adrenergic vasoconstriction) and carries the same **rebound hyperemia risk with regular/prolonged use** — a pharmacology and safety profile [[artificial-tears]] does not address, since it is scoped to demulcent/lubricant actives only. No dedicated ingredient-evidence page for ophthalmic decongestants/vasoconstrictors exists yet in this project (same gap flagged on [[Clear Eyes]]). No Visine-brand-specific clinical trial beyond general tetrahydrozoline pharmacology was identified.

## Pros
- Tetrahydrozoline is an FDA-monograph-recognized OTC ophthalmic redness reliever with decades of established use and a well-characterized mechanism
- Combination formulations (redness reliever + lubricant + astringent) address multiple symptoms (redness, dryness, itching) in one product for users with overlapping complaints
- Larger share in its more pharmacologically-accurate category (Ophthalmic Antihistamines/Decongestants, 8%) than in Artificial Tears (3%) — suggests the market/pharmacist recommendation pattern roughly tracks the product's actual primary mechanism

## Cons
- **Category-placement mismatch**, identical pattern to [[Clear Eyes]]: Visine's presence in this database's "Artificial Tears/Ophthalmic Lubricants" list (3% share) reflects Pharmacy Times' survey categorization, not that its core products function as pure lubricants — most Visine formulations are vasoconstrictor-based or vasoconstrictor-combination products
- **Rebound hyperemia risk** with regular or prolonged tetrahydrozoline use — redness can worsen once the drug wears off, a real safety consideration this project's lubricant-focused ingredient page does not cover
- Product-line complexity (multiple named sub-formulations with different active-ingredient combinations) means a consumer cannot assume uniform pharmacology across "Visine" without checking the specific SKU

## Use Cases
Temporary relief of eye redness (vasoconstrictor mechanism); combination products also address dryness (PEG 400 lubricant component) and itching (zinc sulfate astringent/antihistamine-adjacent action). Not intended for regular, sustained daily use.

## Allergies / Warnings
Per label: do not use for more than 72 hours continuously without consulting a doctor; rebound redness risk with overuse; avoid in narrow-angle glaucoma or with MAOIs (general vasoconstrictor-class cautions); discontinue if pain, vision changes, or symptoms worsen.

## Symptoms Treated
Eye redness (primary, vasoconstrictor mechanism); dryness and itching in combination-formula sub-products.
