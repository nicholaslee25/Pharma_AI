## Appears In
Topical Anesthetics - 1%

## Overview
**Two discrepancies flagged.** First: Confirmed via DailyMed, Pomada Dragon's active-ingredient formulation — **camphor 4% + menthol 10% + methyl salicylate 30%** — is **IDENTICAL** to [[BENGAY]] Ultra Strength Non-Greasy Cream's confirmed formulation, the exact "identical formulations under two brand names" pattern this database's research process specifically watches for. Second, and more significant for its category placement: **Pomada Dragon contains NO anesthetic ingredient at all** (no lidocaine, no benzocaine) despite being filed under "Topical Anesthetics" in this database — its actual mechanism is the counterirritant class (camphor/menthol/methyl salicylate) documented on [[topical-counterirritants]], mechanistically identical to Icy Hot, BENGAY, and Tiger Balm, all of which are filed under "Thermal Relief Products" instead. Both of Pomada Dragon's menthol (10%) and methyl salicylate (30%) concentrations exceed the FDA's 2012 burn-risk thresholds (>3% menthol, >10% methyl salicylate), and the product carries the full methyl-salicylate NSAID-class systemic risk profile.

## Pros
- Confirmed FDA Drug Facts label with clearly disclosed active ingredients
- Triple-ingredient counterirritant combination provides a genuinely multi-mechanism sensory/analgesic effect (cooling from menthol, warming/counterirritant from camphor, salicylate-class analgesic action from methyl salicylate)

## Cons
- **Category-placement mismatch:** filed under "Topical Anesthetics" despite containing no anesthetic (numbing) ingredient whatsoever — a consumer specifically seeking a numbing anesthetic (like lidocaine or benzocaine) would not get one from this product, despite its category placement suggesting otherwise
- **Identical formulation to BENGAY Ultra Strength** — both exceed the FDA's 2012 burn-risk thresholds on menthol AND methyl salicylate, and BENGAY was one of the brands explicitly named in that FDA communication's press coverage; the same risk profile applies here under a different brand name
- Carries the full NSAID-class systemic risk profile of methyl salicylate (though this specific label's warnings section, per what was extracted, did not include the explicit stomach-bleeding/aspirin-allergy language seen on the Salonpas and Tiger Balm labels — worth flagging as a possible labeling inconsistency across methyl-salicylate products, not independently resolved in this pass)
- No brand-specific clinical trial for Pomada Dragon specifically was identified beyond the general counterirritant-class evidence, which itself concerns a different product's formulation (Salonpas's 10%/3%, not Pomada Dragon's 30%/10%/4%)

## Use Cases
Temporary relief of minor pain from arthritis, muscle cramps, sprains/strains, bruising, and backache — via counterirritant sensory masking, NOT via local anesthetic numbing despite this database's category placement.

## Allergies / Warnings
Per label: do not apply to open wounds or sunburned/windburned/dry/irritated skin; ask a health professional before use if pregnant/breastfeeding; keep out of reach of children, contact Poison Control if swallowed; adults/children 12+ only. See [[topical-counterirritants]] for the full FDA burn-risk and methyl-salicylate NSAID-class safety data directly applicable to this product's confirmed concentrations.

## Symptoms Treated
Minor musculoskeletal pain via counterirritant mechanism (cooling/warming sensory masking plus salicylate-class analgesic action) — not local anesthetic numbing.
