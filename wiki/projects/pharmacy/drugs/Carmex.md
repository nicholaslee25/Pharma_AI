## Appears In

## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed: "Carmex" spans at least two different formulations depending on SKU. **Carmex Classic Lip Balm** (the likely driver of the Lip Balms 15% share) lists **camphor 1.70% and white petrolatum 45.30%** as active ingredients — a skin-protectant/mild-counterirritant combination for general lip moisturizing and chapped-lip relief, not a dedicated antiviral or anesthetic. **Carmex Multi-Symptom 3-in-1 Cold Sore Treatment** (the likely driver of the Cold Sore Treatments 5% share) instead uses **benzocaine** (external analgesic) **+ white petrolatum 44.69%** (skin protectant) — a materially different active-ingredient set adding a true local anesthetic. This is the same "one brand name, multiple mechanisms depending on SKU" pattern already documented repeatedly in this database (Orajel, Anbesol, Blistex).

## Pros
- The dedicated Cold Sore Treatment SKU's benzocaine component provides genuine local anesthesia, unlike the Classic Lip Balm's camphor/petrolatum combination which is a milder protectant/counterirritant
- Petrolatum-based formulations across both SKUs provide a real occlusive moisturizing barrier, useful for the dry/cracked skin that often accompanies both chapped lips and healing cold sores
- Extremely long-standing, widely recognized OTC lip-care brand with broad consumer familiarity

## Cons
- Benzocaine in the Cold Sore Treatment SKU carries the same disproportionate methemoglobinemia risk documented on [[topical-anesthetics]] as every other benzocaine-based product in this database — a risk NOT present in the Classic Lip Balm SKU, so the two products should not be treated as interchangeable in safety profile
- No antiviral mechanism in either confirmed SKU — neither targets herpes simplex virus replication/entry the way Abreva's docosanol does; relief is symptomatic/protective only
- No brand-specific clinical trial evidence was identified for either SKU's effect on cold sore healing time or chapped-lip recovery
- A consumer reaching for "Carmex" for a cold sore could pick up either the camphor-based Classic Lip Balm (mild, no true anesthetic, no benzocaine risk) or the benzocaine-based Cold Sore Treatment (true anesthetic, methemoglobinemia risk) — the brand name alone does not indicate which

## Use Cases
General chapped/cracked lip moisturizing and protection (Classic Lip Balm); temporary pain relief plus protective barrier for cold sores/fever blisters (Multi-Symptom 3-in-1 Cold Sore Treatment).

## Allergies / Warnings
Classic Lip Balm: standard camphor/petrolatum precautions (external use, avoid eyes, discontinue if irritation develops). Cold Sore Treatment SKU: full benzocaine methemoglobinemia warning applies (stop use and seek immediate medical attention for pale/gray/blue skin, headache, rapid heart rate, shortness of breath, dizziness, fatigue); "caine"-family allergy contraindication.

## Symptoms Treated
Chapped/dry/cracked lips (Classic Lip Balm, protectant mechanism); cold sore pain and protection (Cold Sore Treatment SKU, benzocaine anesthetic + petrolatum protectant) — no antiviral action in either.
