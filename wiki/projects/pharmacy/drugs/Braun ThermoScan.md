## Overview
Braun ThermoScan is a **tympanic (ear) infrared thermometer**, the largest single-brand share (50%, half the entire category) in Digital Thermometers. Brand-specific data found here is notably favorable: a head-to-head study comparing it against a Welch-Allyn oral gold-standard reference (alongside Withings, Wellworks, and [[MOBI]] thermometers, 30 healthy adults, cold-water-immersion protocol) found Braun's tympanic reading (left ear) had the **best agreement of all devices tested — mean error 0.044°C** — and was the only device in that study not significantly different from the gold standard. A separate pediatric reliability study (244 children aged 1-6) found a mean tympanic temperature of 36.65°C with a repeatability statistic of 0.78°C overall (0.55°C left ear, 0.64°C right ear), concluding ThermoScan tympanic thermometry is reliable, with roughly 0.6°C being a clinically significant change. This is a more favorable brand-specific picture than [[digital-thermometers]]'s general tympanic-thermometer literature would suggest on its own — that page's Shi 2020 meta-analysis and the older NOKC 2009 review both describe tympanic thermometry's real accuracy tradeoffs at the class level (older-generation tympanic devices tend to under-detect fever despite high specificity), while this specific brand's data performed better than that general caution might imply.

## Pros
- Best measured agreement with a gold-standard reference among four thermometer brands/technologies tested head-to-head in the sources found (0.044°C mean error) — the strongest brand-specific accuracy figure located in this entire Digital Thermometers category
- Separately confirmed reliable in a large pediatric study (244 children), the population most relevant to routine home fever-checking
- By far the largest market share (50%), reflecting extensive pharmacist and consumer trust

## Cons
- Tympanic thermometry as a class carries real, documented limitations per [[digital-thermometers]] (older-generation devices under-detect fever; even newer-generation tympanic thermometry's best-cutoff performance per Shi 2020 was AUC 0.97, not perfect) — this brand's strong single-study result should not be read as making ear thermometry immune to the site-specific accuracy tradeoffs described generally on that page
- Ear anatomy variation (earwax, canal shape, improper positioning) is a known general source of tympanic-measurement error not specifically quantified for this brand in the sources found
- Measurement site matters: a tympanic reading should not be directly compared to an oral/rectal/temporal threshold without accounting for site-specific normal ranges, per [[digital-thermometers]]'s general caution

## Use Cases
Rapid at-home body temperature measurement via the ear canal (tympanic membrane infrared sensing), for fever screening in both adults and children.
