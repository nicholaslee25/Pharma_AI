## Overview
No FDA drug label exists — Xlear is explicitly marketed as **drug-free**: a saline nasal spray with added xylitol (ingredients per manufacturer: purified water, xylitol, USP sodium chloride, grapefruit seed extract). This is a legitimate "no label found, not a drug" case, unlike its category-mates in Decongestants, Nasal Spray (Afrin, Dristan, Neo-Synephrine, etc.) which all contain an actual vasoconstrictor active ingredient — Xlear's placement in that category is somewhat misleading since it works by a completely different, non-pharmacological mechanism (osmotic/moisturizing action plus xylitol's proposed anti-adhesive effect on bacteria). New finding — no prior ingredient pointer existed since none was applicable.

## Pros
- **No rebound congestion risk** — since there's no vasoconstrictor active ingredient, the central safety concern dominating every other product in its Decongestants category simply doesn't apply; can be used as often as needed without the 3-day limit, per manufacturer marketing
- Xylitol has some proposed antibacterial/anti-adhesive properties in nasal/sinus tissue (general nutritional-chemistry knowledge; not independently verified against a specific clinical trial in this pass)
- Drug-free positioning may suit users wanting to avoid systemic decongestant exposure (e.g., certain cardiovascular patients) — though it also does not provide the same rapid vasoconstrictive decongestant effect those products do

## Cons
- Being drug-free means it will not produce oxymetazoline/phenylephrine-level rapid decongestant relief — a real efficacy trade-off for the safety benefit, not established with a head-to-head trial in this pass
- No FDA drug-facts label means no standardized dosing/warnings panel exists to reference — this database has to rely on manufacturer marketing claims rather than a regulatory document, a real difference in evidence quality from every FDA-labeled decongestant in this batch
- Its listing under "Decongestants, Nasal Spray" alongside genuine vasoconstrictor drugs, without a clear distinguishing note in this database's category data, could mislead a reader into expecting decongestant-drug-level relief

## Use Cases
Nasal moisturizing, mild congestion relief/cleansing, marketed as usable for ongoing daily nasal hygiene without the restrictions that apply to drug decongestants.

## Allergies / Warnings
No FDA-mandated drug warnings (not a regulated drug product). Grapefruit seed extract is a component — general allergy awareness applies as with any botanical extract, though no specific contraindication was located in this pass.

## Symptoms Treated
Nasal dryness, mild congestion, general nasal moisturizing/hygiene — not a substitute for a vasoconstrictor decongestant's rapid relief.
