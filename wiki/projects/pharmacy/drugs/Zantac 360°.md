## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed: Zantac 360° is famotidine 20mg — resolving this database's prior hedge and providing the full recall/reformulation history behind it. **Real, historically significant brand-identity finding:** the original "Zantac" brand (1980s-2019) was ranitidine, a chemically distinct H2RA. In September 2019, an independent pharmacy (Valisure) found NDMA (a probable human carcinogen) contamination in ranitidine samples; the FDA requested market withdrawal of all ranitidine products in April 2020. In 2021, the Zantac brand name relaunched as "Zantac 360°" with an entirely different active ingredient — famotidine, chemically unrelated to ranitidine and with no known NDMA-contamination risk. **This means "Zantac" as a brand name spans two chemically distinct, non-interchangeable drugs depending on purchase date/product** — a consumer's memory of "Zantac" from before 2020 refers to a now-recalled ranitidine product, while any Zantac product purchased today is the reformulated famotidine version. (Separately, the FDA approved a reformulated prescription-only ranitidine, tested to avoid NDMA formation, in late 2025 — but this is a distinct, prescription product, not the OTC Zantac 360° brand.)

## Pros
- Confirmed FDA label; genuinely famotidine-based, sharing [[famotidine]]'s full evidence base (established H2RA mechanism, comparatively clean drug-interaction profile relative to cimetidine/Tagamet HB 200)
- No NDMA-contamination risk (famotidine's chemical structure does not share ranitidine's nitrosamine-formation vulnerability) — a genuine safety improvement over the pre-2020 ranitidine-based Zantac
- Available in a "Cool Mint" flavor variant and multi-blister-count packaging at the same confirmed 20mg dose per DailyMed

## Cons
- **A consumer or caregiver relying on brand-name familiarity from before 2020 could reasonably but incorrectly assume "Zantac" still means ranitidine** — the two are chemically unrelated drugs (H2RAs in the same class, but distinct molecules with distinct safety profiles), and this is a real, historically-grounded brand-identity risk beyond a simple formulation-strength ambiguity
- Shares [[famotidine]]'s own real safety signals: documented thrombocytopenia risk requiring CBC monitoring with long-term use, and pediatric-specific agitation risk (14% in infants vs. <1% in adults)
- No Zantac-360°-specific RCT beyond the general famotidine evidence base was independently located in this pass
- Smaller recommendation share (8%) than Pepcid (83%) within the same H2 Receptor Antagonists category, despite both now being famotidine-based — this database did not independently confirm whether any residual brand-reputation effect from the 2019-2020 ranitidine recall affects Zantac 360°'s market position specifically

## Use Cases
Relief and prevention of heartburn associated with acid indigestion and sour stomach — identical use case and mechanism to Pepcid, since both are now famotidine-based, per [[famotidine]].

## Allergies / Warnings
Per label: max 2 tablets/24h; children under 12: ask a doctor. Per [[famotidine]]: avoid in patients with documented cimetidine hypersensitivity history (H2RA cross-sensitivity); do not use with difficulty/pain swallowing, vomiting blood, or bloody/black stools.

## Symptoms Treated
Heartburn, acid indigestion, sour stomach — via the H2-receptor-antagonist mechanism detailed in [[famotidine]], identical to Pepcid's mechanism.
