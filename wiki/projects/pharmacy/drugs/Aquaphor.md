## Overview
Confirmed: Aquaphor Healing Ointment carries a genuine OTC Drug Facts skin-protectant claim — **White Petrolatum 41%** as its sole active ingredient (a clinical drug-encyclopedia entry corroborates this; not independently pulled from DailyMed directly in this pass). This single core formulation is what spans all five categories this brand appears in (Diabetic Foot Cream, Eczema Care, Foot Care, Incontinence Skin Protectants, Therapeutic Skin Care Moisturizers) — unlike house brands such as [[CeraVe]] that use materially different formulations per category. See [[petrolatum]] for the ingredient-level evidence: that page's own Formulation Caveat already specifically calls out Aquaphor as "a petrolatum-containing combination product (not pure petrolatum)... not independently tested" in the pivotal Smack et al. 1996 JAMA RCT (922 patients, petrolatum vs. bacitracin for post-surgical wound care) — this brand page confirms that caveat rather than resolving it, since Aquaphor also contains panthenol, glycerin, mineral oil, ceresin, bisabolol, and lanolin alcohol alongside its 41% petrolatum. Label saved at `raw/primary-sources/brands/aquaphor/aquaphor-healing-ointment-drug-facts.md`. No brand-specific (as opposed to plain-petrolatum) clinical trial was found for Aquaphor's exact formulation.

## Pros
- Confirmed active ingredient (41% petrolatum) at a notably higher concentration than typical over-the-counter petrolatum products, per the mechanism data on [[petrolatum]] (occlusive barrier reducing transepidermal water loss ~99%)
- Fragrance-, preservative-, and dye-free formulation per manufacturer, reducing contact-irritant risk
- Single core formula used consistently across most of its product categories, unlike some house brands whose SKUs vary by category

## Cons
- Not pure petrolatum — the added panthenol/lanolin/mineral-oil/bisabolol ingredients were not part of the Smack 1996 RCT's tested product, so that trial's favorable infection/allergy-profile data cannot be directly extrapolated to Aquaphor's specific formulation
- No independent brand-specific trial exists distinguishing Aquaphor's real-world outcomes from plain petrolatum or from competing combination ointments
- Like the rest of the Diabetic Foot Cream category, no evidence that it specifically reduces diabetic ulceration or amputation risk — see [[diabetic-foot-care]]

## Use Cases
Chapped/cracked skin protection and relief; minor cuts, scrapes, and burns; general dry-skin/eczema-adjacent moisturizing; incontinence-related skin protection; diabetic foot dry-skin care as part of a broader routine.

## Allergies / Warnings
For external use only. Standard skin-protectant label cautions (avoid eyes; do not apply to deep wounds, puncture wounds, animal bites, or serious burns; stop use and consult a doctor if condition worsens or persists beyond 7 days). No diabetes-specific contraindication identified.

## Symptoms Treated
Chapped, cracked, or dry skin; minor abrasions/burns; general skin-barrier protection.
