## Overview
Cetaphil (Galderma Laboratories) is a large multi-category personal-care brand spanning at least four distinct mechanisms across its six category appearances, confirmed via DailyMed/manufacturer sources:

- **Acne Products (12%):** the "Gentle Clear" line splits into a **benzoyl peroxide 2.6% micronized** cleanser and a separate **salicylic acid** line (2% cream cleanser, 0.5% serum, 0.5% moisturizer) — the same BPO/salicylic-acid dual-mechanism split already documented on [[Clearasil]] and [[AcneFree]] elsewhere in this pass, plus a third, milder concentration tier (0.5% salicylic acid) not seen in those brands.
- **Antibacterial Soaps (17%):** confirmed via DailyMed — Cetaphil's antibacterial soap uses **benzalkonium chloride 0.13% v/v**, a quaternary-ammonium antiseptic, NOT chlorhexidine — see the now-created [[benzalkonium-chloride]] ingredient page (added 2026-08-27 when this pass reached the Antibacterial Soaps subcategory directly), which also documents the real, current finding that this ingredient carries **no final FDA GRASE determination** for antiseptic-wash use, nearly a decade after its 2016 regulatory deferral.
- **Eczema Care/Relief Products (9%) and Therapeutic Skin Care, Cleansers/Moisturizers (24%/11%, the largest single share in this database's Therapeutic Skin Care Cleansers subcategory):** the flagship "Gentle Skin Cleanser" and moisturizing-cream lines are largely **non-drug cosmetics** — no active ingredient, formulated around glycerin/petrolatum-class emollients (specific full label not independently pulled this pass) — consistent with the broad market positioning as a dermatologist-recommended gentle cleanser rather than a medicated treatment.
- **Sunscreen (4%):** Cetaphil's sunscreen SKUs use standard chemical or mineral UV filters (brand-specific SKU-level label not independently pulled this pass; treated as consistent with the category norm already documented under [[Neutrogena]]/[[Aveeno]]).

No Cetaphil-brand-specific clinical trial beyond the general ingredient-level evidence for benzoyl peroxide and salicylic acid was located in this pass.

## Pros
- Confirmed benzoyl peroxide and salicylic acid concentrations for the acne line fall within FDA-monograph OTC ranges, matching [[benzoyl-peroxide]] and [[salicylic-acid]]
- Genuinely broad within-brand choice for acne (three concentration/mechanism tiers) lets a consumer step down to a gentler option (0.5% salicylic acid) if standard 2%/2.6% strength irritates
- "Fragrance-free," "soap-free" formulation across the non-drug cosmetic lines reduces contact-irritant risk, relevant to its Eczema Care category presence
- Antibacterial soap's benzalkonium chloride is a long-established topical antiseptic class, distinct from and complementary to the chlorhexidine-based [[Hibiclens]] elsewhere in this same category

## Cons
- **Six category appearances span at least four unrelated mechanisms** (benzoyl peroxide/salicylic acid for acne, benzalkonium chloride for antibacterial soap, no active ingredient for most Therapeutic Skin Care/Eczema Care SKUs, standard UV filters for sunscreen) — a consumer cannot assume ingredient consistency across "Cetaphil" products, the same pattern already flagged on [[CeraVe]], [[Aveeno]], and [[Neutrogena]]
- **Initial-pass assumption corrected:** this page's antibacterial-soap active was initially assumed to be chlorhexidine (given [[Hibiclens]]'s dominance of that category) before DailyMed confirmed benzalkonium chloride 0.13% instead — flagged as a reminder that brand research in this database is genuinely verified per-SKU, not inferred from category norms
- Benzalkonium chloride's unresolved FDA GRASE status (see [[benzalkonium-chloride]]) applies to Cetaphil's antibacterial soap line just as it does to [[Dial]], [[Dove]], and [[Softsoap]] elsewhere in this category
- This pass did not independently pull SKU-level labels for the Eczema Care, Sunscreen, or general Therapeutic Skin Care Moisturizer lines beyond general market/manufacturer information — treat those category placements as plausible but not independently label-confirmed to the same rigor as the Acne and Antibacterial Soap lines
- No Cetaphil-brand-specific RCT was located; all efficacy evidence is at the shared-ingredient level

## Use Cases
Acne treatment (BPO or salicylic acid, consumer's choice of concentration); antibacterial hand/skin washing (benzalkonium chloride); general gentle cleansing/moisturizing for sensitive or eczema-prone skin (non-drug cosmetic SKUs); sun protection (sunscreen line).

## Allergies / Warnings
Acne line: per [[benzoyl-peroxide]]/[[salicylic-acid]], avoid eyes/mucous membranes, may bleach fabric/hair (BPO), discontinue if excessive irritation. Antibacterial soap line: standard OTC antiseptic-soap label cautions (external use only, avoid eyes, discontinue if irritation develops), per [[benzalkonium-chloride]]. Non-drug cosmetic lines: no FDA Drug Facts warnings (no active ingredient); general cosmetic-allergen caution applies.

## Symptoms Treated
Acne (Acne Products line); reduction of bacteria on skin (Antibacterial Soaps); none — cleansing/moisturizing only (most Eczema Care and Therapeutic Skin Care SKUs); sun protection (Sunscreen).
