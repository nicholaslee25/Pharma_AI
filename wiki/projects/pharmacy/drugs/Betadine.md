## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed: Betadine First Aid Antiseptic's own FDA OTC Drug Facts label lists **povidone-iodine 10% (1% available iodine)** as its active ingredient, functioning as a first-aid antiseptic. This is a third genuinely distinct antiseptic mechanism within the Topical Antibiotics/Antiseptics category (alongside the peptide antibiotics in Neosporin/Bacitraycin Plus/Polysporin, the quaternary-ammonium compound benzalkonium chloride in [[Bactine]]/[[Alocane]], and chlorhexidine gluconate in [[Hibiclens]]) — an iodine-based broad-spectrum antimicrobial with a long, well-established history in wound antisepsis and pre-surgical skin preparation. No brand-specific clinical trial for Betadine's OTC first-aid formulation specifically was identified in a targeted search beyond its long-standing general antiseptic use history.

## Pros
- Confirmed FDA Drug Facts label; povidone-iodine has a very long, well-established history as a broad-spectrum antiseptic (bactericidal, virucidal, fungicidal activity), historically among the most extensively used antiseptics in both hospital and OTC settings
- Different antiseptic mechanism than the antibiotic-ointment products dominating this category, useful for consumers with an antibiotic-ointment allergy (bacitracin/neomycin/polymyxin)
- Broad-spectrum activity (bacteria, viruses, fungi) exceeds the bacteria-only spectrum of the antibiotic-ointment products in this category

## Cons
- Iodine-based products carry a known risk of skin staining and, in a subset of users, iodine sensitivity/allergy — a distinct safety consideration from the antibiotic-allergy or benzalkonium-chloride-sensitivity risks elsewhere in this category
- Label warns against use in the eyes, over large body areas, or by patients with a known povidone-iodine allergy
- Smallest tracked share (3%) of any product in this category; no brand-specific outcome trial for the OTC first-aid formulation was identified
- Thyroid-related caution is a known consideration for iodine-containing antiseptics with repeated/large-area use, though this specific concern was not independently re-verified against the OTC first-aid label's own warnings in this pass

## Use Cases
First aid antisepsis (broad-spectrum: bacteria, viruses, fungi) for minor cuts, scrapes, and burns; also long-used as a pre-surgical/pre-procedural skin antiseptic in clinical settings (a use case outside this OTC category's direct scope).

## Allergies / Warnings
Per label: for external use only; do not use in the eyes or over large areas of the body; do not use if allergic to povidone-iodine or other formulation ingredients; discontinue if irritation or sensitization occurs.

## Symptoms Treated
Reduces infection risk from surface microbial contamination (bacterial, viral, and fungal) in minor cuts, scrapes, and burns; no analgesic action (no pain relief ingredient).
