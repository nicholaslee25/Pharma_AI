## Overview
**Discrepancy flagged:** "Blue-Emu" is named and marketed around emu oil (positioned as a skin-penetration "carrier" ingredient), but emu oil is **NOT the FDA-recognized active ingredient in any confirmed Blue-Emu SKU** — the same "brand name touts an ingredient that isn't the actual active ingredient" pattern already documented for [[Nature's Willow Bug Bite Balm]] (willow bark, also inactive). Confirmed via DailyMed: the flagship **Blue-Emu Maximum Pain Relief Cream / Original Super Strength** line uses **trolamine salicylate 10%** (a salicylate distinct from methyl salicylate, chemically the same active ingredient as non-lidocaine Aspercreme products) as its actual active ingredient, with emu oil, aloe vera, glucosamine HCl, MSM, vitamin E, and allantoin all listed as INACTIVE ingredients. A separate SKU, **Blue-Emu Lidocaine Pain Relief Cream**, uses **lidocaine HCl 4%** instead — likely the product driving this brand's Topical Anesthetics (3%) category share, while the trolamine salicylate line likely drives the Topical Analgesics (1%) share. Trolamine salicylate, like methyl salicylate (see [[topical-counterirritants]]), carries a real aspirin/salicylate-allergy warning on its own label.

## Pros
- Confirmed FDA Drug Facts labels for both the trolamine salicylate and lidocaine SKUs, with active ingredients clearly disclosed (even though marketing emphasizes emu oil, a non-active carrier)
- Offers two mechanistically distinct options under one brand — trolamine salicylate (salicylate-class counterirritant/analgesic) and lidocaine (numbing anesthetic) — for users with different needs or salicylate sensitivities
- The trolamine salicylate line's added inactive ingredients (glucosamine HCl, MSM) are individually studied elsewhere in oral supplement contexts (see [[glucosamine-chondroitin]]), though their topical, inactive-ingredient inclusion here does not carry the same evidence weight as oral use

## Cons
- **Discrepancy flagged:** the entire brand identity centers on emu oil, which is not the FDA-recognized active ingredient in any confirmed SKU — a consumer drawn to the brand for "emu oil" benefits is actually getting a conventional salicylate or lidocaine product with emu oil as an inactive carrier
- Trolamine salicylate carries the same aspirin/salicylate-allergy contraindication and pregnancy caution ("ask a health professional before use") pattern as methyl salicylate products documented on [[topical-counterirritants]]
- A California Proposition 65-style warning notes possible trace diethanolamine exposure — a real, labeled chemical-exposure disclosure worth being aware of, though the label itself doesn't quantify health risk
- No brand-specific clinical trial for either Blue-Emu SKU was identified beyond general trolamine salicylate/lidocaine ingredient-level context
- The Lidocaine Pain Relief Cream's full label warnings/directions text could not be independently re-extracted in this pass (PDF fetch failure) — standard lidocaine OTC conventions are presumed but not word-for-word confirmed for this specific SKU

## Use Cases
Temporary relief of minor aches from arthritis, backache, strains, sprains, and bruises (trolamine salicylate line); temporary relief of minor pain via numbing anesthetic action (lidocaine line).

## Allergies / Warnings
Trolamine salicylate line: allergy alert for aspirin/salicylate sensitivity — consult a doctor before use; do not bandage tightly, use with heating pads, or apply to wounds/damaged skin; avoid eyes/mucous membranes; ask a health professional before use if pregnant/nursing; discontinue if condition worsens, symptoms persist beyond 7 days, or redness/irritation develops. Lidocaine line: standard topical-anesthetic precautions presumed per [[topical-anesthetics]], not independently re-confirmed word-for-word in this pass.

## Symptoms Treated
Minor musculoskeletal aches (arthritis, backache, strains, sprains, bruises) via salicylate-class analgesic action (trolamine salicylate line); general minor pain via numbing (lidocaine line).
