## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed: Advil PM is **ibuprofen 200 mg + diphenhydramine citrate 38 mg** per tablet — resolving this page's prior hedge into a confirmed finding, matching [[ibuprofen]] and the newly built [[oral-diphenhydramine-sleep-aid]]. **Formulation nuance:** Advil PM uses the CITRATE salt of diphenhydramine (38mg) rather than the hydrochloride salt (25mg) used by Tylenol PM and Aleve PM — these are molar-equivalent doses of the same active diphenhydramine molecule, just different salt forms with different molecular weights, not a materially different sleep-aid dose.

## Pros
- Confirmed FDA Drug Facts label combining two individually well-evidenced ingredients for their respective primary uses (ibuprofen's Cochrane-backed analgesic evidence per [[ibuprofen]])
- Addresses combined pain-with-sleeplessness in one product
- Ibuprofen's genuine peripheral anti-inflammatory action (unlike acetaminophen) may be preferable for inflammation-associated pain specifically, alongside the sleep-aid component

## Cons
- **Same real, guideline-level "avoid in older adults" flag applies to the diphenhydramine component** (AGS Beers Criteria, per [[oral-diphenhydramine-sleep-aid]]) as for Tylenol PM and Aleve PM — this is a shared risk across the entire "PM" combination-product category, not specific to any one analgesic pairing
- Combines TWO distinct risk profiles simultaneously: ibuprofen's boxed-warning cardiovascular/GI-bleed risk (per [[ibuprofen]]) AND diphenhydramine's anticholinergic/elderly-risk profile — a more complex overall safety picture than a single-ingredient product
- Independent systematic-review evidence describes diphenhydramine's OTC sleep-aid efficacy generally as lacking robust support, per [[oral-diphenhydramine-sleep-aid]]
- Smallest share (23%) of the three PM combination products in this database despite arguably the most differentiated analgesic (NSAID vs. Tylenol PM's acetaminophen) — market share here likely reflects brand loyalty patterns rather than any demonstrated superiority

## Use Cases
Combined temporary relief of headache/minor aches and pains WITH accompanying occasional sleeplessness, via an NSAID rather than acetaminophen base.

## Allergies / Warnings
Per label conventions matching the ibuprofen risk profile (per [[ibuprofen]]: GI bleeding, cardiovascular thrombotic risk, aspirin-allergy cross-reaction) plus the diphenhydramine warnings shared across all PM products (avoid other diphenhydramine-containing products, alcohol, or sedatives without medical guidance; drowsiness will occur).

## Symptoms Treated
Headache, minor aches and pains, and occasional sleeplessness accompanying pain.
