## Overview
AZO Cranberry is the category-dominant brand in this database's Cranberry Supplements listing (61% share). No AZO-brand-specific clinical trial was located in this pass; the brand's marketing and efficacy claims rest on the general cranberry/proanthocyanidin evidence base covered in [[cranberry]]. That evidence is real but genuinely **population-dependent, not a blanket "prevents UTIs" claim**: per a large, current Cochrane review (50 studies, 8,857 participants), cranberry meaningfully reduces UTI risk in children (RR 0.46) and in people made susceptible by a medical intervention like catheterization (RR 0.47), and more modestly in women with recurrent UTIs (RR 0.74) — but shows little or no benefit in elderly institutionalized patients, pregnant women, or those with neuromuscular bladder dysfunction, three populations who are nonetheless commonly told to try cranberry supplements. Cranberry is also explicitly **not a treatment for an active infection** — only for preventing new ones. This pass did not verify AZO Cranberry's specific PAC (proanthocyanidin) dose or standardization against the label, which the underlying evidence page notes matters (dose comparisons remain unclear/very-low-certainty even in the Cochrane review itself).
