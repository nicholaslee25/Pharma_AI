## Overview
**Missing-ingredient-page finding resolved, with a real, unusual regulatory history.** Confirmed via DailyMed: Emetrol is phosphorated carbohydrate solution (dextrose/fructose + phosphoric acid) — a mechanism with no prior dedicated ingredient page in this database. A new [[phosphorated-carbohydrate-solution]] page was created in this pass specifically because this ingredient has a genuinely unusual regulatory history: **the FDA's own antiemetic OTC monograph rulemaking rated it Category I for safety but Category III for effectiveness**, meaning the FDA's review process found insufficient evidence of antiemetic effectiveness — yet the product remains commercially marketed and is this database's second-largest brand share (32%) in Nausea Remedies.

## Pros
- Long safety track record (FDA Category I safety rating) — a genuinely low-toxicity ingredient
- Manufacturer-sponsored 1989 study and more recent secondary sourcing report meaningful nausea-score reduction (60-70%) within 30-45 minutes for mild/self-limited nausea (viral gastroenteritis, dietary overindulgence) — real if not FDA-monograph-validated supporting data
- Non-drowsy, non-anticholinergic mechanism — a genuine differentiator from the antihistamine-based Nausea Remedies category-mates (Dramamine Nausea/meclizine) for a consumer wanting to avoid sedation

## Cons
- **The FDA's own antiemetic rulemaking rated this ingredient's effectiveness evidence insufficient (Category III)** — a real, sourced regulatory finding directly relevant to interpreting this brand's 32%/21% recommendation shares; per this database's standing rule, a high recommendation share should not be read as strong efficacy evidence, and here the FDA's own review process specifically found the evidence lacking
- **Not recommended as first-line treatment under current clinical guidelines**, which favor evidence-based antiemetics instead, per general reference sourcing reviewed in this pass
- **Contains fructose — contraindicated in hereditary fructose intolerance (HFI)**, a specific, real, genetic-disease-related contraindication not shared by any other brand in this database's Nausea Remedies/Upset Stomach Remedies categories
- No Emetrol-brand-specific RCT beyond the manufacturer-sponsored 1989 study was independently located in this pass, and that study's design/rigor was not independently re-verified against the original source

## Use Cases
Relief of upset stomach/nausea from overindulgence in food or drink — a mild, self-limited-nausea use case per label, not positioned (or FDA-validated) for more serious antiemetic needs.

## Allergies / Warnings
**Contains fructose — contraindicated in hereditary fructose intolerance.** No other significant contraindication identified in the sources reviewed in this pass; general high-sugar-content relevance for diabetic patients monitoring carbohydrate intake, though not independently detailed with a specific label warning.

## Symptoms Treated
