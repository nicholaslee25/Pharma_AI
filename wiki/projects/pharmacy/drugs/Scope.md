## Overview
Confirmed: **Scope Classic (Original Formula)** — the flagship product and by far the most recognized "Scope" SKU, most plausibly the one driving this category share — is explicitly marketed by its own manufacturer (Procter & Gamble/Crest) as "the #1 fresh breath brand among **non-drug** mouthwashes." Its ingredient list (water, alcohol ~15%, glycerin, flavor, polysorbate 80, sodium saccharin, sodium benzoate, cetylpyridinium chloride, benzoic acid, colorants) includes cetylpyridinium chloride, but the product is marketed and filed as a cosmetic — no therapeutic (antigingivitis/antiplaque/anticavity) claim is made for this specific SKU, consistent with the FDA's own cosmetic-vs-drug mouthwash distinction (a product claiming only breath/taste/appearance benefits is a cosmetic; a product claiming to treat/prevent a condition is a drug). Separate sibling products under the same brand ARE drugs: **Scope Outlast** (cetylpyridinium chloride + domiphen bromide, antigingivitis claim) and **Crest Scope Advanced** (sodium fluoride 0.02%, anticavity claim) both carry genuine Drug Facts panels.

## Pros
- Long-standing, widely recognized brand with a large installed base of consumer trust for everyday breath-freshening use
- The plain Classic formula's cosmetic (non-drug) status means it carries no drug-specific contraindication or interaction profile — a lower-stakes product for routine daily use
- Sibling "Scope Outlast" and "Scope Advanced" SKUs exist under the same brand for consumers who specifically want antigingivitis or anticavity drug claims, without switching brands entirely

## Cons
- The flagship Classic formula provides only cosmetic (odor-masking/taste) benefit — it does not treat, prevent, or reduce plaque, gingivitis, or cavities despite containing cetylpyridinium chloride, an ingredient that DOES have antiplaque activity at appropriate concentrations/claims in other products (e.g., Crest Scope Original's dedicated antigingivitis SKU, or SmartMouth's Clinical DDS formula)
- Contains ~15% alcohol, which can cause a burning sensation and is a relevant consideration for children, recovering alcoholics, or people with dry mouth (alcohol has a drying effect on oral mucosa)
- "Scope" as a brand name spans cosmetic (Classic) and drug (Outlast, Advanced) products with materially different regulatory status and claims — a consumer should check the specific SKU rather than assume uniform classification

## Use Cases
General breath freshening and taste improvement (Classic/Original, cosmetic); antigingivitis/antiplaque protection (Outlast SKU, drug); anticavity protection (Advanced SKU, drug — see [[fluoride-toothpaste]] for the fluoride mechanism shared with toothpaste products).

## Allergies / Warnings
Classic (cosmetic) SKU: no FDA drug warnings apply; standard alcohol-containing-rinse caution (not recommended for young children who might swallow it, due to alcohol content). Outlast/Advanced (drug) SKUs: standard cetylpyridinium chloride or fluoride precautions apply respectively.

## Symptoms Treated
Bad breath/unpleasant taste (Classic, cosmetic masking effect only); gingivitis/plaque (Outlast, genuine antiplaque drug claim); cavities (Advanced, fluoride anticavity claim) — the specific SKU driving this database's 18% Cosmetic Mouthwashes share provides no therapeutic effect on any of these underlying conditions.
