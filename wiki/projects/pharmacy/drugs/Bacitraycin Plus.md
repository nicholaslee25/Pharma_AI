## Overview
**Ingredient-page gap flagged.** Confirmed via DailyMed: Bacitraycin Plus's own FDA OTC Drug Facts label lists **bacitracin zinc 500 units (first aid antibiotic) and pramoxine HCl 10 mg (external analgesic)** as active ingredients. The bacitracin component is covered by [[topical-antibiotics]] (single-agent bacitracin, neomycin-free — a narrower allergy-risk profile than Neosporin's triple-antibiotic formula per that page's NACDG patch-test data). **Pramoxine, however, is a topical anesthetic in a chemically distinct class from both benzocaine and lidocaine** (it is not an ester like benzocaine or an amide like lidocaine — pramoxine has its own separate structure) and is **not covered by [[topical-anesthetics]]**, whose scope is explicitly titled "Topical Anesthetics (Benzocaine / Lidocaine)." This is a real gap: Bacitraycin Plus's analgesic component has no dedicated evidence page in this database, and pramoxine's own safety profile (notably, whether it shares any of benzocaine's methemoglobinemia risk — it generally does not, pramoxine is not implicated in the Guay 2009 review's case breakdown) is not independently documented here.

## Pros
- Confirmed FDA Drug Facts label with two clearly disclosed active ingredients addressing both infection-risk (bacitracin) and pain (pramoxine) in one product
- Neomycin-free formula (bacitracin + pramoxine only) avoids neomycin's specific, well-documented contact-allergy/anaphylaxis risk that Neosporin Original carries
- Pramoxine is generally considered to have a favorable safety profile relative to benzocaine specifically (no documented disproportionate methemoglobinemia signal), though this is not independently verified against a dedicated ingredient page in this database

## Cons
- **No dedicated ingredient-evidence page exists for pramoxine in this database** — a real, flagged gap, not a silent assumption of safety or equivalence to lidocaine/benzocaine
- Bacitracin still carries its own well-documented contact-allergy risk (~6.9% NACDG patch-test prevalence, "Contact Allergen of the Year" 2003) per [[topical-antibiotics]] — the pramoxine addition does not change this
- No brand-specific clinical trial for the bacitracin+pramoxine combination specifically was identified beyond the general bacitracin evidence

## Use Cases
First aid to help prevent infection and for temporary relief of pain in minor cuts, scrapes, and burns.

## Allergies / Warnings
Per label: for external use only; discontinue and consult a doctor if condition persists/worsens or a rash/allergic reaction develops. See [[topical-antibiotics]] for bacitracin's documented contact-allergy data. Pramoxine-specific allergy data not independently documented in this database (flagged gap).

## Symptoms Treated
Minor cut/scrape/burn pain (pramoxine) plus infection-risk reduction (bacitracin) — a genuine dual-action product, unlike plain Neosporin/Polysporin which address infection risk only.

This should be treated as a missing-coverage note, not a contradiction — no incorrect claim currently exists about pramoxine, simply an absence of dedicated evidence.
