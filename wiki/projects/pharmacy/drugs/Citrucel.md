## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed: Citrucel is methylcellulose (2g per heaping tablespoon, powder form; tablet form also available) — matching [[fiber]]'s existing note that this is a DIFFERENT fiber type than psyllium/pectin, the only two types shown significantly effective in that page's cited 2022 meta-analysis. Label explicitly markets methylcellulose as "non-allergenic fiber," a real, genuine differentiator from Metamucil's psyllium (which carries an explicit allergy-alert warning per its own label) and Benefiber's wheat-derived content (a real wheat-allergy consideration flagged separately in this pass).

## Pros
- Confirmed FDA OTC label; labeled "non-allergenic," a genuine and verifiable differentiator from psyllium (real allergy-alert warning) and wheat dextrin (real wheat-allergy consideration) — a meaningful option specifically for fiber-supplement users with a known or suspected psyllium/wheat sensitivity
- Available in both powder and tablet forms, offering format flexibility not confirmed for every brand in this category

## Cons
- **Not psyllium or pectin** — per [[fiber]]'s own cited meta-analysis, methylcellulose was not one of the two fiber types shown to produce significant treatment-response improvement; consistent with the pattern found across most bulk-fiber brands in this database's research pass
- Per label: do not take if difficulty swallowing exists; must be mixed with at least 8oz water/fluid to avoid choking risk, same general bulk-fiber caution as Metamucil/FiberCon
- No Citrucel-brand-specific RCT was independently located in this pass; general methylcellulose efficacy evidence for constipation is not covered by [[fiber]]'s own reviewed meta-analysis source

## Use Cases
Relief of occasional constipation (bowel movement in 12-72 hours per label) — via methylcellulose's soluble-fiber mechanism, marketed specifically toward fiber-allergy-sensitive users.

## Allergies / Warnings
Per label: do not use with difficulty swallowing; mix with at least 8oz water/fluid to avoid choking. Labeled "non-allergenic" — a genuine, verifiable claim distinct from psyllium's own allergy-alert warning.

## Symptoms Treated
Occasional constipation — via methylcellulose's soluble-fiber mechanism, a different specific fiber type than Metamucil's psyllium, per [[fiber]].

## Brand-Specific Alignment Check
Confirms [[fiber]]'s existing note that Citrucel (methylcellulose) is not one of the meta-analysis-significant fiber types — the same pattern found for Benefiber, FiberCon, Mirafiber, Vitafusion Fiber Well, and Phillips' Fiber Good in this same research pass. Distinguishing positive: Citrucel's "non-allergenic" labeling is a real, accurate differentiator within this otherwise-mixed-evidence category.
