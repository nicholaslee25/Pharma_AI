## Overview
Confirmed via the same head-to-head study cited on [[Braun ThermoScan]] (30 healthy adults, cold-water-immersion protocol, Welch-Allyn oral gold-standard reference): the **MOBI infrared forehead thermometer** showed a **mean difference of -0.184°C** versus the gold standard — noticeably less accurate than Braun's tympanic reading (-0.044°C) in the same study, though MOBI's agreement was still better than several other devices tested (Withings, Wellworks, thermal imaging). A real, specific limitation found: MOBI's readings "did not change significantly between baseline and cold-water phase," meaning the device showed reduced sensitivity to actual body-temperature changes during the study's cooling/recovery protocol — a concrete, quantified responsiveness limitation beyond just a static bias offset. Smallest share (2%) of the five thermometer brands in this category.

## Pros
- Directly tested head-to-head against a gold-standard reference (not merely a class-level inference) — one of the more concretely evidenced brands in this category
- Outperformed several other devices in the same comparative study (Withings, Wellworks, thermal imaging), even though it underperformed Braun specifically
- Forehead/infrared design offers speed and non-invasiveness similar to other non-contact thermometer types

## Cons
- **Directly measured as less accurate than the category's market-leading brand** in the same head-to-head study: mean error -0.184°C (MOBI) vs. -0.044°C ([[Braun ThermoScan]]) — over 4x the deviation from the gold standard
- Documented reduced sensitivity to actual temperature change during the study's controlled cooling/recovery protocol — a real, specific finding beyond a simple constant-offset bias, suggesting potential lag or under-responsiveness to genuine temperature shifts
- Smallest market share (2%) in this category, consistent with (though not proven caused by) its comparatively weaker measured accuracy

## Use Cases
At-home forehead-based infrared temperature measurement for fever screening.

## Allergies / Warnings
Not a drug — no allergy profile. Given the documented tendency to underestimate temperature and show reduced responsiveness to real changes, a normal/afebrile MOBI reading in a clinically ill-appearing individual should be confirmed with a more accurate method (e.g. the tympanic or oral route) rather than assumed definitive.

## Symptoms Treated
Not applicable — a diagnostic device, not a treatment; used to detect fever.
