## Overview
Confirmed (manufacturer: Tender Corp, dba Adventure Ready Brands, Littleton NH): Natrapel is a **DEET-free, single-active-ingredient repellent — picaridin 20%** — across its core line (spray, pump, wipes), EPA-registered (Reg. Nos. 56575-14 and 56575-15 confirmed for specific SKUs), claiming up to 12-hour protection against mosquitoes and ticks. Unlike [[Off!]] and [[Cutter]], which each span DEET, picaridin, PMD, and essential-oil sub-lines, Natrapel presents a genuinely simpler single-ingredient brand identity — a real, positive contrast worth noting given how often multi-mechanism brand spread is flagged as a source of consumer confusion elsewhere in this database. See [[insect-repellents-deet-picaridin]] for the comparative DEET/picaridin efficacy evidence (140+ study review finding no significant performance difference between the two at equivalent concentrations) and picaridin's comparatively shorter real-world safety-surveillance history relative to DEET's documented (if EPA-uncausally-linked) pediatric seizure case reports.

Some product-line naming (e.g., "Natrapel 8 Hour" vs. "Natrapel Picaridin 12 Hour") suggests differing protection-duration claims across specific SKUs despite a shared 20% picaridin concentration — not independently reconciled in this pass, plausibly reflecting different delivery/spray formats rather than different active-ingredient content.

## Pros
- Single, consistent active ingredient (picaridin 20%) across the core product line — a simpler, more legible brand identity than the multi-mechanism spread seen in [[Off!]] and [[Cutter]]
- DEET-free, avoiding the documented pediatric seizure case-report history associated with DEET specifically (see [[insect-repellents-deet-picaridin]])
- Per the comparative 140+-study review cited on [[insect-repellents-deet-picaridin]], picaridin performs comparably to DEET at equivalent concentrations — this is not a lower-efficacy DEET alternative
- EPA-registered (not exempt/minimum-risk like [[Babyganics]]'s essential-oil line), meaning it has undergone actual EPA efficacy/safety review

## Cons
- Picaridin has a shorter overall real-world usage/post-market-surveillance history than DEET, per [[insect-repellents-deet-picaridin]] — fewer decades of accumulated safety data, though no comparable adverse-event signal has emerged in the sources reviewed
- Differing protection-duration claims ("8 Hour" vs. "12 Hour") across SKUs sharing the same 20% concentration were not independently reconciled in this pass
- No brand-specific clinical trial was sought, consistent with this database's FDA-label-centric methodology (repellent efficacy is an EPA, not FDA, question)
- Smallest recommendation share (7%) among the three EPA-registered "conventional" repellent brands in this category ([[Off!]] 58%, [[Cutter]] 16%, Natrapel 7%)

## Use Cases
Prevention of insect bites/stings (mosquitoes, ticks) via picaridin's olfactory-detection-blocking mechanism, up to a claimed 8-12 hours depending on SKU.

## Allergies / Warnings
Standard EPA label guidance for picaridin-containing products: apply to exposed skin/clothing as needed; avoid eyes and mucous membranes; wash treated skin after returning indoors; discontinue if irritation develops.

## Symptoms Treated
None — a preventive repellent, not a symptom-treatment product.
