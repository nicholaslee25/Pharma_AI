## Overview
TheraBreath's flagship marketing ingredient across its "Fresh Breath"-line products is **OXYD-8, a stabilized (non-chlorine-dioxide) form of sodium chlorite**, which the manufacturer states creates an oxygen-rich environment hostile to the anaerobic, sulfur-compound-producing bacteria that cause bad breath. No DailyMed Drug Facts label was located for this specific flagship formula in this pass — the DailyMed listings found for "TheraBreath" instead belong to clearly separate, differently-named sub-lines: **TheraBreath Anticavity Oral Rinse** (confirmed sodium fluoride, an anticavity drug per [[fluoride-toothpaste]]'s mechanism) and **TheraBreath Deep Clean Fresh Mint** (confirmed cetylpyridinium chloride, an antigingivitis/antiplaque drug). This pattern — a flagship cosmetic-marketed product plus separate genuine-drug sub-lines under one name — mirrors what was found for Scope in this same category.

## Pros
- Offers a choice of mechanism under one brand: cosmetic odor-neutralizing (flagship OXYD-8/sodium chlorite), anticavity fluoride, or antigingivitis/antiplaque cetylpyridinium chloride, depending on the specific sub-line chosen
- The Anticavity and Deep Clean sub-lines carry the respective real evidence bases already documented in this database for fluoride ([[fluoride-toothpaste]]) and CPC-type antiplaque agents
- Widely recommended specifically among pharmacists across three different Oral Care subcategories (Cosmetic Mouthwashes, Dry Mouth Therapy, Therapeutic Mouthwashes), suggesting broad perceived versatility

## Cons
- No independently-located clinical trial evidence supports the flagship OXYD-8/stabilized-sodium-chlorite mechanism's actual bad-breath-reduction efficacy — the manufacturer's own explanation of "creating an oxygen-rich environment" is a plausible-sounding but not independently trial-verified mechanistic claim in the sources gathered here
- The Dry Mouth Therapy share sits atop [[saliva-substitutes]]'s broader weak evidence base for topical dry-mouth therapies generally (Cochrane: "no strong evidence that any topical therapy is effective")
- "TheraBreath" spans at least three different active-ingredient profiles (none/cosmetic, fluoride, CPC) depending on sub-line — a consumer reaching for "TheraBreath" for gingivitis specifically needs the Deep Clean product, not the flagship Fresh Breath formula
- Manufacturer marketing explicitly contrasts its "stabilized" sodium chlorite against competitors' "unstable active chlorine dioxide," a claim not independently verified against a head-to-head trial in this pass

## Use Cases
General bad-breath odor reduction (flagship Fresh Breath line, cosmetic mechanism); dry mouth symptom relief (Dry Mouth Therapy share, likely the same moisturizing-rinse approach as other brands in that category); cavity prevention (Anticavity sub-line, fluoride); gingivitis/plaque reduction (Deep Clean sub-line, cetylpyridinium chloride).

## Allergies / Warnings
Flagship line: no FDA drug warnings identified (consistent with likely cosmetic/non-drug status). Anticavity sub-line: standard fluoride precautions (do not swallow, supervise children under 6, professional consultation for children under 2). Deep Clean sub-line: standard cetylpyridinium chloride antigingivitis-rinse precautions (supervise children 6-12, do not use under 6).

## Symptoms Treated
Bad breath/oral malodor (flagship mechanism, unverified independent trial evidence); dry mouth discomfort (moisturizing mechanism, weak class-wide evidence per [[saliva-substitutes]]); cavities (Anticavity fluoride sub-line); gingivitis/plaque (Deep Clean CPC sub-line).
