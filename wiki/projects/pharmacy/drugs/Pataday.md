## Appears In

## Overview
Confirmed: Pataday's (Alcon) own FDA labels list **olopatadine hydrochloride** as the active ingredient — sold OTC in two strengths, 0.2% (Once Daily Relief, ~16h duration) and 0.7% (Extra Strength, 24h duration), both dosed once daily. Olopatadine is a dual-acting H1-antihistamine/mast-cell stabilizer — see [[ophthalmic-antihistamines]] for the class evidence. Per a 2023 meta-analysis of 7 RCTs comparing olopatadine to ketotifen ([[SYSTANE ZADITOR]]/[[Alaway]]'s active), olopatadine showed significantly lower hyperemia (redness) than ketotifen but no significant difference in itching relief — meaning Pataday's category-leading share (35%, more than double the next brand) is not backed by a demonstrated itch-relief advantage over its main competitors, even though it may reduce redness somewhat more. No Pataday-brand-specific clinical trial beyond the general olopatadine class evidence was identified.

## Pros
- Category leader by a wide margin (35% share)
- Once-daily dosing (vs. twice-daily for ketotifen-based Zaditor/Alaway) — a real convenience advantage
- Per the cited meta-analysis, modestly better redness (hyperemia) reduction than ketotifen, though comparable itch relief

## Cons
- Two different OTC strengths (0.2% vs. 0.7%) exist under the same "Pataday" name with different duration claims — a consumer needs to check the specific SKU
- Per the cited meta-analysis, no itching-relief advantage over the cheaper, twice-daily ketotifen alternatives ([[SYSTANE ZADITOR]], [[Alaway]]) despite Pataday's much larger market share
- No brand-specific outcome trial beyond the general olopatadine-vs-ketotifen class comparison exists

## Use Cases
Temporary relief of itchy eyes due to pollen, ragweed, grass, animal hair/dander (allergic conjunctivitis) in adults and children 2+.

## Allergies / Warnings
Per label: do not use if allergic to olopatadine; not for use with contact lenses if eye is red; discontinue and consult a doctor if irritation worsens or persists.

## Symptoms Treated
Itchy eyes from seasonal/environmental allergens; some redness reduction per the class mechanism.
