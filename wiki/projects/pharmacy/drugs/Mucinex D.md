## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed: Mucinex D Maximum Strength = **guaifenesin 1200mg + pseudoephedrine HCl 120mg**, extended-release, dosed every 12 hours. This resolves the earlier hedge's "not independently confirmed" caveat on the pseudoephedrine component — it is genuinely pseudoephedrine, not phenylephrine. Touches [[guaifenesin]] and [[pseudoephedrine]] at specific, now-known doses, both consistent with each ingredient page's own confirmed Mucinex/Sudafed reference doses.

## Pros
- Pseudoephedrine component carries [[pseudoephedrine]]'s "moderate-to-high" evidence grade for nasal decongestion — a stronger evidentiary basis than the many phenylephrine-based combination products in this batch (DayQuil, Theraflu, Tylenol Sinus Severe, etc.)
- Guaifenesin dose (1200mg) matches the exact dose tested in the Bennett 2015 brand-specific RCT on [[guaifenesin]] — same evidentiary advantage already noted for Mucinex DM
- 12-hour extended-release dosing for both components reduces pill burden

## Cons
- Because it contains pseudoephedrine, this product is subject to behind-the-counter purchase restrictions (ID-logged, quantity-limited), unlike phenylephrine-based combination competitors
- Must track total daily guaifenesin (2,400mg/day ceiling) and pseudoephedrine intake if combining with other Mucinex or Sudafed products
- Sympathomimetic cardiovascular cautions apply (per [[pseudoephedrine]])

## Use Cases
Combined chest congestion/mucus thinning and nasal decongestion.

## Allergies / Warnings
Not for children <12y. Do not use with MAOIs. Ask a doctor before use with high blood pressure, heart disease, thyroid disease, or diabetes. Do not exceed 2,400mg/day guaifenesin across all guaifenesin products combined.

## Symptoms Treated
Chest congestion, nasal/sinus congestion.
