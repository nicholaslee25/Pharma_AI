## Appears In

## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed/manufacturer labeling: Enfamil Poly-Vi-Sol is a **liquid infant/toddler multivitamin drop** (9 vitamins: A, C, D, E, B6, B12, niacin, riboflavin, thiamin), 1mL daily serving via dropper, marketed for infants ~4-6 months transitioning to solids. **Real brand-name/SKU distinction worth flagging**: the base "Poly-Vi-Sol" formulation contains **no iron**, but a separately-sold "Poly-Vi-Sol WITH Iron" product exists under the same core brand name — a caregiver could easily select the wrong SKU for a child who specifically needs iron supplementation (e.g., breastfed infants ≥4-6 months, per general AAP iron-supplementation guidance) without realizing the base product doesn't provide it. The confirmed inactive-ingredient list also includes **sulfites** (used as an antioxidant stabilizer for the iron in the "With Iron" variant) — a real, labeled allergen for sulfite-sensitive individuals, worth noting since sulfite sensitivity is an under-recognized pediatric allergy consideration.

## Pros
- Liquid dropper format is appropriate for the pre-chewable-tablet infant age range this product targets, unlike most other brands in this category (Flintstones, One A Day Kids, etc.) which are tablet/gummy formats for older children
- Long-established brand (Mead Johnson/Enfamil) with a straightforward, non-proprietary-blend ingredient list
- Base formulation's iron-free status may be appropriate for infants already receiving adequate iron from iron-fortified formula or complementary foods — a real potential positive depending on the individual infant's diet, not a universal negative

## Cons
- **The "Poly-Vi-Sol" vs. "Poly-Vi-Sol WITH Iron" brand-name distinction is a genuine, clinically consequential formulation split** — infants specifically needing iron supplementation (exclusively/predominantly breastfed infants past ~4-6 months, per general pediatric iron-supplementation guidance) would not receive it from the base product; this is a real caregiver-facing risk of selecting the wrong SKU under a near-identical brand name, distinct from the many "different active ingredient" brand-splits found elsewhere in this database
- Contains sulfites (antioxidant stabilizer, confirmed on the ingredient list reviewed) — a labeled allergen consideration
- No brand-specific pediatric RCT located; relies on per-nutrient ingredient-page evidence ([[vitamin-d]], [[iron]] if the With-Iron variant applies) rather than a trial of this specific combination product
- Per [[multivitamins]]'s general evidence review: broad disease-prevention claims for multivitamin use are not established in a large modern RCT (adult COSMOS data; no comparable infant-outcome trial exists)

## Use Cases
Vitamin-gap supplementation for infants/toddlers transitioning to solid foods, particularly those with limited dietary vitamin intake; the With-Iron variant specifically for infants needing supplemental dietary iron.

## Allergies / Warnings
Shake well before use; use only the enclosed dropper; do not exceed the recommended 1mL daily serving without a doctor's direction; sulfite-sensitive individuals should note the sulfite content; verify which specific SKU (with or without iron) matches the child's actual nutritional need before purchase.

## Symptoms Treated
General infant/toddler nutrient-gap correction; not a treatment for any specific disease or symptom.

## Brand-Specific Alignment Check
