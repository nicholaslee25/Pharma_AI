# Zilactin

## Appears In
Cold Sore Treatments - 2%

## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed: plain "Zilactin" (Early Relief Cold Sore Gel and the original bioadhesive gel formula) uses **benzyl alcohol 10%** as its active ingredient — a topical anesthetic/antiseptic chemically distinct from both benzocaine (ester) and lidocaine (amide), forming a bioadhesive protective film over the sore that the manufacturer states can provide pain relief and dryness protection for up to 6 hours. This is a materially different active ingredient from its sibling product **Zilactin-B** (benzocaine gel, marketed for canker/mouth sores rather than cold sores specifically) — "Zilactin" as a brand name spans two different anesthetic chemical classes depending on the specific product, another instance of this database's recurring one-brand-multiple-formulations pattern.

## Pros
- Benzyl alcohol is not implicated in the same disproportionate methemoglobinemia risk documented for benzocaine on [[topical-anesthetics]] (per the Guay 2009 review's case breakdown, which found benzocaine and prilocaine, not benzyl alcohol, driving the overwhelming majority of cases)
- Bioadhesive film-forming mechanism provides a physical protective barrier in addition to anesthetic action, similar in concept to GUM Canker-X's barrier approach but with an added active anesthetic ingredient
- Manufacturer-stated up to 6-hour duration of relief from a single application, longer than the typical benzocaine gel's ~4-times-daily reapplication schedule

## Cons
- No dedicated antiviral mechanism — benzyl alcohol does not target herpes simplex virus replication/entry the way Abreva's docosanol does; relief is symptomatic/protective only
- Contains 65% alcohol by volume in some Zilactin formulations (per the related Zilactin-B label), which may cause stinging on application to already-irritated or open sores — a real tolerability tradeoff not present in non-alcohol-based competitors
- No brand-specific clinical trial evidence was identified for benzyl alcohol's effect on cold sore healing time or duration, beyond the product's own marketing claims
- "Zilactin" spans two different active-anesthetic-ingredient classes (benzyl alcohol vs. benzocaine) across its Cold Sore and canker-sore-focused "Zilactin-B" sub-brand — do not assume uniform composition

## Use Cases
Temporary relief of pain and protection via a bioadhesive film for cold sores/fever blisters, canker sores, and other mouth/gum irritations.

## Allergies / Warnings
Per label: do not use for more than 7 days unless directed by a physician or dentist; avoid contact with the eyes; seek medical attention if symptoms do not improve within 7 days or if swelling, rash, or fever develops.

## Symptoms Treated
Cold sore/fever blister pain and dryness, via benzyl alcohol's local anesthetic/antiseptic action plus a protective bioadhesive barrier — no antiviral action.
