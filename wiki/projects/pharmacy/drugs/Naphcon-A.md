## Appears In
Ophthalmic Antihistamines/Decongestants - 13%

## Overview
Confirmed: Naphcon-A's own FDA/DailyMed label lists **naphazoline hydrochloride 0.025% (redness reliever) + pheniramine maleate 0.3% (antihistamine)** — a genuine combination product treating both redness and itching in one drop, unlike the pure antihistamines ([[Pataday]], [[SYSTANE ZADITOR]], [[Alaway]]). Per [[ophthalmic-decongestants]], naphazoline is a non-selective alpha-1/alpha-2 adrenergic agonist carrying the same well-documented rebound-hyperemia risk as [[Clear Eyes]] and [[Visine]]'s actives — a real safety consideration not shared by the selective-alpha-2 [[Lumify]] or by the pure-antihistamine competitors in this category. Third-largest share (13%). No Naphcon-A-brand-specific clinical trial beyond general naphazoline/pheniramine pharmacology was identified.

## Pros
- Combination formulation treats both redness and itching in a single product, a real convenience for users with both symptoms
- Established, decades-long market history for both actives
- Nearly identical formulation to [[Opcon-A]] gives consumers a familiar alternative if one is unavailable

## Cons
- **Rebound hyperemia risk** with regular/prolonged naphazoline use, per [[ophthalmic-decongestants]] — the same risk flagged on [[Clear Eyes]] and [[Visine]], not shared by [[Lumify]]'s selective-alpha-2 mechanism
- Not intended for regular, sustained daily use — masks redness rather than treating an underlying allergic cause
- No brand-specific outcome trial exists beyond general class pharmacology

## Use Cases
Temporary relief of redness and itching due to ragweed, pollen, grass, animal dander/hair. Not intended for prolonged daily use.

## Allergies / Warnings
Per label: do not use for more than 72 hours without consulting a doctor; avoid in narrow-angle glaucoma or with MAOI use; discontinue if pain, vision changes, or symptoms worsen; rebound redness risk with overuse.

## Symptoms Treated
Eye redness (vasoconstrictor mechanism) and itching (antihistamine mechanism) from allergic conjunctivitis.
