## Appears In
Liquid Bandages - 60%

## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed: New-Skin (both liquid/brush and spray forms) uses **benzethonium chloride 0.2%** as its sole active ingredient, labeled "First Aid Antiseptic." The flexible protective film itself comes from an inactive solvent base (nitrocellulose, amyl/butyl/ethyl acetate, camphor, SD alcohol 3-C) that evaporates on application — meaning the FDA-recognized therapeutic action is antisepsis, while the "bandage" film-forming effect is technically a delivery-vehicle byproduct rather than a labeled active ingredient. This places New-Skin in the "antiseptic film-former" sub-class of [[liquid-bandages]], mechanistically distinct from cyanoacrylate-based wound-closure liquid bandages (e.g., the RCT-tested product referenced on that page) — New-Skin's specific chemistry was not independently tested in that RCT. New-Skin is the dominant brand in this category by a wide margin (60% share).

## Pros
- Confirmed FDA Drug Facts label; simple single-active-ingredient formulation (benzethonium chloride 0.2%)
- By far the largest share (60%) in Liquid Bandages, reflecting broad consumer/pharmacist familiarity
- Practical for awkward-to-bandage areas (fingertips, knuckles) where adhesive strips don't adhere well

## Cons
- No liquid-bandage-specific RCT for New-Skin's own antiseptic-film-former chemistry was located — the one real RCT identified on [[liquid-bandages]] (Buchanan/Eaglstein 2002, PMID 11896780) tested a cyanoacrylate wound-closure product, a different chemistry than New-Skin's benzethonium chloride/nitrocellulose formula; New-Skin's evidence rests on benzethonium chloride's general antiseptic profile rather than a liquid-bandage-specific trial
- **Real, specific label warning: "intentional inhalation misuse can be harmful or fatal"** — reflects the volatile solvent base (acetates + SD alcohol), a genuine safety consideration distinct from the antiseptic ingredient itself, and worth flagging given the product's easy accessibility
- Flammable; do not use over large body areas, more than 1 week without medical direction, on infected/draining wounds, over sutures, or combined with other first aid creams/lotions
- Will stain floors/countertops/finished surfaces — a real practical/handling caution from the label
- No brand-specific clinical trial for New-Skin specifically was identified beyond the general benzethonium chloride antiseptic evidence

## Use Cases
Aids in preventing bacterial contamination in minor cuts and scrapes during initial wound care, while forming a flexible, waterproof protective film over the wound.

## Allergies / Warnings
Per label: external use only; flammable; do not use in eyes/mucous membranes, over large body areas, more than 1 week without medical direction, on infected/draining wounds, over sutures, or combined with other first aid products; ask a doctor first for deep/puncture wounds, animal bites, serious bleeding, diabetes, circulation problems, or severe burns; do not store above 120°F; **intentional inhalation misuse can be harmful or fatal**; stop use and consult a doctor if condition worsens/persists or infection develops; keep out of reach of children, contact Poison Control if swallowed.

## Symptoms Treated
Not a pain/itch-relief product — provides antiseptic action (bacterial contamination prevention) plus a physical protective/waterproof barrier over minor cuts and scrapes.
