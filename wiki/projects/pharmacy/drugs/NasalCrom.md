## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed: NasalCrom's active ingredient is **cromolyn sodium 5.2mg per spray**, labeled as a "nasal allergy symptom controller." This is a genuinely different drug class from every other product in this batch — cromolyn sodium is a **mast-cell stabilizer**, not a vasoconstrictor decongestant (unlike Afrin/oxymetazoline or Neo-Synephrine/phenylephrine) and not a corticosteroid (unlike FLONASE). No ingredient page in this database covers cromolyn sodium — a genuine data gap, since this ingredient's mechanism and safety profile can't be checked against anything already in this vault. This is a new, previously-unlinked confirmation (no prior pointer existed). Label saved at `raw/primary-sources/brands/nasalcrom/dailymed-nasalcrom.md`.

## Pros
- **Not a vasoconstrictor** — does not carry the rebound congestion (rhinitis medicamentosa) risk that dominates the safety profile of every oxymetazoline/phenylephrine product in this same category (see [[oxymetazoline]]) — genuinely safe for long-term daily use, unlike its category-mates
- Labeled safe for ages 2 and up, broader pediatric eligibility than most decongestant sprays (which restrict to ≥6y)
- Preventive mechanism (mast-cell stabilization) may suit allergy sufferers who can anticipate exposure (seasonal allergies) and start use before symptoms begin, a different use pattern than as-needed decongestants

## Cons
- Works prophylactically/preventively rather than for rapid symptom relief — likely slower onset than a vasoconstrictor decongestant, though this pass did not locate specific onset-time data to quantify the difference
- No dedicated ingredient page exists in this database to provide efficacy grade, trial data, or a fuller safety profile — this is a real research gap, not a claim that the drug lacks evidence
- Its placement in both "Intranasal Allergy Products" (2%) and "Decongestants, Nasal Spray" (10%) categories in this database's source data is itself slightly misleading, since cromolyn is not a decongestant in the vasoconstrictor sense — worth the reader knowing this before assuming it works the same way as its category-mates

## Use Cases
Prevention and relief of nasal allergy symptoms (sneezing, runny nose, itchy/stuffy nose) — allergic rhinitis specifically, used preventively before or at onset of allergen exposure.

## Allergies / Warnings
Ages 2 and older. Ask a doctor before use for children under 2. No FDA-mandated rebound-congestion warning since this is not a vasoconstrictor — a genuinely different risk profile from most of its category-mates.

## Symptoms Treated
Sneezing, runny nose, itchy nose, nasal congestion associated with allergic rhinitis.
