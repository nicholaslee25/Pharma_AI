## Appears In
Jock Itch/Antifungal Products - 5%

## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed (manufacturer: Kobayashi Healthcare International, Inc.): FUNGICURE spans **two chemically distinct antifungal actives** across its product line — another "one brand, multiple mechanisms" case in this category:

- **FUNGICURE Intensive / Liquid Gel / Manicure & Pedicure Liquid Gel: clotrimazole** — the same azole active as [[Lotrimin]]'s base "AF" line (see [[clotrimazole]]), marketed as effective against 6 dermatophyte species (E. floccosum, T. mentagrophytes, M. canis, M. gypseum, T. tonsurans, T. rubrum).
- **A separate FUNGICURE sub-line: undecylenic acid** — an older fatty-acid antifungal mechanism (prevents fungal growth on skin via membrane disruption). **Ingredient page [[undecylenic-acid]] built 2026-08-27** (Toe/Foot Antifungal Products research, prompted by [[Fungi-Nail]] using it as a primary rather than secondary active) — documents real but dated placebo-controlled trial evidence and a materially important nuance: FDA labeling for undecylenic-acid nail products claims only to improve fungal-nail *appearance*, not to cure onychomycosis.

No FUNGICURE-brand-specific clinical trial was located in this pass; the clotrimazole sub-line's evidence sits at the [[clotrimazole]] ingredient level.

## Pros
- Confirmed FDA Drug Facts labels across multiple SKUs; the clotrimazole line's broad multi-species antifungal claim is consistent with clotrimazole's established mechanism
- Offers both an azole (clotrimazole) and a fatty-acid (undecylenic acid) mechanism under one brand, giving consumers an alternate option if one doesn't suit them
- Manicure & Pedicure-specific liquid gel format addresses a real, distinct application need (nail-adjacent skin) not offered by every competing brand

## Cons
- **Two distinct active-ingredient mechanisms sold under one brand name** without a unifying "FUNGICURE = X ingredient" identity — a consumer must check the specific product, not just the brand name
- No FUNGICURE-brand-specific clinical trial was located; evidence for the clotrimazole line is at the general [[clotrimazole]] ingredient level, and undecylenic acid's evidence base was not independently reviewed in this pass (flagged gap)
- Per [[clotrimazole]]'s general evidence: not effective for onychomycosis (nail fungus) or scalp infections — a real limitation despite the brand's "Manicure & Pedicure" nail-adjacent product naming, which could imply nail-fungus efficacy it does not actually deliver
- Smallest recommendation share (5%) among this category's antifungal-drug brands (excluding Zeasorb, whose mechanism is separately researched)

## Use Cases
Treatment of athlete's foot, jock itch, and ringworm via clotrimazole's azole antifungal mechanism (4-week course for ringworm/athlete's foot, 2-week course for jock itch, per standard clotrimazole dosing); a separate undecylenic-acid-based sub-line for similar indications via a fatty-acid mechanism.

## Allergies / Warnings
Per [[clotrimazole]] (for the azole sub-line): external use only; avoid eyes; azole hypersensitivity; not effective for nail/scalp infections; seek medical care if no improvement within labeled duration. Undecylenic acid sub-line warnings not independently reviewed in this pass.

## Symptoms Treated
Itching, burning, cracking, and scaling from athlete's foot, jock itch, and ringworm — mechanism depending on which FUNGICURE sub-line (clotrimazole vs. undecylenic acid) is used.
