## Appears In
Topical Poison Ivy/Oak Remedies - 7%

## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed: **calamine 14.0% (skin protectant) + benzyl alcohol 10.5% (external analgesic) + diphenhydramine HCl 2.0% (external analgesic)**, for temporary relief of pain/itching from poison ivy/oak/sumac, insect bites, and minor skin irritations, and to dry oozing/weeping poison-plant lesions. This is a three-active-ingredient combination — more ingredients than most competitors in this category (Caladryl and CeraVe Itch Relief each use one primary anesthetic; Ivarest layers calamine's astringent effect, benzyl alcohol's mild analgesic/preservative effect, and diphenhydramine's antihistamine-derived anesthetic effect together). A separate DailyMed listing shows some Ivarest SKUs use **zinc oxide** rather than calamine as the skin-protectant component — a minor protectant-ingredient variation across the product line, not a change to the anesthetic mechanism.

**Real safety cross-reference, per [[topical-diphenhydramine]]:** Ivarest's label explicitly warns "do not use with other diphenhydramine products (oral or topical)" — the same warning documented on that ingredient page as mapping to a real, case-report-documented (including one pediatric fatality) systemic-absorption toxicity risk. Ivarest's 2% diphenhydramine concentration matches Benadryl Extra Strength's topical concentration, meaning the same overdose-risk mechanism applies here. No Ivarest-brand-specific clinical trial was located; evidence rests on the general calamine/benzyl-alcohol/diphenhydramine literature.

## Pros
- Confirmed, layered three-ingredient formula addressing drying (calamine), mild analgesia (benzyl alcohol), and antihistamine-based anesthesia (diphenhydramine) in one product
- Label explicitly instructs "apply as soon as possible after exposure," a genuinely proactive-use framing relative to some competitors
- Consistent formulation across the calamine-based SKU, with a zinc-oxide variant available for users preferring a non-tinting protectant

## Cons
- **Diphenhydramine 2% carries the same systemic-absorption toxicity risk documented on [[topical-diphenhydramine]]** (including a documented pediatric fatality) if applied to large body areas, damaged/eczematous skin, or combined with any other diphenhydramine-containing product (oral Benadryl, other topical diphenhydramine SKUs, diphenhydramine-based sleep aids) — Ivarest's own label carries this exact warning, and it is not a generic caution
- Explicitly labeled not to use on chicken pox or measles without a doctor's direction, and not on large body areas — real, specific-use restrictions beyond typical "ask a doctor" boilerplate
- No Ivarest-brand-specific clinical trial was located
- Calamine can stain skin/clothing (shared limitation with Caladryl's classic formula)

## Use Cases
Temporary relief of pain and itching from poison ivy, poison oak, and poison sumac rashes, insect bites, and minor skin irritations; dries oozing/weeping poison-plant lesions. Best applied as soon as possible after exposure per label directions.

## Allergies / Warnings
Per label: for external use only; do not use on large body areas; do not use with other diphenhydramine-containing products (oral or topical) — a real overdose-risk interaction, see [[topical-diphenhydramine]]; ask a doctor before use on chicken pox or measles; avoid eye contact; stop use and consult a doctor if condition worsens or symptoms persist beyond 7 days or recur.

## Symptoms Treated
Pain, itching, and oozing/weeping skin reactions from poison ivy/oak/sumac; insect bites; minor skin irritations.

## Brand-Specific Alignment Check
No mislabeling found — the three confirmed active ingredients match the "temporary pain/itch relief and drying" marketing claim. The notable finding is safety-related rather than a mismatch: this brand's 2% diphenhydramine concentration directly implicates the systemic-absorption toxicity risk already documented in detail on [[topical-diphenhydramine]], and Ivarest's own label reflects this with explicit combination and large-area-use warnings.
