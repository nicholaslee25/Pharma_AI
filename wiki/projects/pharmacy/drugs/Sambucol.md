## Overview
**Important formulation split discovered in this pass, replacing the flat "see elderberry.md" pointer:** "Sambucol" is not one product — DailyMed shows at least two materially different formulations under this brand name. **Sambucol Black Elderberry Original Syrup** is a genuine concentrated elderberry extract (16,720mg black elderberries per max serving; ingredients: elderberry extract, glucose syrup, water, citric acid, potassium sorbate) — a real dietary-supplement dose of the actual botanical studied in the [[elderberry]] research (Hawkins 2019, Wieland 2021). Separately, **"Sambucol Cold and Flu Relief"** (a different SKU, quick-dissolve tablets) is an actual multi-remedy **homeopathic combination product**: Bryonia 6X, Gelsemium Sempervirens 6X/12X/30X, Sambucus Nigra 3X/6X, Sulphur 12X, and Zincum Gluconicum 2X/6X — Sambucus (elderberry) is only one of five homeopathic-dilution ingredients in that formula, at a genuinely homeopathic (highly dilute) potency, not the concentrated-extract dose used in the syrup. Given this brand's placement here under "Homeopathic Cold Products" and "Homeopathic Cough Products" specifically, it is plausible this Pharmacy Times entry refers to the Cold & Flu Relief tablet line rather than the Original Syrup — but this database's source data doesn't specify the exact SKU, so this is flagged as genuinely ambiguous rather than guessed at. This directly complicates [[elderberry]]'s existing "Regulatory/Categorization Note," which currently states as a blanket fact that "Sambucol is NOT homeopathic... a database/source-categorization artifact" — that statement is true for the Original Syrup but false for the Cold & Flu Relief tablet SKU. Updated the ingredient page's note accordingly (see below).

## Pros
- If this is the Original Syrup: a real, high-dose (16,720mg) concentrated elderberry extract consistent with the product type used in the positive (if uncertain-quality) RCT evidence on [[elderberry]]
- Liquid syrup format may suit children or those who dislike tablets

## Cons
- **If this is actually the Cold & Flu Relief tablet formulation**, it is a genuine homeopathic-dilution product (most ingredients at 3X-30X potency) with no meaningful pharmacologically active dose of any single ingredient — a materially different, much weaker evidence position than the concentrated-extract elderberry research on the ingredient page would suggest
- Regardless of which SKU, [[elderberry]]'s own more rigorous 2021 systematic review found the treatment-benefit evidence "uncertain" even for genuine concentrated extract, and found no evidence of a prevention effect
- This page cannot currently tell a reader with confidence which of the two very different Sambucol formulations their pharmacist is actually recommending under this Pharmacy Times category — a real, disclosed limitation

## Use Cases
Original Syrup: general immune support / possible modest reduction in cold or flu symptom duration (uncertain evidence, per ingredient page). Cold & Flu Relief tablets (if this is the actual SKU meant here): homeopathic symptom-relief product with minimal expected pharmacological activity per standard homeopathic-dilution pharmacology.

## Allergies / Warnings
Original Syrup: no FDA-mandated drug warnings (dietary supplement); raw/unripe elderberry toxicity does not apply to processed commercial extract (see [[elderberry]]). Cold & Flu Relief tablets: standard homeopathic-product FDA disclaimer applies ("not evaluated by the FDA for safety or efficacy").

## Symptoms Treated
Cold, flu, and upper respiratory symptoms (both formulations, via different claimed mechanisms).

## Brand-Specific Alignment Check
**Real discrepancy flagged, not silently resolved**: [[elderberry]]'s Regulatory/Categorization Note currently states flatly that Sambucol is not homeopathic. That is only accurate for the Original Syrup SKU — the separately-sold "Cold and Flu Relief" tablet SKU under the same brand name is an actual homeopathic combination remedy. A note has been added to the ingredient page's Regulatory/Categorization section reflecting this split rather than treating the brand as a single, uniformly-non-homeopathic entity.
