## Overview
**Missing-ingredient-page finding.** Confirmed via DailyMed: Tagamet HB 200 is cimetidine 200mg — a genuinely different molecule from Pepcid's famotidine, despite both being filed under the same "H2 Receptor Antagonists" category and this database having only a [[famotidine]] ingredient page (no dedicated cimetidine page exists). This matters beyond naming: cimetidine has a materially different drug-interaction profile than famotidine or ranitidine within the same H2RA class — it is a notable inhibitor of multiple cytochrome P450 enzymes (CYP1A2, CYP2C9, CYP2D6, CYP3A4), most clinically documented via a well-established interaction with **warfarin** (reduced warfarin clearance, raised INR risk), an interaction famotidine and ranitidine do not share to the same degree (ranitidine binds CYP450 4-10x less avidly than cimetidine per pharmacology literature). A consumer assuming all "H2 Receptor Antagonists category" brands are interchangeable would be missing a real, clinically significant difference specific to this brand's active ingredient.

## Pros
- Confirmed FDA OTC label; established H2RA mechanism (acid suppression via H2-receptor blockade), same general class-level relief pattern as famotidine
- Long OTC track record (cimetidine was the first H2RA approved, in the 1970s) — extensive real-world use history

## Cons
- **No dedicated cimetidine ingredient page exists in this database** — a genuine data gap, since cimetidine's CYP450-interaction profile is materially more complex than famotidine's (the only H2RA ingredient page this database currently has) and should not be assumed equivalent
- **Real, clinically significant CYP450 drug-interaction risk not present to the same degree with Pepcid (famotidine)** — most notably with warfarin (reduced clearance, elevated INR, bleeding risk with concurrent use, requiring close INR monitoring if combined) — this is the single most important brand-specific distinction in this category and is not surfaced by [[famotidine]]'s own drug-interaction section, since that page is famotidine-specific
- Smallest recommendation share of any brand in either category it appears in (1% Acid Reducers, 9% H2 Receptor Antagonists) — consistent with cimetidine's real-world decline in favor of famotidine, likely at least partly attributable to this interaction burden, though this database did not independently confirm that causal claim
- No Tagamet-HB-specific RCT beyond general cimetidine pharmacology was independently located in this pass

## Use Cases
Relief and prevention of heartburn associated with acid indigestion and sour stomach — same general use case as Pepcid, via the H2-receptor-antagonist mechanism, but with cimetidine as the specific molecule rather than famotidine.

## Allergies / Warnings
Per label: do not use if allergic to cimetidine or other acid reducers. **Ask a doctor or pharmacist before use if taking any prescription drug** — the label itself flags this broad interaction risk explicitly, consistent with cimetidine's real CYP450-inhibition profile. Stop use and see a doctor if needed for more than 14 days. Pregnant/breastfeeding: ask a health professional.

## Symptoms Treated
Heartburn, acid indigestion, sour stomach — via H2-receptor blockade, mechanistically similar to famotidine's action described on [[famotidine]] but via a different specific molecule (cimetidine) with a distinct interaction profile.
