## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed: Chestal spans multiple homeopathic formulations. Chestal Cold and Cough Honey syrup = Solanum dulcamara, Ferrosoferric phosphate, Goldenseal (Hydrastis canadensis), Potassium dichromate, Strychnos nux-vomica seed (9C HPUS potencies cited for the Hydrastis/Kali bichromicum/Nux vomica components at 22.23mg each). Chestal Adult Pellets = a different nine-ingredient combination (antimony potassium tartrate, bryonia alba root, protortonia cacti, drosera rotundifolia, ipecac, rumex crispus root, spongia officinalis, pulsatilla vulgaris, lobaria pulmonaria). This confirms the general framing on [[homeopathic-cold-products]] with concrete, brand-specific ingredient lists across two genuinely different Chestal formulations (syrup vs. pellets). ## Pros
- Same manufacturer (Boiron) as Oscillococcinum and ColdCalm, with consistent transparent homeopathic labeling
- Multiple formats (syrup, pellets) and pediatric variants (Chestal Kids/Children's) let users choose based on age and preference

## Cons
- Same class-wide evidence limitation documented on [[homeopathic-cold-products]]: no convincing evidence of clinically meaningful benefit at these dilutions, with the Hawke 2018 Cochrane review's quality-vs-benefit inverse relationship applying equally here
- The syrup and pellet formulations use entirely different ingredient sets under the same "Chestal" name — a consumer should not assume interchangeability between Chestal syrup and Chestal pellets beyond the shared brand name and homeopathic category
- Nine-ingredient pellet combination makes attributing any claimed effect to a specific component especially difficult
