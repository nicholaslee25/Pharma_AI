## Overview
**Discrepancy flagged — FDA regulatory finding:** iVizia's (Théa Pharma) own FDA label lists **povidone** as its official active ingredient, but its formulation also contains sodium hyaluronate (hyaluronic acid) and trehalose, listed on the label as *inactive* ingredients. On **July 9, 2025, the FDA sent Théa Pharma a warning letter** determining that iVizia is an **unapproved new drug and misbranded product** under FDCA sections 505(a) and 301(d): hyaluronic acid and trehalose are not permitted active ingredients under the FDA's OTC ophthalmic demulcent monograph (M018), yet the company's own marketing (phrases like "nature's moisturizer" and claims the product "improves comfort during computer work") described these two "inactive" ingredients in ways that FDA determined implied real therapeutic/pharmacological activity — effectively marketing them as active ingredients while labeling them as inactive. This is a real, dated regulatory enforcement action, not a hypothetical concern. **Clarification: this is a warning letter, not a product recall** — as of the most recent source checked (October 2025), iVizia remains on sale (temporarily pulled from some e-commerce platforms after payment processors flagged the FDA action, but available directly and through major retailers), and the manufacturer has been revising marketing language to comply. The FDA gave Théa Pharma 15 business days to respond, warning that failure to adequately address the matter could lead to seizure/injunction action. The same July 2025 warning-letter wave also named two smaller eye-drop brands not tracked in this database (Covalent Medical's Revitalize Energizing Eye Drops, containing undisclosed-as-active menthol/camphor/caffeine/taurine; and Revitalize Energy Inc.'s FOCUS PI Post-Injection Eye Drops) — establishing this as a genuine FDA compliance-enforcement pattern in the OTC eye-drop category during 2025, not an isolated iVizia-specific issue. [[Optase]], also tracked in this database, separately received its own FDA warning letter (~August 28, 2025) on related grounds — see that brand's page.

## Pros
- Povidone (the actual labeled active ingredient) is a recognized OTC ophthalmic demulcent with an established safety record
- Trehalose and hyaluronic acid, while not FDA-monograph-permitted as active ingredients, are backed by independent published research (e.g. trehalose/sodium hyaluronate combinations studied in post-cataract ocular surface disorders, PMC8364531) suggesting real mechanistic plausibility even though the FDA has not approved marketing them as therapeutic actives in this monograph category
- Manufacturer has reportedly revised marketing language in response to the warning letter rather than ignoring it, per industry trade press

## Cons
- **The core marketing claim that distinguished iVizia from generic lubricants (hyaluronic acid + trehalose as therapeutic actives) is exactly what the FDA determined makes it an unapproved, misbranded drug** — this is the single clearest, most concrete "drug-free/unapproved-active marketing claim contradicted by regulatory action" pattern found in this research pass
- A consumer relying on iVizia's pre-2025 marketing claims about HA/trehalose therapeutic benefit was relying on claims FDA has since determined are not permitted for this product category
- [[artificial-tears]]'s general lubricant-class evidence does not cover this brand-specific regulatory issue — a reader consulting only the ingredient page would not learn about this finding

## Use Cases
Temporary relief of dry-eye discomfort via the labeled povidone active ingredient; the marketed benefits attributed to hyaluronic acid/trehalose are the subject of the FDA finding above and should be weighed accordingly.

## Allergies / Warnings
Per label: standard OTC lubricant cautions (discontinue and see a doctor for eye pain, vision changes, or symptoms persisting beyond 72 hours). No indication of a safety (as opposed to regulatory-classification) concern in the sources reviewed — the FDA action concerns unauthorized marketing/classification, not a reported safety signal with the ingredients themselves.

## Symptoms Treated
Dry eye discomfort (via povidone, the FDA-recognized active ingredient); other claimed benefits (from HA/trehalose) are not FDA-recognized therapeutic claims for this product category per the 2025 warning letter.
