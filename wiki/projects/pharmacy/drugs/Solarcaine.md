## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed: Solarcaine Burn Pain Relief Spray uses **lidocaine HCl 4%** (external analgesic) — placing it on the lower-methemoglobinemia-risk side of [[topical-anesthetics]]'s benzocaine/lidocaine divide, a genuinely favorable finding for the category leader (36% share). A separate "Solarcaine Cool Aloe Spray" SKU exists per DailyMed's product listing but its specific formulation could not be independently confirmed in this pass. **Historical FDA note (flagged, not currently active):** a December 1988 FDA Pink Sheet report describes a Solarcaine "Severe Sunburn Relief Spray" product as "seriously misbranded," reflecting an FDA finding that a lidocaine/aloe vera combination lacked, at that time, substantial evidence of Generally Recognized As Safe and Effective (GRASE) status specifically for SEVERE sunburn. This is a 38-year-old regulatory event; the full resolution was not independently retrieved, and the current DailyMed label reviewed here does not make a "severe" sunburn claim — flagged for completeness/transparency rather than as an active concern.

## Cons
- A historical (1988) FDA "seriously misbranded" finding exists for a "Severe Sunburn Relief" variant of this brand, tied to insufficient evidence for that specific ingredient combination/severe-sunburn claim at the time — flagged for transparency; current resolution status and whether today's product lineage is affected were not independently confirmed in this pass
- Label carries an unusual specific warning — "intentional inhalation can be harmful or fatal" — reflecting the pressurized spray/propellant format, a real safety consideration distinct from the lidocaine active ingredient itself
- A separate "Cool Aloe Spray" SKU exists whose specific formulation was not independently verified in this pass — do not assume it is identical to the 4% lidocaine Burn Pain Relief Spray without checking
- No brand-specific clinical trial for Solarcaine's lidocaine formulation specifically was identified beyond the general lidocaine ingredient-level evidence on [[topical-anesthetics]]

## Use Cases
Temporary relief of discomfort and itching from sunburn, minor burns, cuts, scrapes, insect bites, and minor skin irritations.

## Allergies / Warnings
Per label: external use only; do not apply large quantities over raw/blistered areas; avoid eyes; do not puncture or incinerate the pressurized container; avoid storage above 40°C (104°F); discontinue and consult a doctor if condition worsens, persists beyond 7 days, or recurs; keep away from children, contact Poison Control if ingested.

## Symptoms Treated
