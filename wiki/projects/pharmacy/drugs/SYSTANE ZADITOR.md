## Appears In

## Overview
Confirmed: SYSTANE ZADITOR's own FDA/DailyMed label lists **ketotifen fumarate ophthalmic solution 0.025%** as its active ingredient — Alcon's branded version of the same ketotifen active sold under the original "Zaditor" name and, separately, by Bausch + Lomb as [[Alaway]]. All three are essentially the same drug at the same concentration (0.025%), dosed twice daily. Second-largest share in the category (14%). Per a 2023 meta-analysis of 7 RCTs, ketotifen showed comparable itch relief to olopatadine ([[Pataday]]) but somewhat higher hyperemia (redness) scores — see [[ophthalmic-antihistamines]] for the full class evidence. No SYSTANE ZADITOR-brand-specific clinical trial beyond general ketotifen pharmacology was identified.

## Pros
- Comparable itch-relief efficacy to olopatadine ([[Pataday]]) per the cited meta-analysis, at a twice-daily dosing schedule
- Long, well-established market history for the ketotifen active (originally approved as Zaditor)
- Alcon's Systane-line branding/packaging familiarity may aid pharmacist recommendation

## Cons
- Twice-daily dosing (every 8-12h) is less convenient than Pataday's once-daily options
- Per the cited meta-analysis, somewhat higher hyperemia (redness) than olopatadine, though itch relief is comparable
- Essentially the same drug and concentration as [[Alaway]] and generic ketotifen — brand choice among these three is unlikely to matter clinically
- No brand-specific outcome trial beyond the general ketotifen-vs-olopatadine class comparison exists

## Use Cases
Temporary prevention/relief of itchy eyes due to pollen, ragweed, grass, animal hair/dander in adults and children 3+.

## Allergies / Warnings
Per label: do not use if allergic to ketotifen; keep out of reach of children (contact Poison Control if swallowed); discontinue if irritation worsens.

## Symptoms Treated
Itchy eyes from seasonal/environmental allergens.
