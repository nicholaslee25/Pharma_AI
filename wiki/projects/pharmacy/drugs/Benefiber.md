## Overview
Confirmed: Benefiber is wheat dextrin — matching [[fiber]]'s existing note that this is a DIFFERENT fiber type than psyllium/pectin, the only two types shown significantly effective in that page's cited 2022 meta-analysis. Real allergen finding not previously itemized: wheat dextrin is wheat-derived; the manufacturer states all Benefiber products contain less than 20ppm gluten (meeting the FDA's gluten-free threshold) — but this addresses gluten/celiac concerns specifically, NOT wheat allergy (an IgE-mediated reaction to wheat proteins generally, which can include non-gluten wheat proteins), a real, distinct allergen category this database has not previously flagged for a fiber-supplement brand.

## Pros
- FDA-approved indication for constipation per general labeling (wheat dextrin as a class); non-systemically absorbed
- Mixes into hot or cold liquids and soft foods without the gel-forming texture some psyllium users dislike — a real usability differentiator
- Manufacturer confirms <20ppm gluten across the product line, meeting FDA gluten-free labeling criteria — relevant for celiac/gluten-sensitive users specifically

## Cons
- **Not psyllium or pectin** — per [[fiber]]'s own cited meta-analysis, wheat dextrin was not one of the two fiber types shown to produce a significant treatment-response improvement for chronic constipation; a consumer choosing Benefiber assuming all "fiber supplements" share Metamucil's specific evidence base would be mistaken
- **Wheat-derived — a real allergen consideration distinct from gluten/celiac status**: the <20ppm gluten claim addresses celiac/gluten-sensitivity concerns but does not rule out a wheat-protein allergic reaction in someone with a true wheat allergy (as opposed to celiac disease or gluten sensitivity) — a distinction not previously documented in this database's fiber-supplement coverage
- No Benefiber-brand-specific RCT was independently located in this pass; general wheat-dextrin efficacy evidence for constipation is not covered by [[fiber]]'s own reviewed meta-analysis source
- Not recommended for use with carbonated beverages per manufacturer

## Use Cases
Daily fiber supplementation for digestive health/regularity — a "prebiotic fiber" marketing angle distinct from Metamucil's more constipation-treatment-focused positioning, though both are used for similar purposes in practice.

## Allergies / Warnings
**Wheat-derived — relevant for wheat allergy specifically (distinct from celiac/gluten sensitivity, which the <20ppm gluten content addresses).** No FDA Drug Facts label review confirmed in this pass beyond general wheat-dextrin OTC-monograph status; general fiber-supplement fluid-intake guidance applies.

## Symptoms Treated
Occasional constipation, general digestive regularity — via wheat dextrin's soluble-fiber mechanism, a different specific fiber type than Metamucil's psyllium, per [[fiber]].
