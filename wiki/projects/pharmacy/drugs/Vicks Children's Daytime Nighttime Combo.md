## Appears In
Children's Cough - 1%

## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed: this is a distinct Vicks children's co-pack from `drugs/Vicks Children's DayQuil-NyQuil.md` researched earlier in this pass — **"Vicks Children's Cough & Congestion"** Day/Night formulation. Daytime: **phenylephrine HCl + dextromethorphan HBr + guaifenesin**. Nighttime: **phenylephrine HCl + diphenhydramine HCl**. Ages 4+, "Free of artificial dyes & flavors, high fructose corn syrup & alcohol." **Key difference from the other Vicks children's line already documented in this database: BOTH the day and night formulations here contain phenylephrine**, whereas the "Free Of" DayQuil/NyQuil Kids line's confirmed SKUs contained no phenylephrine at all (dextromethorphan+guaifenesin day, diphenhydramine-only night). This is a third distinct Vicks pediatric cough/cold formulation family identified in this research pass (after the two already found under "Vicks Children's DayQuil/NyQuil"), reinforcing that "Vicks Children's" as an umbrella brand spans even more formulation variety than initially documented on that page.

## Pros
- Day/night pairing (non-sedating daytime combination vs. sedating nighttime combination) follows the same clinically sensible symptom-timing logic already noted for the DayQuil/NyQuil Kids line
- Combines three evidence-paged ingredients in the daytime formula (dextromethorphan, guaifenesin, phenylephrine), allowing direct cross-reference to existing ingredient pages rather than an unstudied blend

## Cons
- **Unlike the DayQuil/NyQuil Kids "Free Of" line, this formulation is NOT acetaminophen-free by virtue of avoiding phenylephrine — it actively includes phenylephrine in BOTH day and night formulas**, meaning it fully inherits [[oral-phenylephrine]]'s FDA-flagged efficacy problem (2023 advisory committee "not GRASE" finding; pending November 2024 proposed OTC-monograph removal order) at twice the dosing exposure across a full day/night regimen compared to a single-dose-per-day phenylephrine product
- **Nighttime formula combines a sympathomimetic decongestant (phenylephrine) with a sedating first-generation antihistamine (diphenhydramine) in the same dose** — a combination pharmacologically working in partially opposing directions (stimulant vs. sedative), a common OTC nighttime-cold-medicine pattern but one worth naming explicitly rather than treating as risk-neutral
- **Standing 2008 FDA/CHPA pediatric cough-cold labeling history applies** — ages 4+ label reflects the industry's post-2008 conservative floor
- Per [[dextromethorphan]] and [[guaifenesin]]'s own evidence grades: limited-to-no subjective symptom benefit demonstrated over placebo in the best-designed RCTs located
- No brand-specific pediatric RCT located for either formulation as a combination product
- **Third distinct "Vicks Children's" formulation family identified in this single research pass** — a caregiver reaching for "Vicks Children's" cough/cold product cannot assume a consistent ingredient set across the brand's own product line without checking the specific SKU

## Use Cases
Daytime: cough, chest congestion, and nasal/sinus congestion relief. Nighttime: congestion relief plus sedating antihistamine to support sleep during a cold. Ages 4+.

## Allergies / Warnings
Per label: not for children under 4; do not use with any other product containing dextromethorphan, guaifenesin, a decongestant, or an antihistamine; ask a doctor before use if the child has heart disease, high blood pressure, thyroid disease, diabetes, glaucoma, or a breathing problem; nighttime formula may cause drowsiness.

## Symptoms Treated
Cough, chest congestion, nasal/sinus congestion (daytime); nasal/sinus congestion with sedation support (nighttime).

## Brand-Specific Alignment Check
**New finding, added to both this page and [[oral-phenylephrine]]:** this is now the third Vicks Children's product line and the third pediatric brand overall in this Pediatrics batch (after Children's Mucinex and Children's Sudafed PE) built around an ingredient the FDA has formally proposed removing from the OTC monograph — and the only one so far confirmed to use it in both a day AND night formulation of the same product line.
