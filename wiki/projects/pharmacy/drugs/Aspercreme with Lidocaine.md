## Overview
Confirmed via multiple DailyMed listings: Aspercreme with Lidocaine is a multi-SKU line (creme, lotion, spray, roll-on/no-mess applicator, and patch — including a "Foot Pain Creme" variant), all consistently built around **Lidocaine HCl 4%** as the active ingredient, purpose "topical analgesic." This is the same pattern already seen with brand-spans-many-formulations product lines elsewhere in this database (e.g. Diabetic Tussin, Robitussin) — the brand name is reliable for the active ingredient here (unlike those cough lines), since every SKU checked uses the same 4% lidocaine. Lidocaine is covered at [[topical-anesthetics]], which already lists Aspercreme with Lidocaine among its confirmed lidocaine-based brands and specifically credits it with the largest share (37%) of the Topical Anesthetics category — a category the ingredient page notes is dominated by lidocaine (the comparatively safer anesthetic) rather than benzocaine, which carries the disproportionate methemoglobinemia risk documented there. No brand-specific clinical trial beyond the general lidocaine evidence was found.

## Pros
- Confirmed, consistent active ingredient (lidocaine 4%) across essentially all SKUs in the product line — unusually reliable brand-to-ingredient mapping for this database
- Lidocaine carries a materially safer methemoglobinemia profile than benzocaine per the Guay 2009 review on [[topical-anesthetics]] (~5% of 242 reviewed cases involved lidocaine vs. ~66% benzocaine)
- Available across multiple formats (creme, spray, roll-on, patch, foot-specific creme), allowing format preference for different application sites, including feet for neuropathy use
- Largest single-brand share in both categories it appears in (37% Topical Anesthetics, 33% Diabetic Neuropathy Products), suggesting broad pharmacist familiarity and comfort recommending it

## Cons
- No brand-specific RCT (as distinct from the general lidocaine-molecule evidence already on [[topical-anesthetics]]) was identified in a targeted search
- Topical lidocaine provides temporary symptomatic numbing, not a disease-modifying treatment for underlying diabetic neuropathy — patients should not substitute it for glycemic control or neuropathy-specific medical management
- Standard local-anesthetic cautions apply (avoid broken/irritated skin, avoid overuse across very large body areas given systemic absorption risk, per general lidocaine labeling)

## Use Cases
Temporary relief of minor muscle/joint pain and neuropathic burning/tingling/numbness discomfort, including diabetic peripheral neuropathy symptom relief in the feet and hands.

## Allergies / Warnings
For external use only; do not apply to wounds, broken/irritated skin, or use with a heating pad. Do not use if allergic to lidocaine or other local anesthetics of the amide class. Ask a doctor before use if pregnant/breastfeeding. See [[topical-anesthetics]] for broader local-anesthetic systemic toxicity (LAST) and special-population data.

## Symptoms Treated
Minor muscle and joint pain; neuropathic burning, tingling, and numbness discomfort (symptomatic relief only, not a treatment for the underlying neuropathy).
