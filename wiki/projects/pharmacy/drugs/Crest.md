## Appears In
Toothpaste, General Use - 28%

## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed: **Crest Cavity Protection** (the flagship line most plausibly driving this 28% General Use share) lists **sodium fluoride 0.243% (0.15% w/v fluoride ion)** as its active ingredient, purpose "anticavity toothpaste," helping protect teeth and roots against cavities — a standard [[fluoride-toothpaste]] formulation. Crest 3D White (Brilliance, Advanced Whitening, Radiant Mint, Advanced Deep Clean) and Kids Crest Cavity Protection are also confirmed sodium fluoride pastes per their own DailyMed labels. **Brand-family nuance (already documented on a separate page):** Crest Pro-Health, sold under the same "Crest" master brand, is a materially different formulation — confirmed **stannous fluoride**, not sodium fluoride — per `drugs/Crest Pro-Health.md`. So "Crest" as a shelf name spans at least two distinct active-ingredient chemistries depending on sub-line (Cavity Protection/3D White = sodium fluoride; Pro-Health = stannous fluoride), each with a different evidence/safety profile (staining tradeoff documented for stannous fluoride, not for sodium fluoride). Historically, Crest has real brand-specific trial pedigree: P&G's joint research program with Dr. Joseph Muhler at Indiana University in the early 1950s — the original basis for Crest's fluoride formulation and its "ADA Accepted" status (the first fluoride toothpaste to receive that seal, 1960) — reported a 49% average cavity reduction in children ages 6–16 in early clinical testing, a real brand-specific historical data point distinct from the general fluoride ingredient-class evidence on [[fluoride-toothpaste]].

## Pros
- Confirmed FDA Drug Facts label with a well-established anticavity active ingredient (sodium fluoride) for the core Cavity Protection/3D White lines
- Real brand-specific historical clinical pedigree (Indiana University/Muhler-era trials, ADA's first-ever fluoride toothpaste acceptance in 1960) beyond generic ingredient-class evidence
- By far the largest share in Toothpaste, General Use (28%), reflecting broad consumer/pharmacist familiarity

## Cons
- "Crest" spans at least two chemically different active ingredients across its own product family — sodium fluoride (Cavity Protection, 3D White) vs. stannous fluoride (Pro-Health) — a consumer should check the specific sub-line, not assume one fixed formulation from the brand name alone
- No independent, modern brand-specific RCT (as opposed to the 1950s-era historical trial or the general fluoride ingredient-class Cochrane evidence) was identified for the current Cavity Protection formulation specifically
- Per [[fluoride-toothpaste]]'s own caveat, effect size depends on brushing frequency/technique/supervision — brand choice alone does not guarantee the ingredient-class benefit

## Use Cases
Cavity prevention via daily brushing (sodium fluoride, Cavity Protection/3D White lines); see `drugs/Crest Pro-Health.md` separately for the stannous-fluoride multi-benefit (anticavity + antigingivitis + antiplaque) sub-line.

## Allergies / Warnings
Per label: keep out of reach of children under 6; if more than used for brushing is accidentally swallowed, get medical help or contact a Poison Control Center. Standard fluoride precautions per [[fluoride-toothpaste]] (pea-sized amount for ages 3–6, supervise brushing, dentist consultation under age 2).

## Symptoms Treated
Cavity prevention (all sodium-fluoride Crest lines). Does not address gum sensitivity or gingivitis under the core Cavity Protection/3D White formulation — see Crest Gum & Sensitivity and Crest Pro-Health separately for those benefits.
