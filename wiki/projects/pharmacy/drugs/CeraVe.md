## Overview
No individual FDA/DailyMed label exists for CeraVe products researched in this pass — CeraVe is a cosmetic line, not an OTC monograph drug, across the SKUs checked. For the Diabetic Foot Cream category specifically, the relevant product is **CeraVe Diabetics' Dry Skin Relief Hand and Foot Cream**: no "Active Ingredient" drug-facts panel, formulated instead around 3 ceramides + urea + bilberry extract (antioxidant) using CeraVe's patented MVE (multivesicular emulsion) slow-release delivery technology. One real regulatory-relevant note: the product's own labeling carries an AHA sun-sensitivity warning ("contains an Alpha Hydroxy Acid that may increase your skin's sensitivity to the sun"), consistent with an exfoliating-acid component alongside the ceramide/urea moisturizing base. This is a different formulation from the general CeraVe Therapeutic Skin Care, Moisturizers line already covered at [[moisturizers-ceramides]] (32% share) — same underlying ceramide/occlusive mechanism, but the diabetic-specific SKU adds urea and an AHA the general moisturizer may not carry in the same amount. No brand-specific clinical trial for the diabetic foot/hand cream SKU was found; the general ceramide/moisturizer efficacy evidence on [[moisturizers-ceramides]] is the closest available evidence, not a diabetic-population-specific trial. See also [[diabetic-foot-care]] for the broader evidence context: no cream ingredient (this one included) has been shown in the literature to reduce diabetic ulceration/amputation risk — that outcome is driven by education, screening, and footwear, not moisturizer choice.

## Pros
- Ceramide + occlusive-based formulation, mechanistically consistent with the real flare-reduction evidence for moisturizers in dry/eczematous skin (see [[moisturizers-ceramides]])
- Fragrance-free formulation reduces contact-irritant risk relative to fragranced competitors
- MVE slow-release delivery is a genuine (if brand-proprietary, not independently trial-tested) technology distinction from plain-cream competitors

## Cons
- Not an FDA-regulated drug for this SKU — no active ingredient, no clinical claim independently verified by label pull
- Contains an AHA with a labeled sun-sensitivity warning, a real (if minor) added consideration not present in plain petrolatum-based competitors like [[Aquaphor]]
- No diabetic-population-specific outcome data (ulceration/amputation risk) exists for this or any competing foot cream — see [[diabetic-foot-care]]
- "CeraVe" spans 6 very different product categories under one house-brand name; a consumer cannot assume ingredient consistency across SKUs

## Use Cases
Dry, rough, or cracked skin on hands and feet, particularly in diabetic patients seeking a fragrance-free, ceramide-based moisturizer as part of a broader foot-care routine (not a substitute for the screening/footwear protocol on [[diabetic-foot-care]]).

## Allergies / Warnings
Contains an Alpha Hydroxy Acid — may increase skin's sun sensitivity; use sunscreen/protective clothing during and after use. For external use only; discontinue if irritation develops. No diabetes-specific contraindication identified.

## Symptoms Treated
Dry, rough, cracked skin on hands and feet; general xerosis.
