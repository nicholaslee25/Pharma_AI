## Appears In
Lip Balms - 5%
Therapeutic Skin Care, Moisturizers - 2%

## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed and product-ingredient sources (Beiersdorf): "Nivea" lip products span **two regulatory categories under one brand name**. (1) **Nivea Original Care / Caring Lip Balm** — a cosmetic moisturizer (shea butter, sunflower oils/wax, glycerin, castor oil, etc.) with **no declared active ingredient and no Drug Facts panel** — it makes no skin-protectant or sunscreen drug claim. (2) **Nivea Smoothness Lip Care SPF 15** — a genuine OTC sunscreen drug, active ingredients including **avobenzone and octocrylene** (broad-spectrum SPF 15), with FDA-mandated sunburn-prevention/skin-cancer-risk-reduction labeling. This is the same "cosmetic base line + separate sunscreen-drug SKU under one brand" pattern already seen with e.l.f., eos, and (partially) Palmer's in this same subcategory.

This pass covers **Lip Balms only**; Nivea's separate 2% share in Therapeutic Skin Care, Moisturizers is not yet researched and is deferred to that subcategory's dedicated pass.

No Nivea-brand-specific clinical trial was located for either lip product.

## Pros
- Non-SPF base line: simple, fragrance-forward cosmetic moisturizer with no chemical-sunscreen-filter irritation/allergy risk for consumers who don't need sun protection
- SPF 15 line: adds genuine, appropriately-labeled broad-spectrum sun protection for lips
- Consumer choice between a cosmetic-only and a drug-labeled SKU under one recognizable brand

## Cons
- **The 5% Lip Balms share cannot be attributed to a single formulation** — the base cosmetic line and the SPF 15 drug-labeled line are materially different products (one has no active ingredient or FDA drug claim at all; the other is a regulated sunscreen), and the source data does not distinguish between them
- Dedicated evidence pages for avobenzone/octocrylene (or the broader sunscreen-actives class) have not yet been built in this database — deferred to the dedicated Sunscreen subcategory pass
- No Nivea-brand-specific clinical trial was located for either SKU
- The non-SPF base line, being a cosmetic with no Drug Facts panel, offers no confirmed skin-protectant/chapped-lip drug claim despite being marketed and tracked in this database's "Lip Balms" OTC category

## Use Cases
Base line: cosmetic lip moisturization (no drug claim). SPF 15 line: broad-spectrum sun protection for lips via avobenzone/octocrylene.

## Allergies / Warnings
Base line: no FDA Drug Facts label exists; standard cosmetic-ingredient sensitivity precautions apply (fragrance, shea butter, castor oil). SPF 15 line: standard chemical-sunscreen warnings apply.

## Symptoms Treated
Base line: none confirmed (cosmetic, no drug claim). SPF line: sunburn prevention on lips.

## Brand-Specific Alignment Check
**Real category-composition note, not a full mismatch like [[Lice Shield]]:** a meaningful share of what's tracked as "Nivea" in the Lip Balms OTC category is, for at least one common SKU, not an FDA-regulated drug product at all — a cosmetic moisturizer with no Drug Facts panel. This is flagged for accuracy, though lip balms as a product type legitimately include both drug and cosmetic SKUs industry-wide, unlike the Lice Treatments mismatch which involved a specific deceptive-claims regulatory action.
