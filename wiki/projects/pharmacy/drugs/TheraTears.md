## Overview
This page covers both TheraTears product families now that the Vitamins and Dietary Supplements pass has reached this brand. **Eye drop line** (Artificial Tears/Ophthalmic Lubricants, 3% share): confirmed via FDA label (DailyMed) that the active ingredient is **carboxymethylcellulose sodium 0.25%** — a lower CMC concentration than [[Refresh]]'s 0.5% — combined with a proprietary "OxyLytes" electrolyte system (borate buffers, sodium bicarbonate, and the five electrolytes found in natural tears) as supporting inactive ingredients, marketed on the premise that natural tears are hypotonic and electrolyte-balanced. Per [[artificial-tears]]'s cited Semp et al. 2023 review, this is a single-ingredient CMC-based formulation, the category found to underperform PEG-containing and combination formulations. No TheraTears-brand-specific outcome trial isolating the electrolyte-system claim from generic CMC lubrication was identified.

**Ocular Nutritional Supplements line** (8% share) is a completely different product: an oral fish-oil/organic-flaxseed-oil softgel (450mg EPA, 300mg DHA, 1g flaxseed oil, plus vitamin E), marketed for dry-eye support via omega-3 metabolism rather than direct lubrication. **This is a genuinely different mechanism than [[PreserVision]]/[[Ocuvite]]'s AREDS2-based AMD-progression formula** — TheraTears' nutritional line does not contain the AREDS2 nutrient combination (lutein/zeaxanthin/zinc/copper at trial-matched doses) and is not evidenced for AMD progression; see [[areds2-eye-vitamins]] for that separate evidence base. Omega-3's role in dry-eye disease specifically is noted in [[omega-3-fish-oil]] as a studied use case, but that page does not detail dry-eye trial data in depth — a real evidence gap this pass did not fully resolve with a dedicated TheraTears-brand-specific dry-eye RCT.

## Pros
- Single, well-characterized labeled active ingredient (CMC 0.25%) with a straightforward, unambiguous FDA label
- Electrolyte-system marketing rationale (matching natural tear composition/osmolarity) has real mechanistic grounding in dry-eye disease pathophysiology (tear-film hyperosmolarity is a documented driver of DED per [[artificial-tears]]'s StatPearls source), even though no dedicated outcome trial isolates the electrolyte system's independent contribution
- Lower CMC concentration (0.25% vs. Refresh's 0.5%) may suit users preferring a lighter-viscosity drop, a real formulation choice rather than a deficiency

## Cons
- Single-ingredient CMC formulation sits in the category the cited systematic review found to underperform PEG-containing and combination-ingredient tears — the same formulation-tier disadvantage noted for [[Refresh]]
- The "OxyLytes" electrolyte-system marketing claim is not itself an FDA-recognized active ingredient or independently outcome-trial-tested against plain CMC tears in the sources reviewed — a plausible-but-unconfirmed added-value claim, not a false one
- Smallest recommendation share (3%, tied with several others) in a crowded category

## Use Cases
Eye drops: temporary relief of burning, irritation, and discomfort due to dryness of the eye or exposure to wind or sun. Nutritional supplement line: oral omega-3/flaxseed-oil support for dry-eye symptoms via a different (metabolic/anti-inflammatory) mechanism than the drops — see [[omega-3-fish-oil]] for the general ingredient evidence, which does not detail dry-eye-specific trial data in depth in this database.

## Allergies / Warnings
Per label: for external use only; discontinue and see a doctor for eye pain, vision changes, continued redness/irritation, or symptoms persisting beyond 72 hours.

## Symptoms Treated
Dry eye burning, irritation, discomfort from wind/sun exposure or reduced tear production.

## Brand-Specific Alignment Check
Consistent with [[artificial-tears]] for the eye-drop line: no discrepancy found, though the electrolyte-system marketing claim goes beyond what the ingredient page's cited evidence directly tests — noted as a plausible-but-unconfirmed nuance, not a contradiction. For the nutritional line: no discrepancy found, but flagged as a real category-placement nuance worth noting — TheraTears' Ocular Nutritional Supplements product uses a genuinely different mechanism (omega-3/flaxseed) than category-leaders [[PreserVision]]/[[Ocuvite]]'s AREDS2 antioxidant-carotenoid formula, and should not be assumed to carry the same AMD-progression evidence documented in [[areds2-eye-vitamins]].
