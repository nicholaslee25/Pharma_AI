## Overview
This pass researches TheraTears' **eye drop line** (Artificial Tears/Ophthalmic Lubricants, 3% share); the brand's separate **Ocular Nutritional Supplements** line (8% share, an oral omega-3/nutraceutical product, not an eye drop) is a different product family entirely and is deferred to the Vitamins and Dietary Supplements category pass later in this project — similar to the [[Dr. Scholl's]] and [[CeraVe]] multi-category cases already noted. For the eye drops: confirmed via FDA label (DailyMed) that the active ingredient is **carboxymethylcellulose sodium 0.25%** — a lower CMC concentration than [[Refresh]]'s 0.5% — combined with a proprietary "OxyLytes" electrolyte system (borate buffers, sodium bicarbonate, and the five electrolytes found in natural tears) as supporting inactive ingredients, marketed on the premise that natural tears are hypotonic and electrolyte-balanced. Per [[artificial-tears]]'s cited Semp et al. 2023 review, this is a single-ingredient CMC-based formulation, the category found to underperform PEG-containing and combination formulations. No TheraTears-brand-specific outcome trial isolating the electrolyte-system claim from generic CMC lubrication was identified.

## Pros
- Single, well-characterized labeled active ingredient (CMC 0.25%) with a straightforward, unambiguous FDA label
- Electrolyte-system marketing rationale (matching natural tear composition/osmolarity) has real mechanistic grounding in dry-eye disease pathophysiology (tear-film hyperosmolarity is a documented driver of DED per [[artificial-tears]]'s StatPearls source), even though no dedicated outcome trial isolates the electrolyte system's independent contribution
- Lower CMC concentration (0.25% vs. Refresh's 0.5%) may suit users preferring a lighter-viscosity drop, a real formulation choice rather than a deficiency

## Cons
- Single-ingredient CMC formulation sits in the category the cited systematic review found to underperform PEG-containing and combination-ingredient tears — the same formulation-tier disadvantage noted for [[Refresh]]
- The "OxyLytes" electrolyte-system marketing claim is not itself an FDA-recognized active ingredient or independently outcome-trial-tested against plain CMC tears in the sources reviewed — a plausible-but-unconfirmed added-value claim, not a false one
- Smallest recommendation share (3%, tied with several others) in a crowded category

## Use Cases
Temporary relief of burning, irritation, and discomfort due to dryness of the eye or exposure to wind or sun. (The separate Ocular Nutritional Supplements line is not covered in this pass; see note above.)

## Allergies / Warnings
Per label: for external use only; discontinue and see a doctor for eye pain, vision changes, continued redness/irritation, or symptoms persisting beyond 72 hours.

## Symptoms Treated
Dry eye burning, irritation, discomfort from wind/sun exposure or reduced tear production.
