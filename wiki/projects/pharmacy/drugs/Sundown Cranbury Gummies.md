## Overview
**Naming note flagged, not corrected:** this database's own filename ("Cranbury") appears to be a typo for "Cranberry" — the actual retail product is "Sundown Naturals Cranberry Gummies." Confirmed via manufacturer product data: 500mg Pacran cranberry concentrate powder per gummy (a 50:1 concentrate, stated equivalent to 25,000mg of fresh cranberries). See [[cranberry]] for the full ingredient evidence base — Cochrane-level review found cranberry products can modestly reduce UTI recurrence in some populations (notably women with recurrent UTIs and children), but with very-low-certainty evidence on optimal dose/PAC content, and no benefit shown for elderly or catheterized populations.

## Pros
- Uses a named, standardized concentrate (Pacran, 50:1) rather than an unspecified "cranberry powder" — a real, if modest, transparency advantage over some competing products
- Gummy format may improve adherence for users who dislike capsules, a practical (not clinical) advantage
- Sits within a real, cited evidence base for cranberry's UTI-recurrence-reduction effect in specific populations, per [[cranberry]]

## Cons
- **No PAC (proanthocyanidin) content was disclosed** in the sources reviewed — the same industry-wide gap already flagged on [[cranberry]] for every brand in that category; per the Cochrane review, PAC dose is the more clinically relevant metric than raw cranberry-equivalent weight, and its absence here is a real information gap
- Gummy formulations typically add sugar (corn syrup, sugar are listed among the product's main ingredients per manufacturer data) — a real, practical consideration for diabetic users or anyone limiting added sugar, not addressed by the product's health-supplement framing
- No Sundown-brand-specific RCT was located in this pass; efficacy rests on the general cranberry literature, which itself is described as very-low-certainty for optimal dosing

## Use Cases
Marketed to support healthy urinary tract function (per label) — consistent with cranberry's general, population-specific UTI-recurrence-reduction evidence per [[cranberry]].

## Allergies / Warnings
No brand-specific recall or lawsuit located in this pass. General cranberry-supplement caution: high-dose cranberry products carry a small kidney-stone-risk consideration in susceptible individuals (per the broader cranberry literature) and a theoretical warfarin-interaction concern — neither independently re-confirmed against a Sundown-specific source in this pass.

## Symptoms Treated
Marketed for general urinary tract health support; per [[cranberry]], best-evidenced specifically for reducing UTI recurrence risk in certain populations (not treating an active infection).
