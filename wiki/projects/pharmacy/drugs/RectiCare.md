## Appears In
Hemorrhoidal Preparations - 11%

## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed (labeler: Ferndale Laboratories, Inc.): base RectiCare is **lidocaine 5% w/w**, purpose "local anesthetic," relieving pain, itching, and burning associated with hemorrhoids and other anorectal disorders — the highest lidocaine concentration confirmed among the OTC anesthetics tracked in [[topical-anesthetics]] (most other brands in that page's "Topical Analgesics" categories run 4% or lower). A separate **"RectiCare Advanced"** SKU adds phenylephrine HCl 0.25% (vasoconstrictor, see [[rectal-phenylephrine]]) plus mineral oil 17% and white petrolatum 39% (protectants) — combining an anesthetic and a vasoconstrictor mechanism in one product, a real formulation distinction from the base RectiCare.

**Label correctly restricts to external use only, consistent with the general clinical principle that rectal mucosal tissue lacks sensory nerve endings** — RectiCare's own label explicitly warns "do not put this product into the rectum by using fingers or any mechanical device or applicator," meaning its anesthetic benefit is confined to visible/palpable external perianal tissue, not internal hemorrhoids. This is a correct, appropriately-labeled use of lidocaine given the underlying anatomy, not a mismatch. No RectiCare-brand-specific clinical trial was located; evidence is at the [[topical-anesthetics]] (lidocaine) and [[rectal-phenylephrine]] (Advanced SKU only) ingredient level.

## Pros
- Confirmed FDA Drug Facts label with a clear, single active ingredient in the base formula (lidocaine 5%) — a simpler, more legible formulation than [[Preparation H]]'s multi-mechanism spread
- Lidocaine carries a materially better methemoglobinemia safety profile than benzocaine per [[topical-anesthetics]]'s Guay 2009 comparative data, relevant given how sensitive/vascular anorectal tissue is
- Label correctly restricts use to external application only, matching the actual anatomy/mechanism (rectal mucosa lacks the sensory nerve density that would make internal anesthetic application meaningful) — a genuinely well-designed label relative to the general category
- RectiCare Advanced's added phenylephrine offers a second, complementary vasoconstrictive mechanism for consumers who need both anesthesia and swelling reduction from a single product

## Cons
- No RectiCare-brand-specific clinical trial was located for either SKU; evidence is entirely at the ingredient level
- 5% lidocaine is a relatively high anesthetic concentration — while lower-risk than benzocaine on methemoglobinemia specifically, any topical anesthetic used repeatedly (up to 6 times daily per label) on irritated/broken perianal skin carries a real, if uncommon, systemic-absorption consideration not eliminated by choosing lidocaine over benzocaine
- RectiCare Advanced's phenylephrine component carries the same real cardiovascular/thyroid/diabetes/BPH contraindication considerations and lack-of-RCT-for-bleeding/prolapse-outcomes limitation flagged on [[rectal-phenylephrine]]
- Contact dermatitis is a labeled possibility for topical anesthetics generally per [[topical-anesthetics]] and uspharmacist.com's general anorectal-product guidance

## Use Cases
Temporary relief of pain, itching, and burning from hemorrhoids and other anorectal disorders via lidocaine's local anesthetic mechanism (base SKU); RectiCare Advanced additionally addresses swelling via phenylephrine's vasoconstriction.

## Allergies / Warnings
Per label: for external use only; do not insert into the rectum with fingers or any mechanical device/applicator; avoid eye contact; do not exceed recommended dosage without doctor approval; stop use and consult a doctor if rectal bleeding occurs, symptoms worsen after 7 days, allergic reactions develop, or symptoms don't subside. RectiCare Advanced additionally carries [[rectal-phenylephrine]]'s cardiovascular/thyroid/diabetes/BPH cautions.

## Symptoms Treated
Pain, itching, and burning from hemorrhoids and other anorectal disorders (base SKU); additionally swelling (RectiCare Advanced, via phenylephrine).

## Brand-Specific Alignment Check
Consistent with [[topical-anesthetics]] and [[rectal-phenylephrine]] (Advanced SKU) — no discrepancy found. The label's external-use-only restriction is correctly matched to the underlying anatomy (rectal mucosa's lack of sensory nerve endings), a positive finding worth noting explicitly rather than assuming every anorectal product gets this right by default.
