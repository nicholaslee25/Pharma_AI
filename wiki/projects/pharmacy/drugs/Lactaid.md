## Overview
Confirmed: Lactaid is a pure lactase-enzyme dietary supplement, consistent with [[lactase]]'s existing sourcing (that ingredient page was already anchored on Lactaid as the named category example). Real formulation-family note not previously itemized: Lactaid caplets are sold at multiple confirmed FCC-unit strengths — 4,500 FCC and 9,000 FCC ("Original Strength") — a real, quantified 2x potency spread under one brand name, consistent with the one-brand-multiple-formulations pattern recurring across this database. Category-dominant (79% share, by far the largest of any brand in Lactose Intolerance Products).

## Pros
- Genuinely and mechanistically correct for its category — unlike Beano (researched separately in this pass), Lactaid's lactase enzyme directly targets lactose, the actual sugar responsible for lactose-intolerance symptoms
- Category-dominant share (79%)
- Non-systemic mechanism, acting locally/pre-digestively in the gut or in milk — low general safety-risk profile per [[lactase]]

## Cons
- Per [[lactase]]'s own Efficacy Evidence (Shaukat et al. 2010 AHRQ systematic review): evidence was genuinely **insufficient** to establish that lactase/lactose-reduced products outperform simply tolerating a modest amount of regular dairy (most adults tolerate 12-15g lactose, about 1 cup of milk, without any product) — a real, humbling finding for this category's dominant brand specifically
- No standardized numeric dose exists across the lactase-supplement market per [[lactase]]; this brand's real 4,500 vs. 9,000 FCC-unit spread means "take a Lactaid pill" does not specify a consistent dose without checking the specific product
- As a dietary supplement (not an FDA-monograph OTC drug), Lactaid did not go through the same FDA Drug Facts label review process as brands elsewhere in this Gastrointestinal section (e.g. Tums, Pepcid) — the FDA does not verify that supplement labels accurately state ingredient content
- No Lactaid-brand-specific RCT beyond the general lactase/lactose-intolerance evidence base was independently located in this pass

## Use Cases
Symptomatic management of lactose intolerance (bloating, diarrhea, gas, abdominal pain after dairy) — taken just before or with the first bite of a lactose-containing food, per [[lactase]].

## Allergies / Warnings
No FDA Drug Facts-mandated warnings located (dietary supplement, not an FDA-monograph drug). No specific contraindications or safety signal identified for lactase supplementation itself in [[lactase]]'s reviewed sources — flagged there as a genuine gap rather than assumed benign without a source.
