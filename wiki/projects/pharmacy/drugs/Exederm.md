## Appears In
Eczema Care/Relief Products - 1%

## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed: Exederm's flagship product, **Flare Control Cream for Eczema & Dermatitis** (labeler Bentlin Products LLC, manufactured by Sheffield Pharmaceuticals LLC), lists **hydrocortisone acetate USP 1.0% w/w** as its active ingredient, purpose "anti-itch." This is a genuinely different mechanism from the colloidal-oatmeal-based majority of this category ([[Aveeno Eczema Therapy]], [[Eczema Honey]], [[Gold Bond Eczema Relief]], [[La Roche-Posay Lipikar Eczema Cream]]) and puts Exederm in the same corticosteroid class as [[Cortizone-10]] — though at a distinct ester salt form.

**Ester-form nuance flagged:** Exederm uses **hydrocortisone acetate**, not free hydrocortisone (the form confirmed for [[Cortizone-10]] and Vanicream HC). Hydrocortisone acetate is a related ester that is generally considered pharmacologically comparable to free hydrocortisone at matched label concentrations (both are Class VII, least-potent topical corticosteroids per general dermatology references), but [[hydrocortisone]]'s existing evidence base and safety data were sourced from studies/labels of the free-base form — this pass did not locate ester-specific comparative absorption or efficacy data, so an assumption of exact equivalence, while standard clinical practice, is not independently re-verified against a head-to-head trial in this database.

Marketing positions Exederm as an "ultra-sensitive formula for highly sensitive skin," but no allergen-free/fragrance-free claim or independent clinical study was located in this pass (per Pharmacy Times' own "Daily OTC Pearl: Exederm" summary) — a notable absence given competing brands in this category (Vanicream, La Roche-Posay) build their identity substantially around such claims. No Exederm-brand-specific clinical trial was located.

## Pros
- Confirmed FDA Drug Facts label (hydrocortisone acetate 1%, anti-itch) — a genuine OTC drug, not merely a cosmetic moisturizer
- Corticosteroid mechanism directly addresses the inflammatory/itch component of eczema flares, a different and complementary mechanism to the occlusive/emollient approach used by most of this category's other top brands
- Standard, well-established topical-corticosteroid safety profile at this concentration per general dermatology literature (see [[hydrocortisone]]), assuming ester-to-free-base equivalence

## Cons
- **Uses hydrocortisone acetate rather than free hydrocortisone** — a distinction not independently evidence-checked against [[hydrocortisone]]'s existing (free-base-sourced) safety/efficacy data in this pass; flagged rather than assumed identical
- No Exederm-brand-specific clinical trial located for either the corticosteroid mechanism or any broader "sensitive skin" claim
- Despite "ultra-sensitive" positioning, no allergen-free/fragrance-free claim or supporting data was found in this pass — weaker substantiation than competing sensitive-skin brands in the same category
- Smallest or tied-smallest recommendation share (1%) in this category
- Per [[hydrocortisone]]'s general safety data: not for diaper rash use, avoid in children under 2 without medical guidance, should not be combined with other hydrocortisone-containing products without medical guidance, and not for use beyond 7 days without a doctor's direction

## Use Cases
Temporary relief of itching from minor skin irritation, inflammation, and rashes caused by atopic eczema, contact dermatitis (from soaps/detergents/cosmetics), seborrheic dermatitis, and psoriasis — via hydrocortisone acetate's corticosteroid anti-inflammatory mechanism.

## Allergies / Warnings
Per label: external use only; not for diaper rash treatment; avoid use in children under 2 without doctor supervision; avoid eye contact; do not combine with other hydrocortisone-containing products without medical guidance; discontinue if condition worsens or symptoms persist beyond 7 days. See [[hydrocortisone]] for the broader free-base corticosteroid safety/contraindication profile (bacterial/fungal infection masking, skin atrophy with prolonged use), applied here with the ester-form caveat noted above.

## Symptoms Treated
Itching, inflammation, redness, and rash from atopic eczema, contact dermatitis, seborrheic dermatitis, and psoriasis.
