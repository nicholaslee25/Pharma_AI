## Appears In
Cold Sore Treatments - 6%

## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed: Anbesol Cold Sore Therapy is an ointment with **allantoin, benzocaine, camphor, and petrolatum** as active ingredients — a materially different, four-ingredient combination formula from plain Anbesol Maximum Strength's single-agent benzocaine 20% gel (see `drugs/Anbesol.md`). This SKU combines a skin protectant (allantoin, petrolatum) with a local anesthetic (benzocaine) and a counterirritant/mild antipruritic (camphor), targeting both symptomatic comfort and a protective barrier over the lesion — a genuinely different formulation philosophy than Abreva's antiviral docosanol mechanism, the category leader in this same list.

## Pros
- Multi-mechanism formula addresses both pain (benzocaine) and barrier protection/moisturizing (allantoin, petrolatum) in one product, rather than a single-purpose ingredient
- Familiar "Anbesol" brand name may drive purchase confidence despite the different formulation from the parent brand's flagship toothache/canker-sore product
- Camphor and petrolatum both have long OTC safety track records as skin protectants/counterirritants

## Cons
- Contains benzocaine, carrying the same disproportionate methemoglobinemia risk documented on [[topical-anesthetics]] as every other benzocaine-based product in this database
- Does not contain docosanol or any antiviral mechanism — unlike Abreva (the category leader), this product does not target the underlying herpes simplex virus replication/entry process; it addresses symptoms and provides a protective barrier only
- No brand-specific clinical trial evidence was identified for this specific four-ingredient combination's efficacy on cold sore healing time or symptom relief
- The "Anbesol" brand name spans two materially different formulations (this four-ingredient ointment vs. Maximum Strength's single-agent benzocaine gel) — a consumer should not assume uniform composition across the Anbesol line

## Use Cases
Temporary relief of pain, itching, and discomfort associated with cold sores/fever blisters, plus a protective barrier over the lesion (allantoin/petrolatum).

## Allergies / Warnings
Per benzocaine-class label conventions (specific Cold Sore Therapy label warnings not independently itemized beyond the confirmed active-ingredient list): methemoglobinemia risk; do not use if allergic to "caine"-family anesthetics; standard external-use-only precautions for camphor/petrolatum-based ointments.

## Symptoms Treated
Cold sore/fever blister pain and itching (via benzocaine), with a protective, moisturizing barrier (via allantoin and petrolatum) — no antiviral action.

## Brand-Specific Alignment Check
New finding, flagged on [[topical-anesthetics]] and cross-referenced on `drugs/Anbesol.md`: this is a mechanistically distinct sub-line from the parent Anbesol brand's benzocaine-only formula, and also mechanistically distinct from the category-leading Abreva (docosanol antiviral) — three different approaches (pure anesthetic, multi-ingredient anesthetic+protectant, antiviral) all compete under the "Cold Sore Treatments" umbrella.
