## Overview
**Resolves a prior hedge on [[dimenhydrinate]], and confirms a real one-brand-three-mechanisms finding.** Confirmed: Dramamine Nausea (Long Lasting Formula) is **meclizine HCl 25mg** — a genuinely different active ingredient from Dramamine for Kids/Dramamine Motion Sickness (dimenhydrinate, per [[dimenhydrinate]]). A new dedicated [[meclizine]] ingredient page was created in this pass since this ingredient had none despite being the single largest brand share (36%) in this database's Nausea Remedies category. The "Dramamine" brand also separately markets a ginger-based "Advanced Herbals" non-drowsy line (not further researched in this pass) — meaning the brand spans THREE mechanistically distinct products: dimenhydrinate (antihistamine), meclizine (a different antihistamine), and ginger (herbal, non-drug mechanism) — the most extensive one-brand-multiple-mechanisms finding in this database's Gastrointestinal section, exceeding even Dulcolax's two-mechanism split (bisacodyl/docusate, researched separately).

## Pros
- Confirmed labeling; meclizine's longer duration of action (up to 24h) allows simpler once- or twice-daily dosing versus [[dimenhydrinate]]'s every-6-8-hour regimen
- Per [[meclizine]]: generally considered less sedating than dimenhydrinate/diphenhydramine, though sharing the same core anticholinergic mechanism and side-effect class
- Established FDA-approved mechanism for motion sickness and vestibular-disease-related dizziness per [[meclizine]]'s StatPearls sourcing

## Cons
- **A consumer choosing between "Dramamine" products based on brand trust alone, without checking the specific formulation, could get any of three mechanistically distinct products** (dimenhydrinate, meclizine, or ginger) — a real, consequential brand-identity risk, especially relevant for a consumer with a known sensitivity/allergy to one specific antihistamine molecule but not realizing a same-brand alternative uses a different one
- Not recommended in children under 12 per manufacturer labeling — a real age-cutoff difference from Dramamine for Kids (dimenhydrinate, labeled down to age 2)
- Some older monograph sources describe meclizine as contraindicated in women who are or may become pregnant, per [[meclizine]] — a real, sourced caution flagged rather than resolved definitively
- No Dramamine-Nausea-brand-specific RCT beyond general meclizine literature was independently located in this pass

## Use Cases
Prevention and relief of nausea, dizziness, and vomiting for up to 24 hours per dose — via meclizine's H1-antihistamine/anticholinergic mechanism, per [[meclizine]], most effective when taken prophylactically before the triggering activity.

## Allergies / Warnings
Per label: adults/children ≥12y only; take 1-2 tablets once daily. Per [[meclizine]]: some sources describe a pregnancy contraindication; general anticholinergic-class cautions (drowsiness, dry mouth, blurred vision) apply, generally to a lesser degree than dimenhydrinate/diphenhydramine.

## Symptoms Treated
Nausea, dizziness, vomiting (motion-sickness- and vestibular-disease-related) — via the meclizine mechanism detailed in the new [[meclizine]] page.
