## Appears In
Aspirin, Recurrent Heart Attack/Stroke Prevention - 3%

## Overview
VAZALORE is confirmed as a genuinely different aspirin delivery technology from its category competitors — a liquid-filled capsule using the PLxGuard/PL2200 platform, engineered to release aspirin in the duodenum (first part of the small intestine) rather than the stomach, distinct from Ecotrin/St. Joseph's simpler enteric-coating approach. FDA approved it as the first OTC liquid-filled aspirin capsule. Real, dedicated supporting evidence exists: the Cryer study (204 healthy volunteers, ages 50-75, randomized to immediate-release aspirin vs. VAZALORE with baseline/follow-up endoscopy) found a statistically significant reduction in gastric ulcerations with VAZALORE by day 7.

## Pros
- **Real, dedicated, brand-specific RCT evidence** (the Cryer study) exists showing a statistically significant GI-safety benefit over immediate-release aspirin — a genuinely stronger direct evidence link to this specific product's mechanism than the simpler enteric-coating approach used by Ecotrin/St. Joseph, which rests on general coating-mechanism plausibility rather than a comparably dedicated brand trial (per this pass's search)
- Liquid-filled capsule technology avoids some of the absorption-delay/reliability concerns associated with older enteric-coating approaches, per general pharmacokinetic reasoning (not independently re-confirmed with dedicated bioavailability data in this pass)

## Cons
- **The Cryer study is a single, short-duration (about 7 days) trial** — independent reviewers (per this pass's sourced coverage) note more long-term data is needed before concluding VAZALORE is definitively safer than standard aspirin for CHRONIC cardioprotective use, which is the actual real-world use case for this category (ongoing secondary prevention, not a 7-day course)
- Reduced gastric ulceration (a surrogate/intermediate endpoint) has not been shown in this pass's sourced evidence to translate into a lower rate of actual clinically significant GI bleeding events or improved long-term cardiovascular outcomes specifically for VAZALORE users — a real gap between the study's endpoint and the clinically meaningful outcome
- Smallest share (3%) of the three tracked brands in this category, and typically higher-cost than conventional enteric-coated generics, a real practical (not clinical) consideration
- Same core systemic aspirin risks apply regardless of delivery technology (GI bleeding via COX-1 inhibition, Reye syndrome in children/teens, anticoagulant interactions) — VAZALORE's technology addresses local gastric-mucosa contact, not aspirin's systemic bleeding-risk mechanism

## Use Cases
Doctor-directed secondary prevention of recurrent heart attack/stroke (325mg formulation, per DailyMed) — marketed with a GI-safety-focused positioning relative to standard aspirin.

## Allergies / Warnings
Standard aspirin-class cautions apply per [[aspirin]] regardless of delivery technology: Reye syndrome risk in children/teens with viral illness; systemic GI-bleeding risk; anticoagulant-interaction caution.

## Symptoms Treated
Secondary prevention of recurrent heart attack/stroke — same core use case as [[Ecotrin]] and [[St. Joseph Low Dose Aspirin]], with a real, differentiated GI-safety-focused delivery mechanism.

Presented as a genuine, if incomplete, positive rather than a settled verdict.
