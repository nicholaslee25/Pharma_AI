## Overview
**Discrepancy flagged — FDA regulatory finding:** Optase's (Scope Health) main FDA labels list **glycerin** as the active ingredient across its Dry Eye Intense and related drop lines. However, on **July 9, 2025 — the same date as the [[iVizia]] warning letter, part of the same FDA enforcement wave** — the FDA sent Scope Health a warning letter identifying **seven Optase products** as unapproved new drugs/misbranded products. Four specific products contained ingredients labeled "inactive" that FDA determined function as active ingredients based on the company's own website marketing claims: **Allegro Eye Drops** (hydroxyethyl cellulose, Ectoin), **MGD Advanced Dry Eye Drops** (glycerin, sacha inchi seed oil, trehalose, sodium hyaluronate), **Dry Eye Intense Drops** (glycerin, sodium hyaluronate), and **HYLO Night Eye Ointment** (Vitamin A, listed as inactive despite being marketed for active use). This is the same enforcement pattern found on [[iVizia]] — ingredients marketed on the company website as therapeutically active while listed as "inactive" on the FDA label, violating the OTC ophthalmic demulcent monograph (M018). **Not a recall or reported safety issue**: Scope Health stated it provided a detailed FDA response and updated its website to remove non-permitted claims, and stated the warning letter "does not call into question the safety of our products" (company statement, not independently verified here). Products remain on sale.

## Pros
- Glycerin (the FDA-recognized active ingredient in the main Dry Eye Intense line) is a legitimate OTC lubricant active with an established safety record
- Company responded promptly to the FDA warning letter and revised marketing claims rather than continuing to make the flagged statements, per trade-press reporting
- No safety recall or adverse-event signal identified — the FDA action concerns marketing/labeling classification, not a reported harm

## Cons
- **Same FDA marketing-violation pattern as [[iVizia]]**: ingredients (sodium hyaluronate, trehalose, Vitamin A, sacha inchi seed oil, Ectoin, hydroxyethyl cellulose depending on sub-product) were marketed on the company website with implied therapeutic benefit while listed as inactive on the FDA label — a "drug-free"/inactive-ingredient marketing claim contradicted by FDA's own determination, the exact discrepancy pattern this project's research process is designed to catch
- Product-line complexity (7+ named sub-products, only some of which were cited) means a consumer cannot assume every Optase SKU shares the same regulatory status — the flagged issues are specific to the named products above, not necessarily all Optase products
- [[artificial-tears]]'s general lubricant-class evidence does not cover this brand-specific regulatory finding

## Use Cases
Temporary relief of dry-eye discomfort via the labeled glycerin (or other monograph-permitted) active ingredient in each sub-product; marketed benefits attributed to the ingredients named in the FDA warning letter should be weighed against that finding.

## Allergies / Warnings
Per label: standard OTC lubricant cautions (discontinue and see a doctor for eye pain, vision changes, or symptoms persisting beyond 72 hours). No independently-verified safety signal beyond the regulatory/marketing-classification issue described above.

## Symptoms Treated
Dry eye discomfort via the labeled active ingredient in each specific sub-product; other claimed benefits are the subject of the July 2025 FDA warning letter.
