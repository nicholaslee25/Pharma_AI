## Overview
**Discrepancy flagged:** Clear Eyes' (Prestige Brands) own FDA OTC Drug Facts labels (DailyMed) show its flagship products — Clear Eyes Redness Relief (naphazoline HCl 0.012%), Clear Eyes Maximum Redness Relief (naphazoline HCl 0.03%) — are **vasoconstrictor redness relievers**, not lubricant-only artificial tears. Naphazoline is an alpha-adrenergic agonist that constricts conjunctival blood vessels to reduce visible redness; it carries a real, well-documented **rebound hyperemia risk with regular/prolonged use** (redness worsens as the vasoconstrictor effect wears off, prompting more frequent use) — a pharmacologically distinct mechanism and safety profile from the demulcent/lubricant actives (PEG, CMC, hypromellose, glycerin) covered on [[artificial-tears]], which does not address vasoconstrictor pharmacology at all. Clear Eyes Maximum Itchy Eye Relief adds zinc sulfate (an astringent) and naphazoline together, more clearly belonging to the Ophthalmic Antihistamines/Decongestants subcategory the brand also appears in (7% share) than to the lubricants category. **No dedicated ingredient-evidence page for ophthalmic decongestants/vasoconstrictors exists yet in this project** — noted as a gap to fill when that EENT subcategory is researched directly, per this pass's missing-ingredient-page convention. No Clear Eyes-brand-specific clinical trial beyond general naphazoline pharmacology was identified.

## Pros
- Naphazoline is an FDA-monograph-recognized OTC ophthalmic redness reliever with a well-established, decades-long safety and mechanism record for short-term use
- Product line offers graduated strength options (0.012% standard vs. 0.03% maximum) letting users choose intensity
- Fast, visibly effective redness reduction for occasional/minor eye irritation, a real and distinct benefit from the slower-acting lubrication approach of pure artificial tears

## Cons
- **Category-placement mismatch:** Clear Eyes' flagship products are vasoconstrictor redness relievers, not lubricants — being listed in this database's "Artificial Tears/Ophthalmic Lubricants" Pharmacy Times category (4% share) does not mean the product functions the same way as [[Systane]], [[Refresh]], or other genuine lubricant-only brands in that list
- **Rebound hyperemia risk**: regular or prolonged naphazoline use can cause redness to worsen once the drug wears off, potentially leading to a cycle of increasing use — a real, clinically significant safety consideration not covered by [[artificial-tears]]'s lubricant-focused evidence
- Masks redness rather than treating an underlying cause — persistent redness warrants medical evaluation rather than continued self-treatment, per standard OTC decongestant-eye-drop labeling caution

## Use Cases
Temporary relief of eye redness due to minor irritation; Maximum Itchy Eye Relief variant also targets itching (antihistamine/astringent combination). Not intended for regular, sustained daily use.

## Allergies / Warnings
Per label: do not use for more than 72 hours continuously without consulting a doctor; not for use with contact lenses in most formulations (check specific SKU); may cause rebound redness with overuse; avoid in narrow-angle glaucoma or if using MAOIs (general vasoconstrictor-class cautions). Discontinue if pain, vision changes, or symptoms worsen.

## Symptoms Treated
Eye redness (vasoconstrictor mechanism, not lubrication); itching in the Maximum Itchy Eye Relief variant.
