## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed: "Childrens Tylenol Cold Plus Cough Plus Runny Nose" suspension = **acetaminophen 160mg + chlorpheniramine maleate 1mg + dextromethorphan HBr 5mg per 5mL**, labeled for ages 6-11 (grape-flavored). This combines [[acetaminophen]] and [[dextromethorphan]] (both already in this database) with chlorpheniramine, a first-generation antihistamine with no dedicated ingredient page here — the same "no dedicated first-gen antihistamine page beyond diphenhydramine" gap already flagged on `drugs/Children's Dimetapp.md` and `drugs/Children's Dimetapp Cold and Allergy.md` (which use brompheniramine instead). Note the separately-filed `drugs/Children's Tylenol.md` (Children's Analgesics category) is a distinct, simpler acetaminophen-only product — **the "Children's Tylenol" brand spans single-ingredient analgesic and multi-symptom combination product lines**, the same pattern already documented for Dimetapp, Mucinex, and Delsym above.

## Pros
- Acetaminophen component draws on this database's most thoroughly documented analgesic/antipyretic evidence base ([[acetaminophen]]) rather than an unstudied combination ingredient
- Ages 6-11 labeling is more conservative than the age-4 floor set by some competitor combination products (Children's Mucinex, Children's Robitussin CF), directly reflecting the post-2008 industry caution around pediatric multi-ingredient cough/cold products

## Cons
- **Directly implements the 2008 FDA/CHPA pediatric cough-cold labeling standard** (see `drugs/Children's Dimetapp Cold and Allergy.md` for full regulatory history) — but at an even higher age floor (6, not 4) than several competitor combination products in this same category, worth noting as a genuine differentiator rather than an equivalent risk profile across all Children's Cough/Cold Combinations brands
- **Combination-product acetaminophen-dosing risk**: because acetaminophen is combined here with two other actives rather than sold alone, a parent who also gives a separate acetaminophen-containing product (e.g., plain Children's Tylenol for fever) risks unintentional double-dosing — the same real-world overdose mechanism [[acetaminophen]]'s evidence page documents as a leading cause of unintentional acetaminophen overdose generally, now with concrete pediatric relevance for this specific brand family
- No dedicated ingredient page exists for chlorpheniramine in this database — flagged as a gap, consistent with the brompheniramine gap already noted for Dimetapp
- No brand-specific pediatric RCT located for this triple combination beyond the individual-ingredient evidence on [[acetaminophen]] and [[dextromethorphan]]

## Use Cases
Relief of headache, fever, body aches, runny nose, sneezing, and cough due to the common cold, ages 6-11.

## Allergies / Warnings
Per label: not for children under 6; severe liver damage may occur if used with other acetaminophen-containing products or beyond the labeled dose (same core acetaminophen safety warning as [[acetaminophen]]); do not use with any other cough, cold, or antihistamine product; ask a doctor before use if the child has a breathing problem, glaucoma, or trouble urinating due to an enlarged prostate.

## Symptoms Treated
Headache, fever, body aches, runny nose, sneezing, cough.
