## Overview
Roche's **Accu-Chek** line (Performa, Aviva Nano, Performa Nano models identified in sources) has brand-specific accuracy data spanning a genuinely wide range: a nine-year retrospective study of Accu-Chek Performa meters found **MARD results ranging from 2.3% to 20.5%** — an unusually wide single-brand spread, wider even than [[FreeStyle]]'s Libre CGM range (7.8-18.33%), though drawn from a longer time window and possibly more varied conditions. Despite this spread, multiple studies confirm Accu-Chek Performa models met minimum ISO 15197:2003 and ISO 15197:2013 system-accuracy requirements when formally evaluated. Accu-Chek is the only brand in this category with equal (26%/26%) share across both the Blood Glucose Monitors and Blood Sampling Devices/Lancets categories, suggesting closely paired meter-and-lancet adoption in practice. No data distinguishing independent from Roche-sponsored studies was clearly available in the sources gathered.

## Pros
- Multiple published studies confirm ISO 15197:2003 and :2013 compliance for tested Accu-Chek models when formally evaluated
- Long observational history (nine-year retrospective dataset) provides a real longitudinal accuracy record, unusual among brands in this category
- Equal, high share (26%) in both paired categories (monitors and lancets), suggesting a well-integrated meter+lancet system in practice

## Cons
- The widest single-brand MARD range located in this pass (2.3% to 20.5%) — nearly a 9-fold spread — meaning "Accu-Chek accuracy" cannot be summarized as one number any more confidently than for other brands, despite formal ISO compliance
- As with all brands in this category, the general manufacturer-funding-bias caveat from King et al. 2018 (on [[blood-glucose-monitors]]) applies — the sources did not clearly separate independent from Roche-sponsored data
- Subject to the same general interference/hematocrit/strip-lot failure modes documented on [[blood-glucose-monitors]]

## Use Cases
Blood glucose self-monitoring for diabetes management via fingerstick capillary sampling.
