## Overview
**Real, serious, and directly relevant discrepancy found here — among the most significant findings in this entire database's supplement research.** One A Day Prenatal is the subject of an active class-action lawsuit (Kharaeva et al. v. Bayer AG et al., Case No. 2:22-cv-00640) alleging that **independent testing found the product contains heavy metals AND far less folic acid than promised on the label**. This is a uniquely severe combination given [[prenatal-vitamins]]'s own evidence page: folic acid is documented there as having one of the strongest, most consistent protective effects in all of preventive medicine (77% neural-tube-defect risk reduction specifically attributable to folic acid, per a 296,816-participant umbrella review) — meaning an under-dosed folic acid product doesn't just fail to add value, it fails to deliver the single most clinically load-bearing benefit this entire product category exists to provide. This is a real, brand-specific escalation of [[prenatal-vitamins]]'s own newly-added category-wide GAO finding (11 of 12 tested prenatal products had at least one nutrient level differing from label, though the GAO sample itself found lead/cadmium below FDA's daily threshold — this lawsuit alleges a more serious combination specific to this brand). This sits alongside [[One A Day]]'s own separately-documented brand-wide pattern of health-claim litigation (prostate-cancer selenium claims, heart/immunity/energy claims, "natural" labeling) already found on that page.
