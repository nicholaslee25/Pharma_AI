## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed: Halls' active ingredient across its lozenge line is **menthol**, dosed differently by variant — Mentho-Lyptus 5.4mg, Honey 1.8mg, Sugar Free Honey Lemon 7.6mg per drop, labeled as both a cough suppressant and oral anesthetic. **No dedicated ingredient page exists in this database for menthol** — this is a significant, previously-unnoticed data gap given menthol is the active ingredient across most of this "Topical Cough Suppressants, Lozenges" category (Halls, Ricola, Fisherman's Friend, Honees, Burt's Bees, and Vicks VapoCOOL's plain SKUs all confirmed as menthol-based during this research pass). Label saved at `raw/primary-sources/brands/halls/dailymed-halls.md`.

## Pros
- Dual labeled action (cough suppressant + oral anesthetic) in a single, simple, well-tolerated ingredient
- Wide dose range across variants (1.8-7.6mg/drop) lets users choose intensity
- Largest recommendation share (19%) in its category in this database

## Cons
- No ingredient page exists in this database to provide menthol's formal evidence grade, mechanism detail, or safety profile — this page cannot currently state how strong the evidence is for menthol's cough-suppressant claim specifically, a genuine research gap worth flagging (a future `ingredients/menthol.md` page covering the shared active ingredient across most of this lozenge category would be a high-value addition, since it would resolve this same gap for at least 5-6 brands at once)
- Menthol dose varies substantially by variant (over 4x between Honey and Sugar Free Honey Lemon) — a consumer switching variants should not assume equivalent strength

## Use Cases
Temporary relief of cough due to minor throat/bronchial irritation; oral anesthetic action for minor throat/mouth irritation.

## Allergies / Warnings
Standard OTC lozenge age/dose directions apply (specific per-variant directions not fully extracted in this pass). Ask a doctor if cough persists >7 days or recurs.

## Symptoms Treated
