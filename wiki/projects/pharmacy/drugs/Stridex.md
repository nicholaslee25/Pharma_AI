## Overview
Stridex (Blistex, Inc.; originally a Bayer trademark, acquired by Blistex in 1996) is confirmed via multiple DailyMed labels as a **salicylic-acid-only** line today, spanning three concentrations across its current SKUs — a narrower, single-mechanism version of the multi-concentration pattern seen brand-wide in this pass:

- **Stridex Sensitive** — salicylic acid 0.5% w/w, "with Aloe"
- **Stridex Essential** — salicylic acid 1% w/w, "with Vitamins"
- **Stridex Maximum** and **Stridex XL Face & Body Pads** — salicylic acid 2% w/w

Confirmed active/purpose/uses (Stridex Maximum label): **salicylic acid 2.0% (w/w)**, purpose "acne medication," uses "treatment of acne... reduces pimples and blackheads while promoting skin healing... helps prevent new acne pimples from forming."

**Historical note (real, not currently sold):** Stridex previously also sold "Power Pads" containing 2.5% **benzoyl peroxide** — a genuinely different mechanism from the rest of the line — now discontinued per retail/review-site listings. Current DailyMed search returned only salicylic-acid-labeled Stridex products; no current benzoyl-peroxide Stridex SKU was located in this pass. This resolves [[benzoyl-peroxide]]'s prior "plausible, unconfirmed" listing of Stridex — the brand is confirmed NOT currently benzoyl-peroxide-based, though it was historically.

**Brand-specific trial (real, and unusually specific for this pass):** a 12-week, double-blind, randomized, vehicle-controlled trial by Shalita et al., cited in a JCAD (Journal of Clinical and Aesthetic Dermatology) review of OTC acne treatments, tested **Stridex-brand 0.5% salicylic-acid pads specifically** (attributed to "Blistex, Oak Brook, Illinois" in the review) applied twice daily against vehicle (placebo) pads in mild-to-moderate acne. Result: the actively-treated group showed greater reduction in both inflammatory lesions and open comedones than vehicle. This is a genuine brand-name-specific finding, not merely generic salicylic-acid evidence — full original citation details (journal/year/PMID) were not independently pulled beyond the secondary JCAD citation in this pass, flagged as a minor sourcing gap.

## Pros
- Confirmed salicylic-acid concentrations (0.5%/1%/2%) across the current line fall within FDA-monograph OTC range
- Rare among brands in this pass: an actual brand-name-specific RCT (Shalita et al., via JCAD secondary citation) supports the lowest-concentration (0.5%) product specifically, not just the ingredient generally
- Three-tier concentration ladder (Sensitive/Essential/Maximum) gives consumers a legitimate step-up option within one brand as tolerance allows, rather than requiring a brand switch
- Single-mechanism (salicylic-acid-only) product line today — simpler for a consumer to reason about than dual-mechanism brands like [[OXY]], [[Cetaphil]], or [[La Roche-Posay]]

## Cons
- Original Shalita et al. trial's full bibliographic details (journal, year, PMID) were not independently confirmed in this pass — sourced via a secondary review's citation, not the primary paper itself
- Historical Power Pads (2.5% benzoyl peroxide) discontinuation was confirmed only via retail/review-site mentions, not an official Blistex statement — treated as likely-accurate but not primary-source-confirmed
- No brand-specific trial was located for the 1% (Essential) or 2% (Maximum) concentrations specifically — only the 0.5% (Sensitive-equivalent) formulation has the Shalita et al. citation
- Concentration is not obvious from product naming alone ("Essential" = 1%, "Sensitive" = 0.5%, "Maximum" = 2%) — a consumer must check the Drug Facts panel, not just the marketing tier name, to know the actual strength

## Use Cases
Acne treatment via salicylic acid's keratolytic/comedolytic action, in a stepped concentration ladder (0.5% sensitive-skin option up to 2% maximum strength) within one product family.

## Allergies / Warnings
Per [[salicylic-acid]] and the Stridex Maximum label directly: for external use only; do not use if allergic to salicylic acid; skin irritation/dryness more likely if combined with other topical acne medications; keep away from eyes, lips, mucous membranes; keep out of reach of children; contact Poison Control if swallowed; discontinue or reduce frequency if excessive dryness/peeling occurs.

## Symptoms Treated
Acne pimples, blackheads, and general acne-prone skin — via salicylic acid across three graduated concentrations.
