## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed (CeraVe Developed with Dermatologists Itch Relief Moisturizing Cream and Lotion, manufacturer L'Oreal USA): **pramoxine hydrochloride 1%**, purpose "external analgesic," for temporary relief of itching from minor skin irritations. This is a single-active-ingredient formula, the same anesthetic class already documented for Caladryl and Sarna in this category — pramoxine HCl, chemically distinct from both benzocaine and lidocaine (see [[topical-anesthetics]]'s coverage-gap note). Both cream and lotion formats share the identical active ingredient and Drug Facts information.

CeraVe's broader brand identity centers on ceramide-containing moisturizer formulations (the "Cera" in CeraVe) — the Itch Relief line combines the pramoxine anesthetic with the brand's standard ceramide/moisturizing base, a genuine and non-contradictory combination (moisturizing a dry, irritated area while numbing the itch sensation) rather than a mismatch. No CeraVe-Itch-Relief-brand-specific clinical trial was located in this pass; evidence rests on the general pramoxine ingredient literature.

## Pros
- Confirmed, single, consistent active ingredient (pramoxine HCl 1%) across cream and lotion formats
- Combines a genuine anesthetic with the brand's established ceramide-based moisturizing formulation — plausibly beneficial for the dry, irritated skin common to poison-ivy/eczema-type reactions, though this combination's added benefit over pramoxine alone was not independently verified via a dedicated trial in this pass
- Dermatologist-developed branding and a well-established parent product line (CeraVe) known for barrier-repair moisturizers

## Cons
- No dedicated ingredient page for pramoxine exists in this database (shared gap with Caladryl, Sarna, Ivarest, Preparation H, Bacitraycin Plus) — its safety profile is not independently documented in as much depth as benzocaine/lidocaine or diphenhydramine
- No CeraVe-Itch-Relief-brand-specific clinical trial was located
- Single-ingredient anesthetic formula, without calamine's drying effect for actively oozing/weeping poison-ivy lesions (unlike Caladryl/Ivarest) — may be less suited to the wettest phase of a reaction, though not independently confirmed as a meaningful clinical difference in this pass

## Use Cases
Temporary relief of itching associated with minor skin irritations, including poison ivy/oak/sumac rashes, insect bites, and sunburn (per general pramoxine-product labeling).

## Allergies / Warnings
Per label: for external use only; avoid contact with eyes and mucous membranes; stop use and consult a doctor if condition worsens, symptoms persist beyond 7 days, or symptoms clear and recur within days; children under 2 should consult a doctor before use.

## Symptoms Treated
Itching from poison ivy/oak/sumac rashes, insect bites, and minor skin irritations.
