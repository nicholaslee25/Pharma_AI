## Overview
**Real mismatched-ingredient finding.** [[antacids]]'s existing "Brands Using This Ingredient" note named Gaviscon among "other real-world antacid brands" using "varying combinations of calcium carbonate, magnesium hydroxide, and/or aluminum hydroxide — not individually verified per SKU." Confirmed via DailyMed: **Gaviscon (US) contains NO calcium carbonate at all** — its actual active ingredients are **aluminum hydroxide + magnesium carbonate** (liquid and chewable-tablet formulations both confirmed this combination; tablet formulations also list alginic acid among the ingredients). This makes Gaviscon mechanistically distinct from this category's dominant brand, Tums (pure calcium carbonate), and closer in composition to Mylanta/Gelusil (both aluminum + magnesium combinations, confirmed separately in this same research pass). Second-largest Antacids share (19%) in this database, after Tums (52%).

## Pros
- Confirmed FDA OTC label; aluminum hydroxide + magnesium carbonate is an established antacid combination per [[antacids]]'s general drug-class evidence
- Combining aluminum hydroxide (constipating) with magnesium carbonate (a magnesium salt, generally loosening) may partially offset each salt's individual bowel-habit side effect — a plausible formulation rationale, though not independently confirmed by a comparative trial in this pass
- No calcium content means Gaviscon avoids the milk-alkali syndrome risk specifically tied to calcium-carbonate antacids like Tums

## Cons
- **Not calcium-carbonate-based despite sitting in a category whose largest brand (Tums, 52% share) and this database's representative-agent ingredient-page sourcing are both calcium carbonate** — a consumer assuming category-mates share a mechanism would be mistaken
- Per [[antacids]]'s own Key Safety Data: aluminum-hydroxide-containing antacids carry a real toxicity risk profile in renal failure patients and infants (osteopenia, microcytic anemia, neurotoxicity, constipation) — a materially different safety profile than Tums' calcium-carbonate-specific risks (hypercalcemia, milk-alkali syndrome)
- Per [[antacids]]'s Drug Interactions: magnesium-containing antacids (like this one) specifically bind tetracycline/fluoroquinolone antibiotics, impeding their absorption — a real interaction risk not shared by Tums' pure-calcium formulation to the same degree
- No Gaviscon-specific RCT beyond the general antacid-class evidence base was independently located in this pass
- Real-world Gaviscon products elsewhere (notably the UK/international "Gaviscon" formula) use a different alginate-raft mechanism than the US aluminum/magnesium formula confirmed here — this database's finding applies specifically to the US-market DailyMed label, not necessarily to Gaviscon products sold under the same name in other countries, a genuine cross-market naming ambiguity worth flagging

## Use Cases
Relief of heartburn, acid indigestion, sour stomach via aluminum hydroxide/magnesium carbonate acid neutralization — same general antacid use case as Tums, but a mechanistically distinct salt combination.

## Allergies / Warnings
Per label: do not use if the printed inner safety seal is broken/missing; do not exceed maximum dosage for more than 2 weeks without doctor supervision. Per [[antacids]]: caution in renal failure (aluminum accumulation risk) and in patients on tetracycline/fluoroquinolone antibiotics (absorption interference). Ask a doctor/pharmacist before use if taking any prescription drug.

## Symptoms Treated
Heartburn, acid indigestion, sour stomach — via an aluminum-hydroxide/magnesium-carbonate antacid mechanism, distinct from Tums' pure calcium-carbonate mechanism despite both being filed under the same Antacids category.

## Brand-Specific Alignment Check
**Real finding, added to both this page and [[antacids]]:** Gaviscon (US formulation) is confirmed as aluminum hydroxide + magnesium carbonate, NOT calcium carbonate — resolving the ingredient page's prior "not individually verified per SKU" hedge for this specific brand, and correcting any assumption that this category's brands share Tums' calcium-carbonate mechanism by default.
