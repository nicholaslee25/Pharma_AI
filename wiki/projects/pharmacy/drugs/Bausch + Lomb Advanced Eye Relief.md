## Overview
"Advanced Eye Relief" is a multi-formulation product line, not a single active ingredient — FDA labels (DailyMed) confirm genuinely different formulations depending on sub-product: **Advanced Eye Relief Dry Eye**: glycerin 0.3% + propylene glycol 1.0% (both lubricants, a genuine combination-lubricant formula consistent with [[artificial-tears]]'s favorably-evidenced combination category); **Advanced Eye Relief Maximum Redness**: hypromellose 0.5% (lubricant) + naphazoline HCl 0.03% (vasoconstrictor redness reliever) — the same rebound-hyperemia-risk mechanism flagged on [[Clear Eyes]], present here in a lubricant/vasoconstrictor combination rather than [[Clear Eyes]]'s vasoconstrictor-only formula; **Advanced Eye Relief Eye Wash**: purified water 99.05% (a rinse product, not a therapeutic lubricant in the same sense). A consumer buying "Advanced Eye Relief" without checking the specific sub-product name could reasonably end up with any of three pharmacologically distinct products. No brand-specific clinical trial beyond the general glycerin/propylene glycol/hypromellose/naphazoline ingredient-level evidence already on [[artificial-tears]] was identified.

## Pros
- Dry Eye sub-formula's glycerin + propylene glycol combination sits in the favorably-evidenced combination-lubricant category per [[artificial-tears]]'s cited review
- Product-line breadth (pure lubricant, lubricant+redness-reliever combo, and plain saline washout) covers three genuinely different use cases under clear sub-product naming
- Bausch + Lomb has long-standing ophthalmic manufacturing experience across multiple brands in this database ([[Soothe]], [[Lumify]] also B+L products)

## Cons
- **Formulation is not uniform across the "Advanced Eye Relief" name** — the Maximum Redness variant contains a vasoconstrictor (naphazoline) with the same rebound-hyperemia risk flagged on [[Clear Eyes]], a materially different safety profile from the pure-lubricant Dry Eye variant sold under the same parent brand name
- The Eye Wash sub-product (essentially purified water) provides mechanical rinsing only, not therapeutic lubrication or redness relief — a consumer expecting "eye relief" from this specific SKU would get a different benefit than from the other two variants
- No independent brand-specific outcome trial exists for any of the three sub-formulations beyond general ingredient-level evidence

## Use Cases
Dry Eye variant: temporary relief of dry-eye burning/irritation. Maximum Redness variant: temporary relief of eye redness plus lubrication, with the naphazoline overuse/rebound caution that applies. Eye Wash: mechanical flushing of the eye (e.g. after exposure to irritants), not therapeutic lubrication.

## Allergies / Warnings
Varies by sub-product: standard lubricant cautions for Dry Eye and Eye Wash variants; Maximum Redness variant carries the naphazoline-class cautions (do not use more than 72 hours continuously; rebound redness risk with overuse; avoid in narrow-angle glaucoma) — see [[Clear Eyes]] for the same active-ingredient safety profile.

## Symptoms Treated
Dry eye burning/irritation (Dry Eye variant); eye redness plus dryness (Maximum Redness variant); mechanical eye irritant flushing (Eye Wash variant).

## Brand-Specific Alignment Check
Consistent with [[artificial-tears]] for the Dry Eye sub-formula (no discrepancy, genuine combination lubricant). **Discrepancy flagged for the Maximum Redness sub-formula**, same pattern as [[Clear Eyes]]: it contains a vasoconstrictor active (naphazoline) with rebound-hyperemia risk not addressed by the lubricant-focused ingredient page — worth checking which specific "Advanced Eye Relief" sub-product a given recommendation actually refers to before assuming lubricant-only pharmacology.
