## Overview
**Real, significant discrepancy found here.** Neuriva (Reckitt), the second-largest brand in this database's Brain Health category (24% share), uses whole coffee fruit concentrate (WCFC) and phosphatidylserine as its key ingredients, marketed as "clinically tested" and "scientifically proven to improve brain function." Real class-action litigation alleges these specific claims are false or misleading; per [[brain-health-nootropics]], independent expert re-analysis of the company's own clinical trial data — applying standard, well-accepted clinical-trial analysis principles — concluded the product and its two main ingredients perform **no better than placebo**. Separately, the underlying biological premise (that orally-ingested coffee cherry extract meaningfully raises brain BDNF levels or improves brain performance) is described in the litigation record as lacking scientific or clinical proof, with the cited studies themselves acknowledging further research is needed. An initial $8 million settlement was reached but later vacated by the Eleventh Circuit on procedural (plaintiff standing) grounds — not because the court found the underlying advertising claims valid.
