## Appears In
Ear Wax Removal - 3%

## Overview
Confirmed: NeilMed ClearCanal's own FDA/DailyMed label describes a **two-part kit**: carbamide peroxide 6.5% drops (same active ingredient as [[Debrox]]) to soften/loosen wax, plus a separate USP sodium chloride (9 mg/mL) / sodium bicarbonate (0.5-1.0 mg/mL) saline rinse spray to irrigate it out. This two-step approach (soften, then irrigate) matches the sequence [[cerumenolytics]]'s cited StatPearls source describes as standard practice, packaged as one product. Per that same page's Cochrane review, no evidence exists that combining a cerumenolytic with saline irrigation outperforms either step alone, or that carbamide peroxide outperforms plain water/saline in the first place — so the kit's two-step design is a reasonable, standard-practice-aligned approach, not one with independently demonstrated superior outcomes. No NeilMed-brand-specific clinical trial was identified.

## Pros
- Combines both standard steps (soften with a cerumenolytic, then irrigate with saline) in one kit, matching StatPearls' described standard-of-care sequence per [[cerumenolytics]]
- Uses the same well-established carbamide peroxide active as the category leader (Debrox)
- Saline-based irrigation step avoids introducing a second chemical agent beyond the carbamide peroxide drops

## Cons
- Per [[cerumenolytics]], no evidence exists that this combination approach outperforms carbamide peroxide alone, water/saline alone, or manual removal
- Irrigation carries its own documented caveats per [[cerumenolytics]]: water temperature must match body temperature to avoid caloric vertigo, and irrigation is less effective than manual removal under direct visualization in patients over 70
- No brand-specific trial exists to confirm this specific kit's real-world effectiveness over its individual components used separately

## Use Cases
Occasional two-step softening (carbamide peroxide drops) and irrigation (saline spray) removal of excessive earwax.

## Allergies / Warnings
Per [[cerumenolytics]]-class cautions: avoid with perforated eardrum, ear pain/discharge, or recent ear surgery; irrigation-specific caution around water temperature to avoid vertigo; discontinue and consult a doctor if new symptoms occur.

## Symptoms Treated
Excessive/impacted earwax causing fullness, hearing reduction, or itching.
