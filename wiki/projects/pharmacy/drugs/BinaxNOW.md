## Overview
Abbott's **BinaxNOW COVID-19 Ag Card** (rapid antigen self-test, FDA EUA granted August 26, 2020, expanded to OTC/at-home asymptomatic multi-test use March 31, 2021) is the market-leading brand in this category by a wide margin (53% share, more than the next four brands combined). Brand-specific clinical performance varies substantially by study context — the manufacturer's own EUA-supporting interim clinical study reported **91.7% sensitivity / 100% specificity** against a high-sensitivity molecular reference, but an independent rural-population validation study found meaningfully lower **67.6% sensitivity (95% CI 50.2–81.9%) / 100% specificity**, and a community-based study using a Ct<30 cutoff in largely asymptomatic/late-testing individuals found **100% sensitivity / 98.9% specificity** at that cutoff, dropping to 97.5%/99.7% at Ct<35. This spread across three real studies is a concrete illustration of [[at-home-covid-tests]]'s own core point: no single "accuracy" number applies, and manufacturer-supported studies tend to report higher sensitivity than independent ones — the same pattern flagged for blood glucose meters on [[blood-glucose-monitors]] (King et al. 2018's manufacturer-funding bias finding). Analytical limit of detection: 4.04×10⁴–8.06×10⁴ viral copies/swab. No independent clinical trial for this specific brand was found beyond the studies already summarized on [[at-home-covid-tests]] and cited here.

## Pros
- By far the largest recommendation share (53%) in this category, reflecting extensive real-world deployment and pharmacist familiarity
- One of the most extensively independently studied antigen tests, with multiple published real-world validation studies beyond the manufacturer's own EUA data — genuinely more external scrutiny than most competing brands
- No major safety recall identified in this pass (contrast [[Ellume]]'s Class I recall for false positives)
- Its 100% specificity finding was consistent across all three studies reviewed — false positives were not the concern in the sources found; sensitivity variability was

## Cons
- Sensitivity in independent studies (67.6% in the rural population study) fell well below the manufacturer's own EUA interim result (91.7%) — a real, quantified gap between company-reported and independently-verified performance, consistent with the manufacturer-funding bias pattern documented for glucose meters on [[blood-glucose-monitors]]
- Per the class-level Cochrane data on [[at-home-covid-tests]], sensitivity is inherently lower in asymptomatic testing and in the second week of symptoms — this brand is not exempt from that biological limitation
- A single negative result should not be treated as ruling out infection, particularly amid declining sensitivity over the course of illness — serial testing is the recommended mitigation, not a brand-specific fix

## Use Cases
Rapid (~15 minute) at-home qualitative detection of SARS-CoV-2 nucleocapsid antigen from an anterior nasal swab, in symptomatic or asymptomatic individuals, intended for serial/repeat testing per FDA authorization terms.

## Allergies / Warnings
Not a drug — no allergy profile. Standard diagnostic-device cautions: follow swab technique exactly per instructions; a negative result does not rule out infection, especially early or late in the course of illness or in asymptomatic testing; confirm unexpected results with a molecular (PCR) test per public health guidance current at time of use.

## Symptoms Treated
Not applicable — this is a diagnostic test, not a treatment. It detects, but does not treat, SARS-CoV-2 infection.
