## Overview
**Major category-placement finding:** Lice Shield (made by Lornamead, Inc., still actively sold as of 2026 through Walmart, Target, Amazon, and others) is **not an FDA-regulated pediculicide drug at all** — no DailyMed listing exists for it (unlike [[Nix]], [[RID]], [[Licefreee!]], and [[Vamousse]], all of which have DailyMed drug labels). It is a **lice repellent/prevention shampoo, conditioner, and leave-in spray line**, formulated around a blend of five essential oils — reported ingredient concentrations: citronella oil 0.995%, geraniol 0.697%, rosemary oil 0.129%, cedar oil 0.119%, lemongrass oil 0.050%, alongside sodium lauryl sulfate 8.004% as a shampoo base surfactant. These essential oils are EPA 25(b) minimum-risk-pesticide-exempt ingredients, consistent with a repellent-cosmetic regulatory pathway rather than an FDA OTC drug monograph.

**A real, confirmed regulatory action exists on this exact product line:** in 2014, the **FTC charged Lornamead** with deceptive advertising for claiming Lice Shield products would "dramatically reduce" the risk of head lice infestation and were "scientifically shown to repel head lice" — the FTC found insufficient evidence to support these prevention claims and required Lornamead to pay **$500,000** and to have at least one well-controlled human clinical study before making such claims again. Lice Shield's own marketing still centers on "repel lice and super lice" language via the same essential-oil blend as of 2026.

**This creates a genuine category-placement mismatch:** Lice Shield is tracked in this database's source data as a "Lice Treatments" brand at 6% share, but its actual products are marketed for *preventing* infestation in people who do not yet have lice, not for *treating* an active infestation — a functionally different use case from every other brand in this category. No evidence was found that Lice Shield sells an FDA-labeled active pediculicide (permethrin, pyrethrins, or otherwise) for treating an existing infestation.

## Pros
- Non-pesticide-class formulation (essential oils), generally low acute toxicity for topical/scalp use
- Marketed for daily/prophylactic use rather than reserved for an active infestation
- Widely available at major retailers

## Cons
- **Not FDA-regulated as a drug — no DailyMed label found** — unlike every other brand in this category, meaning there is no FDA Drug Facts label to verify claims against
- **Subject to a real 2014 FTC deceptive-advertising settlement ($500,000) specifically over this product line's "prevents/reduces risk of lice" claims**, which the FTC found lacked adequate scientific support at the time
- **Likely category mismatch**: this is fundamentally a prevention/repellent product, not a treatment for active head lice infestation — the "Lice Treatments" category placement and 6% share may not reflect what this product actually does
- No clinical trial specific to Lice Shield's current formulation was located confirming repellent efficacy meets the FTC's post-settlement evidentiary requirement
- Essential-oil repellent efficacy in general has, per broader literature, only mild and inconsistent support (some lab studies show modest repellent effects; systematic reviews find insufficient clinical evidence for real-world prevention claims)

## Use Cases
Marketed for daily prophylactic use to reduce the risk of head lice infestation (a preventive claim with a documented, unresolved regulatory history) — not a treatment for lice already present. Consumers seeking to treat an active infestation should look to an FDA-labeled pediculicide (see [[Nix]], [[RID]]) rather than this product.

## Allergies / Warnings
No FDA Drug Facts label exists to cite. As an essential-oil-based cosmetic/repellent product, standard precautions apply: possible skin/scalp irritation or fragrance sensitivity from citronella, geraniol, rosemary, cedar, or lemongrass oils; avoid eye contact; discontinue if irritation occurs.

## Symptoms Treated
None confirmed for active infestation — marketed as a prevention product, not a treatment, for head lice.
