## Appears In
Memory Support - 6%

## Overview
**Real, confirmed, adjudicated discrepancy found here.** BrainStrong Adult (900mg DHA, made by i-Health/Martek Biosciences) was the subject of a **2014 FTC settlement** in which the marketers agreed to resolve charges that "BrainStrong Adult" memory-improvement claims were deceptive — the FTC's complaint specifically alleged the company falsely claimed to have clinical proof that the product improves adult memory and prevents cognitive decline. This is a real, government-adjudicated (settled) enforcement action, not merely an unresolved private lawsuit. Separately, general DHA research does show some real, if modest and variable, cognitive benefit — one study found DHA supplementation improved memory and reaction time specifically in healthy young adults whose habitual diets were low in DHA — but no supplement (including this one) is FDA-approved to treat, prevent, or cure any cognitive disorder, consistent with the FTC's own finding against this specific brand's stronger claims. Memory Support (6% share) is covered by [[brain-health-nootropics]] for general category context; see [[omega-3-fish-oil]] for the general omega-3/DHA evidence base (which does not focus specifically on cognitive outcomes).

## Pros
- DHA itself has some real, if modest, general supporting literature for cognitive outcomes, particularly in populations with low habitual DHA intake
- High DHA dose (900mg) relative to typical dietary supplementation
- No new litigation beyond the historical 2014 FTC settlement was located in this pass

## Cons
- **Real, government-adjudicated 2014 FTC settlement specifically over deceptive "clinically proven" memory-improvement and cognitive-decline-prevention claims for this exact product** — a real, resolved enforcement action, not merely an allegation
- General DHA-cognition research shows modest, variable effects, most clearly in people with low baseline DHA intake — not the strong, general "prevents cognitive decline" claim the FTC found this brand had made without adequate support
- No FDA-approved cognitive-disorder treatment/prevention claim exists for DHA or any other supplement, consistent with the FTC's own finding
- Consistent with the broader, striking cross-brand pattern already documented on [[brain-health-nootropics]] for this entire category

## Use Cases
DHA supplementation with modest, general (not disease-specific) cognitive-support evidence, particularly relevant for those with low dietary DHA intake — not evidenced for memory improvement or cognitive-decline prevention as strongly as this brand was found by the FTC to have claimed.

## Allergies / Warnings
