## Appears In
Children's Mouthwashes - 50%

## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed: ACT Kids Anticavity Fluoride Rinse (Bubblegum, Wild Watermelon, Berry Blast, and related ACT Anticavity Fluoride varieties) is **sodium fluoride 0.05% (0.02% w/v fluoride ion)**, matching [[fluoride-toothpaste]]'s general fluoride evidence base extended here to the rinse format. **Discrepancy/comparison worth flagging directly:** this is roughly DOUBLE the fluoride-ion concentration of the category's #2 brand, Colgate Kids (sodium fluoride 0.02%, 0.01% w/v fluoride ion — see `drugs/Colgate Kids.md`) — a real, quantified potency difference between the two market-leading children's mouthwash brands that a caregiver comparing products by fluoride content specifically should be aware of.

## Pros
- Confirmed FDA Drug Facts label with a well-established anticavity active ingredient, backed by [[fluoride-toothpaste]]'s strong general fluoride evidence base (though that page's specific Cochrane review, Marinho 2003, concerns toothpaste rather than rinse formulations specifically — the underlying fluoride mechanism transfers, but this database has not independently confirmed a rinse-specific efficacy trial)
- Category-dominant share (50%) among Children's Mouthwashes in this database
- Higher fluoride-ion concentration than the #2 competitor (Colgate Kids), potentially a meaningful factor for a dentist recommending a higher-fluoride option for a higher-caries-risk child, though this database did not independently confirm a dose-response rinse-specific trial

## Cons
- Higher fluoride concentration also means a correspondingly higher risk if accidentally swallowed in quantity beyond normal rinsing use — the label's standard warning (contact Poison Control if more than used for rinsing is swallowed) is proportionally more consequential at this higher concentration than at Colgate Kids' lower one
- Labeled for ages 6+ with supervision for under 12 — not appropriate for younger children given fluorosis risk during tooth development (per [[fluoride-toothpaste]]'s general fluorosis-risk framing)
- No independent, ACT-brand-specific rinse efficacy RCT was identified in this pass — relies on the general fluoride ingredient-class evidence, which is itself toothpaste-focused rather than rinse-focused per [[fluoride-toothpaste]]'s own sourcing

## Use Cases
Cavity prevention via daily fluoride mouth rinse, ages 6+.

## Allergies / Warnings
Per label: keep away from children; if more than used for rinsing is accidentally swallowed, get medical help or contact a Poison Control Center right away; do not swallow; avoid eating/drinking for 30 minutes after use; supervise children under 12; under 6, consult a dentist or doctor.
