## Appears In
Children's Homeopathic Cough Products - 8%

## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed: a **homeopathic** combination (10 organic-certified constituents: Aconitum napellus 12X, Allium cepa 6X, Bryonia alba 9X, Chamomilla 6X, Echinacea angustifolia 6X, Euphrasia officinalis 6X, Gelsemium sempervirens 12X, Ipecacuanha 12X, Pulsatilla 9X, Sambucus nigra 6X), chewable tablets, ages 4-11, marketed "Certified Organic & Non-GMO," "Physician-Formulated," and "Homeopathic Remedy Made Clean." **Genuinely notable brand-family contrast worth flagging**: `drugs/Genexa.md` (Children's Analgesics, 2% share) is confirmed to be a real, evidence-based acetaminophen product (identical active ingredient/dose to Children's Tylenol) marketed with the same "Made Clean"/organic inactive-ingredient positioning — meaning the SAME brand's "clean" marketing language is applied identically to a genuine FDA-monograph drug (acetaminophen) on one product and a homeopathic remedy with no established pharmacologic mechanism on another. A caregiver could reasonably (but incorrectly) assume consistent evidentiary rigor across the Genexa product line based on shared branding language ("Physician-Formulated," "Made Clean") alone.

## Pros
- No conventional drug active ingredient (no dextromethorphan, antihistamine, or decongestant), avoiding those specific side-effect profiles
- Organic-certified inactive-ingredient sourcing may appeal to caregivers with specific dietary/allergen preferences, independent of the product's therapeutic claims
- No serious adverse events reported for general homeopathic cold/cough products per the Cochrane review on [[homeopathic-cold-products]]

## Cons
- **"Physician-Formulated" and "Made Clean" marketing language is applied to a homeopathic product with no established pharmacologic mechanism, using the same brand-trust language Genexa also applies to its genuinely evidence-based acetaminophen product** — this is a real, worth-flagging marketing-consistency concern: the phrase implies a level of clinical rigor that does not actually differ between Genexa's homeopathic and conventional-drug lines, when the underlying evidence bases for the two products are entirely different
- Per [[homeopathic-cold-products]]'s Cochrane-level review: no convincing evidence of benefit beyond placebo for general homeopathic cold/cough products at these dilutions; better-designed trials found nothing while weaker trials found an effect
- No product-specific RCT for this exact 10-constituent combination was located
- 10 combined remedies makes it especially hard to attribute any observed effect to a specific ingredient even under a homeopathic evidence framework, consistent with the same critique already applied to Boiron ColdCalm's 9-remedy adult formulation

## Use Cases
Marketed for multi-symptom cough, cold, sore throat, and congestion relief in children ages 4-11, via homeopathic mechanism.

## Allergies / Warnings
Not for children under 4. Standard homeopathic-product FDA disclaimer applies ("FDA is not aware of scientific evidence to support homeopathy as effective," per the product's own labeling).

## Symptoms Treated
Cough, cold, sore throat, congestion (unproven beyond placebo per current evidence).

## Brand-Specific Alignment Check
