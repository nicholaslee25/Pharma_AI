## Overview
Pedialyte (Abbott Nutrition) is category-leading in this database's Hydration Support listing (44% share) and is confirmed to be formulated to the **WHO oral rehydration solution (ORS) standard** — sodium chloride, trisodium citrate, potassium chloride, and glucose in a specific ratio (~1,035mg sodium/L, ~780mg potassium/L), independently confirmed clinically effective and similar in outcome to WHO-recommended reference solutions for infants with mild-to-moderate dehydration. This is a real, long-established, guideline-level medical intervention, not a marketing claim — Pedialyte-type ORS is standard of care for pediatric gastroenteritis-related dehydration. See the new [[oral-rehydration-electrolytes]] ingredient page built specifically for this category, which documents the real mechanistic reason (sodium-glucose cotransport ratio) this formulation category outperforms standard sports drinks for actual rehydration efficiency, not just fluid/electrolyte intake. Hydration Support (44%) is covered by [[oral-rehydration-electrolytes]].

## Pros
- **Genuinely strong, guideline-level clinical evidence**: WHO-ORS-standard formulation with independently confirmed clinical effectiveness for pediatric mild-to-moderate dehydration — a real medical intervention, not merely a "better" sports drink
- Sodium content (~1,035mg/L) substantially higher than standard sports drinks — directly matched to the sodium-glucose cotransport ratio shown to maximize actual fluid absorption, per [[oral-rehydration-electrolytes]]
- Long-established manufacturer (Abbott) with real pediatric-medicine credibility

## Cons
- Not indicated or evidenced for severe dehydration, which requires IV fluids and medical care — a real, important scope limitation
- No Pedialyte-specific RCT beyond the general WHO-ORS-class evidence was independently distinguished from the broader ORS-class literature in this pass
- Higher sodium content, while therapeutically appropriate for actual dehydration, is a real consideration for anyone on a sodium-restricted diet (e.g., some cardiac/renal patients) who should consult a doctor before routine use

## Use Cases
Prevention/treatment of mild-to-moderate dehydration from diarrhea, vomiting, or gastroenteritis, especially in infants/children — the population with the strongest clinical evidence per [[oral-rehydration-electrolytes]].

## Allergies / Warnings
Not a substitute for medical care in severe dehydration. Check specific flavor/formulation for dye or artificial-sweetener content if relevant to individual sensitivities. See [[oral-rehydration-electrolytes]] for general class-level information.

## Symptoms Treated
Mild-to-moderate dehydration from diarrhea, vomiting, or exertion — see [[oral-rehydration-electrolytes]] for the evidence base.
