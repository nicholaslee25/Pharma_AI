## Appears In
Iron Supplements - 7%

## Overview
Vitron-C is confirmed to combine iron with vitamin C (as ascorbic acid/sodium ascorbate, ~125mg) specifically to enhance absorption — the same absorption-enhancement mechanism [[iron]]'s own evidence page identifies (vitamin C as a real, documented nonheme-iron-absorption enhancer). Sourcing found some inconsistency in the specific iron salt across product listings — most consistently described as **ferrous fumarate** (65mg elemental iron per tablet), though at least one source described a carbonyl-iron variant, suggesting (similar to [[Feosol]]) this brand name may span more than one iron-salt formulation across its product line; this pass could not fully resolve which specific salt is in the exact SKU tracked in this database's 7%-share listing. Manufacturer/label guidance specifies taking on an empty stomach (1 hour before or 2 hours after meals) and avoiding antacids, dairy, tea, or coffee within 2 hours, since these reduce absorption — real, specific, actionable dosing guidance. Iron Supplements (7%) is covered by [[iron]], which directly names Vitron-C as an iron-plus-vitamin-C combination product.

## Pros
- Vitamin C co-formulation has a real, documented absorption-enhancing mechanism for nonheme iron, per [[iron]]'s own evidence page
- Specific, actionable dosing guidance provided (timing relative to food, avoidance of antacids/dairy/caffeine) — more detailed practical guidance than found for several competitors in this pass
- Directly named in this database's own [[iron]] evidence page as a confirmed combination-product example

## Cons
- **Some inconsistency found across sourcing regarding the exact iron salt used** (ferrous fumarate vs. carbonyl iron reported in different listings) — this pass could not fully resolve which specific salt is in the tracked SKU, a real formulation-clarity gap similar to the one confirmed for [[Feosol]]
- Empty-stomach dosing requirement (1 hour before/2 hours after meals) may itself worsen the GI-tolerability issues common to iron supplements generally, since food can buffer GI irritation — a real practical tradeoff between maximizing absorption and tolerability
- No Vitron-C-specific clinical trial was located testing the combination product as a whole
- No independent third-party quality certification (USP Verified, NSF) was located for Vitron-C in this pass

## Use Cases
Iron-deficiency anemia treatment/prevention with enhanced absorption via co-formulated vitamin C — see [[iron]] for the evidence base.

## Allergies / Warnings
**Real pediatric poisoning risk from iron overdose** — keep out of reach of children. Avoid antacids, dairy, tea, or coffee within 2 hours of dosing (reduces absorption per label guidance). See [[iron]] for drug interactions.

## Symptoms Treated
Iron-deficiency anemia — see [[iron]] for the evidence base.
