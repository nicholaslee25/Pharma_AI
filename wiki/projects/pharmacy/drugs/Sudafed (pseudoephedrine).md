## Appears In
Decongestants, Oral - 45%

## Overview
Already fully confirmed on [[pseudoephedrine]] — that page's dosing table and sourcing are built directly from Sudafed's own FDA OTC label: **pseudoephedrine HCl 30mg (immediate-release) or 120mg/240mg (12hr/24hr extended-release)**. This upgrades the earlier bare "name-confirmed" note to a full confirmation with dosing detail. The brand spans multiple duration formats (immediate, 12hr, 24hr) plus a pediatric liquid (15mg/5mL).

## Pros
- By far the largest recommendation share (45%) in its category — more than 3x the second-place product (Claritin-D at 14%)
- [[pseudoephedrine]]'s evidence grade is "moderate-to-high for short-term objective and subjective nasal decongestion" — genuinely better-supported than oral phenylephrine's "low/negative" grade, a real, evidence-backed reason for this brand's dominant market position beyond just brand recognition
- Multiple duration options (4-6h, 12h, 24h) let users match dosing frequency to their preference

## Cons
- Behind-the-counter purchase requirement (ID-logged, quantity-limited under the Combat Methamphetamine Epidemic Act) due to its use as a precursor in illicit methamphetamine synthesis — a real access friction not shared by phenylephrine-based competitors, and part of why some pharmacies stock more phenylephrine products despite the weaker evidence
- Sympathomimetic cardiovascular stimulant effects (elevated BP/heart rate) — ask a doctor before use with high blood pressure, heart disease, thyroid disease, or diabetes
- Not for children <6y

## Use Cases
Temporary relief of nasal and sinus congestion/pressure from the common cold, hay fever, or other upper respiratory allergies.

## Allergies / Warnings
Ask a doctor before use with high blood pressure, heart disease, thyroid disease, diabetes, or trouble urinating due to enlarged prostate. Do not use with MAOIs. Children <6y: do not use.

## Symptoms Treated
Nasal congestion, sinus congestion/pressure.
