## Overview
**Real, significant, and repeated marketing-claim litigation history found here.** One A Day (Bayer) has faced at least three separate real legal/regulatory challenges over specific health claims: (1) a $3.3 million settlement (2010, with California/Illinois/Oregon attorneys general) over One A Day Men's claims that selenium reduces prostate cancer risk — a claim directly undercut by the largest-ever prostate-cancer-prevention trial (SELECT), which found selenium no more effective than placebo; (2) a 2014 class-action lawsuit alleging One A Day's heart-health/immunity/physical-energy claims lacked scientific support, citing that randomized clinical trials show multivitamins do not affect illness number, severity, or duration — Bayer was unsuccessful in getting this dismissed; and (3) a separate, ongoing false-advertising challenge over "natural" labeling given the product's synthetic ingredient content, which a federal judge allowed to proceed. This is a real, repeated pattern of specific health-benefit claims being legally challenged as unsupported — directly relevant to this database's general [[multivitamins]] evidence page, which itself documents that multivitamins have NOT been shown (in the large COSMOS RCT) to reduce cancer, cardiovascular disease, or mortality risk. Multivitamins (27% share, second-largest) is covered by [[multivitamins]].
