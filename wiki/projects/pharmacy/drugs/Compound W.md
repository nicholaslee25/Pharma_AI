## Appears In
Wart Removers - 68%

## Overview
Already confirmed via DailyMed during this database's dedicated [[salicylic-acid]] ingredient-page research: **salicylic acid 17% w/w liquid**, the category leader by a wide margin (68% share — more than double the next brand). Directions: wash affected area (optional 5-minute warm-water soak), dry thoroughly, apply one drop at a time to cover each wart, allow to dry, repeat once or twice daily as needed for up to 12 weeks.

Salicylic acid for wart removal has the strongest evidence base of any product in this "Wart Removers" category: a Cochrane review of 85 trials/8,815 participants (Kwok et al. 2012) found salicylic acid vs. placebo RR 1.56 (95% CI 1.20-2.03), pooled cure rate 73% (SA) vs. 48% (control), with a head-to-head comparison against cryotherapy (the mechanism behind [[Dr. Scholl's Freeze Away]]) showing no significant difference between the two approaches (RR 1.23, 95% CI 0.88-1.71) — see [[salicylic-acid]] for the full evidence breakdown, including the real site-dependent effectiveness finding (hands respond much better than feet) and the genuine systemic salicylate toxicity risk from excessive/repeated application.

No Compound-W-brand-specific clinical trial beyond the ingredient-level Cochrane evidence was located in this pass.

## Pros
- Confirmed, single, well-established active ingredient (salicylic acid 17%) with the largest and highest-quality evidence base (Cochrane, 85 trials) of any brand in this category
- Head-to-head evidence shows salicylic acid performs comparably to cryotherapy overall, with genuine site-specific advantages for hand warts (RR 2.67 vs. cryotherapy's less dramatic effect)
- Self-administered, low-cost, widely available — no special equipment or device required, unlike the cryogenic-device mechanism of [[Dr. Scholl's Freeze Away]]
- Category-leading 68% share reflects both long-standing brand recognition and a genuinely well-evidenced active ingredient

## Cons
- Explicit label contraindications for diabetes and circulatory problems — both conditions impair wound healing and raise infection/ulceration risk from a keratolytic (tissue-destroying) product, per [[salicylic-acid]]
- Real systemic salicylate toxicity risk with excessive/repeated application, especially over large areas or in children — same toxic mechanism class as aspirin overdose (see [[salicylic-acid]] Key Safety Data)
- Site-dependent effectiveness: meaningfully less effective for plantar (foot) warts (RR 1.29) than hand warts (RR 2.67) per the Cochrane data — a real efficacy gap for foot-wart users specifically, a population overlapping with [[Dr. Scholl's Freeze Away]]'s marketing
- Slower course of treatment (up to 12 weeks of repeated daily application) than a single or few cryotherapy sessions

## Use Cases
Removal of common and plantar warts via keratolytic (skin-softening/dissolving) action, applied once or twice daily for up to 12 weeks.

## Allergies / Warnings
Per label: do not use on irritated, infected, or reddened skin; on moles, birthmarks, hairy warts, genital warts, or facial warts; or if you have diabetes or circulatory problems. Avoid eye contact (flush 15 minutes if exposed); do not inhale vapors. See [[salicylic-acid]] for full systemic-toxicity data.

## Symptoms Treated
Common warts and plantar (foot) warts.
