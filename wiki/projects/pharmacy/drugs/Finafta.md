## Appears In
Oral Pain Relievers, Adult - 3%

## Overview
**Discrepancy flagged — inconsistent formulation across DailyMed-listed SKUs:** Finafta (Efficient Laboratories Inc.) appears under at least three distinct DailyMed listings with different formulations: (1) "FINAFTA - benzocaine, glycerin liquid" (benzocaine + glycerin base, no alcohol/salicylic acid mentioned); (2) "FINAFTA - ethyl alcohol, salicylic acid, benzocaine liquid" — benzocaine 5% w/v + ethyl alcohol (USP) 60% v/v + salicylic acid (USP) 1% w/v; and (3) "FINAFTA MULTIORAL - benzocaine spray," a separate spray delivery format. The inclusion of salicylic acid in one SKU is notable and not something this database has documented for any other benzocaine-based oral-pain product — salicylic acid's role here (likely a mild keratolytic/preservative function rather than the primary analgesic action) was not independently confirmed in this pass. All confirmed SKUs share benzocaine as the core analgesic, but the benzocaine concentration itself varies (5% w/v in the alcohol/salicylic-acid SKU vs. unspecified in the glycerin SKU) — notably LOWER than the 20% benzocaine concentration confirmed for Orajel, Anbesol, Kank-A, DenTek Instant Pain Relief, and Red Cross Oral Pain Relief in this same product category.

## Pros
- Confirmed benzocaine-based active ingredient across all identified SKUs, consistent with [[topical-anesthetics]]'s established local-anesthetic evidence
- The lower 5% benzocaine concentration (vs. the category-standard 20% used by most competitors) could plausibly carry a somewhat lower methemoglobinemia risk per dose, though this was not independently confirmed via a dose-response citation in this pass — flagged as a plausible inference, not a verified finding

## Cons
- **Real, unresolved formulation inconsistency across the brand's own SKUs** — a consumer cannot assume "Finafta" means one fixed benzocaine concentration or one fixed set of additional ingredients (alcohol, salicylic acid, or neither) without checking the specific product
- Still carries benzocaine's disproportionate methemoglobinemia risk documented on [[topical-anesthetics]], regardless of the specific concentration — the Guay 2009 review's key point ("a single spray... may induce methemoglobinemia... even if you have used this product before") does not establish a safe threshold below which this risk disappears
- The 60% v/v ethyl alcohol content in one SKU is notably high and could cause additional local irritation/stinging on already-irritated oral tissue, a real tradeoff not present in glycerin-based or alcohol-free competitors
- No independent brand-specific clinical trial evidence was identified for Finafta specifically in this pass

## Use Cases
Temporary relief of oral pain from canker sores, mouth and gum irritation, via benzocaine's local anesthetic action, marketed under Finafta's specific SKU variants.

## Allergies / Warnings
Per label conventions matching benzocaine's category-wide warnings (per [[topical-anesthetics]]): methemoglobinemia risk; avoid if allergic to local anesthetics ("caine" family); do not use for more than 7 days without dentist/doctor direction. The high-alcohol-content SKU specifically warns of possible additional local irritation.

## Symptoms Treated
Canker sores, mouth and gum irritation.

Flagged here and on [[topical-anesthetics]].
