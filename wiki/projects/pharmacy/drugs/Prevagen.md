## Overview
**Real, extremely significant discrepancy — this is among the most serious findings in this entire database.** Prevagen (active ingredient apoaequorin, a jellyfish-derived calcium-binding protein) is the category-dominant brand in this database's Brain Health listing (50% share) and was the subject of a 2024 federal jury trial (FTC and New York Attorney General v. Quincy Bioscience) that found the company's memory-improvement claims were **not supported by science and were misleading**. The core scientific problem, per [[brain-health-nootropics]]: apoaequorin is very likely digested by stomach pepsin into amino acids before it could be absorbed intact, and even if it survived digestion, its molecular size and poor fat solubility would prevent it from crossing the blood-brain barrier — no study has shown orally-administered apoaequorin reaches the human brain. The company's own "Madison Memory Study" **failed its pre-specified primary endpoint**; the only positive results the company has ever cited came from more than 30 post-hoc subgroup analyses, a statistical approach widely regarded as unreliable for establishing efficacy due to multiple-comparisons risk. This is a real, adjudicated (jury-decided, not merely alleged) finding against the category's largest brand.
