## Appears In
Insect Bite and Sting Management - 23%

## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed: "After Bite" is the widest-spanning brand-name/formulation split identified in this database so far — **at least five distinct active-ingredient classes** across its SKU lineup: **sodium bicarbonate** (After Bite Advanced, After Bite Kids, After Bite X Tech), **diphenhydramine HCl 2%** (After Bite Xtra — see [[topical-diphenhydramine]]), **ethyl alcohol + lidocaine HCl** (After Bite Wipe — see [[topical-anesthetics]]), **benzocaine + alcohol** (After Bite Wipe Insect Sting Relief — see [[topical-anesthetics]]), and reportedly **ammonium hydroxide ~3.5%** in the classic original pen/roll-on formula (per secondary sources — a DailyMed label for this specific classic SKU was not located/confirmed directly in this pass, flagged as unverified). A separate "After Bite Sensitive" SKU (baking soda/aloe/tea tree oil) could not be confirmed as a DailyMed-listed drug at all in this pass — it may be a cosmetic product rather than an OTC drug. This means the 23% Insect Bite and Sting Management share is not attributable to one formula — it is spread across chemically unrelated mechanisms (an antihistamine, two different local anesthetics with materially different methemoglobinemia risk per [[topical-anesthetics]], an alkalinizing/neutralizing agent, and possibly a cosmetic).

## Pros
- FDA Drug Facts labels confirmed for most SKUs (Xtra, Advanced, Wipe, Wipe Insect Sting Relief, Kids)
- Offers multiple different mechanisms of action under one trusted brand name, which may suit different user preferences/allergy profiles (e.g., a lidocaine option for someone benzocaine-sensitive, or vice versa)
- Long-standing, widely recognized brand specifically for insect bites/stings (second-largest share in this category at 23%)

## Cons
- **Discrepancy flagged:** "After Bite" spans at least five materially different active-ingredient formulations under one brand name — a consumer cannot assume any given "After Bite" product contains a particular ingredient without checking the specific SKU. This is a wider spread than any other brand identified in this database to date (compare [[Dermoplast]] and [[Bactine MAX]], each spanning two formulations).
- The diphenhydramine-based Xtra SKU carries the systemic-absorption toxicity risk documented on [[topical-diphenhydramine]] (including a pediatric fatality case report) if applied to large areas, broken skin, or combined with other diphenhydramine products
- The benzocaine-based Wipe Insect Sting Relief SKU carries the disproportionate methemoglobinemia risk documented on [[topical-anesthetics]], relative to the lidocaine-based Wipe SKU
- The classic ammonium-hydroxide "original" formula's exact label/warnings were not independently confirmed against a DailyMed listing in this pass — sourced from secondary references only
- "After Bite Sensitive" could not be confirmed as an actual FDA-regulated drug product in this pass — possibly a cosmetic marketed alongside the drug SKUs under the same brand umbrella, a distinction worth being aware of
- No brand-specific clinical trial for any After Bite SKU was identified beyond the general ingredient-level evidence

## Use Cases
Temporary relief of itching/pain from insect bites and stings (all SKUs); the Wipe SKUs additionally offer first-aid antiseptic action (alcohol) alongside their anesthetic.

## Allergies / Warnings
Varies by SKU — see [[topical-diphenhydramine]] (Xtra: do not use on large areas or with other diphenhydramine products; ask a doctor before use on chicken pox/measles) and [[topical-anesthetics]] (Wipe/Wipe Insect Sting Relief: do not use if allergic to "-caine" anesthetics; benzocaine SKU carries methemoglobinemia risk). External use only across all SKUs; avoid eyes; discontinue and consult a doctor if condition worsens or persists beyond 7 days.

## Symptoms Treated
Itching and pain from insect bites and stings; Advanced/Kids/X Tech SKUs also cover poison ivy/oak/sumac itching.

## Brand-Specific Alignment Check
**Discrepancy flagged on this page, [[topical-diphenhydramine]], and [[topical-anesthetics]]:** After Bite is the most formulation-fragmented single brand name identified in this database — five+ distinct active-ingredient classes, two of which (benzocaine vs. lidocaine) carry materially different safety profiles per [[topical-anesthetics]], and one of which (diphenhydramine, Xtra SKU) carries a serious, case-report-documented systemic toxicity risk not shared by the others. Never silently assume a specific "After Bite" product's ingredient without checking its exact SKU name.
