## Appears In
Hemorrhoidal Preparations - 73%

## Overview
Confirmed via multiple DailyMed labels (labeler: Haleon US Holdings LLC): Preparation H, by far the category leader (73% share), spans **three genuinely different active-ingredient mechanisms** across its product line — a wide "one brand, multiple mechanisms" spread consistent with several other high-share brands in this database:

- **Phenylephrine HCl 0.25% (vasoconstrictor)** — the most common active, present in the standard Ointment, Suppository, and Cocoa Butter kit formulations. See [[rectal-phenylephrine]] (new ingredient page built this pass) for the mechanism and evidence base — notably, **no prospective RCT was located showing this class reduces hemorrhoidal bleeding or prolapse**, only symptom (swelling/itching) relief.
- **Pramoxine HCl (topical anesthetic)** — added in the Maximum Strength Cream (alongside phenylephrine and protectants), resolving [[topical-anesthetics]]'s previously-flagged pramoxine coverage gap with a second confirmed brand example.
- **Hydrocortisone 1% (corticosteroid)** — in the Soothing Relief Anti-Itch / Hydrocortisone line, an entirely different mechanism (anti-inflammatory, not vasoconstrictive or anesthetic) — see [[hydrocortisone]].

A consumer picking "Preparation H" off a shelf could get a vasoconstrictor, an anesthetic, a corticosteroid, or a combination, entirely depending on which specific SKU they select — the brand name alone does not indicate the mechanism. No Preparation-H-brand-specific clinical trial was located for any SKU in this pass; the phenylephrine and hydrocortisone lines' evidence sits at the [[rectal-phenylephrine]] and [[hydrocortisone]] ingredient level respectively, both with real, explicitly-noted limitations (no RCT for bleeding/prolapse outcomes for phenylephrine; hydrocortisone's evidence base is for general dermatologic anti-inflammatory use, not hemorrhoid-specific trials).

## Pros
- Confirmed FDA Drug Facts labels across every identified SKU — genuine regulatory transparency, not merely cosmetic marketing
- Offering three distinct mechanisms under one trusted brand name gives consumers/pharmacists a same-shelf option to switch approaches (vasoconstrictor vs. anesthetic vs. corticosteroid) depending on symptom profile, without switching brands
- Category-dominant recommendation share (73%) reflects decades of brand trust and availability across formats (ointment, cream, suppository, pads)
- Protectant-only cocoa butter/petrolatum base ingredients in several SKUs are well-tolerated per [[petrolatum]]'s general safety profile

## Cons
- **Three distinct, non-interchangeable active-ingredient families sold under one brand name** with no single unifying "Preparation H = X ingredient" identity — a consumer or caregiver must read the specific product label, not just the brand name, to know what mechanism they are using, and whether it is appropriate given contraindications (e.g., phenylephrine's cardiovascular/thyroid/diabetes/BPH cautions per [[rectal-phenylephrine]] do not apply to the hydrocortisone or pramoxine lines, and vice versa)
- **No prospective RCT evidence exists showing the phenylephrine-based majority of this line's SKUs reduce hemorrhoidal bleeding or prolapse** — the dominant mechanism in this database's largest-share hemorrhoid brand treats swelling/itching symptoms only, not the underlying vascular pathology, per [[rectal-phenylephrine]]
- No Preparation-H-brand-specific clinical trial was located for any SKU; evidence is entirely at the ingredient level
- Combining SKUs (e.g., a phenylephrine ointment plus a hydrocortisone cream, assuming "more Preparation H helps") would combine two independently active mechanisms without established combination safety data specific to this brand
- Standard OTC hemorrhoid-product contraindications apply and are easy to overlook given the brand's over-the-counter, low-key packaging: not for rectal bleeding, prolapse, or use beyond 7 days without a doctor

## Use Cases
Temporary relief of itching, swelling, and discomfort from hemorrhoids via one of three mechanisms depending on SKU: local vasoconstriction (phenylephrine, most products), local anesthesia (pramoxine, Maximum Strength Cream), or anti-inflammatory corticosteroid action (hydrocortisone, Soothing Relief/Hydrocortisone line).

## Allergies / Warnings
Universal across SKUs: external use only (most formats); discontinue and consult a doctor if rectal bleeding occurs, symptoms worsen, or no improvement within 7 days; not for use in children under 12 without doctor consultation. Phenylephrine-containing SKUs specifically: per [[rectal-phenylephrine]], caution/avoid in heart disease, hypertension, thyroid disease, diabetes, or urination difficulty from enlarged prostate, and in those on prescription hypertension/depression medications. Hydrocortisone-containing SKUs: do not begin use of any other hydrocortisone product without consulting a doctor first (an important cross-brand interaction warning, since hydrocortisone appears in many other OTC products in this database); not for diaper rash.

## Symptoms Treated
Itching, swelling, burning, and discomfort associated with hemorrhoids — mechanism (vasoconstriction, anesthesia, or anti-inflammatory) depending on the specific SKU chosen.
