## Overview
"Soothe" (Bausch + Lomb) is a multi-formulation line with genuinely different active ingredients per sub-product, confirmed via FDA labels (DailyMed): **Soothe XP / XP Preservative Free**: light mineral oil 1.0% + mineral oil 4.5% (an oil-based emulsion, lipid-layer-targeted formulation — mechanistically distinct from the water-based CMC/PEG/hypromellose products covered elsewhere in [[artificial-tears]]); **Soothe Maximum Hydration**: povidone 2.0%; **Soothe (standard/preservative-free liquid)**: glycerin 0.6% + propylene glycol 0.6% (combination lubricant). The oil-emulsion (XP) formulation specifically targets evaporative dry eye by supplementing the tear film's lipid layer, a different mechanism from the aqueous-deficient-DED-focused CMC/PEG/hypromellose products — [[artificial-tears]] notes evaporative DED "specifically benefits from liposome-containing formulations" per its cited Semp et al. 2023 review, though mineral-oil emulsion is a related but not identical lipid-supplementation approach to liposomal formulations, a distinction worth preserving rather than treating as equivalent. No Soothe-brand-specific outcome trial beyond general mineral-oil/povidone/glycerin ingredient evidence was identified.

## Pros
- Soothe XP's oil-emulsion (lipid-layer) mechanism offers a genuinely different formulation approach from most other brands in this category, potentially relevant for evaporative-type dry eye specifically
- Product-line breadth (oil-based XP, povidone-based Maximum Hydration, glycerin/propylene-glycol standard) lets users match formulation type to their specific DED subtype, at least in principle
- Bausch + Lomb has an established ophthalmic manufacturing track record across several brands in this database

## Cons
- The oil-emulsion mechanism, while mechanistically plausible for evaporative DED, is not the same as the liposome-specific formulations that [[artificial-tears]]'s cited review actually studied — a meaningful formulation-category distinction that shouldn't be blurred even though both target the tear film's lipid layer
- Smallest recommendation share (2%) among the 14 tracked brands in this category
- No independent brand-specific outcome trial isolates any Soothe sub-formulation's real-world performance from its general ingredient-class evidence

## Use Cases
Soothe XP: evaporative/lipid-layer-deficient dry eye. Maximum Hydration and standard formulas: general dry-eye burning/irritation relief via povidone or glycerin/propylene-glycol lubrication respectively.

## Allergies / Warnings
Per label: for external use only; discontinue and see a doctor for eye pain, vision changes, continued redness/irritation, or symptoms persisting beyond 72 hours. Oil-based formulations (XP) may transiently blur vision more than aqueous drops, a formulation-inherent effect rather than a defect.

## Symptoms Treated
Dry eye burning, irritation, and discomfort, with the XP sub-line specifically addressing evaporative/lipid-layer-related dryness.

## Brand-Specific Alignment Check
Mostly consistent with [[artificial-tears]], with one nuance worth flagging: the ingredient page's evaporative-DED discussion specifically cites liposome-containing formulations, while Soothe XP uses a mineral-oil emulsion — a related but distinct lipid-supplementation approach not identical to what that review tested. Not a contradiction, but a formulation-category distinction worth preserving rather than treating Soothe XP as direct evidence-backed by that specific liposome finding.
