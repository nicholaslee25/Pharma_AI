## Overview
**Sigvaris** (7% share, smaller than [[Futuro]]/[[Dr. Scholl's]]/[[ACE]] but positioned differently) is a specialist medical-compression manufacturer offering the full European compression-class range (Class 1: 18–21 mmHg through Class 3: 30–35 mmHg and higher), rather than the informal US retail "mild/moderate/firm" tiers used by the more mass-market brands in this category. No FDA drug label exists (device, not drug), same Class II gradient-stocking framework (21 CFR 880.5780) as the rest of [[support-hosiery]]. Sigvaris's own published materials explain compression-level selection by indication, consistent with the graded consensus-guideline recommendations (Rabe/Partsch 2017, PMID 28549402) summarized on [[support-hosiery]] — higher, physician-directed compression classes for venous ulcer/lymphedema care, lower classes for general comfort. No brand-specific clinical trial for Sigvaris products specifically was found; the applicable evidence is the class-level consensus statement and Cochrane review on [[support-hosiery]].

## Pros
- Positioned toward the higher, physician-directed compression classes where the strongest evidence exists (Grade 1A for venous ulcer healing/recurrence and lymphedema maintenance, per [[support-hosiery]]) — a better evidence-alignment than brands marketed primarily for general leg comfort
- Full European compression-class range gives clinicians a wider, more granular prescribing option than single-tier consumer products
- Established specialist reputation in the medical/phlebology compression space, consistent with being cited as reference material in this category's own patient-education content

## Cons
- Smallest recommendation share among the brands with more than negligible presence in this category (7%, ahead only of [[Truform]]'s 3%) — likely reflects a smaller retail/OTC footprint relative to mass-market brands, not necessarily a quality signal
- Higher compression classes generally require proper fitting/measurement and sometimes physician guidance to select correctly, a real access/convenience trade-off against the ease of an OTC sock
- No brand-specific trial exists; efficacy claims rest entirely on the class-level evidence (which itself shows a real gap between strong consensus-guideline support for ulcer/lymphedema indications and weaker RCT support for general varicose-vein comfort — see [[support-hosiery]])

## Use Cases
Physician-directed or self-selected graduated compression across a wide mmHg range, from general leg-comfort tiers through higher medical-grade compression for diagnosed venous insufficiency, venous ulcer management, and lymphedema maintenance.

## Allergies / Warnings
Not a drug — no allergy/ingredient profile. Standard compression-garment cautions apply; higher compression classes in particular should not be self-selected without proper measurement/fitting, and are contraindicated in significant peripheral arterial disease — consult a physician before using higher-class compression hosiery.

## Symptoms Treated
