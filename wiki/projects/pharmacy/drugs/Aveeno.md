## Overview
Aveeno (Kenvue/J&J) is a large multi-category personal-care brand. Confirmed via DailyMed across its six category appearances, the brand spans **at least five distinct active-ingredient mechanisms**, plus non-drug cosmetic SKUs — a mechanism diversity comparable to [[CeraVe]] and [[Neutrogena]] elsewhere in this pass:

- **Acne Products (4%):** "Clear Complexion" line — **salicylic acid** across moisturizer, cleanser, foaming cleanser, and cleansing-pad SKUs.
- **Foot Care Products (5%):** no Aveeno-specific foot product was independently located on DailyMed in this pass; the most plausible candidate is the brand's general **Daily Moisturizing Lotion (dimethicone skin protectant)** applied to feet, but this is inferred rather than confirmed against a foot-specific label — flagged as a genuine gap rather than asserted as fact.
- **Sunscreen (6%):** Aveeno's sunscreen lines (Baby/Kids/Positively Mineral) are confirmed **zinc-oxide-only mineral filters** (21.6% concentration on the SKUs checked) — notably, unlike [[Neutrogena]]'s sunscreen line, no chemical-filter (avobenzone/octinoxate-type) Aveeno sunscreen was located in this pass, suggesting Aveeno's sunscreen positioning may skew consistently mineral/"sensitive skin," though this was not exhaustively verified across the full SKU range.
- **Therapeutic Skin Care, Cleansers (6%) and Moisturizers (8%):** split between confirmed **dimethicone skin protectant** SKUs (Daily Moisturizing Lotion) and non-drug cosmetic SKUs with no Drug Facts panel (Daily Moisturizing Body Wash) — the same cosmetic/drug split pattern flagged on [[Neutrogena]] and [[CeraVe]].
- **Topical Poison Ivy/Oak Remedies (6%):** Anti-Itch Concentrated Lotion, confirmed **calamine (skin protectant) + pramoxine HCl (external analgesic)** — a genuinely different, two-active combination not seen elsewhere in this brand's other categories.

Separately, **Aveeno Eczema Therapy** (16% of Eczema Care/Relief Products) is tracked as its own brand page — see [[Aveeno Eczema Therapy]] — using colloidal oatmeal, distinct from all six mechanisms documented here.

**Net finding:** across its six tracked categories (plus the separately-paged Eczema Therapy line), Aveeno spans salicylic acid, zinc oxide, dimethicone, calamine + pramoxine HCl, colloidal oatmeal, and multiple non-drug cosmetic SKUs — at least five distinct drug mechanisms under one "oat-based, dermatologist-recommended" brand identity.

## Pros
- Confirmed salicylic-acid, zinc-oxide, dimethicone, and calamine/pramoxine concentrations all fall within their respective FDA-monograph OTC ranges
- Mineral-only (zinc oxide) sunscreen line is a real, consistent option for chemical-filter-sensitive users, in contrast to brands that mix chemical and mineral filters under one name (e.g. [[Neutrogena]])
- Anti-Itch Concentrated Lotion's calamine + pramoxine HCl combination gives both a drying/protectant action AND a topical anesthetic — a genuinely rational two-mechanism pairing for weepy poison-ivy rashes
- Colloidal oatmeal is a real FDA OTC skin-protectant monograph ingredient (see [[Aveeno Eczema Therapy]]), giving the brand's flagship "oat" identity actual regulatory grounding in at least one of its product lines

## Cons
- **Six-plus distinct mechanisms under one brand name** — a consumer cannot assume ingredient consistency across "Aveeno" products, the same pattern already flagged on [[CeraVe]] and [[Neutrogena]]
- **Foot Care Products (5%) active ingredient not independently confirmed** — this pass could not locate an Aveeno-specific foot-care drug label; treated as a genuine research gap, not resolved by inference from the general moisturizer line
- No dedicated ingredient page exists in this database for calamine or pramoxine HCl specifically (pramoxine appears in [[topical-anesthetics]] context elsewhere, but a direct cross-reference was not confirmed this pass)
- No Aveeno-brand-specific RCT beyond general ingredient-level evidence was located for any of the six categories in this pass
