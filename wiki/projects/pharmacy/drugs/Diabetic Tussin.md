## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed: Diabetic Tussin is a multi-SKU line, not one formulation. Core **Diabetic Tussin DM** = dextromethorphan HBr 10mg + guaifenesin 100mg per 5mL (touches both [[dextromethorphan]] and [[guaifenesin]]). Other line SKUs found on DailyMed: DM Maximum Strength (higher-dose dextromethorphan/guaifenesin), Expectorant (guaifenesin 100mg/5mL alone), Sore Throat and Cough Cherry lozenges (benzocaine + dextromethorphan — see [[topical-anesthetics]] for the benzocaine methemoglobinemia risk, though this is an oral lozenge use), Sore Throat Cherry spray (phenol, not otherwise covered by an ingredient page in this database), and Nighttime Cold and Flu (acetaminophen + dextromethorphan + diphenhydramine — diphenhydramine has no dedicated ingredient page yet per the known gap list). Same brand-architecture pattern as [[Robitussin]]/[[Safetussin]]: the "Diabetic Tussin" name does not map to one ingredient, and its 63% dominance in the Diabetic Cough Products category (the largest single-brand share we've recorded for this category) is driven by being sugar/alcohol-free, not a distinct active ingredient. ## Pros
- Sugar- and alcohol-free formulation, explicitly marketed as diabetic-friendly — relevant for patients avoiding sugar-based syrups
- Core DM formulation pairs a cough suppressant with an expectorant, addressing both dry cough and mucus in one product
- Line includes a guaifenesin-only Expectorant SKU for patients who specifically want to avoid dextromethorphan
- Widest recommendation share (63%) of any brand in this category, well ahead of Robitussin (28%) and Safetussin (9%)

## Cons
- Same brand-spans-many-formulations risk as Robitussin/Safetussin: a consumer cannot assume a specific ingredient profile from the "Diabetic Tussin" name alone without reading the specific SKU box
- Its "diabetic" positioning is a sugar/excipient claim, not a pharmacological one — the active ingredients (dextromethorphan, guaifenesin) have no diabetes-specific mechanism or evidence advantage
- Dextromethorphan MAOI interaction risk applies identically to any other DM-containing product
- Guaifenesin's own evidence page notes a negative objective RCT result (no significant effect on mucociliary/cough clearance vs. placebo) — the same caveat applies here as to any guaifenesin product

## Use Cases
Cough suppression and chest congestion/mucus relief in patients (particularly diabetics) seeking a sugar-free, alcohol-free cough product; sore throat relief (lozenge/spray SKUs); nighttime cold/flu symptom relief (Nighttime SKU).

## Allergies / Warnings
Do not use with MAOIs or within 2 weeks of stopping one. Ask a doctor before use if cough is accompanied by excessive phlegm, or is chronic (smoking, asthma, chronic bronchitis, emphysema). Stop and ask a doctor if cough lasts >7 days, returns, or occurs with fever/rash/headache. Consult a health professional if pregnant/breastfeeding.

## Symptoms Treated
Cough (dry and productive), chest congestion, sore throat (lozenge/spray SKUs), cold/flu symptoms including fever and runny nose (Nighttime SKU).

## Brand-Specific Alignment Check
No discrepancy found with [[dextromethorphan]] or [[guaifenesin]] — the DM SKU's doses (10mg/100mg per 5mL) match standard OTC monograph amounts already reflected on those pages. Confirms the general pattern already flagged on Robitussin's page: brand names in this category describe a product line, not a single ingredient.
