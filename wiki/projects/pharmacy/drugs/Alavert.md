## Appears In
Antihistamines, Oral - 5%

## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed OTC label: Alavert Allergy's active ingredient is **loratadine 10mg**, formulated as an orally disintegrating tablet (dissolves on the tongue, no water required). This replaces the earlier hedged pointer. Functionally the same active ingredient and dose as Claritin — see [[second-generation-antihistamines]] for ingredient-level evidence. Label saved at `raw/primary-sources/brands/alavert/dailymed-alavert.md`.

## Pros
- Orally disintegrating tablet format — convenient for those who have difficulty swallowing pills or lack access to water
- Same active ingredient/dose as Claritin (10mg loratadine) but typically positioned/priced as the value option in this molecule class
- Non-drowsy at labeled OTC dose

## Cons
- No Alavert-brand-specific clinical trial was found (as distinct from the generic loratadine evidence already on the ingredient page) — one registered trial comparing a different loratadine+pseudoephedrine generic to Claritin-D (NCT01055756) was withdrawn and does not involve Alavert
- Same class-level limitation noted on the ingredient page: loratadine was not among the top-ranked individual agents for nasal-symptom efficacy in the 2026 network meta-analysis (cetirizine, ebastine, bilastine, rupatadine ranked higher)

## Use Cases
Seasonal/perennial allergic rhinitis: sneezing, runny nose, itchy watery eyes, itchy nose/throat.

## Allergies / Warnings
Ask a doctor before use with liver or kidney disease. Contact Poison Control for overdose.

## Symptoms Treated
Sneezing, runny nose, itchy/watery eyes, itchy nose or throat.
