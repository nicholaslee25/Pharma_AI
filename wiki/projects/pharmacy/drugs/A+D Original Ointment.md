## Overview
**Same underlying product as [[A&D]] — confirmed, not a fresh finding.** [[A&D]]'s own DailyMed research (Topical Antibiotics/Antiseptics category, 3% share) already establishes that "A and D Original" ointment's FDA Drug Facts label lists **lanolin 15.5% and petrolatum 53.4%** as active ingredients, both classified as skin protectants — and that label's own primary indication is diaper rash prevention/treatment. This "A+D Original Ointment" listing (Diaper Rash Products, 14% share) is that same product's third category appearance in this database, alongside [[A&D]] (Topical Antibiotics/Antiseptics) and [[A+D First Aid Ointment]] (Burn Treatments) — filed separately here per this database's convention of one page per category-listing name (same approach used for the Triaminic/Children's Triaminic and A+D Ointment/A+D First Aid Ointment/A&D family elsewhere). Unlike its appearance under Topical Antibiotics/Antiseptics (a genuine mismatch, since it contains no antimicrobial ingredient) or Burn Treatments (a genuine mismatch, since it's not an antibiotic/anesthetic), **this Diaper Rash Products listing is actually the label's own primary, correctly-matched indication** — no mechanism mismatch here, unlike its two sibling listings.

## Pros
- Confirmed FDA Drug Facts label; simple, well-characterized, decades-old formulation (lanolin + petrolatum)
- **This is the one category listing (of three in this database) where A+D's actual labeled indication correctly matches its category placement** — diaper rash prevention/treatment is literally the primary use on its own FDA label, unlike its Topical Antibiotics/Antiseptics and Burn Treatments appearances
- Petrolatum's occlusive barrier mechanism has real supporting RCT evidence for uncomplicated wound care (Smack et al. 1996, on [[petrolatum]]), and per [[zinc-oxide]]'s Alkhamis et al. 2026 review, barrier preparations generally show favorable (if low-grade, Grade C) evidence for diaper dermatitis
- No antibiotic-allergy risk exposure, unlike some other Diaper Rash Products category-mates that may combine antimicrobial ingredients

## Cons
- Lanolin carries its own known (if uncommon) contact-allergy risk
- Per [[zinc-oxide]]'s Alkhamis et al. 2026 review: petroleum jelly/Vaseline specifically showed NO efficacy for reducing diaper rash severity/incidence in at least one included study — a real negative finding for the petrolatum component of this specific lanolin+petrolatum formulation, though the review's overall evidence quality is graded low (Grade C) and results were comparator-dependent
- No brand-specific clinical trial for diaper rash specifically was identified; the supporting evidence (Smack et al. 1996, on [[petrolatum]]) tested plain white petrolatum in a post-surgical wound context, not this specific lanolin-petrolatum combination product in a diaper-rash context
- Unlike zinc-oxide-based competitors in this same category (Desitin, Balmex, Boudreaux's Butt Paste, Triple Paste), this product's protectant mechanism is purely occlusive/moisturizing rather than zinc oxide's barrier-plus-mild-astringent action — comparative efficacy between the two mechanisms for diaper rash specifically was not independently confirmed in this pass

## Use Cases
Diaper rash prevention/treatment via moisture-barrier/skin-protectant action; also labeled for general minor-cuts skin protection (a use case outside this specific category).

## Allergies / Warnings
Per label: for external use only; discontinue if condition worsens or does not improve; some users may have lanolin contact sensitivity.

## Symptoms Treated
Diaper rash / irritant diaper dermatitis, via occlusive moisture-barrier protection (not antimicrobial).
