## Overview
Instaflex presents a genuinely two-sided, real evidence picture rather than a simple pass/fail. On one hand, this pass located an actual **independently published, peer-reviewed, double-blind, placebo-controlled community trial** (PMC4176106) testing the complete Instaflex Joint Support formulation itself (not just its individual ingredients) over 8 weeks in adults with self-reported joint pain, which found real improvement in joint pain severity and daily-activity difficulty, most pronounced in subjects with knee pain specifically — a genuinely stronger evidence position than most brands in this category (which rely only on ingredient-class-level evidence). On the other hand, this pass confirmed a **real, settled ($4.5–4.8 million) class-action lawsuit** (Mullins v. Direct Digital, filed 2013, settled 2017) alleging the brand's "scientifically formulated" and "clinically tested" label claims were false and misleading, with the plaintiff specifically arguing that glucosamine (Instaflex's active ingredient family) "has not been linked to joint health in any scientific research" — an allegation consistent with [[glucosamine-chondroitin]]'s own landmark GAIT trial null result for the general population. The lawsuit predates or overlaps with the independently published trial's timeline, meaning the settlement may reflect the state of evidence available at the time the challenged claims were made, not necessarily a verdict on the later-published trial itself — this pass could not fully resolve that timing question. Joint Health (6% share) is covered by [[glucosamine-chondroitin]].
