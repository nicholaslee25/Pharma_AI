## Appears In
Decongestants, Nasal Spray - 5%

## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed: Neo-Synephrine = **phenylephrine hydrochloride nasal spray** (Mild, Regular, and Maximum strength variants) — a genuinely different active ingredient from the oxymetazoline products dominating this category (Afrin, Dristan, Mucinex Nasal Spray, Vicks Sinex). This is a new confirmation, and an important one to distinguish carefully from [[oral-phenylephrine]]: **that ingredient page's evidence (the FDA advisory panel's 2023 conclusion that oral phenylephrine is not significantly more effective than placebo) is specifically about the ORAL/systemic route** — oral phenylephrine suffers from extensive first-pass hepatic metabolism, leaving very little active drug reaching systemic circulation. Nasal/topical phenylephrine, as in this product, is applied directly to the nasal mucosa and bypasses that first-pass metabolism problem entirely — so the oral-phenylephrine efficacy controversy should **NOT** be assumed to apply to this product. No dedicated ingredient page exists for topical/nasal phenylephrine specifically in this database — a genuine gap, and one worth flagging since conflating it with [[oral-phenylephrine]] would be a real error. Label saved at `raw/primary-sources/brands/neo-synephrine/dailymed-neo-synephrine.md`.

## Pros
- As a topical application, avoids the first-pass-metabolism efficacy problem that undermines oral phenylephrine — plausibly a genuinely more effective decongestant route for this same molecule, though this page did not locate a topical-phenylephrine-specific RCT to confirm the magnitude of that difference
- Multiple strength options (Mild/Regular/Maximum) allow dose titration, useful for sensitive users or children under appropriate guidance

## Cons
- Shorter duration of action than oxymetazoline — dosed every 4 hours vs. oxymetazoline's 10-12 hour interval, meaning more frequent dosing and more opportunities to lose track of the 3-day usage limit
- Still carries the same rebound congestion (rhinitis medicamentosa) risk class-wide for topical decongestants, per the general warning found on this label ("frequent or prolonged use may cause nasal congestion to recur or worsen") — same underlying mechanism concern as documented in detail on [[oxymetazoline]], even though phenylephrine and oxymetazoline are different molecules
- No dedicated ingredient page exists for nasal phenylephrine to provide a fuller evidence-grade/safety comparison

## Use Cases
Short-term (≤3 days) relief of nasal/sinus congestion from a cold, hay fever, or upper respiratory allergies.

## Allergies / Warnings
Do not exceed 3 consecutive days of use. Do not use more often than every 4 hours. Ask a doctor before use with heart disease, high blood pressure, diabetes, thyroid disease, or enlarged prostate.

## Symptoms Treated
Nasal and sinus congestion.
