## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed: Advil Cold & Sinus = **ibuprofen 200mg + pseudoephedrine HCl 30mg** (tablet/liquid-filled capsule). This replaces the earlier hedged "presumed ibuprofen base... not verified" pointer — both active ingredients are now confirmed, touching [[ibuprofen]] and [[pseudoephedrine]] (this is a genuine pseudoephedrine product, not phenylephrine, which matters since pseudoephedrine has meaningfully better oral-decongestant efficacy evidence than phenylephrine per current literature). A separate "Advil Allergy Sinus" SKU adds chlorpheniramine maleate (antihistamine) — a third, distinct formulation under a similar name, not confirmed to be the same product tracked in this database's three category listings above. ## Pros
- Uses pseudoephedrine, not phenylephrine — pseudoephedrine has a more favorable oral bioavailability/efficacy profile for nasal decongestion than phenylephrine (whose OTC efficacy an FDA advisory panel questioned in 2023), making this a genuinely stronger decongestant choice within the ibuprofen-combination category
- Ibuprofen provides both pain relief and anti-inflammatory action, useful for the pressure/headache component of sinus symptoms, not just fever
- No antihistamine or cough suppressant, so this product avoids sedation and stays targeted to pain + congestion only

## Cons
- Because pseudoephedrine is a behind-the-counter (not on open shelves) product in the US (Combat Methamphetamine Epidemic Act), this specific formulation requires an ID-logged pharmacy purchase, unlike phenylephrine-based Advil variants — a real practical/access difference worth noting for this specific SKU
- Ibuprofen carries the full NSAID risk profile on [[ibuprofen]] (GI bleeding, cardiovascular risk, boxed warnings) — a meaningfully different safety profile than an acetaminophen-based combination product like DayQuil for the same symptom set
- Pseudoephedrine itself carries cardiovascular stimulant effects (elevated BP/heart rate) — ask a doctor before use with high blood pressure, heart disease, thyroid disease, or diabetes, per [[pseudoephedrine]] if covered there
- The similarly-named "Advil Allergy Sinus" (with chlorpheniramine) risks being confused with this product despite a different ingredient profile

## Use Cases
Sinus pressure, headache, nasal/sinus congestion, minor aches and fever associated with a cold or sinus infection.

## Allergies / Warnings
Do not use if allergic to NSAIDs/aspirin (aspirin-triad asthma risk per [[ibuprofen]]). Ask a doctor before use with high blood pressure, heart/thyroid disease, diabetes, or enlarged prostate (pseudoephedrine cautions). Do not exceed labeled ibuprofen dose given NSAID GI/CV risk.

## Symptoms Treated
Sinus pressure, headache, nasal congestion, body aches, fever.
