## Overview
**Major discrepancy flagged — the most fundamental category-placement mismatch identified in this database to date.** "OFF!" is not an FDA-regulated drug at all — it is an insect **repellent**, regulated by the **EPA as a pesticide** under FIFRA. Confirmed: no DailyMed / FDA Drug Facts label exists for any OFF! product (searched directly, zero results), because repellents are not drugs. OFF! spans multiple active ingredients depending on product line — **DEET** (5-99% depending on SKU, e.g. Deep Woods/FamilyCare lines), **picaridin** (DEET-free, e.g. Clean Feel/Defense lines), **PMD** (plant-derived, ~10%, "Botanicals" line), and various **essential oils** (rosemary, citronella, geranium, cedarwood, peppermint, lemongrass, cornmint, clove — "Kids" line). Every other brand in Insect Bite and Sting Management is an FDA-regulated topical drug that TREATS an existing bite/sting; OFF! PREVENTS bites from occurring in the first place — a fundamentally different use case, mechanism, and regulatory category from its 7 category-mates, despite being tabulated in the same pharmacist-recommendation-share list.

## Pros
- EPA has reviewed DEET (the most common OFF! active ingredient) twice (1998, 2014) and concluded DEET-containing repellents "do not present a health concern" when used as directed, with no age restriction for children
- Offers genuine choice across active-ingredient classes (DEET, picaridin, PMD, essential oils) for users with different sensitivities or preferences
- Prevention (not having a bite at all) is arguably a stronger outcome than any treatment product in this category, when repellent use is practical

## Cons
- **Not comparable to any other product in this category on a pros/cons or efficacy basis** — it does not treat pain, itching, or infection risk from an existing bite/sting; it has no drug-facts label, no "active ingredient for symptom relief," and cannot be evaluated by the same criteria as [[Cortizone-10 Itch Relief]], [[After Bite]], [[Benadryl Topical]], [[BiteMD]], [[Sting-Kill]], [[Dermoplast]], or [[Nature's Willow Bug Bite Balm]]
- Spans at least four different active-ingredient classes under one brand name, each with its own EPA-reviewed safety/efficacy profile and protection-duration claims that were not independently verified against primary EPA registration data for each specific SKU in this pass (manufacturer-stated durations only)
- DEET-specific label precautions exist (do not apply over cuts/wounds/irritated skin; avoid children's eyes/mouth/hands; wash treated skin after returning indoors) — real safety guidance, though EPA's own conclusion is that normal labeled use is not a health concern
- No brand-specific clinical trial was sought, since this is a pesticide-efficacy question (bite prevention rates) rather than a drug-efficacy question — outside the scope of this database's FDA-label-centric methodology

## Use Cases
Prevention of insect bites/stings (mosquitoes, ticks, and other biting insects) by repelling insects from skin/clothing before contact occurs — NOT treatment of an existing bite or sting.

## Allergies / Warnings
Varies by active ingredient/SKU; DEET-specific EPA guidance: do not apply over cuts, wounds, or irritated skin; avoid application near children's eyes, mouth, and hands; use only enough to cover exposed skin/clothing; wash treated skin with soap and water after returning indoors; for sprays, apply to hands first then rub onto face rather than spraying directly on the face.

## Symptoms Treated
None — this is a preventive repellent, not a symptom-treatment product. It does not relieve pain, itching, or any symptom of an existing bite or sting.
