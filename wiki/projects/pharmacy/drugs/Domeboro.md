## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed: Domeboro's active ingredient is **aluminum acetate**, delivered in two different formats depending on SKU — a **powder/tablet form** (aluminum sulfate tetradecahydrate + calcium acetate monohydrate, which combine with water to form the actual aluminum acetate solution — the classic "Burow's Solution") and a ready-to-use **gel** (aluminum acetate 0.5% directly). Purpose: **astringent**. This is a mechanistically distinct approach from every other brand in this category (which use anesthetics like pramoxine/diphenhydramine/benzyl alcohol, or mechanical urushiol removal): astringents work by constricting tissue and drawing fluid away from the skin, drying out weeping/oozing lesions and forming a protective layer to allow healing underneath — a genuinely different, complementary mechanism to anesthetic itch-numbing.

Domeboro is also labeled for a broader range of uses than most competitors in this category: poison ivy/oak/sumac, insect bites, athlete's foot, and rashes from soaps/detergents/cosmetics/jewelry — a wider indication list reflecting the astringent mechanism's general applicability to any weepy/inflamed skin condition, not poison-ivy-specific chemistry. No Domeboro-brand-specific clinical trial was located in this pass; the astringent mechanism (aluminum acetate/Burow's solution) has a long history of dermatologic use, though a dedicated modern RCT was not independently verified here.

## Pros
- Genuinely different, complementary mechanism (astringent drying/protective-layer formation) to the anesthetic-based majority of this category — potentially useful in combination with, rather than instead of, an anesthetic product for actively weeping lesions
- Broad labeled indication range (poison ivy/oak/sumac, insect bites, athlete's foot, contact-irritant rashes) reflects a genuinely general-purpose astringent mechanism
- Long clinical-use history as "Burow's Solution," a well-established dermatologic astringent formulation predating most modern OTC brand names in this category

## Cons
- No dedicated ingredient evidence page for aluminum acetate/astringents exists in this database — a real coverage gap, since this is a mechanistically distinct category from every other ingredient reviewed in Topical Poison Ivy/Oak Remedies
- Powder/tablet SKUs require dilution in water before use — an extra preparation step relative to ready-to-use creams/lotions/sprays from competitors, a real usability difference worth noting even though it isn't a safety issue
- No Domeboro-brand-specific clinical trial (as opposed to the general, older astringent/Burow's-solution literature) was located
- Provides no direct anesthetic/anti-itch action on its own — a consumer expecting itch relief comparable to Caladryl/Sarna/CeraVe may find the drying/protective mechanism addresses a different symptom (oozing) than the itching most poison-ivy sufferers seek relief from first

## Use Cases
Temporary relief of minor skin irritations from poison ivy, poison oak, poison sumac, insect bites, athlete's foot, and rashes caused by soaps, detergents, cosmetics, or jewelry — via astringent drying and protective-layer formation over weeping/oozing skin.

## Allergies / Warnings
Per label: for external use only; avoid eye contact; stop use and ask a doctor if condition worsens or symptoms persist beyond 7 days.

## Symptoms Treated
Oozing, weeping, and general irritation from poison ivy/oak/sumac and other contact-irritant skin reactions; athlete's foot-related skin irritation.
