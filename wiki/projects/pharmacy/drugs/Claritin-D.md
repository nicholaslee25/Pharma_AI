## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed: Claritin-D 24 Hour = **loratadine 10mg + pseudoephedrine sulfate 240mg**, extended-release, once daily. A separate Claritin-D 12 Hour SKU also exists (loratadine/pseudoephedrine at presumably half the 24-hour dose per standard "-D" combination convention, not independently confirmed in this pass). This confirms the earlier "widely known as... not independently confirmed" hedge exactly as guessed. Touches [[second-generation-antihistamines]] (that page is written around cetirizine, not loratadine specifically — see the plain Claritin brand page for loratadine's own confirmed 10mg dose) and [[pseudoephedrine]]. Notably, unlike Zyrtec-D and Alavert D-12 (both of which use a reduced antihistamine dose relative to their standalone products), Claritin-D 24 Hour uses the **full 10mg loratadine dose** — the same as plain Claritin — a genuine difference in combination-product design across brands worth flagging. ## Pros
- Full-strength loratadine dose (10mg) combined with a substantial once-daily pseudoephedrine dose (240mg) — unlike Zyrtec-D/Alavert D-12's reduced antihistamine approach, a user gets the same antihistamine potency as the standalone product plus decongestant coverage
- Second-largest recommendation share (14%) in the Decongestants, Oral category in this database, behind only plain Sudafed
- Evidence-backed decongestant component per [[pseudoephedrine]]'s "moderate-to-high" evidence grade

## Cons
- Behind-the-counter purchase restriction applies due to the pseudoephedrine component
- 240mg pseudoephedrine in one extended-release tablet is a large total daily dose — must not be combined with any other pseudoephedrine-containing product without careful total-dose accounting, same caution as Allegra-D elsewhere in this batch
- The full-dose loratadine design (vs. Zyrtec-D/Alavert D-12's half-dose antihistamine approach) means this page cannot assume a uniform "-D combination convention" across brands — each brand's specific dose split should be checked individually

## Use Cases
Non-drowsy relief of nasal/sinus congestion plus allergy symptoms (sneezing, runny nose, itchy/watery eyes, itchy throat/nose) for indoor/outdoor allergies and colds.

## Allergies / Warnings
Ask a doctor before use with liver/kidney disease (loratadine), high blood pressure, heart disease, thyroid disease, or diabetes (pseudoephedrine).

## Symptoms Treated
Nasal/sinus congestion, sneezing, runny nose, itchy/watery eyes, itchy throat/nose.
