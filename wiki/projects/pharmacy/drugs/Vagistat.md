## Overview
**Real, confirmed brand-name-collision finding:** "Vagistat" refers to genuinely different active ingredients depending on the specific product/manufacturer. The classic **Vagistat-1** (per its own DailyMed label) is **tioconazole 6.5% ointment** — a single-dose vaginal antifungal, chemically a different azole from miconazole/clotrimazole (though the same broad drug class, see [[miconazole-nitrate-topical]]). Separately, a **Vagisil-branded "Vagistat"** product is confirmed via DailyMed as **miconazole nitrate cream** — a different active ingredient under the same core product name. This database cannot confirm which specific "Vagistat" product corresponds to the 4% share tracked in this category — resolving this correctly matters because, while both are legitimate azole antifungals, they are not the identical molecule/formulation a consumer might assume from the shared name.

## Pros
- Both possible "Vagistat" products (tioconazole-based Vagistat-1, miconazole-based Vagisil Vagistat) are genuine, FDA-monograph azole antifungals with real established efficacy for uncomplicated vulvovaginal candidiasis
- Vagistat-1 specifically offers single-dose (one applicator) treatment, a real convenience advantage over Monistat 3/7's multi-day regimens for tioconazole specifically

## Cons
- **Real brand-identity ambiguity**, flagged explicitly rather than resolved: "Vagistat" is not a single, unambiguous product — at least two different manufacturers/formulations (tioconazole 6.5% vs. miconazole nitrate) have used this name, and this database cannot determine which one drives the 4% share tracked in this category's Pharmacy Times data
- Tioconazole ointment base may interact with rubber or latex products (condoms, diaphragms) — the label specifically advises against using such products within 72 hours following treatment, a real, specific, and fairly long post-treatment caution not shared by every antifungal in this category
- No dedicated Vagistat-brand RCT distinct from the general tioconazole/miconazole azole-class literature was located in this pass

## Use Cases
Treatment of vaginal yeast infections (vulvovaginal candidiasis) — single-dose regimen if the tioconazole (Vagistat-1) formulation; multi-day if the miconazole (Vagisil-brand) formulation.

## Allergies / Warnings
Azole hypersensitivity is a contraindication for either formulation. Tioconazole specifically: avoid latex/rubber contraceptive products (condoms, diaphragms) for 72 hours post-treatment due to a real material-compatibility interaction disclosed on its own label.

## Symptoms Treated
Vaginal yeast infection: itching, burning, irritation, discharge.
