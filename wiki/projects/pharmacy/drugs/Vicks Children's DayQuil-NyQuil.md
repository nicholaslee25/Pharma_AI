## Appears In
Children's Cough/Cold Combinations - 3%

## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed: this is a **paired day/night co-pack**, spanning multiple ingredient sets depending on the specific SKU. The "Free Of" (artificial dyes/sugar) line pairs **DayQuil Kids** (dextromethorphan HBr 10mg + guaifenesin 200mg per 15mL — no acetaminophen, no alcohol) with **NyQuil Kids** (diphenhydramine HCl 12.5mg per 15mL — an antihistamine used here for its sedating/cough-suppressant effect, again no acetaminophen). A separate "Honey" flavor SKU instead uses guaifenesin + phenylephrine HCl + dextromethorphan HBr for the daytime product. Ages 6-under-12, 15mL every 4 hours, max 4 doses/24h. Notably, **neither confirmed SKU contains acetaminophen** — a genuine differentiator from most other combination products in this category (Children's Tylenol Cold+Cough+Runny Nose, Children's Mucinex Night Time) that DO combine acetaminophen with other actives, meaningfully reducing this product's overdose-stacking risk relative to those competitors.

## Pros
- Acetaminophen-free formulation (both confirmed SKUs) eliminates the specific double-dosing/hepatotoxicity risk that applies to every acetaminophen-containing combination competitor in this category — a real, brand-specific safety advantage worth naming explicitly rather than treating all combination products as equivalent-risk
- Day/night pairing (non-sedating DXM/guaifenesin daytime vs. sedating diphenhydramine nighttime) matches a clinically sensible symptom-timing approach, letting cough suppression continue through sleep without daytime sedation
- Uses dextromethorphan and guaifenesin, both with existing evidence pages in this database, rather than an unstudied proprietary blend

## Cons
- **Standing 2008 FDA/CHPA pediatric cough-cold labeling history applies** — labeled ages 6-under-12, consistent with the industry's post-2008 conservative approach to pediatric combination products
- Per [[dextromethorphan]] and [[guaifenesin]]'s own evidence grades (low-to-moderate each): the best-designed RCTs located for each individual ingredient found limited-to-no subjective symptom benefit over placebo
- If the Honey-flavor SKU applies: inherits [[oral-phenylephrine]]'s FDA-flagged efficacy problem and the pending November 2024 proposed OTC-monograph removal order — the same brand-name-spans-multiple-formulations pattern already flagged repeatedly in this batch (Delsym, Robitussin, Sudafed PE, PediaCare)
- NyQuil Kids' diphenhydramine component carries the same first-generation-antihistamine sedation/anticholinergic profile already documented on [[oral-diphenhydramine-sleep-aid]] and `drugs/Children's Benadryl.md` — a real CNS-depressant effect, not merely a mild side effect, worth weighing against the "day/night pairing" framing above as a genuine trade-off rather than a pure positive
- No brand-specific pediatric RCT located for either SKU beyond individual-ingredient evidence

## Use Cases
Daytime: cough and chest-congestion relief without sedation. Nighttime: cough suppression via a sedating antihistamine, intended to support sleep during a cold. Ages 6-under-12.

## Allergies / Warnings
Per label: not for children under 6; do not use with any other product containing dextromethorphan, guaifenesin, or an antihistamine; ask a doctor before use if the child has a breathing problem, glaucoma, or trouble urinating due to an enlarged prostate; NyQuil Kids specifically may cause drowsiness — avoid daytime activities requiring alertness after dosing.

## Symptoms Treated
Cough, chest congestion (daytime); cough with nighttime sedation support (nighttime).
