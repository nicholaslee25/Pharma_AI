## Overview
**Discrepancy flagged — same finding as [[A+D First Aid Ointment]].** Confirmed via DailyMed: "A and D Original" ointment's own FDA OTC Drug Facts label lists **lanolin 15.5% and petrolatum 53.4%** as active ingredients, both classified as **skin protectants**. This is not an antibiotic or antiseptic ingredient of any kind — A&D is mechanistically identical to [[petrolatum]]'s occlusive barrier mechanism, filed here under "Topical Antibiotics/Antiseptics" alongside genuinely antimicrobial products (Neosporin, Bacitraycin Plus, Polysporin, Hibiclens, Betadine). The label's own indication is diaper rash prevention/treatment and general minor-cuts skin protection, not infection prevention via antimicrobial action.

## Pros
- Confirmed FDA Drug Facts label; simple, well-characterized formulation with a long safety track record
- Petrolatum's occlusive mechanism has real supporting RCT evidence for uncomplicated wound care (Smack et al. 1996, on [[petrolatum]])
- No antibiotic-allergy risk exposure (no bacitracin/neomycin/polymyxin), unlike most of its category-mates

## Cons
- **Category-placement mismatch, identical to [[A+D First Aid Ointment]]:** contains no antimicrobial or antiseptic active ingredient despite being filed under "Topical Antibiotics/Antiseptics" — its actual mechanism is moisture-barrier/skin protection, not infection prevention via antimicrobial action
- Lanolin carries its own known (if uncommon) contact-allergy risk
- No brand-specific clinical trial for this specific use context was identified

## Use Cases
Skin protectant/moisture barrier — labeled primarily for diaper rash prevention/treatment, with general minor-cuts/skin-protection use; not an antimicrobial or infection-prevention product despite its category placement here.

## Allergies / Warnings
Per label: for external use only; discontinue if condition worsens or does not improve; some users may have lanolin contact sensitivity.

## Symptoms Treated
Not antimicrobial — provides a protective moisture barrier, consistent with petrolatum's general occlusive wound-care mechanism.
