## Overview
Midol is a brand family spanning multiple distinct formulations, not one fixed product. Midol Complete (Gelcaps/Caplets/Caffeine Free) is confirmed via DailyMed as acetaminophen 500mg + caffeine 60mg + pyrilamine maleate 15mg per tablet — a triple-combination analgesic (acetaminophen)/diuretic (caffeine)/antihistamine (pyrilamine, addressing bloating/water retention via its mild anticholinergic effect). This database's own [[acetaminophen]] page carries the core analgesic evidence; pyrilamine and caffeine's specific menstrual-symptom contributions were not independently re-verified with dedicated trial data in this pass — a real evidence gap distinct from acetaminophen's own well-documented efficacy. Real-world market data also confirms other Midol sub-lines exist under the same brand name: Midol Extended Relief (naproxen sodium-based, an NSAID rather than acetaminophen), Midol PM (adds diphenhydramine as a sleep aid, see [[oral-diphenhydramine-sleep-aid]]), and Midol Teen (acetaminophen only, no caffeine/antihistamine) — a real one-brand-multiple-formulations pattern consistent with this database's recurring finding across other combination-product brands (Advil, Tylenol, Dramamine). The 33% share tracked in this category is for "Midol" generally and cannot be attributed to one specific sub-formulation.

## Pros
- Acetaminophen component carries this database's standard, well-established analgesic/antipyretic evidence base (see [[acetaminophen]])
- Product-line variety (Complete, Extended Relief, PM, Teen) lets a buyer match formulation to symptom profile (pain vs. pain+sleep vs. simple pain relief for teens)
- Caffeine as a mild diuretic component directly targets the water-weight/bloating symptom cluster specific to this category, a real, if modestly evidenced, symptom-matching rationale

## Cons
- **No brand-specific RCT for the full triple-combination (acetaminophen + caffeine + pyrilamine) was located in this pass** — efficacy for the "Complete" formulation as a whole rests on the individual components' general pharmacology, not a dedicated Midol Complete trial
- Acetaminophen's standard hepatotoxicity risk applies (severe liver damage warning at >8 caplets/24h on the Pamprin-family label convention shared by this drug class) — same class-wide caution as every acetaminophen-containing product in this database
- Brand-name ambiguity: "Midol" alone (without specifying Complete/Extended Relief/PM/Teen) does not identify which active-ingredient combination a consumer is buying — a real labeling-navigation burden for consumers comparing products by brand name rather than active ingredient
- Pyrilamine, like other first-generation antihistamines, carries anticholinergic-class side effects (drowsiness, dry mouth) not always anticipated by consumers focused on the "pain reliever" framing of the product

## Use Cases
Temporary relief of menstrual symptoms — cramps, headache, backache, irritability, bloating, muscle aches, water-weight gain (per Midol Complete's own label uses).

## Allergies / Warnings
Standard acetaminophen liver-injury warning (avoid exceeding labeled max dose or combining with other acetaminophen-containing products). Pyrilamine carries the standard first-generation-antihistamine caution (avoid with alcohol/sedatives; caution in glaucoma, breathing problems, enlarged prostate per class convention). Caffeine-sensitive individuals should note the Caffeine Free variant exists specifically for this reason.

## Symptoms Treated
Menstrual cramps, headache, backache, bloating, water-weight gain, irritability, muscle aches (per label); pain and sleeplessness specifically for the Midol PM sub-line.

## Brand-Specific Alignment Check
**Real, database-worthy finding:** "Midol" is not one formulation — it spans acetaminophen-based (Complete, Teen), naproxen-based (Extended Relief), and diphenhydramine-added (PM) sub-lines, each with a genuinely different active-ingredient safety/interaction profile. A consumer or caregiver should confirm which specific Midol product they mean before assuming a shared safety profile across the brand name — the same pattern already documented for Advil, Tylenol, and Dramamine elsewhere in this database.
