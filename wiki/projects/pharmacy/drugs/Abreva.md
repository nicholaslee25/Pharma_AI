## Appears In
Cold Sore Treatments - 69%

## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed: Abreva's active ingredient is **docosanol 10%** — the only FDA-approved-under-monograph antiviral (fusion-inhibitor mechanism) for cold sores/fever blisters, per [[docosanol]]'s full evidence writeup. This is the category leader by a wide margin (69%, more than 10x the next brand). The pivotal registration RCT (Sacks et al. 2001, PMID 11464183, N=737) found a real but genuinely modest effect: median healing time 4.1 days vs. placebo, only ~18 hours faster (P=0.008) — and the "aborted episode" outcome (lesion never fully developing, arguably what patients care about most) was NOT statistically significant (40% vs. 34%, P=0.109). Abreva's dominant market share should not be read as proof of dramatic efficacy; it is genuinely the most evidence-backed OTC option in this category, but the evidence itself is modest.

## Pros
- The only ingredient in this category with a dedicated, published, placebo-controlled pivotal RCT (two identical trials, 21 sites, 737 total participants) directly supporting its labeled antiviral mechanism
- Clean safety profile — adverse events in the pivotal RCT were mild and similar in frequency to placebo
- Works via a genuinely different mechanism (viral-entry fusion inhibition) than every anesthetic-based competitor (Anbesol Cold Sore Therapy, most of Blistex's line) — it targets the underlying virus rather than just numbing symptoms

## Cons
- The real, quantified benefit is modest: ~18 hours faster healing, not a cure, and does not reliably prevent a lesion from developing once triggered (the "aborted episode" outcome was not statistically significant)
- Early application (at first tingle/itch) is essential to the trial's design and the label's own directions — delayed application likely reduces whatever benefit exists
- Not labeled for use in children under 12 without medical consultation first
- No head-to-head trial vs. any anesthetic-based competitor (Anbesol Cold Sore Therapy, Zilactin, etc.) was identified — Abreva's antiviral mechanism and the anesthetics' symptom-numbing mechanism address different aspects of the problem and are not directly comparable on a single efficacy scale

## Use Cases
Treatment of cold sores/fever blisters (herpes simplex labialis) on the face or lips, applied at the first sign of symptoms (tingle, itch, bump), 5 times daily until healed.

## Allergies / Warnings
Per label: for external use only, not for ophthalmic or intraoral use; wash hands before and after applying; do not share product/applicator (risk of spreading infection); discontinue and consult a doctor if the sore worsens or does not heal within 10 days; rare allergy alert for hives, facial swelling, or difficulty breathing.

## Symptoms Treated
Cold sore/fever blister duration and associated tingling, pain, burning, and itching — via viral-entry inhibition rather than symptomatic numbing.
