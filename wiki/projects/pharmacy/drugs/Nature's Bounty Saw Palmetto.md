## Overview
Nature's Bounty Saw Palmetto (450mg per capsule per manufacturer supplement facts) is confirmed via the product's own marketing to be positioned for "prostate and urinary health" — **saw palmetto's entire evidence base is for benign prostatic hyperplasia (BPH) in men**, a condition specific to the male prostate gland, which does not exist in women. This is a real, significant category-placement mismatch: this database's Urinary Health category (Women's Health section) tracks Nature's Bounty Saw Palmetto at 3% share alongside phenazopyridine-, cranberry-, and probiotic-based products genuinely aimed at female urinary-tract symptoms/UTI risk — but saw palmetto has no established mechanism or trial evidence for that population or those symptoms at all. Compounding this, even saw palmetto's OWN core evidence base (male BPH) is weak: a 2023 review of 27 studies found saw palmetto alone provides little or no benefit for BPH symptoms, and recent studies have failed to show benefit over placebo (per NCCIH).

## Cons
- **No evidence located in this pass supports any urinary-health benefit specifically in women** — the entire clinical evidence base for saw palmetto addresses BPH, a male-prostate-specific condition; this database found no trial, review, or mechanism connecting saw palmetto to female urinary-tract symptoms, UTI prevention, or bladder health
- **Even the male-BPH evidence base is weak**: a 2023 review of 27 studies found saw palmetto alone provides little or no BPH-symptom benefit, and recent trials failed to show benefit over placebo — meaning this product's category placement rests on evidence that is both population-mismatched AND, on its own terms, unfavorable
- Real risk that a consumer selecting this product from the "Urinary Health" category listing (which otherwise contains genuinely female-urinary-tract-relevant products) could reasonably but incorrectly assume it addresses the same symptom set as its category-mates
