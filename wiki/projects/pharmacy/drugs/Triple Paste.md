## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed: Triple Paste's standard "Medicated for Diaper Rash" formula is **zinc oxide 12.8%**, while a separate "Triple 3X Max Diaper Rash" line is **zinc oxide 40%** — a fourth brand in this database's Diaper Rash Products category (after Desitin, Boudreaux's Butt Paste) confirmed to span a standard-vs-40%-maximum-strength split, with 40% again appearing as the apparent FDA OTC monograph ceiling for zinc oxide as a skin protectant, consistent with Desitin's and Boudreaux's Maximum Strength lines. Talc-free, fragrance-free, paraben-free, and non-nano zinc oxide per manufacturer labeling for the standard formula.

## Pros
- Confirmed FDA Drug Facts labels for both the standard 12.8% and 3X Max 40% formulations, matching [[zinc-oxide]]'s barrier-protectant evidence base
- Talc-free, fragrance-free, paraben-free formulation (standard line) — a "clean label" positioning independent of therapeutic efficacy
- Marketed and positioned as a "medical grade" product for more severe/persistent rash, with a real 40% maximum-strength option available like Desitin and Boudreaux's

## Cons
- **Same brand-name/SKU concentration ambiguity as Desitin and Boudreaux's Butt Paste**: this database's category-mapping data ("Triple Paste," 9%/5% share) does not specify standard (12.8%) vs. 3X Max (40%) — now the third brand in this exact category confirmed to have this same ambiguity
- Per [[zinc-oxide]]'s own evidence review: overall evidence quality for zinc-oxide barrier preparations is graded low-level (Grade C) regardless of brand, concentration, or "medical grade"/pediatrician-recommended marketing language
- No Triple Paste-specific RCT was independently located in this pass beyond the general zinc-oxide evidence base
- "Medical grade" and "pediatrician recommended" marketing claims were not independently verified against a specific clinical trial or formal medical-grade regulatory designation in this pass

## Use Cases
Prevention and treatment of diaper rash (irritant diaper dermatitis) in infants; prevention/treatment of incontinence-associated skin irritation in adults — via zinc oxide's barrier-protectant mechanism, at either 12.8% or 40% concentration depending on product line.

## Allergies / Warnings
Per label conventions for this ingredient/category: for external use only; discontinue and consult a doctor if condition worsens or does not improve. Talc-free, fragrance-free, paraben-free (standard formula) — a positive consideration for fragrance-sensitive infants, though not independently confirmed as an FDA-recognized allergen-avoidance claim.

## Symptoms Treated
Diaper rash / irritant diaper dermatitis (infants); incontinence-associated dermatitis (adults) — via zinc oxide's moisture-barrier mechanism, per [[zinc-oxide]].
