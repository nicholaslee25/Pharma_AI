## Appears In
Laxatives, Bulk/Fiber - 3%

## Overview
**Real brand-confusion finding.** MiraFIBER is a MiraLAX-brand-family "Dietary Supplement Gummy" (8g fiber/serving) whose active fiber is **inulin from chicory root** — a mechanistically UNRELATED ingredient to MiraLAX itself, which per [[peg-3350]] is polyethylene glycol 3350, an osmotic laxative with a completely different mechanism (water retention via hydrogen bonding, not fiber bulking). The shared "Mira-" brand prefix creates real potential for a consumer to assume MiraFIBER shares MiraLAX's FDA-approved, meta-analysis-strength osmotic-laxative evidence and potency — it does not. Separately, inulin is also not one of the two fiber types ([[fiber]]'s cited meta-analysis: psyllium, pectin) shown to produce significant treatment-response improvement for chronic constipation, and MiraFIBER is a dietary supplement gummy, not an FDA-monograph OTC drug like MiraLAX.

## Pros
- Inulin has a genuine prebiotic mechanism (supports gut bacterial fermentation) distinct from, and complementary to, MiraLAX's own PEG-3350 mechanism
- Gummy format may improve compliance for users who dislike mixing a powder (MiraLAX's own format) or swallowing capsules/tablets

## Cons
- **Real, brand-driven confusion risk: "MiraFIBER" shares a brand prefix with MiraLAX but has a completely different active ingredient and mechanism** (inulin/fiber-bulking vs. PEG-3350/osmotic) — a consumer assuming MiraFIBER is simply "MiraLAX in gummy form" or a milder version of the same drug would be mistaken; this database's [[peg-3350]] page's own strong RCT evidence (p<0.0001 trial cited there) does NOT extend to this product
- **Not psyllium or pectin** — per [[fiber]]'s own cited meta-analysis, inulin was not one of the two fiber types shown to produce significant treatment-response improvement
- **Not an FDA-approved OTC drug** — a dietary supplement gummy, unlike MiraLAX itself (an FDA OTC monograph drug per [[peg-3350]]), meaning it did not go through the same FDA Drug Facts label review
- No MiraFIBER-brand-specific RCT was independently located in this pass

## Use Cases
Marketed for daily fiber/prebiotic gut-health support — via inulin, a mechanistically distinct product from MiraLAX itself despite the shared brand name.

## Allergies / Warnings
No FDA Drug Facts label located (dietary supplement, not an FDA-monograph drug). General fiber-supplement gas/bloating tradeoff applies per [[fiber]]'s cited meta-analysis findings for fiber supplementation generally.
