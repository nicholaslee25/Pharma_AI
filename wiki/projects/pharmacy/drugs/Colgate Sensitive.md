# Colgate Sensitive

## Appears In
Toothpaste, Sensitive Gums/Teeth - 15%

## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed: the Colgate Sensitive line (Enamel Health, Whitening, Complete Protection, Prevent and Repair, Plus Whitening) lists **potassium nitrate 5%** (antisensitivity) combined with **sodium fluoride 0.24% (0.14% w/v fluoride ion)** (anticavity) as its active ingredients — confirming [[potassium-nitrate]]'s prior "plausibly potassium-nitrate-based" placeholder for this specific brand. This is the same active-ingredient combination as Sensodyne's flagship formula (potassium nitrate + sodium fluoride), meaning Colgate Sensitive and Sensodyne compete directly on an identical active-ingredient basis in this category, unlike Crest Gum & Sensitivity's materially different stannous-fluoride mechanism (see `drugs/Crest Gum & Sensitivity.md`).

## Pros
- Confirmed FDA Drug Facts label matching the combination already documented on [[potassium-nitrate]] and [[fluoride-toothpaste]]
- A real head-to-head brand-specific RCT exists: Colgate Sensitive Maximum Strength (5% potassium nitrate) vs. Fresh Mint Sensodyne, both potassium-nitrate dentifrices — Colgate's formulation showed statistically significant improvement over Sensodyne on tactile and air-blast sensitivity scores after 4 weeks, attributed to an "activated silica technology" delivery difference
- Dual anticavity + antisensitivity benefit from one product, same structural logic as Sensodyne's combination formula

## Cons
- Per [[potassium-nitrate]]'s Cochrane evidence summary (Poulsen 2006), the broader potassium-nitrate ingredient class shows a real, statistically significant effect on OBJECTIVE (air blast/tactile) measures, but the patient-reported (subjective) benefit failed to reach statistical significance — the same evidentiary caution applies to Colgate Sensitive as to any potassium-nitrate product, regardless of brand-specific head-to-head results
- The Colgate-vs-Sensodyne comparative trial is very likely Colgate-sponsored research (delivery-technology comparison, not an independent third-party replication) — a real limitation on how much weight to put on its "superior to Sensodyne" conclusion
- No independent, non-manufacturer-sponsored brand-specific trial was identified in this pass

## Use Cases
Dentin hypersensitivity relief (cold/heat/acid/sweet/contact triggers) and cavity prevention — identical use case and mechanism to Sensodyne, competing brands with the same active-ingredient combination.

## Allergies / Warnings
Per label: if sensitivity persists after 4 weeks of regular use, consult a dentist; stop use and consult a dentist if the problem worsens (may signal a more serious condition); keep away from children; seek immediate medical attention if swallowed in excess of brushing amounts.

## Symptoms Treated
Tooth sensitivity to cold, heat, acids, sweets, or contact; cavity prevention.
