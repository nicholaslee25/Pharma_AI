## Overview
Balneol Hygienic Cleansing Lotion is confirmed via manufacturer ingredient disclosure to be a **non-medicated** cosmetic lotion — water, mineral oil, propylene glycol, lanolin oil, and related emollient/preservative ingredients, with no FDA-recognized active ingredient of any kind. The manufacturer's own product description explicitly uses the word "non-medicated." This is consistent with this database's recurring finding that several products in the Vaginal Care and Hygiene category are cosmetics rather than FDA-monograph drugs.

## Pros
- Marketed and formulated as a gentle, alcohol-free, soap-free cleansing lotion — a reasonable, low-irritant option for perianal/perineal hygiene given its simple emollient-based ingredient list
- Marketed as safe for use during pregnancy, postpartum, and for hemorrhoid- or diarrhea-associated irritation — a broad usability claim consistent with its non-medicated, low-active-ingredient formulation (not independently verified against a dedicated safety trial in this pass, but plausible given the mild ingredient profile)

## Cons
- **Explicitly non-medicated per the manufacturer's own description, yet marketed to "relieve" anal and vaginal irritation and itching** — a real, if modest, tension: a cleansing/moisturizing lotion with no active analgesic or anti-itch ingredient (unlike, e.g., Vagisil's benzocaine/resorcinol-based Anti-Itch Creme) cannot be expected to relieve itching or irritation through any FDA-recognized pharmacological mechanism, only through general moisturizing/cleansing action
- Contains methylparaben (a preservative) — a real, if generally low-risk, consideration for anyone specifically avoiding parabens
- No brand-specific clinical trial was located in this pass supporting the marketed "relieves irritation and itching" claim beyond the product's general emollient properties

## Use Cases
Gentle perianal/perineal/vaginal-area cleansing (per manufacturer) — a cosmetic hygiene use, not a treatment for any diagnosed condition.

## Allergies / Warnings
No brand-specific recall or lawsuit located in this pass. Contains lanolin (a known contact-allergen for some individuals) and methylparaben — worth noting for anyone with known sensitivities to either ingredient.

## Symptoms Treated
None with FDA-recognized therapeutic support — marketed relief of irritation/itching rests on general cleansing/moisturizing action rather than an active pharmacological ingredient.
