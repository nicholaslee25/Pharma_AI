## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed (ASPERCREME PAIN RELIEVING cream): **trolamine salicylate 10%**, purpose "topical analgesic," for temporary relief of minor muscle/joint pain from arthritis, backache, strains, sprains, and bruises. This is the ORIGINAL/plain "Aspercreme" formula tracked by this database's Topical Analgesics category rows — a different active ingredient entirely from **Aspercreme with Lidocaine** (lidocaine HCl 4%, filed separately in Topical Anesthetics/Diabetic Neuropathy Products — see `drugs/Aspercreme with Lidocaine.md`), a brand-spans-two-mechanisms pattern already documented for many brands in this database.

**Brand-specific trial found:** a double-blind, placebo-controlled, crossover trial of 10% trolamine salicylate cream in 50 osteoarthritis patients (49 completed) found the active cream produced statistically significant pain relief over placebo at 45 minutes (P=0.0401) and significantly faster stiffness relief (35 vs. 58 minutes to onset). Much of the drug effect had worn off by 2 hours after a single application, and no significant adverse effects were reported. This trial is specifically for the 10% trolamine salicylate formulation Aspercreme uses, not a generic salicylate-class trial — a genuine positive for this brand, though a single 50-patient trial from an older era of NSAID-topical research is a modest evidence base by modern standards.

Trolamine salicylate is a salicylate ester, mechanistically/pharmacologically related to the methyl-salicylate counterirritants documented on [[topical-counterirritants]], though it is typically classified and marketed as a distinct "non-counterirritant" rub-in analgesic (odorless, doesn't produce the strong heating/cooling sensation of menthol/methyl-salicylate combination products). No dedicated ingredient page for trolamine salicylate exists in this database yet; this brand page and [[topical-counterirritants]] (which already tracks Blue-Emu Original Cream's trolamine salicylate 10% formulation) are the closest coverage.

## Pros
- Confirmed, consistent active ingredient (trolamine salicylate 10%) for the plain Aspercreme product line, independently verified via DailyMed
- Genuine brand-specific placebo-controlled crossover trial exists (P=0.0401 pain relief, significantly faster stiffness relief) — rarer than the generic-ingredient-only evidence found for many brands in this database
- Marketed as odorless/non-staining relative to menthol/methyl-salicylate counterirritant rubs, a real usability differentiator for some users
- Well-established long-standing OTC brand with broad pharmacist familiarity (9% Topical Analgesics, 8% Arthritis/Joint Pain share)

## Cons
- **Same brand name covers a second, chemically unrelated product line (Aspercreme with Lidocaine, 37% share of Topical Anesthetics) — a consumer scanning shelves for "Aspercreme" could easily end up with a lidocaine anesthetic instead of the salicylate analgesic, or vice versa**, without realizing these are pharmacologically distinct mechanisms (NSAID-class salicylate vs. sodium-channel-blocking local anesthetic)
- The one brand-specific trial located is small (n=49), single-application, short-duration (effect faded within 2 hours) — does not establish long-term or repeat-dosing efficacy
- Trolamine salicylate, as a salicylate, carries the same systemic-absorption/NSAID-class safety concerns as methyl salicylate (aspirin-allergy cross-reactivity, salicylate toxicity risk if ingested or overused) — not independently re-verified against this brand's own label warnings in this pass beyond the general trolamine-salicylate drug-class data
- No dedicated ingredient evidence page for trolamine salicylate exists in this database; the closest coverage ([[topical-counterirritants]]) is scoped to menthol/camphor/methyl salicylate, not trolamine salicylate specifically

## Use Cases
Temporary relief of minor aches and pains of muscles and joints associated with arthritis, simple backache, strains, sprains, and bruises.

## Allergies / Warnings
Aspirin/salicylate-allergic individuals should consult a doctor before use (salicylate cross-reactivity risk); for external use only; avoid broken/irritated skin, eyes, and mucous membranes; do not use with a heating pad; discontinue and consult a doctor if condition worsens or persists beyond 7 days.

## Symptoms Treated
Minor muscle and joint pain, stiffness, arthritis pain, backache, strains, sprains, and bruise-related discomfort.
