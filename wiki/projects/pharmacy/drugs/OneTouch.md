## Overview
LifeScan's **OneTouch Verio** line has among the strongest brand-specific accuracy data located in this category: independent studies (Freckmann et al.) found overall MARD of **4.8-8.9%** against a manufacturer reference instrument, and a March 2018 study (OneTouch Verio Flex) found all three tested strip lots met and exceeded ISO 15197:2013's minimum accuracy requirements. LifeScan also cites a seven-year clinical surveillance program spanning "more than 70,000 clinical data points" as evidence of consistent MARD performance over time — a real, if company-published, longitudinal claim not independently re-verified in this pass. These MARD figures (4.8-8.9%) are notably tighter/better than the wide range found for [[FreeStyle]]'s Libre CGM line (7.8-18.33%) — though the two measure genuinely different technologies (OneTouch Verio is a fingerstick meter; FreeStyle Libre is a CGM), so this should be read as a technology-type difference, not necessarily a simple "OneTouch beats FreeStyle" claim. No independent (non-LifeScan-funded) study specifically was distinguished from company-sponsored ones in the sources gathered — a real limitation given [[blood-glucose-monitors]]'s own documented manufacturer-funding bias finding (King et al. 2018).

## Pros
- Comparatively tight MARD range (4.8-8.9%) among the best brand-specific figures located in this category
- Multiple independent-seeming published studies (not just one) converge on meeting/exceeding ISO 15197:2013 standards across different strip lots
- Second-largest share (28%) of Blood Glucose Monitors and largest (25%) of Blood Sampling Devices/Lancets among directly comparable brands, indicating broad market presence

## Cons
- The sources located did not clearly distinguish independent academic studies from LifeScan-sponsored ones — given the King et al. 2018 finding on [[blood-glucose-monitors]] that manufacturer-funded studies show dramatically higher "pass" odds (OR ~22) than independent ones, this MARD data should be read with that caveat in mind rather than at full face value
- The cited seven-year/70,000-data-point surveillance claim is a company press release figure, not an independently published peer-reviewed dataset with its own accessible methodology in the sources found
- Like all glucose meters, subject to the general interference/hematocrit/strip-variation failure modes documented on [[blood-glucose-monitors]]

## Use Cases
Blood glucose self-monitoring for diabetes management via fingerstick capillary sampling.
