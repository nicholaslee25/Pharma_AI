## Overview
**Real, significant, brand-name-scope discrepancy found here.** Ocuvite is made by the same manufacturer as category-leader [[PreserVision]] (Bausch + Lomb) and offers "several formulas based on the AREDS2 study," but independent sourcing explicitly states: **"PreserVision and Ocuvite are NOT interchangeable... Ocuvite vitamins support general eye health but do not contain the same formula that was studied in the AREDS2 trial"** — for at least some Ocuvite SKUs. This means a consumer choosing "Ocuvite" over "PreserVision" from the same manufacturer, assuming both carry the same NEI-trial-backed protection, may be choosing a product that does not match the actual tested formula. Ocuvite's product line spans multiple distinct sub-formulas (including "Ocuvite Blue Light," with substantially higher lutein 25mg/zeaxanthin 5mg for blue-light-filtering claims not evidenced by the AREDS2 trial), meaning the brand name alone does not indicate which specific evidence tier a given SKU falls into. See [[areds2-eye-vitamins]] for the underlying trial evidence, which is scoped specifically to the exact AREDS/AREDS2 nutrient combination and doses. Ocular Nutritional Supplements (29% share) is covered by [[areds2-eye-vitamins]].

## Pros
- Some Ocuvite formulas are confirmed to be based on the AREDS2 study nutrient combination — for those specific SKUs, the same trial-level evidence in [[areds2-eye-vitamins]] applies
- Broad product-line options (including omega-3-added and blue-light-filtering variants) give consumers formulation choices beyond the single core AREDS2 formula
- Backed by Bausch + Lomb's established eye-care manufacturing and clinical credibility

## Cons
- **Real, confirmed discrepancy: not all Ocuvite formulas match the actual AREDS2 trial formula**, despite the brand's general "AREDS2-based" positioning and its being made by the same manufacturer as the exactly-matched [[PreserVision]] — a consumer cannot assume any given "Ocuvite" product carries the same trial-backed evidence without checking the specific SKU's exact nutrient doses against [[areds2-eye-vitamins]]
- "Ocuvite Blue Light" (25mg lutein/5mg zeaxanthin, well above the AREDS2-tested 10mg/2mg) markets a blue-light-filtering benefit not evidenced by the AREDS2 trial, which tested a specific dose for AMD progression, not screen-related eye strain or blue-light protection
- Per [[areds2-eye-vitamins]], even the exactly-matched AREDS2 formula's evidence applies only to patients already diagnosed with intermediate AMD or advanced AMD in one eye — "general eye health" marketing for the non-AREDS2-matched Ocuvite SKUs specifically lacks even that narrower trial support

## Use Cases
Varies significantly by specific Ocuvite SKU — some match the AREDS2 trial formula (see [[areds2-eye-vitamins]] for that evidence, scoped to diagnosed intermediate/advanced-one-eye AMD patients); others are general multivitamin-style "eye health" products without confirmed trial-level support, and others (Blue Light) address a use case (blue-light filtering) outside the AREDS2 trial's scope entirely.
