## Appears In

## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed: Robitussin Nighttime Cough DM products = **dextromethorphan HBr + doxylamine succinate** (no guaifenesin, no acetaminophen in this specific line — a real formulation distinction from most other "Nighttime" combination products in this batch). Soft Chews: dextromethorphan (15mg equiv.) + doxylamine 6.25mg per tablet. Honey Maximum Strength liquid (per 20mL): dextromethorphan HBr 30mg + doxylamine succinate 12.5mg. This corrects the earlier hedge, which only pointed to guaifenesin — the Nighttime-specific line does not appear to contain guaifenesin at all, unlike plain Robitussin or Robitussin CF/DM daytime products. Touches [[dextromethorphan]]; doxylamine remains an unaddressed ingredient-page gap. Label saved at `raw/primary-sources/brands/robitussin/dailymed-robitussin-nighttime.md`.

## Pros
- No acetaminophen in this specific nighttime line — a genuine safety advantage for users already taking a separate acetaminophen product for fever/pain, since it removes one axis of accidental double-dosing that most other nighttime combination brands in this batch (NyQuil, Tylenol Cold Nighttime, Coricidin HBP Nighttime, Theraflu Nighttime) don't offer
- Simple two-ingredient formula (cough suppressant + sleep-aid antihistamine) targets the core nighttime-cough use case without unnecessary components
- Soft chew format offers an alternative to liquid dosing

## Cons
- Precisely because it lacks acetaminophen, this product does NOT treat fever or body aches at all — a consumer expecting broad "cold and flu" relief (as competitors in the same Pharmacy Times category typically provide) would need a separate product for those symptoms
- Doxylamine's anticholinergic/sedation profile applies, same as other doxylamine-containing products in this batch, with no dedicated ingredient page to check against
- Dextromethorphan's MAOI interaction and misuse-potential warnings apply at both dose levels found

## Use Cases
Nighttime cough suppression with sleep-aid support — specifically not a fever/pain reliever, unlike most of its category-mates.

## Allergies / Warnings
Do not use with MAOIs. May cause marked drowsiness — avoid alcohol, do not drive. Ask a doctor before use if cough persists >7 days or is accompanied by high fever, rash, or persistent headache (a sign a different product/medical evaluation may be needed, especially since this product itself doesn't treat fever).

## Symptoms Treated
Cough, sleeplessness during illness — not fever or body aches.
