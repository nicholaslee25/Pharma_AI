## Appears In
Ear Wax Removal - 86%

## Overview
Confirmed: Debrox's own FDA/DailyMed label lists **carbamide peroxide 6.5%** as its sole active ingredient — a foaming, oxygen-releasing cerumenolytic with weak antibacterial activity. See [[cerumenolytics]] for the full ingredient-class evidence: the Cochrane review (Aaron et al. 2018) found only low-quality evidence that any active cerumenolytic outperforms plain water/saline, and no evidence that one active agent outperforms another. Debrox is the category leader by a very wide margin (86% share) despite this thin comparative-efficacy base — a notable mismatch between recommendation concentration and evidence strength, flagged generally on [[cerumenolytics]]. No Debrox-brand-specific clinical trial beyond the general carbamide-peroxide evidence was identified.

## Pros
- Category leader by a wide margin (86% share), reflecting long market presence and pharmacist familiarity
- Simple, well-established formulation (carbamide peroxide 6.5%) with a long safety track record
- Short, clearly bounded use directions (up to twice daily for up to 4 days) limit prolonged self-treatment

## Cons
- Per [[cerumenolytics]], the best available evidence (Cochrane 2018) found no evidence that active cerumenolytics like carbamide peroxide outperform plain water/saline for wax clearance — Debrox's dominant market share is not backed by a demonstrated comparative-efficacy edge
- Contraindicated with perforated eardrum, ear tubes, ear pain/discharge, or recent ear surgery
- Not effective for all impaction types; per [[cerumenolytics]], irrigation (a separate step often still needed) is less effective than manual removal under visualization in patients over 70

## Use Cases
Occasional softening/loosening/removal of excessive earwax in adults and children over 12 (under-12 use requires physician guidance).

## Allergies / Warnings
Per label: do not use with ear pain, discharge/drainage, irritation, rash, injury, or perforation, or after recent ear surgery unless directed by a doctor; discontinue if dizziness or new symptoms occur.

## Symptoms Treated
Excessive/impacted earwax causing fullness, hearing reduction, or itching.
