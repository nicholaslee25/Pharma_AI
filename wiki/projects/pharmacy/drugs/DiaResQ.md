## Overview
**Real mismatched-category finding, same pattern as this database's homeopathic/supplement/device miscategorizations elsewhere.** DiaResQ is **not an FDA-regulated OTC drug at all** — no DailyMed Drug Facts label exists for it because it is classified as a "Food for Special Dietary Use," a regulatory category that does not require FDA approval before marketing. Its actual ingredients are **bovine (cow) colostrum, egg solids, silicon dioxide, vanilla flavor, and sucralose** — it contains real egg and milk allergens, a genuine allergen consideration not shared by loperamide (Imodium) or bismuth subsalicylate (Pepto-Bismol/Kaopectate), the other two active-ingredient mechanisms in this category. Unlike several other non-drug products flagged elsewhere in this database, colostrum-based diarrhea treatment has real, if mixed, supporting evidence: a broader colostrum evidence review found bovine colostrum supplementation improved diarrhea frequency/stool consistency in a majority of trial arms reviewed (though not duration), and specific hyperimmune-colostrum trials showed strong protection (76–91%) against traveler's-diarrhea-causing ETEC infection specifically — a materially better evidence position than most non-drug products in this database's "mismatched category" pattern.

- Real supporting evidence exists for colostrum in infectious diarrhea generally, and specifically strong (76–91% protection) trial results for hyperimmune bovine colostrum against traveler's-diarrhea-causing ETEC — better-evidenced than many non-drug products flagged elsewhere in this database
- Gluten-free per manufacturer

## Cons
- **Not an FDA-approved OTC drug — filed under "Antidiarrheals" (a drug category) despite being a Food for Special Dietary Use with no FDA premarket approval requirement.** A consumer assuming DiaResQ went through the same FDA OTC monograph review as Imodium or Pepto-Bismol would be mistaken
- **Contains real egg and milk (bovine colostrum) allergens** — a genuine, undisclosed-by-category-name allergen risk not shared by loperamide or bismuth-subsalicylate-based competitors in this same category, and a meaningful consideration for egg- or milk-allergic users
- The evidence reviewed for bovine colostrum is a mix of general infectious-diarrhea trials and specific traveler's-diarrhea/ETEC-prevention trials using hyperimmune colostrum preparations — this database did not confirm whether DiaResQ's own colostrum is hyperimmune (raised against specific pathogens) or standard bovine colostrum, a real product-specific gap given the evidence appears strongest for the hyperimmune variant specifically
- Manufacturer's own label states the product is not intended for chronic diarrhea or for heartburn/gas/upset-stomach relief from overeating — a real, meaningful use-case restriction not shared by Pepto-Bismol's broader multi-symptom coverage
- No DiaResQ-brand-specific RCT (as opposed to general bovine-colostrum literature) was independently located in this pass
