## Overview
AccuRelief (manufactured/marketed by Carex, also sold under the TENS 7000 product family) is an FDA-cleared, over-the-counter TENS device — no prescription required — sold in wireless remote-control and mobile-app-connected configurations. See [[tens-devices]] (built in this same research pass, since no TENS evidence page previously existed in this database) for the full device-category evidence context: Cochrane's Gibson 2019 overview of reviews found it could not confidently confirm TENS effectiveness for chronic pain versus sham treatment, across the device class generally. No AccuRelief-specific FDA recall or MAUDE adverse-event report was identified in this pass (contrast with OMRON TENS, where real Class 3/lowest-severity recall and MAUDE records were found — see `drugs/OMRON TENS.md`); absence of a located record here should be read as "not found in this search pass," not as a confirmed clean safety record.

## Pros
- FDA-cleared for OTC sale, consistent with the standard 510(k) pathway for this device class
- Wireless remote-control and mobile-app connectivity is a genuine usability feature distinguishing it from simpler wired TENS units, potentially easier for reaching hard-to-access body areas
- No AccuRelief-specific recall/adverse-event record was identified in this pass (though this is a "not found" result, not a verified clean record)

## Cons
- Per [[tens-devices]]'s Cochrane-level evidence, it is not possible to confidently state that TENS (as a device class, AccuRelief included) is effective for chronic pain relief beyond sham/placebo
- 510(k) clearance does not require clinical efficacy data — the same regulatory-evidence gap documented for OMRON and the device category generally applies here
- No brand-specific (AccuRelief-vs-competitor) outcome RCT was identified in this pass
- Second-largest but substantially smaller share (22%) than OMRON's dominant 78% in this database's category data — market-share difference reflects brand recognition, not a demonstrated efficacy difference between the two (per the shared device-category evidence gap)

## Use Cases
Temporary relief of minor muscle and joint pain via wireless-controlled electrical nerve stimulation — a non-drug pain-management modality, marketed with an emphasis on ease of use for hard-to-reach areas.

## Allergies / Warnings
Standard TENS device contraindications per [[tens-devices]]: not for use over the front/sides of the neck, over the eyes, transcerebrally, directly on the spine, on broken/irritated skin, or by pacemaker/implanted-device users without physician guidance; not for undiagnosed pain of unknown cause; not for use while driving, operating machinery, or bathing.

## Symptoms Treated
Minor muscle and joint pain, per the device's general indication — not a treatment for the underlying cause of pain.
