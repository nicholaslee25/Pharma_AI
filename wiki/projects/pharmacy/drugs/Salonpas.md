## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed: Salonpas spans at least **five distinct formulations** — but the flagship **Salonpas Pain Relief Patch / Arthritis Pain Patch** (menthol 3% + methyl salicylate 10%) stands out as genuinely exceptional within this database: it received full **FDA New Drug Application (NDA) approval in February 2008**, per Hisamitsu's own claim the first OTC topical analgesic patch approved this way rather than under the OTC monograph system that governs virtually every other product in this database, including every other Thermal Relief Products competitor. Its approval was backed by a real pivotal RCT — **Higashi et al. 2010 (PMID 20171409, Clinical Therapeutics), N=208, randomized/double-blind/placebo-controlled** — reporting (per the manufacturer's own summary of the peer-reviewed results) 40% additional pain relief vs. placebo and 58% good-to-excellent patient satisfaction. This is a case where the ingredient-class evidence (documented on the new [[topical-counterirritants]] page) IS the brand-specific evidence, not a generic extrapolation. However, Salonpas's OTHER SKUs are NOT covered by this same trial: the Pain Relieving Jet spray shares the same 3%/10% concentrations but wasn't itself tested; the Deep Relieving gel uses higher concentrations (menthol 10%, methyl salicylate 15%) exceeding the FDA's 2012 burn-risk thresholds; the plain "Salonpas" patch adds camphor as a third ingredient; and "Salonpas Pain Relieving HOT" replaces methyl salicylate entirely with capsaicin (paired with menthol) — a mechanistically different, NSAID-risk-free formulation.

## Pros
- The Pain Relief Patch/Arthritis Pain Patch SKU carries genuinely superior regulatory status (FDA NDA approval) and dedicated brand-specific RCT evidence (N=208) — a real differentiator versus every other brand in this category
- Offers a capsaicin-based, methyl-salicylate-free alternative ("Pain Relieving HOT") for users who want to avoid the NSAID-class systemic risks of methyl salicylate
- Broad format range (patch, spray, gel) for different application preferences

## Cons
- **The NDA approval and pivotal RCT apply specifically to the 3%/10% Pain Relief Patch formulation — they do NOT automatically extend to Salonpas's other SKUs**, particularly the higher-concentration Deep Relieving gel (10%/15%, exceeding FDA burn-risk thresholds) or the three-ingredient plain "Salonpas" patch. A consumer should not assume "Salonpas" as a brand name carries the same evidence/regulatory weight across every product.
- Methyl salicylate-containing SKUs carry the full NSAID-class risk profile documented on [[topical-counterirritants]]: stomach-bleeding risk factors, aspirin/NSAID-allergy contraindication, and an explicit warning against use at 20+ weeks of pregnancy without a doctor's direction
- Patch SKU restricted to adults 18+ (not 12+ like several competitors), a stricter age threshold worth noting
- Maximum 3 consecutive days of patch use per label — a real duration limit not present on every competitor's label

## Use Cases
Temporary relief of mild-to-moderate aches and pains from strains, sprains, simple backache, arthritis, and bruises (methyl-salicylate SKUs); similar relief via a different, capsaicin-based mechanism (Pain Relieving HOT).

## Allergies / Warnings
Per Pain Relief Patch label: stomach bleeding risk (NSAID) — age 60+, ulcer/bleeding history, blood thinner/steroid/other-NSAID users; do not use on face/rashes/wounds/damaged skin; aspirin/NSAID allergy contraindication; do not use with heating pads or while sweating; do not use from pouches opened 14+ days; do not use before/after heart surgery; stop use if stomach pain worsens, bloody vomit/stools occur, rash develops, or symptoms persist beyond 3 days. **Do not use methyl salicylate at 20 weeks or later in pregnancy unless a doctor directs otherwise.** One patch at a time, max 2 daily, adults 18+ only.

## Symptoms Treated
Mild-to-moderate musculoskeletal aches and pains (strains, sprains, backache, arthritis, bruises) via counterirritant (methyl salicylate/menthol SKUs) or capsaicin-based mechanisms depending on the specific product.
