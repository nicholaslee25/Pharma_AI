## Overview
The pediatric formulation/labeling of Flonase Allergy Relief (fluticasone propionate), already documented on [[intranasal-corticosteroids]]. Per that page's confirmed dosing table: children 4-11 years, 1 spray/nostril once daily, adult-supervised, with **growth monitoring specifically recommended if used more than 2 months per year** — a real, quantified pediatric safety consideration distinct from the adult dosing regimen. Not for use in children under 4.

## Pros
- Backed by [[intranasal-corticosteroids]]'s strong evidence base (Sousa-Pinto et al. 2024, 151-study meta-analysis) — intranasal corticosteroids are documented as superior to antihistamines specifically at reducing nasal inflammation and relieving sneezing/rhinorrhea/nasal blockage
- Same active ingredient and general safety profile as the well-documented adult Flonase product, giving this pediatric use a substantial existing evidence base to draw on

## Cons
- **The pediatric growth-rate monitoring warning is a real, specific safety consideration not shared by the oral antihistamine competitors in this Children's Allergy category** — per [[intranasal-corticosteroids]]'s label data, growth monitoring is specifically recommended if this product is used beyond 2 months/year in children ages 4-11
- Not for use under age 4, a more restrictive age floor than several oral antihistamine competitors in this category (e.g., Children's Xyzal down to 6 months)
- Delayed onset relative to oral antihistamines or intranasal antihistamine sprays (Children's Astepro) — per [[intranasal-corticosteroids]]'s own note that peak effect may take multiple days to develop, a real practical consideration for a caregiver expecting same-day relief
- Smallest-but-one share (2%) of the researched Children's Allergy brands in this database, possibly reflecting caregiver/pharmacist preference for the faster-acting oral options in this age group

## Use Cases
Nasal congestion, itchy nose, runny nose, itchy/watery eyes, and sneezing relief in children ages 4-11, via daily corticosteroid nasal spray requiring several days for full effect.

## Allergies / Warnings
Per [[intranasal-corticosteroids]]'s confirmed label data: not for use under age 4; growth-rate monitoring recommended if used beyond 2 months/year in children 4-11; do not use to treat asthma; ask a doctor before use with glaucoma/cataract history; stop use and see a doctor for chickenpox/measles/tuberculosis exposure, symptoms not improving within 7 days, nasal whistling, allergic reaction, vision changes, or severe/frequent nosebleeds.
