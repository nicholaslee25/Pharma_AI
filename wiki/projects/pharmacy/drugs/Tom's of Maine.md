## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed and manufacturer sourcing: "Tom's of Maine" spans both drug and cosmetic classifications depending on SKU. **Whole Care Mouthwash** (sodium fluoride 0.02%, 0.01% w/v fluoride ion — anticavity drug, per [[fluoride-toothpaste]]'s mechanism) and the **Children's Anticavity Fluoride Rinse** (sodium fluoride 0.04%, the plausible driver of the Children's Mouthwashes 11% share, technically in the Pediatrics category which this database has not yet systematically researched but is documented here given how directly it relates to this brand's other confirmed SKUs) are both genuine fluoride drug products. By contrast, the **"Wicked Fresh!" Fluoride-Free Natural Mouthwash** — explicitly marketed as fluoride-free and using zinc to neutralize odor-causing bacteria — is most plausibly the SKU driving the Cosmetic Mouthwashes/Oral Rinses 6% share, and appears to be a cosmetic (breath-freshening-claim-only) product rather than an anticavity drug. The Toothpaste SKUs (General Use, Sensitive Gums/Teeth) are separately confirmed fluoride-based per [[fluoride-toothpaste]]'s existing brand list.

## Pros
- Offers both a fluoride (drug, anticavity-proven per [[fluoride-toothpaste]]'s strong Cochrane evidence) and a fluoride-free (cosmetic, "natural"-positioned) option under one recognizable brand — genuine consumer choice for those who specifically want to avoid or specifically want fluoride
- Long-standing "natural ingredients" brand positioning (B-Corp certified, avoids artificial flavors/colors/preservatives in at least the Wicked Fresh line) may appeal to a specific consumer segment
- The fluoride-containing SKUs carry the same strong, well-established anticaries evidence base documented on [[fluoride-toothpaste]]

## Cons
- The "Wicked Fresh!" fluoride-free mouthwash provides no anticavity benefit — a consumer choosing it under the general "Tom's of Maine" brand umbrella for cavity protection would not get fluoride's protective effect, unlike the brand's own toothpaste and Whole Care mouthwash lines
- No brand-specific (as opposed to general fluoride ingredient-class) clinical trial evidence was identified for any Tom's of Maine SKU specifically
- "Tom's of Maine" spans drug (fluoride) and cosmetic (fluoride-free, zinc-based) classifications across its four category appearances in this database — a consumer should check the specific product label rather than assume fluoride content from the brand name alone
- The zinc-based "natural" fluoride-free mechanism's real-world breath-freshening efficacy was not independently verified against a located clinical trial in this pass

## Use Cases
Cavity prevention (fluoride toothpaste and Whole Care/Children's mouthwash SKUs); general breath freshening without fluoride (Wicked Fresh! mouthwash, cosmetic, zinc-based odor neutralization); tooth sensitivity is NOT a labeled use for any confirmed Tom's of Maine SKU in this pass (its Sensitive Gums/Teeth toothpaste category share was not independently confirmed to contain potassium nitrate — see [[potassium-nitrate]]'s existing "plausibly... not individually confirmed" note).

## Allergies / Warnings
Fluoride SKUs: standard fluorosis-risk precautions per [[fluoride-toothpaste]] (supervise children under 6, use age-appropriate amounts, avoid swallowing). Wicked Fresh! (fluoride-free): no FDA drug warnings identified, consistent with cosmetic classification; standard alcohol-free-rinse general use.
