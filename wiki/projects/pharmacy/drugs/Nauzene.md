## Overview
**Resolves the formulation split flagged on [[Nauzene Kids]].** Confirmed via DailyMed: adult Nauzene (chewable tablet and syrup formulations) is **sodium citrate** — a mild alkalinizing/antacid-class agent, genuinely different from Nauzene Kids' confirmed homeopathic lactic-acid/wild-hops formulation (see [[Nauzene Kids]] and [[dimenhydrinate]]'s cross-reference). This is a real, brand-internal formulation split by age group: the adult product uses a conventional (if minimally-evidenced for nausea specifically) buffering agent, while the children's product is homeopathic — two entirely different regulatory/evidence categories under one family brand name. No dedicated sodium-citrate ingredient page exists in this database; the modest brand share here does not appear to warrant a full new ingredient page per this database's own threshold (reserved for whole-category gaps), so the finding is documented directly on this brand page instead.

## Pros
- Sodium citrate has an established, understood alkalinizing/antacid mechanism (neutralizes gastric acid, raising stomach pH) — a conventional pharmacological basis, unlike Nauzene Kids' homeopathic mechanism
- Chewable tablet and syrup formats offer flexibility

## Cons
- **No sodium-citrate-specific clinical evidence for the nausea indication specifically was located in this pass** — sodium citrate's antacid mechanism is well-established for acid-related symptoms, but this database did not locate dedicated trial evidence supporting its use for nausea/upset-stomach relief as labeled by this brand, a real evidence gap
- **Real sodium-content safety consideration, not previously flagged for this brand**: each adult chewable tablet contains 60mg sodium, and general sodium-citrate safety literature warns of caution in cardiac failure, hypertension, impaired renal function, and edema — relevant given the labeled adult dose of up to 24 tablets/24h (a potentially substantial cumulative sodium load at the label maximum, though this database did not independently calculate the exact total against the safety thresholds established elsewhere for Zegerid OTC/Alka-Seltzer in this same research pass)
- Label explicitly states the product is not a substitute for a balanced nutritional diet or electrolyte replenishment — a real, specific caution against over-reliance

## Use Cases
Relief of nausea associated with upset stomach, including from overindulgence in food or drink — via sodium citrate's alkalinizing/antacid mechanism, a use case with less specific supporting evidence than the underlying antacid mechanism itself would suggest.

## Allergies / Warnings
Per label: adults, 2-4 tablets (or 1-2 tablespoons syrup), may repeat after 15-30 minutes, not exceeding label maximums (24 tablets or 6 syrup doses/24h) without doctor guidance. Caution suggested for patients with cardiac failure, hypertension, impaired renal function, or on sodium-restricted diets, per general sodium-citrate safety literature reviewed in this pass — not itemized on the product's own consumer-facing label text located in this pass.
