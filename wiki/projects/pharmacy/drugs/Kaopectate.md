## Overview
**Real "identical formulation under a different brand name" finding.** Confirmed via DailyMed: modern Kaopectate is **bismuth subsalicylate** — the exact same active ingredient as Pepto-Bismol, at a matching 525mg/30mL liquid concentration, plus a chewable-tablet form. This is NOT the ingredient the brand's own name implies: Kaopectate's original 1936 formula used kaolin (clay) + pectin, later switched to attapulgite clay in the 1980s, then was reformulated entirely to bismuth subsalicylate in 2004 after a Proposition 65 lawsuit found high lead levels in the attapulgite clay and an FDA 2003 ruling found attapulgite lacked sufficient efficacy evidence. The brand name "Kaopectate" (from "kaolin" + "pectin") has been chemically inaccurate for the actual US product since 2004, and this database's [[bismuth-subsalicylate]] page did not previously document this brand-history/reformulation detail or the fact that Kaopectate and Pepto-Bismol now share an identical active ingredient.

## Pros
- Confirmed FDA label; genuinely effective bismuth-subsalicylate mechanism, per [[bismuth-subsalicylate]]'s evidence base — an improvement in evidence quality over the pre-2004 attapulgite formula, which the FDA itself found lacked sufficient effectiveness evidence
- Removes the lead-contamination concern that prompted the 2004 reformulation lawsuit/settlement in the first place
- Available in liquid (multiple flavors) and chewable tablet formats

## Cons
- **The brand name itself is now a real, chemically inaccurate legacy artifact** — "Kaopectate" implies kaolin/pectin content that has not been present in the US product since 2004; a consumer or caregiver relying on brand-name memory (e.g., recalling it as a "clay-based, gentler" option distinct from Pepto-Bismol) would be factually mistaken about what they are actually taking
- Shares 100% of bismuth subsalicylate's real risk profile with Pepto-Bismol: Reye syndrome risk in children/teens with viral illness (salicylate-class), salicylate-toxicity risk with excessive dosing, rare bismuth neurotoxicity with prolonged overuse, temporary (harmless) black-tongue/black-stool discoloration — none of which applied to the pre-2004 kaolin/pectin or attapulgite formulas
- A consumer alternating between Kaopectate and Pepto-Bismol for different symptoms, unaware they are now the same active ingredient, risks unknowingly double-dosing salicylate — a real, brand-specific safety consideration this database has not previously documented for this ingredient
- No Kaopectate-brand-specific RCT beyond the general bismuth-subsalicylate evidence base was independently located in this pass

## Use Cases
Relief of traveler's diarrhea, diarrhea, and upset stomach from food/drink overindulgence — identical use case and mechanism to Pepto-Bismol, per [[bismuth-subsalicylate]].

## Allergies / Warnings
Per label: children/teenagers with or recovering from chickenpox or flu-like symptoms should not use this product (Reye syndrome risk); contains salicylate (allergy alert); if behavior changes with nausea/vomiting occur during use, consult a doctor (possible early Reye syndrome sign). Pregnant/breastfeeding: ask a health professional. Same salicylate-toxicity and bismuth-toxicity considerations as [[bismuth-subsalicylate]] generally.

## Symptoms Treated
Diarrhea, traveler's diarrhea, upset stomach from overindulgence — via the same bismuth-subsalicylate mechanism as Pepto-Bismol, per [[bismuth-subsalicylate]].

## Brand-Specific Alignment Check
**Real finding, added to both this page and [[bismuth-subsalicylate]]:** Kaopectate and Pepto-Bismol are confirmed to share an identical active ingredient (bismuth subsalicylate) since Kaopectate's 2004 reformulation, despite Kaopectate's name/brand identity implying a completely different (kaolin/pectin-based) mechanism. This is a genuine instance of this database's "identical formulations under multiple brand names" pattern, with the added twist that the brand name itself is now historically/chemically inaccurate.
