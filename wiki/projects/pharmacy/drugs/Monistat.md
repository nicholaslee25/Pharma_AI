## Appears In
Yeast Infection Prevention and Relief - 49%

## Overview
Monistat is confirmed via DailyMed to be a miconazole nitrate vaginal antifungal line spanning three genuinely different dose/duration formulations under one brand name — see [[miconazole-nitrate-topical]]'s Vaginal Indication section for full detail: **Monistat 1** (1200mg vaginal insert, single dose, plus 2% external cream for itch), **Monistat 3** (4% vaginal cream, 3-day course), and **Monistat 7** (2% vaginal cream, 7-day course). A real, worth-noting label-vs-expectation tension: even the "1-day" Monistat 1 product's own FDA label states most women get only SOME improvement within 1 day, with complete relief potentially taking up to 7 days — meaning the "1" in the product name refers to treatment duration, not time-to-complete-relief, a distinction a consumer skimming the product name alone could reasonably miss.

## Pros
- Miconazole nitrate has real, established azole-antifungal efficacy for uncomplicated vulvovaginal candidiasis, the category-standard first-line mechanism (see [[miconazole-nitrate-topical]])
- Three duration/dose options let users match treatment length to personal preference/adherence considerations
- Category-leading share (49%) reflects the largest real-world use base among genuinely antifungal (non-homeopathic) products in this category — a meaningful contrast to [[AZO]]'s homeopathic "Yeast Plus" product also tracked in this same category

## Cons
- **Real labeling-expectation gap:** Monistat 1's own label anticipates up to 7 days for complete symptom relief despite the "1" naming implying a single-day resolution — worth a user's awareness before assuming the shortest-duration product delivers the fastest complete relief
- General azole-antifungal cautions apply (per [[miconazole-nitrate-topical]]): external use only for the topical/skin indication; the vaginal-route Cochrane-level efficacy data specific to miconazole (as opposed to the topical-skin tinea evidence already documented on that page) was not independently pulled in this pass — a real evidence-completeness gap distinct from the drug's well-established general antifungal-class efficacy
- Latex condom/diaphragm compatibility concerns are common to vaginal antifungal cream/ointment bases generally (oil-based components can weaken latex) — not independently re-confirmed against Monistat's specific product base in this pass, but a standard class-wide caution worth flagging

## Use Cases
Treatment of vaginal yeast infections (vulvovaginal candidiasis) — itching, burning, irritation, discharge.

## Allergies / Warnings
Azole hypersensitivity is a contraindication. Consult a doctor if this is the first suspected yeast infection (to confirm diagnosis), if pregnant, or if symptoms don't improve within the labeled treatment window.

## Symptoms Treated
Vaginal yeast infection: itching, burning, irritation, abnormal discharge.

## Brand-Specific Alignment Check
Real finding, not a full discrepancy: the "1/3/7" naming convention describes treatment DURATION, not time-to-complete-symptom-relief — even Monistat 1's own label anticipates up to a week for full relief. Flagged for user awareness rather than as a marketing-accuracy violation, since the label itself discloses this.
