## Appears In

## Overview
**Resolves an existing hedge on [[dimenhydrinate]]** (which previously noted Nauzene Kids' formulation was "not confirmed as dimenhydrinate-based"): confirmed via DailyMed, Nauzene Kids = **homeopathic Lactic Acid 3X (Lacticum Acidum) 20mg + Wild Hops 3X (Bryonia Alba) 20mg** per 15mL — a genuinely homeopathic-dilution product, NOT dimenhydrinate, NOT an antihistamine of any kind, and mechanistically unrelated to the category-leading Dramamine for Kids. Ages 2+. Note also: the base adult "Nauzene" brand (per `drugs/Nauzene.md`, not yet independently researched in this database) appears to span at least two different formulations of its own per DailyMed — "Nauzene Homeopathic" (lactic acid + wild hops, same as this Kids product) and a separate "Nauzene" sodium citrate chewable tablet — another instance of the one-brand-multiple-formulations pattern recurring throughout this database, flagged here for whoever researches the adult Nauzene brand page next. **Resolved 2026-08-27:** adult Nauzene is confirmed sodium citrate (not homeopathic) — a real, brand-internal age-based formulation split. See `drugs/Nauzene.md`.

## Pros
- No antihistamine/anticholinergic side-effect profile (sedation, dry mouth, paradoxical excitation) unlike Dramamine for Kids' dimenhydrinate — a real mechanism-driven safety differentiator for children where sedation is undesired
- No serious adverse events reported for general homeopathic products at these dilution levels per the class-wide evidence reviewed on [[homeopathic-cold-products]] (a cold/flu-focused page, but its general homeopathic-dilution safety framing is not indication-specific)
- Free of artificial flavors/colors and gluten per label

## Cons
- **No convincing mechanism-of-action or efficacy evidence located for lactic acid or wild hops (Bryonia alba) at 3X dilution specifically for motion sickness** — no dedicated ingredient page exists in this database for either constituent, and no product-specific RCT was located. This database's only comparable homeopathic evidence review ([[homeopathic-cold-products]]) is scoped to cold/flu products, not nausea/motion-sickness homeopathic products specifically — a genuine evidence-category gap, not a direct transferable finding
- **This product's mechanism is entirely unrelated to actual motion-sickness pathophysiology** (vestibular/H1-receptor-mediated, per [[dimenhydrinate]]'s mechanism section) — a caregiver assuming "Nauzene Kids" and "Dramamine for Kids" work via comparable mechanisms because they're filed in the same Pharmacy Times category would be incorrect
- 11% recommendation share for a product with no located efficacy evidence specific to its actual ingredients — this database's standing "recommendation share ≠ efficacy" rule applies with particular force here
- The adult "Nauzene" brand's own formulation split (homeopathic liquid vs. sodium citrate tablet) is now confirmed — see `drugs/Nauzene.md`
