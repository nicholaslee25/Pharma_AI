## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed: Children's Benadryl Allergy is **diphenhydramine HCl 12.5mg/5mL** oral solution — a **first-generation** antihistamine, mechanistically and safety-profile distinct from every other researched brand in this Children's Allergy category (Claritin, Zyrtec, Allegra, Xyzal are all second-generation, per [[second-generation-antihistamines]]'s explicit note that Benadryl does NOT belong to that ingredient class despite sharing the category table). This is diphenhydramine used for its ORIGINAL FDA-approved antihistamine/allergy indication, distinct from the off-label-by-convention sleep-aid use documented on the newly built [[oral-diphenhydramine-sleep-aid]] and the topical/itch-relief use on [[topical-diphenhydramine]] — same molecule, three different documented use contexts across this database.

## Pros
- Genuine, FDA-approved antihistamine indication (this is diphenhydramine's original, on-label use, unlike the sleep-aid application) — a well-established, decades-old allergy treatment
- Rapid onset relative to some second-generation agents (general first-generation antihistamine pharmacology — crosses the blood-brain barrier readily, contributing to both faster perceived onset and greater sedation)
- Provides an alternative mechanism for a child who hasn't responded well to a second-generation antihistamine, though switching should be discussed with a pediatrician given the sedation tradeoff below

## Cons
- **Significant sedation risk, a real tradeoff versus every second-generation competitor in this category:** diphenhydramine crosses the blood-brain barrier substantially more than cetirizine/loratadine/fexofenadine/levocetirizine, per [[second-generation-antihistamines]]'s own mechanism section describing second-generation agents as developed specifically to minimize this effect
- **Real FDA-relevant pediatric guidance found in this pass: for children under 6 years, do not give products with more than one active ingredient** (a 2008 FDA-era recommendation applicable to combination products, not Children's Benadryl's single-ingredient formulation specifically, but relevant context for a caregiver considering ANY pediatric allergy/cold product choice — see the fuller 2008 FDA/CHPA voluntary labeling history documented on `drugs/Children's Dimetapp Cold and Allergy.md`)
- Not for use under age 1 (causes sleepiness in most babies, per general pediatric dosing guidance) — a real age restriction not shared by all second-generation competitors in this category
- Anticholinergic side-effect profile (dry mouth, urinary retention, etc.) shared with diphenhydramine's other documented uses in this database
- Smallest-but-one share (9%) of the researched Children's Allergy brands in this database, likely reflecting the sedation tradeoff versus the dominant second-generation options

## Use Cases
Allergy symptom relief (sneezing, runny nose, itchy/watery eyes) in children ages 1+ (formal OTC labeling; some sources cite different specific age cutoffs).

## Allergies / Warnings
Per label conventions and general pediatric dosing guidance: not for use under age 1; use only the enclosed dosing cup; do not give more than 6 doses in 24 hours; drowsiness is a common, expected effect (unlike the second-generation competitors in this category); consult a doctor before combining with any other diphenhydramine-containing or sedating product.

## Symptoms Treated
Sneezing, runny nose, itchy/watery eyes due to allergies (and itching from other causes, per diphenhydramine's broader antihistamine indication).
