## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed: FiberCon is calcium polycarbophil (625mg caplet, equivalent to 500mg polycarbophil) — matching [[fiber]]'s existing note that this is a DIFFERENT fiber type than psyllium/pectin, the only two types shown significantly effective in that page's cited 2022 meta-analysis. **Real marketing-vs-evidence tension:** the manufacturer's own marketing states the product "won't cause gas or bloating and is as effective as fiber powders" — a direct claim of equivalence to psyllium-based competitors that this database's cited meta-analysis does not substantiate, since that review found significant benefit specifically for psyllium and pectin, not calcium polycarbophil.

## Pros
- Confirmed FDA OTC label; caplet format (vs. powder) may suit users who dislike mixing fiber powders
- Manufacturer's "won't cause gas or bloating" claim, if accurate, would be a genuine differentiator from [[fiber]]'s own cited finding that flatulence was significantly higher with fiber supplementation generally — though this claim was not independently verified against a comparative trial in this pass

## Cons
- **Not psyllium or pectin** — per [[fiber]]'s own cited meta-analysis, calcium polycarbophil was not one of the two fiber types shown to produce significant treatment-response improvement for chronic constipation
- **Manufacturer's "as effective as fiber powders" claim is not substantiated by this database's cited meta-analysis evidence**, which found significant benefit specifically for psyllium/pectin at doses >10g/day — a real marketing-claim-vs.-evidence-base tension worth flagging explicitly rather than accepting the manufacturer's equivalence claim at face value
- Contains calcium (as calcium polycarbophil) — while polycarbophil itself is minimally systemically absorbed, a consumer also taking calcium-carbonate antacids (e.g. Tums) or calcium supplements should be aware of the calcium content when tallying total daily calcium intake, a consideration this database has not previously connected across its Antacids and Bulk-Fiber categories
- No FiberCon-brand-specific RCT beyond general calcium-polycarbophil literature was independently located in this pass to substantiate the manufacturer's specific efficacy claims

## Use Cases
Relief of constipation, per label — marketed specifically as a gas/bloating-free alternative to fiber powders.

## Allergies / Warnings
Per label: mix with at least 8oz water/fluid to avoid choking; max 8 caplets/24h unless directed by a doctor. Contains calcium — relevant to total daily calcium intake tracking alongside calcium-based antacids/supplements.

## Symptoms Treated
Occasional constipation — via calcium polycarbophil's soluble-fiber mechanism, a different specific fiber type than Metamucil's psyllium, per [[fiber]].

## Brand-Specific Alignment Check
**New finding, added to both this page and [[fiber]]:** FiberCon's manufacturer marketing claims equivalence to "fiber powders" (implicitly including psyllium-based products like Metamucil) in effectiveness, but this database's cited meta-analysis evidence does not support that specific equivalence claim — flagged explicitly as a marketing-vs.-evidence discrepancy rather than silently accepted.
