## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed: FeverAll is **acetaminophen delivered via RECTAL SUPPOSITORY** — a genuinely different route of administration from every other brand in this category (all oral suspensions/vials/chewables). Three strengths by age: **Infants (80mg)**, **Children's (120mg)**, and **Jr. Strength (325mg)**. This route is clinically meaningful, not just a format preference: a rectal suppository can deliver antipyretic/analgesic treatment to a child who is vomiting, refusing oral medication, or otherwise unable to safely take an oral dose — a real, distinct clinical use case not served by any of this category's other (all-oral) brands.

## Pros
- Genuinely distinct and clinically valuable route of administration — the only rectal option among this database's researched Children's Analgesics brands, useful specifically when oral dosing is not feasible (vomiting, refusal, certain medical conditions)
- Same well-established acetaminophen active ingredient and evidence base as the oral products, per [[acetaminophen]]
- Age-tiered strength system (80mg/120mg/325mg) mirrors the oral products' age-based dosing logic, adapted to suppository form

## Cons
- Rectal absorption pharmacokinetics differ from oral absorption (generally slower and less predictable bioavailability for acetaminophen via the rectal route per general pharmacology) — this database did not independently verify a rectal-specific efficacy/bioavailability study for FeverAll, so this is noted as a general pharmacologic consideration rather than a confirmed brand-specific finding
- Same hepatotoxicity/overdose risk as oral acetaminophen applies — the route changes absorption kinetics, not the fundamental drug risk. Per label: severe liver damage may occur if more than 4 doses/24h are given for ages 6-11 months, more than 5 doses/24h for ages 12-36 months (Infants strength), or more than 5 doses/24h for Children's strength
- A caregiver combining a FeverAll suppository with an oral acetaminophen product (for the same or a different reason) faces the same combination-product-overlap overdose risk documented on [[acetaminophen]] — arguably a higher risk of caregiver oversight here, since the two routes don't "look like" the same drug to someone unfamiliar with the ingredient
- Smallest share (1%) of the researched Children's Analgesics brands in this database, consistent with the rectal route being a specific-circumstance option rather than a general first-line choice

## Use Cases
Fever/pain reduction in infants and children who cannot or will not take oral medication (vomiting, refusal, certain medical situations) — a genuinely different use case than the oral products dominating this category.

## Allergies / Warnings
Per label: severe liver damage may occur if the age-specific maximum dose frequency is exceeded (varies by strength/age, see Cons above) or combined with other acetaminophen-containing products; contact Poison Control immediately in case of overdose.
