## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed: "Clearasil" spans **both major OTC acne mechanisms**, sometimes within a single kit — the same benzoyl-peroxide/salicylic-acid dual-mechanism pattern already documented on [[Cetaphil]] and [[AcneFree]] in this pass. Specific SKUs confirmed: Rapid Rescue Spot Treatment (benzoyl peroxide), Stubborn Acne Control 5in1 Spot Treatment (benzoyl peroxide), Rapid Rescue Deep Treatment Wash (salicylic acid), and — genuinely notable — Ultra Rapid Action Clear Skin / Ultra Acne System, **combination kits containing BOTH salicylic acid and benzoyl peroxide as co-packaged but separately-applied products**. Historically, Clearasil has also marketed sulfur/resorcinol and triclosan-based formulations per third-party summaries, though this pass did not independently confirm those on a current DailyMed label — flagged as unconfirmed rather than asserted.

## Pros
- Confirmed BPO and salicylic-acid concentrations fall within FDA-monograph OTC ranges, matching [[benzoyl-peroxide]] and [[salicylic-acid]]
- The combination kits (BPO + salicylic acid as separate co-packaged steps, not mixed in one product) allow a dual-mechanism regimen — bactericidal/sebostatic action from BPO plus keratolytic exfoliation from salicylic acid — a genuinely rational pairing reflected in dermatology practice, without needing two separately purchased products
- Multiple format options (spot treatment, wash, full system) let a consumer target either isolated blemishes or broader breakouts

## Cons
- **Brand name spans two (and per unconfirmed historical/third-party sources, possibly more) unrelated acne mechanisms** — a consumer buying "Clearasil" without checking the specific product name (Rapid Rescue Spot Treatment vs. Rapid Rescue Deep Treatment Wash, for example) could get a fundamentally different active ingredient
- Per [[benzoyl-peroxide]]'s own Cochrane evidence, withdrawal due to adverse effects is significantly higher with BPO vs. placebo (RR 2.13) — a real tolerability consideration for the BPO-containing SKUs specifically, not the salicylic-acid-only ones
- Per [[salicylic-acid]]'s own Evidence Grade note, this database's salicylic-acid evidence is strongest for wart removal and comparatively thinner/less specifically documented for acne — a caveat that applies to Clearasil's salicylic-acid SKUs as much as to any other brand's
- Combination-kit products used simultaneously (rather than the intended alternating/separate-step regimen) risk compounding irritation from two independently irritating actives — a real, if manageable, risk if label directions aren't followed
- No Clearasil-brand-specific RCT beyond the general benzoyl-peroxide/salicylic-acid evidence base was independently located in this pass; historical sulfur/resorcinol/triclosan formulation claims from third-party summaries were not independently confirmed against a current label

## Use Cases
Treatment of mild-to-moderate acne vulgaris via consumer's or kit's choice of benzoyl peroxide (bactericidal/sebostatic), salicylic acid (keratolytic), or both in combination.

## Allergies / Warnings
Per [[benzoyl-peroxide]]: external use only, avoid eyes/lips/mouth, may bleach fabric/hair, discontinue if excessive irritation. Per [[salicylic-acid]]: avoid eyes/mucous membranes, do not use with other topical acne medications without doctor's advice. Combination-kit products: follow labeled sequencing/timing to reduce compounded irritation risk.

## Symptoms Treated
Acne vulgaris — via benzoyl peroxide, salicylic acid, or both mechanisms depending on the specific Clearasil SKU selected.
