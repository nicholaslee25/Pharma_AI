## Appears In

## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed: CeraVe Baby Healing Ointment is **petrolatum 41%** — matching Aquaphor Baby's exact confirmed concentration (also 41%) elsewhere in this category, and correcting [[zinc-oxide]]'s earlier general assumption that this brand was "plausibly zinc-oxide-based by real-world market identity." Correctly labeled to help treat/prevent diaper rash, developed with pediatric dermatologist input per manufacturer marketing. Formulated with ceramides, hyaluronic acid, and vitamin E as supporting (non-active) ingredients, and is explicitly **fragrance-free and lanolin-free** — a genuine positive contrast with the multiple lanolin-containing competitors (A&D, A+D Original Ointment, Calmoseptine, Boogie Diaper Rash Spray, Resinol) already flagged in this same category.

## Pros
- Confirmed FDA Drug Facts label with a correctly-matched, diaper-rash-specific indication
- **Explicitly lanolin-free and fragrance-free** — avoids the recurring lanolin-contact-allergy risk flagged for at least five other brands in this category, and avoids the fragrance-related sensitization concerns raised for the essential-oil-heavy "natural" competitors (Burt's Bees Baby, Maty's, Zarbee's Naturals)
- No tree-nut, soy, or other major-allergen ingredients identified in the ingredient list reviewed in this pass — a comparatively clean allergen profile relative to Burt's Bees Baby Diaper Rash Ointment's confirmed almond-oil/soybean-oil findings
- Petrolatum's occlusive barrier mechanism has real supporting RCT evidence for uncomplicated wound care (Smack et al. 1996, on [[petrolatum]])
- Same 41% petrolatum concentration as Aquaphor Baby, allowing a caregiver to reasonably compare the two products' core active-ingredient strength directly

## Cons
- Per [[zinc-oxide]]'s Alkhamis et al. 2026 systematic review: petroleum jelly/Vaseline specifically showed NO efficacy for reducing diaper-rash severity/incidence in at least one included study — the same general caveat already noted for Aquaphor Baby's petrolatum-based mechanism applies here too
- Zinc oxide's barrier-plus-mild-astringent mechanism (used by most of this category's other brands) is mechanistically distinct from this product's purely occlusive petrolatum approach; comparative efficacy was not independently confirmed in this pass
- No CeraVe-Baby-specific RCT for diaper rash was independently located in this pass
- Lowest recommendation share (2%) among the 12 ranked brands in this category, though this reflects pharmacist recommendation patterns rather than any confirmed efficacy shortfall

## Use Cases
Diaper rash prevention/treatment via occlusive moisture-barrier protection, marketed for extra-dry/cracked infant skin specifically.

## Allergies / Warnings
Per label: for external use only; discontinue and consult a doctor if condition worsens or does not improve. Fragrance-free, lanolin-free, paraben-free, dye-free, phthalate-free, and sulfate-free per manufacturer labeling — a comparatively conservative allergen/irritant profile relative to several other brands in this category.

## Symptoms Treated
Diaper rash / irritant diaper dermatitis, extra-dry/cracked infant skin — via petrolatum's occlusive moisture-barrier mechanism, per [[petrolatum]].
