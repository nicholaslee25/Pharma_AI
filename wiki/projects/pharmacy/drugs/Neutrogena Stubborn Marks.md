## Appears In

## Overview
Confirmed via product-ingredient sources: **Neutrogena Stubborn Marks PM Treatment** (part of the Rapid Tone Repair-adjacent line, marketed to "help reverse the look of post-acne marks & uneven skin tone") lists **retinol** as its key active ingredient, alongside sodium hyaluronate and ascorbic acid (vitamin C) in a silicone/dimethicone-based vehicle. **No FDA Drug Facts panel was found for this product** — retinol, unlike adapalene ([[adapalene]]) or tretinoin, is not an FDA OTC-monograph drug active; it is sold as a cosmetic anti-aging/skin-tone ingredient. This is distinct from the separate, genuinely FDA-regulated **Neutrogena Stubborn Texture/Body Acne** line (salicylic acid 1-2%, confirmed on DailyMed) — meaning "Neutrogena Stubborn ___" spans both a cosmetic sub-line (Marks, retinol) and genuine OTC drug sub-lines (Texture/Body Acne, salicylic acid) under closely similar naming.

No independent Neutrogena Stubborn Marks-brand-specific clinical trial was located in this pass; retinol's general cosmetic-dermatology evidence base (real but distinct from prescription-strength retinoid trial evidence) was not independently re-verified here.

## Pros
- Retinol has a reasonably well-established general cosmetic-dermatology track record for skin-tone/texture improvement, even without FDA drug status
- Contains sodium hyaluronate and vitamin C as supporting antioxidant/hydrating ingredients
- Backed by a major, quality-consistent manufacturer (Kenvue/Neutrogena) with broad retail availability

## Cons
- **No FDA Drug Facts panel or drug-monograph active ingredient found** — this product's presence in an OTC "Scar Treatments" drug category is not backed by any confirmed FDA drug claim, consistent with the pattern already found for [[Bio-Oil]], [[Palmer's Cocoa Butter Skin Therapy Oil with Vitamin E]], [[Cicatricure]], and [[Differin Resurfacing Scar Gel]] in this same subcategory
- **The "Neutrogena Stubborn ___" naming convention spans both cosmetic (Marks, retinol) and genuine OTC drug (Texture/Body Acne, salicylic acid) sub-lines** — a real risk that a consumer assumes consistent FDA drug status across the naming family
- Retinol is generally weaker and slower-acting than the FDA-regulated retinoids (adapalene, tretinoin) used elsewhere in this database's Acne Products research — a real potency gap, not merely a regulatory-labeling technicality
- No independent clinical trial specific to this product was located

## Use Cases
Cosmetic reduction in the appearance of post-acne marks and uneven skin tone via retinol, vitamin C, and hyaluronate — no confirmed FDA drug claim.
