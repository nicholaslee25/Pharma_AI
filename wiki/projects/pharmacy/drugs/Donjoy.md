## Overview
**DonJoy** (DJO, LLC — now part of Enovis; 5% share, the smallest of the five Joint Support/Braces brands but a distinct product tier) is the outlier in this category: rather than a generic elastic/neoprene sleeve, DonJoy's flagship products (Defiance III, A22, Armor FourcePoint, FULLFORCE) are **custom-fabricated, hinged functional ligament braces**, marketed specifically for post-ACL-reconstruction and chronic ligament-instability use — described by the manufacturer as the world's largest knee-brace manufacturer by volume. No FDA drug label exists (device, not drug). DonJoy's marketing cites biomechanical strain-gauge data claiming its "Four Points of Leverage" design and "Fourcepoint" hinge technology reduce ACL graft strain by "at least 50%" — this is a real category of evidence (cadaveric/in-vivo biomechanical strain measurement), but it is a different evidence type from a clinical outcome trial (e.g. re-injury rate), and no independent re-injury-rate RCT for this specific brace design was located in this pass. DJO/Enovis backs its custom ligament braces with a manufacturer warranty: if a patient re-tears a reconstructed ACL while wearing a properly-fitted A22, Defiance, Armor FourcePoint, or FULLFORCE brace within one year of purchase, the company will pay the patient's surgical insurance deductible — a real, verifiable business commitment, though it is a warranty/confidence signal, not itself clinical trial evidence. FDA MAUDE adverse-event records exist for DonJoy knee braces (e.g. Reaction Knee Brace, X-ROM Post-Op Knee Brace), consistent with normal post-market surveillance for a widely-used device line.

## Pros
- Distinct, more clinically-targeted product tier than the other four brands in this category — designed and marketed specifically for post-surgical/ligament-instability use rather than general joint comfort
- Real biomechanical strain-reduction data exists for its hinge technology (a genuine, if narrower, evidence type than a full outcome trial)
- Backed by a manufacturer insurance-deductible warranty for ACL re-injury while wearing a properly-fitted brace, a concrete (if promotional) confidence signal
- Custom-fitting process is more individualized than an off-the-shelf sleeve, which may improve real-world fit and compliance versus generic sizing

## Cons
- **No independent re-injury-rate clinical trial for any specific DonJoy hinge design was located** — the "50% ACL strain reduction" claim is a biomechanical (lab-measured) finding, not a demonstrated real-world re-injury-rate reduction; this is a meaningful evidence-tier distinction, not a confirmed clinical outcome, and should not be conflated with the marketing framing
- Significantly higher cost and more involved fitting process than an off-the-shelf ACE/Futuro/Mueller sleeve, appropriate to its different (post-surgical/custom) use case but a real access/cost consideration
- Still ultimately subject to the same general finding on [[joint-support-braces]] that outcome-trial evidence for knee bracing overall is inconclusive — DonJoy's biomechanical data is a partial, not complete, answer to that gap
- FDA MAUDE records exist for adverse events on specific DonJoy models, as is normal for any widely-used device — not evaluated in depth here beyond noting their existence

## Use Cases
Post-ACL-reconstruction functional bracing; chronic ligament instability requiring hinged, custom-fitted mechanical support; higher-demand/athletic return-to-activity bracing beyond what a generic compression sleeve is designed for.

## Allergies / Warnings
Not a drug — no allergy/ingredient profile. Standard device cautions apply, plus proper professional fitting is more important for this brace type than for a generic sleeve, since hinge/leverage mechanics depend on correct fit and positioning to function as designed.

## Symptoms Treated
Not applicable in the drug sense — provides mechanical ligament-instability support and (per biomechanical data) reduced ACL graft strain under load; does not treat the underlying ligament injury itself and is typically used alongside, not instead of, surgical repair and rehabilitation.
