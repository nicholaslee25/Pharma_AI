## Appears In
Decongestants, Oral - 5%

## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed: Zyrtec-D = **cetirizine HCl 5mg (immediate-release layer) + pseudoephedrine HCl 120mg (extended-release layer)**, bilayer tablet, dosed every 12 hours. This confirms the earlier "widely known as... not independently confirmed" hedge exactly as guessed — genuinely cetirizine + pseudoephedrine. Note the cetirizine dose here (5mg) is half the plain-Zyrtec dose (10mg) already confirmed on [[second-generation-antihistamines]] — a real, worth-noting dose difference between the combination product and the standalone antihistamine. Touches [[second-generation-antihistamines]] and [[pseudoephedrine]].

## Pros
- Combines a well-evidenced antihistamine (cetirizine, per [[second-generation-antihistamines]]) with a well-evidenced decongestant (pseudoephedrine, "moderate-to-high" evidence grade per [[pseudoephedrine]]) — arguably one of the stronger-evidenced combination products in this entire batch on both components
- Bilayer tablet design (immediate + extended release) is a genuine pharmaceutical engineering feature, not just a marketing claim, allowing both a fast antihistamine onset and a sustained 12-hour decongestant effect from one tablet

## Cons
- Behind-the-counter purchase restriction applies due to the pseudoephedrine component
- The lower cetirizine dose (5mg vs. plain Zyrtec's 10mg) means a reader should not assume Zyrtec-D delivers the same antihistamine strength as plain Zyrtec — worth being explicit about, since the two products aren't simply "Zyrtec plus a decongestant" at equal antihistamine potency
- Inherits cetirizine's sedation profile plus pseudoephedrine's cardiovascular stimulant cautions simultaneously — two different classes of side effect to watch for in one product

## Use Cases
Combined allergy symptom relief (sneezing, runny nose, itchy/watery eyes) and nasal/sinus congestion relief.

## Allergies / Warnings
Contraindicated with known hypersensitivity to cetirizine or hydroxyzine. Ask a doctor before use with high blood pressure, heart disease, thyroid disease, diabetes, glaucoma, or enlarged prostate. Avoid alcohol (sedation risk).

## Symptoms Treated
Sneezing, runny nose, itchy/watery eyes, nasal/sinus congestion.

## Brand-Specific Alignment Check
No discrepancy with [[second-generation-antihistamines]] or [[pseudoephedrine]] on the pseudoephedrine dose, but worth flagging the cetirizine-dose difference (5mg here vs. 10mg in plain Zyrtec) as a detail a reader should not overlook when comparing the two products.
