## Appears In

## Overview
OMRON TENS units (including "Max Power Relief TENS Therapy" and "Electrotherapy Pain Relief" lines) are FDA 510(k)-cleared devices, substantially equivalent to legally marketed predicate devices — see [[tens-devices]] for the full device-category evidence context (Cochrane's Gibson 2019 overview of reviews found it could not confidently confirm TENS effectiveness for chronic pain vs. sham treatment). **Real safety records found in this pass:** the FDA's public device-recall database lists **Class 3 recalls** for TENS Device units associated with OMRON (Dalian) Co., Ltd. manufacturing — note that FDA device-recall "Class 3" is the LOWEST-severity device recall category (the reverse convention from FDA drug recalls, where Class I is most severe), reserved for defects unlikely to cause adverse health consequences. Separately, the FDA's MAUDE adverse-event database (a voluntary, unverified reporting system, not a confirmed-causation record) includes a report of an OMRON Max Power Relief TENS unit reportedly catching fire "on the first day of use from the box," and a separate report of a user experiencing hip/thigh pain during use in an area where the device had previously been used successfully elsewhere on the body. These are individual voluntary reports, not confirmed systemic defects, but are disclosed here per this database's flag-rather-than-omit standard.

## Pros
- FDA 510(k)-cleared device with an established, decades-long (since 1977) regulatory track record for this device class
- By far the dominant brand in this category (78% share), reflecting broad consumer/pharmacist familiarity with the OMRON brand generally (also a major blood-pressure-monitor and thermometer manufacturer, per this database's Diagnostics/Durable Goods research)
- Battery-powered, non-drug pain management option with no systemic drug-interaction risk, a genuine alternative modality for patients wishing to reduce NSAID/acetaminophen reliance

## Cons
- **Per [[tens-devices]]'s Cochrane-level evidence, it is not possible to confidently state that TENS (as a device class, OMRON included) is effective for chronic pain relief beyond sham/placebo** — OMRON's dominant market share should not be read as evidence of superior or confirmed efficacy
- Real, disclosed safety records: Class 3 (lowest-severity) FDA device recalls exist for OMRON TENS units, and MAUDE voluntary adverse-event reports include a device-fire report and a pain-during-use report — neither independently verified as a confirmed causal defect in this pass, but both are real filed records worth a consumer's awareness
- 510(k) clearance does not require clinical efficacy data, only safety/equivalence to prior devices — consistent with the broader device-category evidence gap on [[tens-devices]]
- No brand-specific (OMRON-vs-competitor) outcome RCT was identified in this pass

## Use Cases
Temporary relief of minor muscle and joint pain (back, neck, shoulder, arthritis-related joint pain) via electrical nerve stimulation — a non-drug pain-management modality.

## Allergies / Warnings
Standard TENS device contraindications per [[tens-devices]]: not for use over the front/sides of the neck, over the eyes, transcerebrally, directly on the spine, on broken/irritated skin, or by pacemaker/implanted-device users without physician guidance; not for undiagnosed pain of unknown cause; not for use while driving, operating machinery, or bathing.

## Symptoms Treated
Minor muscle and joint pain, per the device's general indication — not a treatment for the underlying cause of pain.
