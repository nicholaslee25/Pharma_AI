## Overview
**Category-placement discrepancy, real and significant:** Zarbee's Naturals Children's Cough Syrup products (Daytime, Nighttime, All-In-One) are **dietary supplements, not FDA-regulated OTC drugs** — every SKU reviewed carries the standard supplement disclaimer ("These statements have not been evaluated by the Food and Drug Administration. This product is not intended to diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any disease") and contains no monograph drug active ingredient. Confirmed ingredients vary by SKU but center on a proprietary blend of **dark honey, black elderberry (Sambucus nigra) extract, vitamin D3, and zinc gluconate** (daytime), or **honey, English ivy (Hedera helix) leaf extract, black elderberry, and German chamomile extract** (a day/night variant) — no dextromethorphan, guaifenesin, or any other conventional cough-drug active ingredient. This means a real product filed in this database's "Children's Cough" and "Children's Cough/Cold Combinations" **drug** categories is, per its own manufacturer labeling, not a drug at all — the same non-drug-miscategorized-as-drug pattern this database's research protocol explicitly watches for. It draws partial evidence support from two ingredient pages already in this database: [[honey-pediatric-cough]] (moderate-certainty Cochrane evidence for honey specifically, ages 1+) and [[elderberry]] (see that page for elderberry's own separate evidence base) — but the specific multi-ingredient proprietary blend as marketed was not itself clinically tested as a unit in any source located.

## Pros
- Built on an ingredient (honey) with real, moderate-certainty Cochrane-level evidence for short-term pediatric nighttime cough relief per [[honey-pediatric-cough]] — genuinely better-evidenced than several homeopathic competitors in adjacent categories
- No sedating antihistamine, dextromethorphan, or decongestant — avoids the CNS/cardiovascular-adjacent side-effect profiles of conventional combination cough/cold drugs, relevant for parents seeking to avoid those specific ingredient classes
- Elderberry and zinc components have their own independent evidence bases in this database ([[elderberry]], [[zinc]]) even though the specific blend wasn't tested as a unit

## Cons
- **Not an FDA-regulated drug despite being filed in this database's drug-focused Children's Cough and Cough/Cold Combinations categories** — a real category-placement mismatch, not a formulation detail; parents should understand these products make no FDA-reviewed efficacy claim as a class, unlike every dextromethorphan/guaifenesin/antihistamine competitor in the same category tables
- **The critical honey-under-12-months contraindication applies directly and by name** — per [[honey-pediatric-cough]], honey is absolutely contraindicated under 12 months due to infantile botulism risk; Zarbee's own labeling should be checked for an explicit age-1+ restriction before use in any infant, and this is flagged here as a real safety-relevant fact this brand shares with every other honey-based product in this batch (Little Remedies Honey Cough Syrup, HoneyWorks Kids, Chestal Honey)
- The specific multi-ingredient proprietary blend (honey + elderberry + vitamin D/zinc, or honey + ivy leaf + chamomile) was not itself independently clinically tested as a combination in any source located in this pass — evidence for honey alone or elderberry alone cannot be assumed to transfer directly to this exact blend
- "Zarbee's Naturals" as a brand also spans an unrelated Melatonin Sleep Aid product (3% share, Melatonin Sleep Aids category) and a children's multivitamin (Zarbee's Multi, Children's Multivitamins category) — a broad supplement-line brand extension pattern, not a sign that all Zarbee's products share ingredients or evidence

## Use Cases
Short-term (per Cochrane data, most effective ≤3 days) relief of nighttime cough in children 12 months and older, via a honey/elderberry-based dietary supplement rather than a conventional drug.

## Allergies / Warnings
