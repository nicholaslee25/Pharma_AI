## Appears In
Lice Treatments - 13%

## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed: RID's classic line (**RID Lice Killing Shampoo**, **RID Lice Treatment Complete Kit**) is **pyrethrins 0.33% + piperonyl butoxide 4%**, applied for 10 minutes with a mandatory repeat application 7-10 days later. See [[permethrin-pyrethrin-lice]] — this mechanism carries the same near-universal US kdr-mutation resistance finding (98.3% mean resistance-allele frequency) documented there, since pyrethrins act on the same sodium-channel target as permethrin.

**A genuine, real "one brand, two mechanisms" finding, discovered this pass:** RID also sells **RID One & Done — ivermectin 0.5% lotion**, confirmed via DailyMed (labeler Oystershell Consumer Health, manufactured by Taro Pharmaceutical; marketing status ANDA210720, genuinely OTC — no prescription required). Ivermectin topical 0.5% (originally Sklice, Rx-only since 2012) completed a real **FDA Rx-to-OTC switch in 2023**. Critically, **ivermectin's mechanism (glutamate-gated chloride channel agonist) is completely unrelated to the pyrethroid sodium-channel target that the kdr mutation confers resistance to** — meaning RID's newer, single-application ivermectin product is not subject to the resistance problem undercutting its own classic pyrethrins line, and is explicitly marketed for "super lice." This database's tracked 13% recommendation share is presumed (not confirmed by the source dossier) to reflect the classic pyrethrins line, since that is RID's long-standing flagship product; it is unclear whether pharmacists surveyed distinguished between RID's two chemically unrelated products when giving this recommendation.

No RID-brand-specific clinical trial (beyond the generic pyrethrins/PBO and ivermectin literature) was located in this pass.

## Pros
- Classic line: confirmed, consistent FDA Drug Facts label (pyrethrins 0.33% + piperonyl butoxide 4%)
- RID One & Done: a genuinely different, resistance-independent mechanism (ivermectin) now available OTC under the same trusted brand name, single 10-minute application, explicitly positioned for resistant ("super") lice
- Category's only brand offering both a legacy and a modern, mechanistically distinct treatment path under one name

## Cons
- **Classic pyrethrins+PBO line carries the same near-universal kdr-mutation resistance problem as [[Nix]]** — see [[permethrin-pyrethrin-lice]]
- **Real "one brand, two mechanisms" ambiguity**: a consumer or even a recommending pharmacist may not realize "RID" now spans two pharmacologically unrelated products (classic pyrethrins/PBO vs. ivermectin One & Done) with very different resistance profiles and application regimens (two applications 7-10 days apart vs. single application) — this database cannot confirm which product the tracked 13% share actually reflects
- Classic line requires two applications 7-10 days apart, a less convenient regimen than [[Nix]]'s single application
- No RID-brand-specific trial exists for either product line

## Use Cases
Classic line: treatment of active head lice infestation via pyrethrins/piperonyl-butoxide, two applications 7-10 days apart. RID One & Done: single-application ivermectin 0.5% lotion for head lice including resistant ("super lice") strains, ages 6 months and up.

## Allergies / Warnings
Classic line (per label): external use only; avoid eyes/mucous membranes; not all pyrethrin products age-cleared the same as permethrin — check specific SKU label; pyrethrin is chrysanthemum-derived, relevant for ragweed/chrysanthemum allergy. RID One & Done (per label): external use only, scalp only; not for children under 6 months; avoid eyes, nose, ears, mouth, vaginal area; consult a doctor if pregnant/breastfeeding.

## Symptoms Treated
Active head lice infestation and nits, including resistant strains (One & Done specifically marketed for this).

## Brand-Specific Alignment Check
**A genuine discrepancy, flagged prominently:** "RID" is not a single formulation — it spans a resistance-vulnerable pyrethrins/PBO line and a resistance-independent ivermectin line, launched under the same brand name after ivermectin's 2023 Rx-to-OTC switch. This database's Pharmacy Times source data tracks "RID" as one 13%-share row, which cannot distinguish between these two products. Flagged on both this page and [[permethrin-pyrethrin-lice]].
