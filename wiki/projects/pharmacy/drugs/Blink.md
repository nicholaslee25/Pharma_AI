## Overview
Confirmed: Blink Tears' (Johnson & Johnson) own FDA OTC Drug Facts label (DailyMed) lists **polyethylene glycol 400 0.25%** as the sole active ingredient, with sodium hyaluronate among the inactive ingredients. Per [[artificial-tears]]'s cited Semp et al. 2023 systematic review, PEG-containing tears outperformed CMC/HPMC-based tears in the underlying comparative evidence — placing Blink's core formulation in the same favorably-evidenced PEG category as [[Systane]], though at a lower PEG concentration (0.25% vs. Systane Ultra's PEG 400 + propylene glycol combination) and as a single-ingredient rather than combination formulation. A separate "Blink Gel Tears" sub-formulation also uses PEG 400 in a higher-viscosity gel base, not independently re-verified here. No Blink-brand-specific clinical trial beyond the general PEG-ingredient evidence already on [[artificial-tears]] was identified.

## Pros
- PEG-based formulation aligns with the ingredient category the ingredient page's cited systematic review found to outperform CMC/HPMC-only tears
- Contains sodium hyaluronate as an inactive/supporting ingredient, a moisturizing polymer with independent mechanistic support in dry-eye literature (though not itself the FDA-recognized active ingredient on this label)
- Gel-based sub-formulation (Blink Gel Tears) available for users wanting longer-lasting viscosity than a standard drop

## Cons
- Lower PEG concentration (0.25%) and single-ingredient formulation, versus [[Systane]] Ultra's combination PEG + propylene glycol formulation, which the cited systematic review found combination formulations generally outperform single-ingredient ones — a real, if modest, formulation-tier distinction
- Smaller recommendation share (6%) than the two category leaders ([[Systane]] 34%, [[Refresh]] 23%)
- No independent brand-specific outcome trial isolates Blink's real-world performance from the general PEG-ingredient evidence

## Use Cases
Temporary relief of burning, irritation, and discomfort due to dryness of the eye or exposure to wind or sun.

## Allergies / Warnings
Per label: for external use only; discontinue and see a doctor for eye pain, vision changes, continued redness/irritation, or symptoms persisting beyond 72 hours.

## Symptoms Treated
Dry eye burning, irritation, discomfort from wind/sun exposure or reduced tear production.

## Brand-Specific Alignment Check
Consistent with [[artificial-tears]]: no discrepancy found. Blink's PEG-based single-ingredient formulation sits in the favorably-evidenced PEG category per the cited review, though as a single-ingredient (not combination) formulation it is not expected to outperform combination products like Systane per that same review's own finding.
