## Appears In
Cold Sore Treatments - 5%

## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed: Campho-Phenique Cold Sore Treatment (gel) lists **camphor (synthetic) and phenol** as active ingredients — a counterirritant/local-antiseptic combination, mechanistically unrelated to Abreva's antiviral docosanol or the benzocaine-based anesthetics elsewhere in this category. A separate "Campho-Phenique Antiseptic" liquid/gel line (also camphor + phenol) is marketed as a general first-aid antiseptic rather than cold-sore-specific — the same two-ingredient combination is repurposed across both use cases under the same brand name, which at least keeps the active-ingredient identity consistent (unlike several other brands in this category).

## Pros
- Single, consistent active-ingredient combination (camphor + phenol) across its cold-sore and general-antiseptic product lines — less internal formulation confusion than Blistex, Carmex, or Mederma in this same category
- Phenol has long-established topical antiseptic and mild local-anesthetic properties recognized under the FDA OTC monograph system
- Long historical OTC track record as a first-aid antiseptic brand, predating most competitors in this category

## Cons
- No dedicated antiviral mechanism — camphor/phenol do not target herpes simplex virus replication or entry the way Abreva's docosanol does; relief here is symptomatic (mild anesthetic/antiseptic) rather than disease-modifying
- No brand-specific clinical trial evidence was identified for this product's effect on cold sore healing time or symptom relief
- Phenol at higher concentrations carries known local tissue-irritation/caustic risk if overused or applied to broken skin extensively — general phenol safety consideration, not independently quantified for this specific low-concentration OTC gel in this pass

## Use Cases
Temporary relief of pain and antiseptic action for cold sores/fever blisters and, in the separate Antiseptic SKU, general minor cuts/scrapes/burns.

## Allergies / Warnings
Standard OTC camphor/phenol precautions: external use only, avoid eyes, do not use over large areas of the body, discontinue if irritation/rash develops or condition worsens; keep away from children.

## Symptoms Treated
Cold sore/fever blister pain and local antiseptic action — symptomatic relief, not antiviral treatment.
