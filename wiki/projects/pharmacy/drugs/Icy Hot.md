## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed: Icy Hot's product line uses **menthol (7.6-10%) + methyl salicylate (29-30%)** across its Balm, Stick, and Cream formats — a classic counterirritant combination (see the new [[topical-counterirritants]] evidence page, added during this research pass). **Icy Hot was one of the brands explicitly named in press coverage of the FDA's September 2012 Drug Safety Communication** reviewing 43 cases (1969-2011) of first- to third-degree chemical burns linked to OTC topical menthol/methyl salicylate/capsaicin products — the FDA identified >3% menthol and >10% methyl salicylate as risk factors for more severe burns, and every Icy Hot SKU confirmed here exceeds BOTH thresholds by a wide margin (menthol 2-3x over, methyl salicylate ~3x over). Additionally, per [[topical-counterirritants]], methyl salicylate carries real NSAID-class systemic risks (stomach bleeding risk factors, aspirin/salicylate allergy contraindication, and a specific pregnancy warning against use at 20+ weeks gestation) that a consumer may not expect from a topical rub. Icy Hot's single formulation (menthol + methyl salicylate, no camphor) drives all three of its category appearances in this database — a simpler, single-formula situation than several other multi-category brands researched in this pass.

## Pros
- Confirmed FDA Drug Facts labels across Balm, Stick, and Cream formats, with concentrations clearly disclosed per SKU
- One of the largest, most recognized brands in Thermal Relief Products (20% share) and present in two additional related categories
- Real (if narrow) RCT support exists for the menthol/methyl salicylate combination class generally (Higashi et al. 2010, PMID 20171409 — see [[topical-counterirritants]]), even though that specific trial tested a lower concentration (10% methyl salicylate + 3% menthol) than Icy Hot's actual products (29-30% / 7.6-10%)

## Cons
- **Named specifically in the FDA's 2012 burn-risk communication**, and its own labeled concentrations exceed both FDA-identified risk thresholds for more severe burns — a real, quantified, brand-specific safety consideration, not a generic caution
- Methyl salicylate's real NSAID-class systemic risks (GI bleeding risk factors, salicylate-allergy contraindication, a specific 20-weeks-pregnancy warning) are easy for a consumer to overlook given the product's "just a rub" framing
- The RCT evidence supporting this ingredient combination class tested a substantially LOWER concentration (10%/3%) than Icy Hot's actual products (29-30%/7.6-10%) — efficacy at the higher, actually-marketed concentration was not independently confirmed by that specific trial
- No brand-specific clinical trial for Icy Hot's own formulation at its actual concentrations was identified

## Use Cases
Temporary relief of minor pain associated with arthritis, simple backache, muscle strains, sprains, bruises, and cramps.

## Allergies / Warnings
Per label: external use only; avoid eyes and mucous membranes; do not use with a heating pad (a real, specific burn-risk-relevant warning); do not bandage tightly; do not apply to wounds or damaged skin; rare cases of serious burns have been reported with products of this type; stop use and consult a doctor if condition worsens or symptoms persist beyond 7 days. See [[topical-counterirritants]] for the full FDA burn-risk and methyl-salicylate NSAID-class safety data.

## Symptoms Treated
Minor musculoskeletal pain (arthritis, backache, muscle strains/sprains/bruises/cramps) via counterirritant sensory masking, not an anti-inflammatory or disease-modifying mechanism.
