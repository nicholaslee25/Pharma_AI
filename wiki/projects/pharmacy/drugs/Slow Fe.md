## Overview
Slow Fe is the category-leading iron brand in this database (37% share), confirmed to use **ferrous sulfate** (325mg/137mg per tablet depending on formulation, ~45mg elemental iron) in a slow/extended-release formulation — a real, meaningful design choice given that [[iron]]'s own evidence page and independent sourcing confirm standard (immediate-release) ferrous sulfate causes significant GI side effects (a systematic review/meta-analysis is cited specifically documenting this). Extended-release formulations like Slow Fe are independently described as remaining an established, standard treatment for iron deficiency with good bioavailability and "acceptable tolerability" in large clinical studies, and one industry source cites 1.3–1.4x higher absorption for extended-release versus rapidly-disintegrating tablets across patient populations (this specific comparative figure was not independently traced to a primary peer-reviewed source in this pass and should be treated as an industry-reported claim pending closer verification). Iron Supplements (37%) is covered by [[iron]].

## Pros
- Extended-release ferrous sulfate formulation is a real, clinically-motivated design choice specifically addressing ferrous sulfate's well-documented GI-tolerability problem (nausea, abdominal discomfort) relative to immediate-release forms
- Uses ferrous sulfate, described in sourcing as "the ingredient most recommended by doctors" for iron deficiency — the most standard, best-studied iron salt overall
- Category-leading share (37%) reflects broad pharmacist/consumer familiarity

## Cons
- The specific "1.3–1.4x higher absorption" comparative claim found in industry sourcing was not independently traced to a primary peer-reviewed source in this pass — treat as an industry claim, not an independently confirmed trial result
- Ferrous sulfate, even in extended-release form, still carries the class-wide iron-supplement risks documented in [[iron]] (real pediatric overdose/poisoning risk from accidental ingestion of adult-strength tablets — a serious concern independent of GI tolerability)
- No independent third-party quality certification (USP Verified, NSF) was located specifically for Slow Fe in this pass

## Use Cases
Iron-deficiency anemia treatment/prevention — see [[iron]] for the general evidence base; extended-release design specifically targets improved GI tolerability over standard ferrous sulfate.

## Allergies / Warnings
