## Appears In
Topical Antibiotics/Antiseptics - 3%

## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed: Bactine Original First Aid's own FDA OTC Drug Facts label lists **benzalkonium chloride** (approximately 0.5g/100mL, i.e. ~0.13%) as its active ingredient, functioning as a first-aid antiseptic — a quaternary ammonium compound, mechanistically distinct from the bacitracin/neomycin/polymyxin peptide antibiotics dominating this category (Neosporin, Bacitraycin Plus, Polysporin) and from chlorhexidine/povidone-iodine (see [[Hibiclens]]/[[Betadine]]). This is the same antiseptic chemical as in [[Alocane]] and [[Bactine MAX]], but **plain Bactine is antiseptic-only, with no anesthetic (no lidocaine) and no antibiotic ingredient** — a materially simpler formula than either of those related-brand products, despite the shared "Bactine" name with Bactine MAX.

## Pros
- Confirmed FDA Drug Facts label; simple, single-active-ingredient antiseptic formula
- Benzalkonium chloride is a genuinely different antiseptic class from the peptide-antibiotic products dominating this category, offering a real mechanistic alternative for a consumer wanting antisepsis without antibiotic-allergy exposure (bacitracin/neomycin/polymyxin)
- No anesthetic ingredient means no local-anesthetic allergy or methemoglobinemia risk exposure

## Cons
- No pain-relief action (unlike Bactine MAX, Alocane, or Bacitraycin Plus) — purely antiseptic, so a consumer wanting both infection-risk reduction and pain relief would need a different product
- No antibiotic action against bacteria already established in a wound (antiseptics and antibiotics work differently) — plain Bactine addresses surface antisepsis, not the same mechanism as bacitracin/polymyxin B's antibacterial action
- Smallest share (3%) in a category dominated by antibiotic-ointment products; no brand-specific clinical trial identified
- Shares the "Bactine" brand name with the materially different, more complex "Bactine MAX" line (see [[Bactine MAX]]) — a consumer should check which specific product they're using

## Use Cases
First aid antisepsis for minor cuts, scrapes, and burns — helps prevent bacterial contamination/skin infection via antiseptic (not antibiotic) action.

## Allergies / Warnings
Per label: for external use only; discontinue and consult a doctor if condition persists/worsens or a rash/allergic reaction develops. Benzalkonium chloride carries its own rare but documented contact-sensitivity risk.

## Symptoms Treated
Reduces infection risk from surface bacterial contamination in minor cuts/scrapes/burns; does not relieve pain or itching (no anesthetic ingredient).

## Brand-Specific Alignment Check
No prior ingredient-page claim existed specifically for plain Bactine; this pass establishes it as a benzalkonium-chloride-only antiseptic, mechanistically consistent with [[Alocane]]'s antiseptic component but without that product's added lidocaine. Worth noting alongside [[Bactine MAX]] as an example of a shared brand name spanning a simpler vs. more complex formulation, not a contradiction.
