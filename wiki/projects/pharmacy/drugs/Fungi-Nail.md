## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed: **undecylenic acid 25%**, a fatty-acid antifungal — new ingredient page [[undecylenic-acid]] built this pass. Fungi-Nail is marketed specifically for **fungal nail** appearance (discoloration, thickening, splitting, crumbling, brittleness) as well as athlete's foot and ringworm on skin.

**Real, FDA-label-confirmed effectiveness nuance, flagged prominently:** the product's own FDA labeling states it "helps inhibit the growth of fungus and **improves the appearance**" of fungal-affected nails — it does **not** claim to cure onychomycosis (nail fungus infection). This is consistent with standard dermatology guidance that topical OTC antifungals generally cannot penetrate and cure a true nail-bed fungal infection (which typically requires prescription oral or specialized topical antifungals). A consumer buying Fungi-Nail as a "toenail fungus treatment" expecting a cure would, per the product's own FDA-approved claim language, be getting a cosmetic-appearance improvement, not a documented cure.

No independent Fungi-Nail-brand-specific clinical trial was located in this pass; the relevant evidence is the general undecylenic-acid literature on [[undecylenic-acid]] (real but dated placebo-controlled trials, notably older than the evidence base for modern azole/allylamine/benzylamine antifungals dominating this category).

## Pros
- Confirmed FDA Drug Facts label with a real, if dated, evidentiary basis (placebo-controlled trials showing substantial improvement over vehicle for tinea pedis)
- Addresses a real, underserved niche (fungal-nail appearance) not directly targeted by this category's leading skin-focused antifungals ([[Lotrimin]], [[Lamisil]])
- Long history of use (since 1949) with a generally well-tolerated safety profile

## Cons
- **The product's own FDA label claim is limited to improving fungal-nail appearance, not curing onychomycosis** — a real, potentially under-recognized effectiveness gap relative to consumer expectations set by the "toenail fungus treatment" framing common in this product category
- Undecylenic acid's evidence base is notably older (1970s-80s trials) than the modern comparative-trial evidence available for this category's leading brands, per [[undecylenic-acid]]
- No independent Fungi-Nail-brand-specific clinical trial was located
- Historical trial cure rates for the skin (tinea pedis) indication (53% vs. 7% placebo in one trial) are meaningfully lower than modern terbinafine/butenafine cure rates documented elsewhere in this database

## Use Cases
Improving the appearance of fungal-affected nails (not a cure claim) and treating athlete's foot/ringworm on skin via undecylenic acid's fatty-acid antifungal mechanism.

## Allergies / Warnings
Per label: external use only; not for children under 2 without doctor guidance; discontinue if irritation develops; seek medical care if no improvement within the labeled treatment duration.

## Symptoms Treated
Fungal-nail discoloration/thickening/splitting appearance (cosmetic-improvement claim, not a cure); itching, burning, cracking, and scaling from athlete's foot and ringworm on skin.
