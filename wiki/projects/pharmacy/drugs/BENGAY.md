## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed: BENGAY spans a genuinely wide concentration spread across its SKUs — from **BENGAY Vanishing Scent** (menthol 2.5% only, the mildest formulation, BELOW the FDA's >3% menthol burn-risk threshold) and **BENGAY Zero Degrees** (menthol 5% only), up to **BENGAY Ultra Strength Non-Greasy Cream** (camphor 4% + menthol 10% + methyl salicylate 30%) — the highest methyl salicylate concentration confirmed anywhere in this database's Thermal Relief Products research, well exceeding both FDA-identified burn-risk thresholds (>3% menthol, >10% methyl salicylate). **BENGAY was explicitly named in press coverage of the FDA's September 2012 Drug Safety Communication** on rare-but-serious burns from topical menthol/methyl salicylate/capsaicin products (see [[topical-counterirritants]]). A consumer choosing "BENGAY" without checking the specific product name is choosing between meaningfully different formulations and risk profiles — from a mild single-ingredient gel to a triple-ingredient, high-concentration cream.

## Pros
- Confirmed FDA Drug Facts labels across multiple SKUs, with concentrations clearly disclosed per product
- Offers a genuine choice of intensity — a mild 2.5% menthol-only option (Vanishing Scent) for users wanting a gentler product, up through the maximum-strength triple-ingredient cream for more severe pain
- Long-established, widely recognized brand across three related categories in this database

## Cons
- **Ultra Strength Non-Greasy is one of the highest-concentration counterirritant products identified in this entire database**, exceeding both FDA burn-risk thresholds on every ingredient, and BENGAY specifically was named in the FDA's 2012 burn-risk communication
- The methyl salicylate-containing SKU carries the full NSAID-class systemic risk profile documented on [[topical-counterirritants]] (stomach bleeding risk factors, aspirin/salicylate allergy contraindication, pregnancy warning at 20+ weeks)
- The wide concentration spread across SKUs (2.5% to 30% combined) means brand-name familiarity alone does not tell a consumer what risk profile they're actually buying
- No brand-specific clinical trial for BENGAY specifically was identified beyond the general counterirritant-class evidence on [[topical-counterirritants]] (which itself concerns a different product's formulation — Salonpas's 10%/3%, not BENGAY's 30%/10%/4%)

## Use Cases
Temporary relief of minor aches and pains of muscles and joints — simple backache, arthritis, strains, bruises, sprains — with intensity varying meaningfully by specific SKU.

## Allergies / Warnings
Per Ultra Strength label: do not use on wounds/damaged skin, with heating pads, or on children under 12 with arthritis-like conditions; avoid eyes/mucous membranes; do not bandage tightly; stop use if condition worsens, symptoms persist beyond 7 days, recur, or excessive skin irritation develops; keep from children, contact Poison Control if swallowed; adults/children 12+ only. See [[topical-counterirritants]] for the full FDA burn-risk and methyl-salicylate NSAID-class safety data, both directly applicable to the Ultra Strength SKU.

## Symptoms Treated
Minor musculoskeletal aches and pains via counterirritant sensory masking; intensity of the cooling/warming counterirritant sensation and pain-masking effect varies substantially by SKU concentration.

## Brand-Specific Alignment Check
Directly substantiates [[topical-counterirritants]]'s FDA burn-risk section — BENGAY Ultra Strength is among the highest-concentration products confirmed in this database and was specifically named in the 2012 FDA communication. Adds a new nuance: BENGAY's OTHER SKUs (Vanishing Scent, Zero Degrees) are meaningfully milder and do not carry the same elevated risk profile — a consumer should not treat "BENGAY" as a single fixed-risk product.
