## Appears In
Toothpaste, Sensitive Gums/Teeth - 5%

## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed: **Arm & Hammer Sensitive Teeth and Gums** lists **potassium nitrate 5.0%** + **sodium fluoride 0.243%** as active ingredients — confirming [[potassium-nitrate]]'s prior "plausibly potassium-nitrate-based" placeholder for this brand. The label itself supplies more mechanistic detail than this database's ingredient page previously had: potassium salts travel along the dentinal tubules and block intradental nerve-fiber excitability, with the label explicitly noting the benefit requires a period of use (14–28 days) before it is established — consistent with the standard "stop use after 4 weeks if unresolved, consult a dentist" warning seen across all potassium-nitrate brands in this database.

## Pros
- Confirmed FDA Drug Facts label matching the same active-ingredient combination already documented for Sensodyne and Colgate Sensitive (potassium nitrate + sodium fluoride) — a consistent, well-characterized mechanism across all three major players in this subcategory
- Dual anticavity + antisensitivity benefit from one product
- Label's own mechanism explanation (nerve-fiber excitability blockade via potassium ion movement through dentinal tubules) is unusually explicit compared to some competitor labels reviewed in this database

## Cons
- Per [[potassium-nitrate]]'s Cochrane evidence summary (Poulsen 2006), the ingredient class shows a real, statistically significant effect on objective (air blast/tactile) sensitivity measures, but patient-reported (subjective) benefit failed to reach statistical significance — the same evidentiary caution applies here as to Sensodyne and Colgate Sensitive
- No brand-specific (as opposed to general potassium-nitrate ingredient-class) clinical trial evidence was identified for Arm & Hammer Sensitive Teeth and Gums specifically, unlike Colgate Sensitive's identified head-to-head trial against Sensodyne
- Smallest market share (5%) of the four brands in this subcategory that use a confirmed active ingredient, despite an identical mechanism to the two larger competitors

## Use Cases
Dentin hypersensitivity relief (cold/heat/acid/sweet/contact triggers) and cavity prevention — same mechanism and use case as Sensodyne and Colgate Sensitive.

## Allergies / Warnings
Per label: do not use longer than 4 weeks unless recommended by a dentist or physician; sensitive teeth may indicate a serious problem needing prompt dental care. Standard potassium-nitrate/fluoride precautions per [[potassium-nitrate]] and [[fluoride-toothpaste]] otherwise apply.

## Symptoms Treated
Tooth sensitivity to cold, heat, acids, sweets, or contact; cavity prevention.
