## Overview
**Category-placement discrepancy flagged:** Band-Aid Hydro Seal is a **hydrocolloid gel bandage/patch** — a solid adhesive patch, not a liquid at all. It is a medical DEVICE, not an FDA drug (no DailyMed Drug Facts listing exists, confirmed by direct search), regulated as an **occlusive wound dressing under 21 CFR 878.4020 (Class II)** — a higher device classification than the simple adhesive-strip bandages on [[bandages-covers-and-gauze]] (Class I, 21 CFR 880.5240). This means Hydro Seal shares its "Liquid Bandages" category with genuinely liquid/paintable products (New-Skin's antiseptic film-former, Bactine MAX's lidocaine liquid sealant) despite being a fundamentally different physical form and mechanism — hydrocolloid gel absorbing wound exudate to maintain a moist healing environment, rather than any liquid drying into a film. See [[liquid-bandages]] for the two liquid sub-classes this product does NOT belong to mechanistically, despite being tracked in the same Pharmacy Times subcategory.

## Pros
- Hydrocolloid dressings have a well-established general evidence base for moist wound healing (a legitimate, mainstream wound-care approach, distinct from unproven "natural" claims seen elsewhere in this database)
- Waterproof/shower-proof, multi-day wear — a genuinely different durability profile than either liquid sub-class or a standard adhesive strip
- No drug-related allergy/active-ingredient risk (it is not a drug) — the main risk category is adhesive contact sensitivity, common to bandages generally

## Cons
- **Category-placement mismatch:** filed under "Liquid Bandages" despite being a solid patch, not a liquid — a real naming/categorization discrepancy a consumer should be aware of if searching specifically for a paintable/liquid product

## Use Cases
Multi-day, waterproof protection and moist-healing environment for blisters, minor wounds, and burns — a fundamentally different use pattern (long-wear patch) than a quick-drying liquid antiseptic or adhesive.

## Allergies / Warnings
Not a drug — no ingredient/allergy profile in the pharmacological sense. Standard device cautions: discontinue use and consult a doctor if a wound shows signs of infection or does not heal; some users may have adhesive contact sensitivity. Manufacturer states the product is not made with natural rubber latex.
