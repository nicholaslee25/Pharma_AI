## Overview
**Truform** (3% share, smallest of the five Support Hosiery brands tracked) offers both OTC comfort-tier compression (15–20, 20–30 mmHg) and higher medical-grade tiers (30–40 mmHg), plus anti-embolism stockings (18 mmHg, targeted at the ankle) for post-surgical/immobile-patient use. No FDA drug label exists (device, not drug). Truform's own marketing states it manufactures at an FDA-registered factory and describes its higher-compression products as Class II medical devices — this is accurate as far as it goes (medical/gradient compression stockings generally fall under 21 CFR 880.5780, Class II, product code ODWL, per [[support-hosiery]]), but it is the standard classification for this entire product category, not a distinction unique to Truform; the marketing framing implies more brand-specific regulatory achievement than the underlying fact supports. This is flagged here and on [[support-hosiery]] as a marketing-framing nuance, not a false claim — the Class II fact itself is correct. No brand-specific clinical trial for Truform products was found; applicable evidence is the class-level consensus statement and Cochrane review on [[support-hosiery]].

## Pros
- Widest single-brand compression range identified in this category: 15–20, 20–30, and 30–40 mmHg tiers plus an 18 mmHg anti-embolism line, covering OTC comfort through medical/post-surgical use in one product family
- Anti-embolism stockings (18 mmHg) address a distinct, well-evidenced use case (post-surgical/immobility DVT-risk reduction) with dedicated sizing (open-toe, specific length options) rather than repurposing a general compression sock
- Over 100 years of manufacturing history cited by the company, with its own FDA-registered production facility — a verifiable regulatory/quality-infrastructure fact

## Cons
- **Marketing framing flagged:** describing Truform products as "Class II medical devices" in promotional copy is factually accurate but presented as if it were a Truform-specific achievement, when it is simply the standard FDA classification that applies to this entire product category (see [[support-hosiery]]) — a consumer reading this framing in isolation could reasonably (if incorrectly) infer a special regulatory distinction
- Smallest recommendation share in this category (3%) — likely reflects narrower retail distribution/brand recognition relative to [[Futuro]] and [[Dr. Scholl's]], not necessarily a quality difference
- No brand-specific trial exists; efficacy claims for the general-comfort tiers rest on the same lower-certainty Cochrane varicose-vein evidence discussed on [[support-hosiery]], not a distinct Truform-specific evidence base

## Use Cases
Graduated compression across a wide range: general leg-comfort/OTC tiers (15–20, 20–30 mmHg), higher medical-grade compression (30–40 mmHg) for diagnosed venous conditions, and anti-embolism stockings (18 mmHg) for post-surgical/reduced-mobility DVT-risk settings.

## Allergies / Warnings
Not a drug — no allergy/ingredient profile. Standard compression-garment cautions apply; higher compression tiers (30–40 mmHg) and anti-embolism stockings are typically intended for use under medical guidance rather than self-selected OTC use, and are contraindicated in significant peripheral arterial disease.

## Symptoms Treated
Not applicable in the drug sense — provides graduated mechanical compression across a range from general leg comfort through post-surgical DVT-risk reduction and diagnosed venous disease management, per compression tier selected.
