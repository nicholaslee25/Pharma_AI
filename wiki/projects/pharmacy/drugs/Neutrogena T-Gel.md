## Appears In
Dandruff Shampoo - 9%

## Overview
Neutrogena T/Gel (Johnson & Johnson/Kenvue) is confirmed via DailyMed as **coal tar 0.5%**-based (Original and Extra Strength formulas), per [[coal-tar]]. Purpose: "anti-dandruff, anti-seborrheic dermatitis, anti-psoriasis." **Labeling nuance confirmed on this pass:** the Drug Facts panel lists "Coal tar 0.5%" but also describes the source as "2% Neutar® Solubilized Coal Tar Extract" — two different numbers referring to raw-extract concentration vs. the coal-tar-equivalent active ingredient concentration. A consumer comparing "T/Gel's 2%" against another brand's plain 3% (e.g., [[MG217 Psoriasis]]'s coal-tar SKU) or 0.5% (Medicasp) figure could be misled without recognizing which number is the actual FDA-labeled active concentration (0.5%).

This is a distinct product/brand line from Neutrogena's other confirmed formulations documented elsewhere in this database (e.g., [[Neutrogena]]'s salicylic-acid acne line, zinc-oxide sunscreen line) — Neutrogena as a house brand spans multiple unrelated mechanisms across categories, consistent with the pattern seen throughout this pass.

## Pros
- Confirmed 0.5% coal-tar concentration sits within the FDA-monograph allowable range (0.5-5%), per [[coal-tar]]
- Broadest labeled indication set in this category (dandruff, seborrheic dermatitis, AND psoriasis) rather than dandruff alone
- FDA's own position (per [[coal-tar]]) is that there is no evidence of increased skin-cancer risk from OTC coal-tar shampoo use at monograph concentrations — a real, affirmative safety finding, even though California's Prop 65 requires a separate cancer warning at this same concentration range

## Cons
- **Dual-numbering label convention ("0.5%" vs. "2% Neutar extract") is a genuine source of potential consumer confusion** when comparing concentration against other coal-tar brands in this category
- Photosensitivity warning (increased sunburn risk for up to 24 hours after use) applies, per [[coal-tar]] — a real, consistently-labeled caution across every coal-tar brand in this pass
- No large independent RCT/Cochrane-style efficacy review specific to coal-tar shampoo was located in this pass — evidence rests primarily on the FDA's own monograph determination rather than the kind of quantified trial data available for [[benzoyl-peroxide]] or [[ketoconazole]] elsewhere in this database
- No Neutrogena-T/Gel-brand-specific clinical trial was located

## Use Cases
Dandruff, seborrheic dermatitis, and psoriasis symptom control via coal tar's cytostatic slowing of epidermal cell turnover.

## Allergies / Warnings
For external use only; avoid eye contact (rinse thoroughly with water if contact occurs); use caution with sun exposure for up to 24 hours after application; do not combine with other psoriasis therapies (UV/phototherapy or prescription drugs) unless directed by a doctor; consult a doctor before prolonged use or if condition covers a large body area or worsens; may temporarily discolor gray/tinted hair.

## Symptoms Treated
Scalp itching, flaking, scaling, and redness associated with dandruff, seborrheic dermatitis, and psoriasis.
