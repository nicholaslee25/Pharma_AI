## Overview
Nature Made Prenatal is the category-leading prenatal brand tracked in this database (32% share) and belongs to a parent brand ([[Nature Made]]) that is otherwise the strongest-verified house brand in this entire database (first-ever USP Verified supplement brand, 90+ USP-Verified SKUs). Against that backdrop, this pass found a **real, significant, currently-active discrepancy specific to this exact product line**: a class-action lawsuit (filed April 18, 2025, N.D. Cal., Case No. 3:25-cv-00933, against Pharmavite LLC) alleges that Nature Made Prenatal Multivitamin Folic Acid + DHA Softgels, independently tested by PlasticList (December 2024 report), contained unsafe levels of **phthalates and bisphenol A (BPA)** — plastic-derived endocrine-disrupting chemicals. The suit specifically names **di-2-ethylhexyl phthalate (DEHP)**, categorized as a probable human carcinogen by the EPA and linked in the cited literature to earlier menopause, low birth weight, pregnancy loss, and preterm birth. Plaintiffs allege the product's own marketing ("Supports the development of baby's brain, eyes & nervous system") implies a purity reasonable consumers would not expect to be compromised by endocrine-disrupting contaminants. This is unresolved litigation as of this pass, not a settled or dismissed finding. See [[prenatal-vitamins]] for the underlying folic-acid evidence (very strong — 77% NTD-risk reduction per a 296,816-participant umbrella review) — that evidence concerns the neural-tube-defect-prevention mechanism and is unaffected by the phthalate/BPA allegation, which is a contamination/purity claim, not an efficacy claim.

## Cons
- **Real, active, and directly on-point lawsuit alleging unsafe phthalate (DEHP) and BPA levels in this exact product** (Nature Made Prenatal Multivitamin Folic Acid + DHA Softgels) — DEHP is EPA-classified as a probable human carcinogen, and the cited literature links it to preterm birth, low birth weight, and pregnancy loss, i.e., harms specifically relevant to the exact population (pregnant/lactating women) this product targets
- This is a genuine contamination/purity allegation layered on top of a brand otherwise found to have unusually strong quality certification — a concrete illustration that USP Verification (which targets label-accuracy and specified contaminants) does not necessarily screen for every possible contaminant class, including plastic-packaging-derived phthalates/BPA
- No Nature-Made-Prenatal-specific efficacy trial (beyond the general folic-acid evidence in [[prenatal-vitamins]]) was located
- As of this pass, the lawsuit is unresolved — neither proven nor dismissed — so this should be read as a real, serious, pending allegation rather than an adjudicated fact
