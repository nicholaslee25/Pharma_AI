## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed: Zeasorb spans **two genuinely different product identities under one brand name** — the same "one brand, multiple mechanisms" pattern already documented for [[Gold Bond]] in this exact category:

- **Zeasorb AF / Athletes Foot / Jock Itch: miconazole nitrate 2%**, a genuine FDA OTC antifungal drug (see [[miconazole-nitrate-topical]], new ingredient page built this pass). Talc-free, paraben-free, aluminum-free, with patented odor-control technology. Twice-daily application for 4 weeks.
- **Zeasorb Excess Moisture Control / Prevention Powder: NO active ingredient at all** — a cosmetic, cornstarch/tapioca-starch-based moisture-absorbing powder (with tricalcium phosphate, maranta arundinacea root extract, calamine as inactive ingredients), explicitly confirmed by the manufacturer to contain no antifungal active. Marketed as absorbing "3x more moisture than plain talcum powder."

**Real, unresolved ambiguity:** the Pharmacy Times source data tracks a bare "Zeasorb" brand row (3% share in each of Jock Itch/Antifungal Products and Toe/Foot Antifungal Products) without specifying which sub-line drives that recommendation share — this pass cannot determine whether pharmacists are recommending the genuine antifungal "AF" line or the non-antifungal moisture-control line under the generic "Zeasorb" name, a real interpretive gap preserved here rather than resolved by assumption.

No Zeasorb-brand-specific clinical trial was located; the AF line's evidence sits at the [[miconazole-nitrate-topical]] ingredient level.

## Pros

- Talc-free, paraben-free, aluminum-free formulation across the whole brand — a real, positive formulation choice addressing talc-safety concerns that have affected the broader powder category historically
- Odor-control technology and high moisture-absorption claims are relevant, practical benefits for the tinea/moisture-prone body areas this category addresses, regardless of which sub-line is used

## Cons
- **Real, unresolved category-tracking ambiguity: it cannot be determined from the Pharmacy Times source data alone whether the "Zeasorb" recommendation share reflects the genuine antifungal AF line or the non-antifungal moisture-control line** — a meaningful gap for a category literally named "Jock Itch/Antifungal Products," since a consumer recommended "Zeasorb" without a specific SKU could end up with a product containing no antifungal activity whatsoever

## Use Cases
Treatment of jock itch and athlete's foot via miconazole nitrate's azole antifungal mechanism (AF/Athletes Foot/Jock Itch sub-line, twice daily for 4 weeks); moisture absorption and odor control with no antifungal action (Excess Moisture Control/Prevention sub-line) — the specific SKU determines which use case actually applies.

## Allergies / Warnings
AF sub-line: external use only; avoid eye contact; not for children under 2 years unless directed by a doctor; clean and dry area before application. Excess Moisture Control sub-line: standard cosmetic-powder precautions (no FDA drug warnings, given no active ingredient).

## Symptoms Treated
Itching, burning, and scaling from jock itch and athlete's foot, specifically via the miconazole-nitrate-based AF sub-line — not via the plain moisture-control powder, which treats no symptom directly.
