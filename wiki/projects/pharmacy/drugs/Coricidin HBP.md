## Overview
Coricidin HBP is a multi-SKU line unified by one genuinely distinctive brand feature confirmed via DailyMed: it is formulated **without a nasal decongestant** (no pseudoephedrine or phenylephrine in any SKU found), making it marketed specifically for cold/flu sufferers with high blood pressure ("HBP" = high blood pressure) who need to avoid decongestant-driven blood pressure spikes. Confirmed SKUs: base **Coricidin HBP Cold and Flu** = acetaminophen + chlorpheniramine maleate (antihistamine used here in place of a decongestant); **Maximum Strength Cold, Cough and Flu** = acetaminophen + dextromethorphan; **Maximum Strength Multi-Symptom Flu** = acetaminophen + chlorpheniramine + dextromethorphan; **Maximum Strength Cold and Flu Day and Night** = acetaminophen + dextromethorphan + guaifenesin ± doxylamine. ## Pros
- **Genuinely brand-distinguishing feature, not just marketing**: the confirmed absence of pseudoephedrine/phenylephrine across the SKUs checked means Coricidin HBP is a real alternative for hypertensive patients who need cold-symptom relief without a decongestant's vasoconstrictive/BP-raising effect — this is a legitimate clinical use case this brand serves that most competing combination products (DayQuil, Theraflu, Alka-Seltzer Plus) don't
- Antihistamine (chlorpheniramine) substitutes for a decongestant's drying effect on nasal secretions in some SKUs
- Multiple SKUs let users pick a formulation matching their exact symptom set (with/without cough suppressant, with/without antihistamine)

## Cons
- Despite the "HBP" branding signaling decongestant-avoidance, chlorpheniramine (a first-generation antihistamine, like Benadryl's diphenhydramine) still carries real sedation and anticholinergic risk — a hypertensive/elderly patient avoiding one risk (decongestant-driven BP rise) may be trading it for another (anticholinergic burden, fall risk, cognitive effects) — this is a real trade-off worth naming explicitly rather than treating "HBP-safe" as risk-free
- Still contains acetaminophen — the same 4,000mg/day duplication-risk consideration on [[acetaminophen]] applies fully here
- The brand name's blood-pressure-safety implication does not extend to every possible comorbidity a user might have (e.g. glaucoma, enlarged prostate — both cautioned against with chlorpheniramine specifically)

## Use Cases
Cold/flu symptom relief (fever, cough, congestion via antihistamine drying effect) specifically marketed for patients avoiding decongestants due to high blood pressure or other cardiovascular concerns.

## Allergies / Warnings
Ask a doctor before use with glaucoma, breathing problems, or enlarged prostate (antihistamine cautions). Do not use with other acetaminophen products. May cause drowsiness (chlorpheniramine-containing SKUs) — avoid alcohol, use caution driving.

## Symptoms Treated
Fever, body aches, cough, nasal congestion (via antihistamine, not decongestant mechanism), sneezing, runny nose.
