## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed: Lamisil AT cream is **terbinafine HCl 1%**, an allylamine antifungal (fungicidal via squalene-epoxidase inhibition — see [[terbinafine]]). **Real brand-specific evidence gap resolved this pass:** a placebo-controlled RCT explicitly naming the Lamisil brand — Budimulja et al. 2001 (Mycoses), "Once daily treatment with terbinafine 1% cream (Lamisil) for one week is effective in the treatment of tinea corporis and cruris" — found **84.2% mycological cure with a 7-day once-daily course vs. 23.3% for placebo**, with terbinafine also significantly superior on clinical response and symptom scores. This directly substantiates Lamisil's shorter (1-week) treatment course relative to [[clotrimazole]]'s 2-4 week regimens.

**Real head-to-head finding, second-largest brand vs. category leader's alternate mechanism:** a direct comparative trial found **butenafine (the active in [[Lotrimin]] Ultra, this category's leading brand) significantly outperformed terbinafine for tinea cruris** — mycological cure 94.87% (butenafine) vs. 62.16% (terbinafine) at day 42, a difference already significant by day 7. See [[butenafine]] for the full trial detail. This is a genuine, evidence-based efficacy gap between this category's #1 (Lotrimin, 49%, spanning both clotrimazole and butenafine sub-lines) and #2 (Lamisil AT, 28%) brands, not merely a marketing distinction.

## Pros
- Confirmed FDA Drug Facts label with a real, brand-named placebo-controlled RCT (84.2% vs. 23.3% mycological cure) — genuine trial-level evidence, not just general ingredient-class characterization
- Notably shorter treatment course (1 week for jock itch/ringworm, 1-2 weeks for athlete's foot) than [[clotrimazole]]'s 2-4 week regimens — a real adherence advantage
- Fungicidal mechanism (kills fungus directly) rather than clotrimazole's fungistatic mechanism (inhibits growth only)

## Cons
- **A direct head-to-head trial found butenafine (Lotrimin Ultra) significantly outperforming terbinafine for the same indication** (94.87% vs. 62.16% mycological cure) — a real, trial-confirmed efficacy disadvantage relative to a same-category competitor's alternate mechanism, not merely a smaller market share
- No Lamisil-AT-brand-specific trial for athlete's foot specifically was located in this pass (the Budimulja 2001 trial covered tinea corporis/cruris, not tinea pedis)
- Second-place recommendation share (28%) behind Lotrimin's combined 49%

## Use Cases
Treatment of jock itch (tinea cruris) and ringworm (tinea corporis) via once-daily application for 1 week; athlete's foot (tinea pedis) via twice-daily application for 1 week (between toes) or 2 weeks (bottom/sides of foot) — per [[terbinafine]]'s labeled dosing.

## Allergies / Warnings
Per [[terbinafine]]: wash affected area first, wear ventilated shoes, wash hands after application; external use only; discontinue and consult a doctor if no improvement within the labeled treatment duration.

## Symptoms Treated
Itching, burning, cracking, and scaling from jock itch, ringworm, and athlete's foot, via terbinafine's fungicidal mechanism.
