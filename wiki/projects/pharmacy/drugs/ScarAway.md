## Overview
**Confirmed category-placement finding:** ScarAway (made by Alliance Pharma Inc.) is regulated by FDA as a **medical device (510(k)-cleared)**, not an OTC drug. Its active ingredient/mechanism is **100% medical-grade silicone (polysiloxanes/silicon dioxide)** in gel form. This aligns with a real, confirmed FDA regulatory action: in 2004, the FDA's Federal Register classified **silicone sheeting intended for management of closed hypertrophic/keloid scars into Class I (general controls)** medical devices — a distinct regulatory pathway from the OTC drug monograph that governs a genuine drug product like [[Mederma]] (allantoin). ScarAway's mechanism (per manufacturer and general silicone-scar-therapy literature) is a moisture-balanced occlusive environment that is thought to reduce collagen overproduction and help flatten scar tissue — a physical/mechanical effect, not a pharmacological one, consistent with device rather than drug classification. A MAUDE adverse-event report exists for this product (Alliance Pharma Inc., ScarAway Silicone Scar Gel), reviewed only for its existence in this pass, not its clinical substance.

This is the same underlying regulatory concept already used elsewhere in this database for genuine medical devices (TENS units, blood glucose monitors, digital thermometers under Durable Goods/Diagnostics) — ScarAway is filed under `drugs/` for consistency with this database's convention, but should not be read as an FDA-approved drug.

Silicone gel/sheeting does have real supporting evidence in the broader scar-management literature (a Cochrane-adjacent systematic review on silicone gel for hypertrophic scars is referenced in general dermatology literature, not independently re-verified in this pass) — the device classification reflects its physical mechanism, not necessarily weaker evidence than a drug classification would imply.

No ScarAway-brand-specific clinical trial was located in this pass (the general silicone-gel-for-scars literature is the relevant evidence base).
