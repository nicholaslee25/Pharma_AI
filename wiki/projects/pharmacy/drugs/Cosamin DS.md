## Overview
**Real, unusually direct brand-to-evidence link found here**: Cosamin DS (Nutramax Laboratories) is, per independent reporting, the actual branded product form used to represent the "combined glucosamine + chondroitin" treatment arm in the landmark NIH-funded GAIT trial that anchors this database's [[glucosamine-chondroitin]] evidence page — a far more direct brand-to-trial connection than exists for any other joint-health brand in this database. That trial (1,583 patients) found the combined therapy did NOT significantly reduce pain in the general knee osteoarthritis population (p=0.09, borderline) — but DID find a significant, though exploratory, benefit specifically in the subgroup with moderate-to-severe baseline knee pain (79.2% responded vs. 54.3% on placebo, p=0.002). Because Cosamin DS is the actual tested product (not merely a same-ingredient-class brand), this subgroup finding applies to this specific brand more directly than to any of its category competitors. A follow-up MOVES trial (published in Annals of the Rheumatic Diseases) is referenced in industry sourcing as extending this research, though this pass did not independently pull its full results. Joint Health (14%) is covered by [[glucosamine-chondroitin]].

## Pros
- **Genuinely the most direct brand-to-clinical-trial link found in this entire Joint Health category**: Cosamin DS was reportedly the actual product tested in the landmark GAIT trial, meaning its evidence base is the real, primary-source trial data itself, not an inference from "same ingredient class"
- The real, if exploratory, significant subgroup benefit in moderate-to-severe knee pain patients (79% vs 54% improvement) is directly and specifically relevant to this product given the direct trial connection
- Adverse events in GAIT were mild, infrequent, and evenly distributed across all arms (including placebo) — no specific safety signal

## Cons
- **The GAIT trial's overall (non-subgroup) result for the combined therapy was NOT statistically significant** (p=0.09) — the primary, pre-specified endpoint found no significant pain reduction for the general osteoarthritis population, and the subgroup benefit, while real, was an exploratory (not pre-specified primary) finding that the trial's own authors caution has not been uniformly replicated in later literature
- This pass could not fully independently confirm the specific manufacturer-supplied-product detail connecting Cosamin DS to the GAIT trial formulation beyond industry/trade-press reporting — worth a closer primary-source check
- No additional Cosamin-DS-specific RCT beyond GAIT/MOVES was independently verified in this pass

## Use Cases
Knee osteoarthritis joint comfort — per [[glucosamine-chondroitin]]'s GAIT evidence, most plausibly beneficial specifically for patients with moderate-to-severe baseline knee pain (the one subgroup with a significant, if exploratory, response), rather than as a general-population treatment.

## Allergies / Warnings
Glucosamine is commonly shellfish-derived — a real, relevant allergy consideration. See [[glucosamine-chondroitin]] for the GAIT trial's safety data (mild, infrequent adverse events, no signal versus placebo).

## Symptoms Treated
Knee osteoarthritis pain, with the strongest (if exploratory) evidence specifically in moderate-to-severe baseline pain — see [[glucosamine-chondroitin]].
