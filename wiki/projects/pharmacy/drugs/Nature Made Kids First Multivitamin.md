## Appears In
Children's Multivitamins - 9%

## Overview
Confirmed via manufacturer labeling: "Nature Made Kids First Multivitamin with Omega-3" gummies combine a standard vitamin panel (A, C, D3 1000IU, E, Niacin, B6, Folate, B12, Zinc) with **113.5mg fish oil (25mg EPA + 5mg DHA omega-3 per serving)** — a genuine combination product, not a plain multivitamin. **The product contains fish (tuna) per its own allergen labeling** — a real, specific allergen relevant to fish-allergic children that would not be obvious from the "multivitamin" framing alone, and is not shared with any other brand in this Children's Multivitamins category researched so far. The Vitamin D3 dose (1000 IU) is on the higher end relative to typical children's multivitamin panels.

## Pros
- Omega-3 (EPA/DHA) inclusion connects this product to a nutrient class with its own separate evidence base on [[omega-3-fish-oil]], distinct from (and in addition to) standard multivitamin evidence — though this database did not independently verify whether that page's specific efficacy findings apply at this product's comparatively small 30mg total omega-3 dose
- No synthetic dyes, no artificial flavors, no high-fructose corn syrup per manufacturer labeling
- 1000 IU Vitamin D3 dose may better address common pediatric vitamin D insufficiency than lower-dose competitor formulations, though this was not independently confirmed against a pediatric vitamin D sufficiency target in this pass

## Cons
- **Contains fish (tuna) — a real, specific, and clinically significant allergen not shared by most competitor multivitamin brands in this category**, and one a caregiver might not expect from a product marketed simply as a "multivitamin" rather than a combination fish-oil product. This is a genuine safety-relevant finding worth flagging explicitly for fish-allergic children.
- The 30mg total omega-3 (25mg EPA + 5mg DHA) dose is small relative to typical adult omega-3 supplement doses discussed on [[omega-3-fish-oil]] — this database did not confirm whether this pediatric dose reaches any threshold associated with that page's own efficacy findings, so the omega-3 inclusion should not be assumed to confer the same benefit profile as a dedicated, higher-dose fish oil product
- Per [[multivitamins]]'s general evidence review: broad disease-prevention claims for multivitamin use are not established in a large modern adult RCT; no pediatric-specific outcome trial exists for this combination product
- No brand-specific pediatric RCT located for the vitamin+omega-3 combination as a unit

## Use Cases
General pediatric nutrient-gap supplementation combined with a small daily omega-3 (EPA/DHA) intake.

## Allergies / Warnings
**Contains fish (tuna) — not appropriate for fish-allergic children.** Otherwise standard multivitamin-gummy cautions (keep out of reach of children; do not exceed labeled serving).

## Symptoms Treated
General nutrient-gap correction plus a small daily omega-3 intake; not a treatment for any specific disease or symptom.

## Brand-Specific Alignment Check
