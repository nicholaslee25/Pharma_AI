## Appears In
Therapeutic Mouthwashes/Oral Rinses - 7%

## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed: Colgate Peroxyl Mouth Sore Rinse lists **hydrogen peroxide 1.5%** as its active ingredient, labeled as an **oral debriding agent/oral wound cleanser** — a materially different purpose than the antigingivitis/antiplaque or anticavity claims of most other brands in this category. It is intended for temporary use cleansing canker sores, minor gum inflammation, and minor oral wounds, not for routine daily plaque/gingivitis prevention. This confirms [[chlorhexidine]]'s existing note that "Colgate Peroxyl's real active ingredient is hydrogen peroxide, not chlorhexidine" — a category-placement nuance since it sits in the "Therapeutic Mouthwashes" list alongside genuine daily-use antigingivitis products.

## Pros
- Confirmed FDA Drug Facts label with a well-established oxidizing/debriding mechanism, useful specifically for actively cleansing an oral wound or sore rather than general plaque prevention
- Alcohol-free formulation, avoiding the tolerability/burning complaints associated with alcohol-containing rinses like classic Listerine
- Distinct, non-overlapping use case (acute wound/sore cleansing) from the daily-hygiene-focused competitors in this same category — a genuinely different job to be done

## Cons
- Not intended for more than 7 days of use unless directed by a dentist/physician — a real duration limit distinguishing it from daily-maintenance mouthwashes in this same category
- Provides no confirmed antigingivitis/antiplaque or anticavity benefit — a consumer using it as a general-purpose daily mouthwash (rather than for its labeled acute-cleansing purpose) would not get the same benefits as, e.g., Listerine, Crest Pro-Health, or Cepacol Antibacterial
- No brand-specific clinical trial evidence was identified for this specific product beyond the general recognized use of dilute hydrogen peroxide as an oral debriding agent

## Use Cases
Temporary cleansing of canker sores, minor oral wounds, and minor gum inflammation — an acute wound-care product, not a daily maintenance mouthwash.

## Allergies / Warnings
Per label: use up to 4 times daily after meals and at bedtime, or as directed; do not use for more than 7 days unless directed by a dentist or physician; discontinue if irritation or worsening occurs.

## Symptoms Treated
Cleansing/debriding of canker sores, minor oral wounds, and minor gum inflammation — via oxidizing action, not antibacterial plaque control.
