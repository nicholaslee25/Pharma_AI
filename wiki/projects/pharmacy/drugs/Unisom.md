## Appears In
Sleep Aids (non-melatonin) - 58%

## Overview
**Real, confirmed one-brand-two-molecules finding:** "Unisom" spans two chemically distinct active ingredients under one brand name, confirmed via DailyMed: **Unisom SleepTabs** is doxylamine succinate 25mg (see the new [[doxylamine]] ingredient page built for this finding), while **Unisom SleepGels** is diphenhydramine HCl 50mg (see [[oral-diphenhydramine-sleep-aid]]). Both are first-generation antihistamines used as OTC sleep aids, but they are genuinely different molecules with different (though related) pharmacology. This database cannot determine what fraction of Unisom's category-leading 58% share is attributable to each sub-product.

## Pros
- Product-line choice (SleepTabs vs. SleepGels) lets a consumer select between two different, established first-generation-antihistamine sleep-aid molecules under one trusted brand name
- Category-leading share (58%) reflects extensive real-world use history for both sub-products combined

## Cons
- **A consumer referring simply to "Unisom" cannot assume a single active ingredient** — SleepTabs (doxylamine) and SleepGels (diphenhydramine) are different molecules, a real navigation/safety-communication gap analogous to the "Dramamine" and "Midol" brand-family findings elsewhere in this database
- Both molecules share the same class-level AGS Beers Criteria "avoid in older adults" flag (anticholinergic burden, fall/delirium/dementia-association risk) per [[oral-diphenhydramine-sleep-aid]] and [[doxylamine]] — neither sub-product is meaningfully "safer" than the other on this specific dimension, despite being marketed as differentiated choices
- Neither sub-product has a dedicated, independently-located RCT with hard efficacy numbers specific to that molecule's OTC sleep-aid use — both rest on the same general "OTC sleep aids as a class lack robust efficacy evidence" finding already documented on [[oral-diphenhydramine-sleep-aid]]
- Not for use in children under 12 (either sub-product)

## Use Cases
Occasional sleeplessness — SleepTabs (doxylamine) or SleepGels (diphenhydramine), per which specific sub-product is used.

## Allergies / Warnings
Avoid combining either sub-product with other diphenhydramine- or doxylamine-containing products (additive anticholinergic/sedation risk). Avoid alcohol/other sedatives without consulting a doctor. AGS Beers Criteria recommends avoidance in most/all older adults for both molecules given anticholinergic-burden risk.

## Symptoms Treated
Occasional sleeplessness (both sub-products) — same low/mixed evidence tier documented on [[oral-diphenhydramine-sleep-aid]].
