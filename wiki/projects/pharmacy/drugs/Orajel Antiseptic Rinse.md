## Appears In
Therapeutic Mouthwashes/Oral Rinses - 3%

## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed: "Orajel Antiseptic Rinse for All Mouth Sores" and "Orajel Antiseptic Alcohol-Free Mouth Sore Rinse" both list **hydrogen peroxide and menthol** as active ingredients — an oxidizing antiseptic mechanism, mechanistically unrelated to the benzocaine-based anesthetic gels that drive Orajel's dominant Canker Sore Treatments (43%) and Toothache Products (68%) shares documented on `drugs/Orajel.md`. This is the same hydrogen-peroxide mechanism as Colgate Peroxyl in this same category, though at an unconfirmed relative concentration (Colgate Peroxyl is confirmed 1.5%; this Orajel product's specific percentage was not independently re-extracted in this pass).

## Pros
- Confirmed FDA Drug Facts label with a well-established oxidizing antiseptic mechanism for mouth sore cleansing
- Alcohol-free variant available, avoiding the tolerability/burning issues associated with alcohol-containing rinses
- Distinct, non-overlapping use case (mouth sore antiseptic cleansing) from Orajel's own benzocaine anesthetic gel line, giving the brand a genuinely differentiated secondary product rather than a redundant one

## Cons
- Provides no anesthetic/numbing relief — a consumer expecting "Orajel" to numb pain (based on the brand's dominant benzocaine-gel identity) would not get that effect from this specific rinse product
- Not intended for the same daily antigingivitis/antiplaque maintenance use as Listerine, Crest Pro-Health, or Cepacol Antibacterial in this same category — a similar acute-use, not daily-hygiene, positioning as Colgate Peroxyl
- No brand-specific clinical trial evidence was identified for this specific product

## Use Cases
Antiseptic cleansing and temporary pain relief for canker sores and other mouth sores via oxidizing action (hydrogen peroxide) plus mild cooling (menthol).

## Allergies / Warnings
Standard hydrogen-peroxide oral-rinse precautions: do not use for more than 7 days unless directed by a dentist/doctor; discontinue if irritation or worsening occurs; keep away from children.

## Symptoms Treated
Mouth sore cleansing and mild pain relief via oxidizing/cooling action — no true local anesthesia (unlike Orajel's benzocaine gel line).
