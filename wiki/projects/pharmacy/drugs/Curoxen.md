## Overview
This appears to be the same underlying product as **Curoxen Mouth Sore Relief** (see `drugs/Curoxen Mouth Sore Relief.md`, Canker Sore Treatments 7%) — the manufacturer's DailyMed listing is a single "Curoxen Oral Care" ointment marketed for mouth sores generally, including both canker sores and cold sores, rather than two chemically distinct products. Confirmed active ingredients: **Calendula 3X HPUS, Clove Essential Oil 1X HPUS, and Olive Extract (Oleuropein) 3X HPUS** — a homeopathic formulation explicitly marketed as "benzocaine-free." As with the Canker Sore Treatments entry, this carries an FDA NDC (manufacturer registration) but the product's own marketing states it is "not FDA evaluated," meaning no premarket efficacy review occurred.

## Pros
- Benzocaine-free — avoids the methemoglobinemia risk documented on [[topical-anesthetics]] for several benzocaine-based competitors in the broader oral-sore-treatment space
- All-natural/organic ingredient positioning for consumers avoiding synthetic anesthetics or petrolatum-derived products
- Same product marketed across two use cases (canker sores and cold sores) rather than requiring separate purchases — unlike several brands in this database that use genuinely different formulations per SKU

## Cons
- No clinical trial evidence (RCT or otherwise) was identified for this product's efficacy on cold sore duration, healing, or symptom relief — "not FDA evaluated" per its own labeling
- No antiviral mechanism — does not target herpes simplex virus replication/entry the way Abreva's docosanol (69% category leader) does
- Homeopathic dilution nomenclature (3X, 1X) reflects a system distinct from standard pharmaceutical concentration; consumers unfamiliar with the convention may misjudge ingredient strength

## Use Cases
Marketed for temporary relief of cold sores/fever blisters and canker sores, via a non-benzocaine, homeopathic ointment.

## Allergies / Warnings
Product-specific warnings not independently retrieved beyond general homeopathic-product labeling conventions; no benzocaine-related methemoglobinemia risk applies given the confirmed benzocaine-free formulation.
