## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed: Cortizone-10's FDA OTC Drug Facts label(s) list **hydrocortisone 1%** as the sole active ingredient across every identified SKU (Cooling Relief Gel, Cream, Intensive Healing Cream, Ointment, Plus Cream, Soothing Aloe Itch Relief Cream, Water Resistant Ointment, Ultra Soothing Creme, Sensitive Skin Creme, Easy Relief liquid) — this resolves the "plausibly hydrocortisone-based, not independently confirmed" gap previously flagged on [[hydrocortisone]]. This confirms Cortizone-10 is the by-far-largest (43%) brand in Insect Bite and Sting Management, and per [[hydrocortisone]] is also the leading brand in Topical Poison Ivy/Oak Remedies (19%) and Eczema Care/Relief Products (9%) elsewhere in this database. Per [[insect-bites]], no dedicated RCT for topical hydrocortisone specifically on insect-bite symptoms was located — the ingredient's broader anti-inflammatory/anti-itch evidence base applies, but the sharpest quantified efficacy data for this category (Karppinen 2000) actually concerns oral antihistamines, not topical hydrocortisone.

## Pros
- Confirmed FDA Drug Facts label(s), consistent active ingredient (hydrocortisone 1%) across all identified SKUs
- Well-established corticosteroid mechanism (see [[hydrocortisone]]) for itch/inflammation across a broad range of minor skin conditions, not just insect bites
- Category leader by a wide margin in Insect Bite and Sting Management (43%)

## Cons
- Does not address the bite/sting reaction mechanism itself or accelerate healing — symptom (itch/inflammation) relief only, per [[insect-bites]]
- No brand-specific clinical trial for Cortizone-10 was identified; efficacy evidence is at the hydrocortisone ingredient level, not product-specific
- Multiple SKUs under one brand name (gel, cream, ointment, liquid, "Plus," "Intensive Healing," "Sensitive Skin," etc.) — formulation differences beyond the shared 1% hydrocortisone (e.g., added aloe, moisturizers) were not individually itemized in this pass
- Per [[hydrocortisone]]'s safety data: should not be used on bacterial or fungal skin infections (masks signs, can worsen fungal infection appearance); not for use beyond 7 days without medical guidance

## Use Cases
Temporary relief of itching from insect bites and other minor skin irritations (eczema, psoriasis, poison ivy/oak/sumac, contact irritants, seborrheic dermatitis, external anal/genital itching).

## Allergies / Warnings
Per label: external use only; avoid eyes; not for diaper rash; not for genital use with vaginal discharge; do not exceed recommended dosage; stop use and consult a doctor if condition worsens or symptoms persist beyond 7 days or recur within a few days. See [[hydrocortisone]] for full contraindication and safety data (bacterial/fungal infection masking, skin atrophy with prolonged use, pediatric HPA-axis sensitivity).
