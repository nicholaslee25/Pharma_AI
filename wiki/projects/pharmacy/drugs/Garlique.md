## Overview
Garlique is the category-leading garlic-supplement brand tracked in this database (36% share). Confirmed via manufacturer product information: Garlique standardizes its formulations to specific **allicin yield** — Healthy Cholesterol Formula caplets yield at least 5,000 mcg allicin, Healthy Blood Pressure Formula at least 1,800 mcg, and Healthy Blood Sugar Formula at least 6,000 mcg. Allicin is the compound [[garlic]]'s own evidence page identifies as the key marker of garlic's biological activity, so this is a real, meaningful, checkable standardization claim rather than a vague "garlic extract" label — a genuine positive relative to unstandardized competitors. No Garlique-brand-specific clinical trial (as opposed to allicin-standardization data) was located in this pass; efficacy evidence is at the general garlic-ingredient level. Garlic Supplements (36%) is covered by [[garlic]] — real, quantified, multi-trial evidence exists for blood pressure (SBP −5.1mmHg, larger in hypertensive subgroups) and cholesterol (~10% LDL/total reduction, but specifically requiring >2 months use and elevated baseline cholesterol), though the review's own author notes these are surrogate-marker effects, not yet confirmed hard-outcome (MI/stroke/death) evidence.

## Pros
- Real, specific, checkable allicin-yield standardization across its three product-line variants (Cholesterol, Blood Pressure, Blood Sugar formulas) — allicin is the exact compound identified as garlic's key bioactive marker in [[garlic]]'s own evidence page, a meaningful positive versus unstandardized garlic products
- Product-line segmentation (separate cholesterol/blood-pressure/blood-sugar formulas) roughly maps onto the three surrogate-marker outcomes [[garlic]]'s evidence actually covers, rather than one generic "garlic" product implying all benefits at once

## Cons
- No Garlique-brand-specific clinical trial was located — the allicin-standardization data is a real formulation detail, but not itself a clinical outcome trial
- Per [[garlic]]'s own reviewed evidence, the underlying benefits are surrogate-marker effects (blood pressure, cholesterol numbers) with the review author explicitly stating hard cardiovascular outcomes (heart attack, stroke, death) remain unconfirmed by long-term trials — this applies to Garlique's marketing framing (Healthy Cholesterol/Blood Pressure/Blood Sugar) as much as to any other garlic brand
- The cholesterol-lowering effect specifically requires >2 months of use and pre-existing elevated cholesterol (>200 mg/dL) per the cited meta-analysis — not a universal or fast-acting effect, a nuance easily lost in "Healthy Cholesterol Formula" branding
- No independent third-party quality certification (USP Verified, NSF) was located for Garlique in this pass
