## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed/manufacturer sources: La Roche-Posay spans **three distinct mechanisms** across its four category appearances, a smaller but still real instance of the multi-mechanism-house-brand pattern seen throughout this pass ([[CeraVe]], [[Neutrogena]], [[Aveeno]], [[Cetaphil]]):

- **Acne Products (4%):** the "Effaclar" line splits into **salicylic acid** (1.5% Acne Treatment Serum, part of a "Triple Acid Complex" also containing glycolic acid and lipo-hydroxy acid) and **benzoyl peroxide** (4% Dual Action Face Wash, 5.5% Effaclar Duo spot treatment/BPO Multi-Target Treatment) — the same dual-acne-mechanism pattern already documented on [[Cetaphil]], [[Clearasil]], and [[AcneFree]].
- **Sunscreen (7%):** the "Anthelios" line is confirmed **chemical-filter-only** — avobenzone (2.7-3%), homosalate (9.6-10%), octisalate (2.9-5%), octocrylene (5.4-7%), with oxybenzone present on some but not all SKUs (e.g., present on the Ultra Light SPF 60 spray, absent on the Dermo Kids and Melt-In Milk lines) — no mineral-only (zinc oxide/titanium dioxide) La Roche-Posay sunscreen was located in this pass, a contrast with [[Aveeno]]'s mineral-only sunscreen line.
- **Therapeutic Skin Care, Cleansers (5%) and Moisturizers (1%):** the "Toleriane" line (Hydrating Gentle Cleanser, Double Repair Face Moisturizer) is a **non-drug cosmetic** formulated around ceramide-3, niacinamide, and glycerin — no FDA Drug Facts panel located, consistent with the cosmetic/drug split pattern seen across nearly every major skincare house brand in this pass.

## Pros
- Confirmed salicylic-acid and benzoyl-peroxide concentrations for the acne line fall within FDA-monograph OTC ranges, per [[salicylic-acid]] and [[benzoyl-peroxide]]
- Sunscreen line's chemical-filter concentrations are disclosed and consistent across most SKUs, with oxybenzone specifically omitted from several newer formulations (Dermo Kids, Melt-In Milk) — a real, consumer-relevant differentiation for those avoiding oxybenzone specifically
- Ceramide/niacinamide-based Toleriane cosmetic line is fragrance-free and formulated for sensitive/reactive skin, consistent with the brand's dermatologist-recommended positioning

## Cons
- **Three distinct mechanisms across four categories** — a consumer cannot assume ingredient consistency across "La Roche-Posay" products, the recurring pattern across nearly every major skincare brand in this pass
- Sunscreen line's chemical filters (avobenzone/oxybenzone-containing SKUs specifically) carry the same photostability and systemic-absorption open questions as any chemical-filter sunscreen; no La Roche-Posay-specific safety data beyond the general ingredient-level context was located
- No dedicated ingredient page exists in this database for niacinamide or ceramide-3 specifically as used in the Toleriane cosmetic line (partial overlap with [[moisturizers-ceramides]], not a full match)
- No La Roche-Posay-brand-specific RCT beyond general ingredient-level evidence was located for any of the four categories in this pass

## Use Cases
Acne treatment via salicylic acid or benzoyl peroxide (consumer's or product's choice); sun protection via chemical UV filters; general facial cleansing/moisturizing for sensitive skin (cosmetic-only Toleriane line, no medicinal claim).

## Allergies / Warnings
Acne line: per [[salicylic-acid]]/[[benzoyl-peroxide]], avoid eyes/mucous membranes, may bleach fabric/hair (BPO), discontinue if excessive irritation. Sunscreen line: standard chemical-filter sunscreen cautions (external use only, avoid eyes, discontinue if rash occurs); oxybenzone-containing SKUs carry the same general oxybenzone-sensitivity/absorption considerations as any oxybenzone sunscreen. Toleriane cosmetic line: no FDA Drug Facts warnings (no active ingredient); general cosmetic-allergen caution applies.

## Symptoms Treated
Acne (Effaclar line); sun protection (Anthelios line); none — cleansing/moisturizing only (Toleriane line).
