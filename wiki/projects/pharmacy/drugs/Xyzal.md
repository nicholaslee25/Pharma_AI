## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed OTC label: Xyzal Allergy 24HR's active ingredient is **levocetirizine dihydrochloride 5mg**. This replaces the earlier hedged pointer. Levocetirizine is the active (R)-enantiomer of cetirizine (Zyrtec's active ingredient) — pharmacologically related but its own distinct FDA-approved molecule/label. See [[second-generation-antihistamines]] for the broader class evidence (that page is written around cetirizine as the representative agent). Label saved at `raw/primary-sources/brands/xyzal/dailymed-xyzal.md`.

## Pros
- Also FDA-labeled for uncomplicated skin hives/urticaria itching, not just hay fever
- As the purified active enantiomer of cetirizine, dosed at half the milligram amount (5mg vs. cetirizine's 10mg) for a comparable effect
- Pediatric oral solution (2.5mg/5mL) available as a separate labeled product for children 6-11y

## Cons
- Labeled for evening dosing specifically (once daily in the evening) — a scheduling constraint not shared by all competitors
- Shares cetirizine's sedation profile and cross-reactivity risk (contraindicated if allergic to cetirizine or hydroxyzine)
- No independent confirmation was found in this pass of the NCT00375713 Xyzal-vs-Zyrtec dermatitis/eczema trial's actual results (only the registry listing was located, not outcome data)

## Use Cases
Seasonal/perennial allergic rhinitis; uncomplicated skin hives (urticaria) itching.

## Allergies / Warnings
Do not use if allergic to levocetirizine, cetirizine, or hydroxyzine. Ask a doctor before use with liver or kidney disease. May cause drowsiness — avoid alcohol; use caution driving or operating machinery.

## Symptoms Treated
Runny nose, sneezing, itchy/watery eyes, nose/throat itching, hives/skin itching.
