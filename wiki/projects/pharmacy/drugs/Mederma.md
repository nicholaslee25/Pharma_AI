## Overview
**Discrepancy flagged:** "Mederma" spans two entirely different regulatory categories depending on SKU. The dominant **Scar Treatments (56%, category leader)** share is driven by **Mederma Advanced Scar** gel, confirmed via DailyMed to contain **allantoin 0.5%** as its active ingredient (a skin protectant) — a genuine FDA OTC drug product. The **Cold Sore Treatments (1%)** share, however, is most plausibly driven by **Mederma Cold Sore Discreet Patch** — a **hydrocolloid gel patch** with no confirmed active pharmaceutical ingredient, functioning as a physical wound-covering/concealment device (similar in concept to a pimple patch) rather than a drug at all. No DailyMed listing was located for this patch product, consistent with it being a cosmetic/device product rather than an OTC monograph drug. This means Mederma's presence in this database's "Cold Sore Treatments" drug category may itself be a category-placement discrepancy, not merely a different-formulation-under-one-brand issue.

## Pros
- The Scar Treatments SKU (allantoin-based) is a confirmed, real OTC drug product with an established skin-protectant active ingredient
- The Cold Sore Discreet Patch, if genuinely non-medicated, avoids all the local-anesthetic and antiviral drug risks/interactions relevant to other cold-sore products in this database (methemoglobinemia, etc.) simply by not being a drug
- The patch format provides discreet concealment of the visible lesion, a cosmetic benefit most drug-based cold sore treatments do not offer

## Cons
- **The Cold Sore Discreet Patch does not appear to be a drug product at all** — if a consumer expects it to treat or accelerate healing of the underlying viral lesion the way Abreva's docosanol does, that expectation is not supported by any identified active pharmaceutical ingredient or clinical trial evidence; its function (per manufacturer marketing) is limited to reducing visible redness/swelling and concealment, not antiviral treatment
- No clinical trial evidence was identified for either Mederma SKU discussed here specific to cold sore outcomes
- A consumer searching "Mederma" for cold sore treatment could easily land on the Scar Treatments-focused Advanced Scar gel (allantoin, intended for post-healing scar appearance, not active-lesion treatment) rather than the Cold Sore Discreet Patch — the brand's dominant market identity (scar treatment) does not match its minor cold-sore listing

## Use Cases
Post-injury/post-surgical scar appearance improvement (Advanced Scar gel, allantoin); cosmetic concealment and reduction of redness/swelling for an active cold sore (Cold Sore Discreet Patch, hydrocolloid, non-drug).

## Allergies / Warnings
Advanced Scar gel: standard allantoin/skin-protectant precautions (external use, discontinue if irritation develops). Cold Sore Discreet Patch: no drug-specific warnings identified, consistent with it not being a confirmed drug product; general adhesive-patch skin-sensitivity caution applies.

## Symptoms Treated
Scar appearance/texture (Advanced Scar gel — the dominant, 56%-share use case); cosmetic concealment and reduced visible redness/swelling of an active cold sore (Cold Sore Discreet Patch) — no confirmed antiviral or healing-acceleration effect for the latter.
