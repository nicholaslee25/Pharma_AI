## Appears In

## Overview
**Discrepancy/near-duplicate finding:** Canker-X (manufactured by Alliance Pharmaceuticals Ltd.) is explicitly marketed as **benzocaine-free, alcohol-free**, using the same core ingredient list already documented for `drugs/GUM Canker-X.md`: water, polyvinylpyrrolidone (PVP), maltodextrin, aloe barbadensis leaf (aloe vera), and sodium hyaluronate. Both products use an identical barrier-film/bioadhesive mechanism ("coats, hydrates, and soothes" / "forms a protective barrier against irritants") rather than true local anesthesia. **This strongly suggests Canker-X and GUM Canker-X are the same underlying formulation, licensed or distributed under two different brand names** (GUM Canker-X's own page already notes it is marketed as based on the internationally-sold "Aloclair" mouth-ulcer gel) — this database did not independently confirm the exact corporate/licensing relationship between Alliance Pharmaceuticals and Sunstar (GUM's parent), but the near-identical ingredient lists and mechanism claims across two separately-branded, separately-tracked Pharmacy Times entries (Canker-X 6% here, GUM Canker-X Pain Relief Gel 3% in this same category, GUM Canker-X 5% in Canker Sore Treatments) make coincidental near-identical formulation unlikely.

## Pros
- No benzocaine, no alcohol — avoids the methemoglobinemia risk documented on [[topical-anesthetics]] entirely, same as GUM Canker-X
- Barrier-film mechanism may provide longer-lasting physical protection against further mechanical irritation than a dissolving gel
- Contains aloe vera, commonly associated with soothing/anti-inflammatory topical use (general property, not independently trial-verified for this specific product)

## Cons
- No clinical trial evidence specific to Canker-X itself was identified in this pass, consistent with the same gap already flagged for GUM Canker-X
- If Canker-X and GUM Canker-X are indeed the same underlying formulation marketed under two brand names (as the ingredient-list match strongly suggests but this database did not fully confirm), then this database's Pharmacy Times data may be counting what is effectively one product's recommendation share as if it were two separate, competing brands — a real potential double-counting concern worth flagging rather than silently accepting the category data at face value
- A barrier-film/PVP mechanism provides no true local anesthesia the way benzocaine does — pain relief is likely more indirect (via physical protection) than benzocaine competitors' direct numbing effect

## Use Cases
Marketed for temporary protection and pain relief of canker sores and mouth ulcers via a bioadhesive barrier film — identical use case to GUM Canker-X.

## Allergies / Warnings
No benzocaine-related methemoglobinemia risk given the confirmed alcohol-free, benzocaine-free formulation. Product-specific Drug Facts warnings beyond the manufacturer's own marketing claims were not independently retrieved in this pass.

## Symptoms Treated
Canker sore and mouth ulcer pain, via barrier-film protection rather than direct anesthesia.
