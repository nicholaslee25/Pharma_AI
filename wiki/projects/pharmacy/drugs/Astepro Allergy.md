## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed: Astepro Allergy = **azelastine HCl 205.5mcg/spray (equivalent to 187.6mcg azelastine)**, labeled as an **antihistamine**, not a corticosteroid. This is a genuinely different drug class from the corticosteroid products dominating this category (FLONASE, Nasacort, Nasonex) — no dedicated ingredient page exists in this database for azelastine or intranasal antihistamines generally, a real data gap since this brand can't be checked against an existing evidence-graded page the way the corticosteroid brands can against [[intranasal-corticosteroids]]. This is a new, previously-unlinked confirmation. Label saved at `raw/primary-sources/brands/astepro/dailymed-astepro.md`.

## Pros
- As a topical antihistamine rather than a corticosteroid, likely has a faster onset of symptom relief than the delayed-onset corticosteroid sprays dominating this category — a genuine, mechanism-based usability advantage for acute/as-needed use, though this page did not locate a head-to-head onset-time comparison to quantify the difference
- Available in a children's formulation (Children's Astepro Allergy) for pediatric perennial allergic rhinitis, per a registered trial (NCT01018862) evaluating safety/efficacy in that population — see `drugs/Children's Astepro.md` (researched 2026-08-27), which appears separately in this database's Children's Allergy category (3% share)

## Cons
- No dedicated ingredient page exists to provide a formal evidence grade, mechanism detail, or fuller safety profile for azelastine — flagged as a genuine research gap
- Per [[intranasal-corticosteroids]]'s own mechanism section, corticosteroids are documented as superior to antihistamines specifically at reducing nasal inflammation and mucosal pathology and more effective at relieving sneezing/rhinorrhea/blockage — meaning a reader should not assume Astepro is a strict substitute for the corticosteroid products it's categorized alongside, even though both treat allergic rhinitis
- Topical antihistamines in this class can carry a bitter taste and mild sedation risk in some users (general antihistamine-class knowledge; not independently confirmed against this product's specific incidence data in this pass)

## Use Cases
Temporary relief of nasal congestion, runny nose, sneezing, and itchy nose due to hay fever or other upper respiratory allergies.

## Allergies / Warnings
For nasal use only — do not spray in eyes or mouth. No corticosteroid-class warnings apply since this is a different drug class.
