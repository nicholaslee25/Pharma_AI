## Appears In
Acne Products - 20% (category leader)

## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed/FDA label (accessdata.fda.gov 021753s004lbl.pdf): Differin Gel's active ingredient is **adapalene 0.1%**, a third-generation topical **retinoid** — mechanistically distinct from the benzoyl peroxide/salicylic acid/sulfur mechanisms dominating most of the rest of this category (see [[Cetaphil]], [[PanOxyl]], [[Clearasil]], [[AcneFree]], [[Neutrogena]] elsewhere in this pass). Adapalene was originally prescription-only (approved 1996) and switched to OTC status in 2016 at the 0.1% strength — the only retinoid currently FDA-approved for OTC acne use; higher adapalene strengths (0.3%) and other retinoids (tretinoin, tazarotene) remain prescription-only. **New ingredient page created this pass:** [[adapalene]], since no retinoid page previously existed in this database despite Differin Gel holding the single largest share (20%) of any brand in Acne Products, and retinoids being mechanistically unrelated to every other acne active ingredient documented so far.

## Pros
- Confirmed single, disclosed active ingredient at its FDA-approved OTC concentration
- Retinoid mechanism (normalizes follicular keratinization, reduces microcomedone formation) is complementary to, not redundant with, benzoyl peroxide/salicylic acid — dermatology guidelines generally favor retinoid + BPO combination therapy over either alone
- Category-leading 20% pharmacist recommendation share in this database, consistent with dermatology-guideline-endorsed first-line status for comedonal acne
- Applied once daily (vs. some BPO regimens' 1-3x/day), a genuine convenience/adherence advantage

## Cons
- Retinoids carry a well-established initial "purging"/irritation phase (erythema, dryness, peeling) in the first weeks of use, distinct from and often more pronounced than BPO/salicylic-acid irritation — not disclosed anywhere in this database's Acne Products category page
- Per FDA label: photosensitizing — sunscreen/sun avoidance guidance applies, similar to other retinoids
- Pregnancy caution on label ("no specific evidence... causes birth defects... used topically as directed" — a hedged, not a cleared, statement); retinoids as a drug class carry known teratogenicity concerns systemically, and while topical absorption is low, the label itself does not affirmatively clear use in pregnancy
- No Differin-brand-specific RCT beyond the original prescription-era adapalene approval trials was independently located in this pass

## Use Cases
First-line topical treatment for comedonal and mild-to-moderate inflammatory acne vulgaris, applied once daily as a thin layer to affected areas.

## Allergies / Warnings
Per FDA label: for external use only; avoid eyes, lips, mucous membranes, and eczematous or sunburned skin; may increase sun sensitivity — use sunscreen and protective clothing; if pregnant or breastfeeding, ask a doctor before use; skin irritation (redness, dryness, scaling) is common, especially in the first 2-4 weeks — reduce frequency or discontinue if severe.

## Symptoms Treated
Acne vulgaris (comedonal and mild-to-moderate inflammatory) — via retinoid-mediated normalization of follicular keratinization, not antibacterial or keratolytic-exfoliant action.
