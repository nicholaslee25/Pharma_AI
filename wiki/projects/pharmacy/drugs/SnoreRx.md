## Overview
Confirmed: SnoreRx is an **FDA-cleared (510(k) pathway), Class II mandibular advancement device** — a boil-and-bite oral appliance with 1mm-adjustable calibration that repositions the lower jaw forward during sleep. Per [[snore-aid-devices]], MADs are the device subtype with the strongest RCT-level support in the broader sleep-medicine literature for actual airway/AHI improvement — a materially stronger evidence class than the nasal-strip/dilator subtypes elsewhere in this category. **Important labeling distinction, confirmed directly from SnoreRx's own FDA clearance:** the clearance covers snoring reduction specifically and explicitly **does not cover sleep apnea (OSA) treatment** — the RCT-level OSA evidence in the broader literature generally involves custom, dentist-fitted MADs, not necessarily this exact consumer boil-and-bite product. No SnoreRx-specific RCT beyond its 510(k) substantial-equivalence clearance (which does not require an independent efficacy trial) was identified.

## Pros
- Belongs to the MAD device subtype with the strongest general evidence base in this category, per [[snore-aid-devices]]
- FDA-cleared via 510(k), with adjustable calibration offering some degree of dentist-like customization without a dental visit
- Clear, explicit labeling scope (snoring only, not OSA) reduces the risk of a consumer over-relying on it for a diagnosed medical condition

## Cons
- **510(k) clearance confirms substantial equivalence and safety, not independently demonstrated efficacy** — no SnoreRx-specific RCT was identified beyond this regulatory pathway
- Per [[snore-aid-devices]], MADs as a class carry a real long-term risk of dental/skeletal side effects (bite changes, tooth movement, jaw discomfort) with regular use
- Explicitly not cleared for sleep apnea treatment — a consumer with suspected OSA should not substitute this product for medical evaluation

## Use Cases
Snoring reduction via mandibular (jaw) repositioning during sleep. Not a substitute for diagnosed-OSA treatment.

## Allergies / Warnings
General oral-appliance cautions: not appropriate for significant dental issues, missing teeth, or TMJ disorders; dental evaluation advisable before regular long-term use given the documented dental/skeletal side-effect risk for this device class.

## Symptoms Treated
Snoring (jaw-repositioning mechanism, opens the airway at the throat/retroglossal level) — not diagnosed OSA severity.
