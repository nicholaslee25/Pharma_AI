## Appears In
Cold Sore Treatments - 2%

## Overview
**Discrepancy flagged:** Confirmed via DailyMed (Quantum, Inc., "Lip Clear Lysine Plus" ointment): the FDA-recognized **active ingredient is menthol 0.1%** — L-lysine, despite being the brand's namesake and headline marketing claim, is listed only among the product's INACTIVE ingredients, alongside olive fruit oil, beeswax, calendula extract, echinacea extract, zinc oxide, and propolis extract. This is the same brand-name-implies-active-ingredient pattern already documented elsewhere in this database (Nature's Willow, Blue-Emu) — a consumer could easily assume "Lysine+" means lysine is doing the pharmacological work, when the FDA Drug Facts panel's actual active ingredient is plain menthol, an external analgesic/counterirritant with no antiviral mechanism.

## Pros
- Contains real olive oil, beeswax, and botanical extracts providing a moisturizing/protective base, similar in spirit to a medicated lip balm
- Menthol provides a mild, genuine cooling/counterirritant sensation that may offer some symptomatic comfort
- No benzocaine — avoids the methemoglobinemia risk documented on [[topical-anesthetics]] for several category competitors

## Cons
- **The "Lysine+" brand name implies L-lysine is the active treatment ingredient, but the FDA label lists lysine only among inactive ingredients** — menthol 0.1% is the sole active ingredient. Separately, oral L-lysine SUPPLEMENTS have their own (separately debated) evidence base for cold sore prevention/frequency reduction, but that evidence — even if real — would not transfer to this topical product's inactive-ingredient-level lysine content
- Per third-party reporting, this product is not FDA-approved specifically for treating cold sores in the way Abreva's docosanol is (menthol is a generically-approved external analgesic under the OTC monograph, not a cold-sore-specific antiviral)
- No antiviral mechanism — does not target herpes simplex virus replication or entry
- No brand-specific clinical trial evidence was identified for this product's effect on cold sore healing time or symptom relief

## Use Cases
Marketed for temporary relief of cold sore/fever blister discomfort via a menthol-containing moisturizing ointment.

## Allergies / Warnings
Standard menthol-product precautions: external use only, avoid eyes, discontinue if irritation develops or condition worsens.

## Symptoms Treated
Marketed relief of cold sore discomfort via mild menthol counterirritant action and a moisturizing/protective botanical base — no confirmed antiviral or lysine-driven pharmacological effect.
