## Overview
Confirmed: Little Remedies for Noses (marketed as "safe for newborns") lists **water, sodium chloride, glycerin, disodium phosphate, potassium phosphate, benzalkonium chloride, disodium EDTA** — an isotonic saline formulation with added glycerin (moisturizing) and phosphate buffers. Per [[saline-nasal-sprays]], isotonic saline has real Cochrane-level evidence for allergic-rhinitis symptom-severity benefit and is the standard safe choice for infants/young children, the population this brand specifically targets. No brand-specific clinical trial was identified beyond the general saline-class evidence.

## Pros
- Isotonic-only formulation is the appropriate, gentler choice for its target population (infants/newborns), consistent with [[saline-nasal-sprays]]'s special-population guidance
- Added glycerin provides extra moisturizing beyond plain saline
- Marketed specifically for the newborn/infant use case, a real product-market fit rather than a generic adult product repackaged

## Cons
- Contains benzalkonium chloride, a preservative some infants/sensitive users may react to
- No brand-specific outcome trial exists beyond the general saline-class evidence
- Isotonic-only means no additional congestion-specific osmotic benefit that hypertonic products offer for older children/adults (not a concern for the infant population this brand targets, since hypertonic saline isn't typically recommended for infants anyway)

## Use Cases
Relief of nasal congestion and dryness in infants and young children ("safe for newborns" per manufacturer).

## Allergies / Warnings
Contains benzalkonium chloride and phosphate buffers — general preservative-sensitivity consideration. Not FDA-evaluated as a drug (no active ingredient).

## Symptoms Treated
Infant/pediatric nasal congestion and dryness.
