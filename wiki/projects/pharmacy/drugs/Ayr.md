## Appears In
Saline Nasal Moisturizers - 26%

## Overview
Ayr sells multiple sub-formulations under one brand: a **gel** (glycerin, aloe vera, sodium chloride, carbomer/xanthan gum as thickeners — no propellant, designed to stay in place for chronic nasal dryness) and a separate **no-drip spray** (similar aloe/glycerin base with sodium chloride). This is a meaningfully different vehicle/delivery approach than the aqueous mist sprays that dominate the rest of this category (e.g. [[Simply Saline]], [[Mucinex Sinus]]) — see [[saline-nasal-sprays]] for the general saline-class evidence. Second-largest share in the category (26%). No Ayr-brand-specific clinical trial was identified; the gel format in particular is not directly addressed by the spray/irrigation-focused Cochrane evidence cited on [[saline-nasal-sprays]].

## Pros
- Gel format specifically designed for chronic nasal dryness (stays in place longer than a spray/mist), a genuine formulation advantage for that specific use case
- Aloe vera and glycerin add moisturizing/soothing properties beyond plain saline
- Second-largest recommendation share (26%), reflecting broad pharmacist familiarity across its multiple sub-formulations

## Cons
- The gel format's specific efficacy is not directly covered by [[saline-nasal-sprays]]'s cited Cochrane evidence, which is centered on spray/irrigation-format saline trials — a real evidence-applicability gap, not a claim the gel doesn't work
- Multiple sub-formulations (gel vs. spray, with different inactive-ingredient lists) mean a consumer needs to check which specific SKU they're using
- No brand-specific outcome trial exists for either format

## Use Cases
Moisturizing dry, irritated nasal passages (gel); general nasal congestion/dryness relief (no-drip spray).

## Allergies / Warnings
Contains aloe vera and, in some SKUs, benzalkonium chloride and other preservatives (chlorphenesin, methylisothiazolinone) — general allergy/sensitivity consideration for preservative-sensitive users. Not FDA-evaluated as a drug (no active ingredient).

## Symptoms Treated
Nasal dryness (gel format specifically well-suited to this); general congestion/irritation (spray format).
