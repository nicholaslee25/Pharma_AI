## Pros
- Combines three separately-evidenced ingredients (zinc, elderberry, vitamin C) in one lozenge — broader nutritional coverage than a single-ingredient zinc lozenge like Cold-EEZE
- 5mg zinc per lozenge is well under the 40mg/day adult tolerable upper limit discussed on [[zinc]], leaving more room before reaching that ceiling even with multiple lozenges/day
- USDA Organic certification (per product marketing) may appeal to consumers prioritizing that standard

## Cons
- 5mg zinc per lozenge is notably lower than Cold-EEZE's confirmed 13.3mg/lozenge — a real dose difference that means this product's zinc contribution alone would need more lozenges to match Cold-EEZE's per-dose amount, which matters given [[zinc]]'s Cochrane evidence being built around specific higher zinc-gluconate lozenge doses (45-276mg/day in the reviewed trials)
- As a dietary supplement without an FDA drug label, there's no standardized dosing/warnings panel to check against — reliance on manufacturer marketing rather than a regulatory document
- Combining three active-feeling ingredients in one product makes it harder to attribute any felt benefit to a specific one
