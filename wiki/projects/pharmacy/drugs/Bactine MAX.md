## Overview
**Discrepancy flagged: "Bactine MAX" spans at least three mechanically distinct formulations across its three category appearances**, confirmed via multiple DailyMed listings:
1. **Bactine MAX Pain Relieving Cleansing Spray** — benzalkonium chloride + lidocaine HCl (antiseptic + anesthetic, no antibiotic) — the likely basis for its **Topical Anesthetics** (3%) share.
2. **Bactine MAX Antibiotic and Lidocaine Max Pain Relief Ointment** — lidocaine 40 mg + bacitracin zinc 500 units + polymyxin B sulfate 10,000 units (anesthetic + 2-agent antibiotic combination, no benzalkonium chloride) — the likely basis for its **Burn Treatments** (5%) share, since this SKU adds real infection-prevention action beyond the spray.
3. **Bactine MAX Liquid Bandage** — lidocaine-containing liquid sealant marketed as killing "99.9% of germs" while providing a flexible waterproof cover — a mechanically different physical-device product (liquid skin sealant, see [[liquid-bandages]]) driving its **Liquid Bandages** (5%) share, unrelated to the ointment/spray chemistry above.
This is a more extreme version of the "single brand name spans mechanically unrelated sub-products" pattern seen elsewhere in this database — here, THREE different active-ingredient combinations sit under one brand name, each mapped to a different Pharmacy Times subcategory. A consumer buying "Bactine MAX" without checking the specific product name (Spray vs. Ointment vs. Liquid Bandage) cannot assume which of these three formulas they're getting.

## Pros
- Real product-line breadth: covers antisepsis-only (spray), antisepsis+antibiotic+anesthesia (ointment), and physical wound-sealing (liquid bandage) needs under one recognizable brand
- The ointment SKU's lidocaine (rather than benzocaine) anesthetic component carries the lower methemoglobinemia risk profile documented on [[topical-anesthetics]]
- The ointment SKU's bacitracin+polymyxin B (no neomycin) antibiotic combination avoids neomycin's specific higher-profile allergy risk, similar to [[Polysporin]]

## Cons
- **Three different active-ingredient formulas under one brand name, mapped across three different Pharmacy Times subcategories** — a genuine, real discrepancy risk if a consumer assumes brand-name consistency implies ingredient consistency
- The Liquid Bandage SKU's antimicrobial marketing claim ("kills 99.9% of germs") is a cleaning/disinfection claim about the product's own application process, not a therapeutic antibiotic claim in the same sense as the ointment's bacitracin/polymyxin B — these should not be conflated
- No brand-specific clinical trial identified for any of the three formulations beyond general ingredient-level evidence

## Use Cases
Depends entirely on the specific SKU: antiseptic-only cleansing and pain relief (Spray); infection prevention plus pain relief for burns/cuts (Ointment); flexible waterproof wound sealing with antimicrobial action for hard-to-bandage areas (Liquid Bandage).

## Allergies / Warnings
Per label(s): for external use only across all three formulas; do not use if allergic to lidocaine or other amide-class local anesthetics; the Ointment SKU carries the same bacitracin/polymyxin B contact-allergy considerations as [[Polysporin]]. Always check the specific product name/SKU, not just the "Bactine MAX" brand name, before assuming ingredient content.

## Symptoms Treated
Pain and itching from minor cuts/scrapes/burns (all SKUs, via lidocaine); infection-risk reduction (Ointment and, via antisepsis, Spray and Liquid Bandage).
