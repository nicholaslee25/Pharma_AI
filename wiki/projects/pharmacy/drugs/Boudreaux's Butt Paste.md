## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed: Boudreaux's Butt Paste spans **Original (16% zinc oxide), Maximum Strength (40% zinc oxide), and "All Natural" (also 16% zinc oxide, aloe-based inactive ingredients replacing the petrolatum/mineral-oil base)** — a real, quantified concentration spread (16% vs. 40%) matching the same pattern just confirmed for Desitin in this same category, and both brands' Maximum Strength lines converge on the same 40% figure, plausibly the FDA OTC monograph's upper concentration limit for zinc oxide as a skin protectant. **New allergen finding, not previously flagged for any zinc-oxide diaper-rash brand in this database:** both the Original AND the "All Natural" formulations list **Balsam of Peru (Peruvian Balsam)** as an inactive ingredient — one of the most common contact allergens identified in dermatology patch testing generally, and its presence here is a genuine, real consideration for skin-sensitive infants that "All Natural" branding does not address (removing petrolatum/mineral oil did not remove this allergen).

## Pros
- Confirmed FDA Drug Facts labels for Original, Maximum Strength, and All Natural variants, matching [[zinc-oxide]]'s barrier-protectant evidence base
- Two-tier concentration system (16% vs. 40%) allows matching product strength to rash severity, similar to Desitin's approach in this same category
- "All Natural" variant removes petroleum-derived inactive ingredients (mineral oil, paraffin, white petrolatum) for caregivers specifically seeking to avoid those, while maintaining the same 16% zinc oxide active-ingredient concentration as Original

## Cons
- **Same brand-name/SKU concentration ambiguity as Desitin**: this database's category-mapping data ("Boudreaux's Butt Paste," 13%/11% share) does not specify Original (16%), Maximum Strength (40%), or All Natural (16%) — a real, quantified concentration spread under one brand name
- **New allergen finding: Balsam of Peru** appears as an inactive ingredient in both Original and "All Natural" formulations — a well-documented, common contact-sensitizer in general dermatology literature, present regardless of which "natural vs. standard" formulation is chosen, undermining the assumption that "All Natural" necessarily means lower allergy risk
- Per [[zinc-oxide]]'s own evidence review: overall evidence quality for zinc-oxide barrier preparations is graded low-level (Grade C) — applies here regardless of concentration or "natural" positioning
- No Boudreaux's-specific RCT was independently located in this pass beyond the general zinc-oxide evidence base

## Use Cases
Prevention and treatment of diaper rash (irritant diaper dermatitis) in infants; prevention/treatment of incontinence-associated skin irritation in adults — via zinc oxide's barrier-protectant mechanism, at either 16% or 40% concentration depending on product line.

## Allergies / Warnings
Per label: for external use only; avoid eye contact; discontinue and consult a doctor if condition worsens or does not improve within a week. **Contains Balsam of Peru (a recognized contact allergen) as an inactive ingredient in both Original and All Natural formulations** — caregivers of infants with known fragrance/balsam sensitivities should check this specifically, since it is not addressed by the product's "all natural"/hypoallergenic-adjacent marketing. Keep away from children to prevent accidental swallowing; contact Poison Control if ingested.

## Symptoms Treated
Diaper rash / irritant diaper dermatitis (infants); incontinence-associated dermatitis (adults) — via zinc oxide's moisture-barrier mechanism, per [[zinc-oxide]].
