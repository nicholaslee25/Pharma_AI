## Appears In
Toothpaste, General Use - 4%

## Overview
**Discrepancy flagged:** Arm & Hammer's entire brand identity is built around **baking soda (sodium bicarbonate)**. However, confirmed via DailyMed — on **Arm & Hammer Complete Care Whole Mouth Protection**, the product's own FDA Drug Facts panel lists **sodium fluoride 0.24% (0.15% w/v fluoride ion)** as the sole active ingredient (anticavity, "aids in the prevention of dental decay"), with **sodium bicarbonate relegated to the INACTIVE ingredients list**. This is the same pattern already documented for CloSYS (chlorine dioxide marketed as the defining technology but not the FDA "active ingredient" on its fluoride SKU, see `drugs/CloSYS.md`) — the ingredient Arm & Hammer markets as its core identity is not the one FDA recognizes as therapeutically active on this SKU. Other Arm & Hammer lines (Kids Healthy Teeth, Truly Radiant, Enamel Defense) are similarly sodium fluoride- or sodium-monofluorophosphate-based per DailyMed, following the same active/inactive split.

## Pros
- The Complete Care SKU carries fluoride's genuine, Cochrane-backed anticaries evidence base per [[fluoride-toothpaste]], regardless of the baking-soda-labeling nuance
- Unlike CloSYS's chlorine dioxide (only supported by a single N=39 RCT), Arm & Hammer's marketed baking-soda technology has a real, larger published evidence base: a double-blind crossover study (n=34) found baking-soda dentifrice removed significantly more plaque than Crest and Colgate comparators, and a pooled analysis across 5 clinical studies (>270 subjects) found baking-soda dentifrices enhanced plaque removal significantly more than non-baking-soda dentifrices
- A separate 6-week RCT found Arm & Hammer's baking-soda-and-peroxide whitening dentifrice more effective at stain reduction/whitening than a silica-based control — real, if company-adjacent, supporting evidence for the marketed technology specifically

## Cons
- **Brand-identity mismatch:** consumers who buy Arm & Hammer specifically for "the baking soda toothpaste" are, per its own FDA label, actually relying on sodium fluoride for the product's officially recognized anticavity claim — baking soda's plaque/whitening benefit is real but exists on a separate evidentiary track from the FDA "active ingredient" designation
- The plaque-removal and whitening studies cited above are baking-soda-ingredient-class research, not independently confirmed to have used the exact Complete Care Whole Mouth Protection SKU specifically — brand-vs-study-product correspondence not individually verified in this pass
- No independent (non-manufacturer-sponsored) replication of the Crest/Colgate plaque-removal comparison was identified

## Use Cases
Cavity prevention (sodium fluoride, the FDA-recognized active ingredient) and, per separately published clinical literature, enhanced plaque removal and tooth whitening from the baking-soda formulation (not itself the FDA "active ingredient" on the fluoride SKU).

## Allergies / Warnings
Per label: do not use if irritation occurs and persists; keep out of reach of children under 6; get medical help or contact Poison Control if swallowed in excess; rinse away toothpaste residue thoroughly after brushing (a directive specific to this brand's label, not universal across all toothpaste brands checked). Standard fluoride precautions per [[fluoride-toothpaste]] otherwise apply.

## Symptoms Treated
Cavity prevention (fluoride, FDA-recognized); plaque accumulation and surface tooth staining (baking soda, independently trial-supported but not the FDA "active ingredient" designation).
