## Appears In
Topical Poison Ivy/Oak Remedies - 6%

## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed across two related SKUs: **"Ivy-Dry Cream"** (benzyl alcohol 10% + camphor 0.6% + menthol 0.4%) and **"Ivy-Dry Super"** spray (benzyl alcohol 10% + camphor 0.5% + menthol 0.25%), both labeled "external analgesic" for temporary relief of itching from insect bites and minor skin irritations. Unlike Caladryl/Ivarest, this brand contains **no calamine and no diphenhydramine** — its anesthetic effect comes from benzyl alcohol (a mild local anesthetic/preservative) combined with low-concentration camphor/menthol counterirritant cooling. Both camphor and menthol concentrations here (0.4-0.6% and 0.25-0.4% respectively) are **well below** the FDA's 2012 burn-risk thresholds (menthol >3%, methyl salicylate >10%) documented on [[topical-counterirritants]] — no methyl salicylate is present at all, so this brand carries neither the burn risk flagged for AleveX/Icy Hot/BENGAY nor the NSAID-class systemic salicylate risk flagged for Aspercreme/Salonpas.

No Ivy-Dry-brand-specific clinical trial was located in this pass; evidence rests on the general benzyl-alcohol/camphor/menthol ingredient literature.

## Pros
- Consistent core formula (benzyl alcohol + low-concentration camphor/menthol) across both Cream and Super spray SKUs
- Camphor and menthol concentrations are meaningfully below the FDA's own burn-risk thresholds — genuinely lower-risk than several higher-concentration counterirritant products documented elsewhere in this database
- No methyl salicylate, avoiding the NSAID-class systemic salicylate risk (aspirin-allergy cross-reactivity, pregnancy warning) present in some competing analgesic-labeled poison-ivy products
- No diphenhydramine, avoiding the systemic-absorption toxicity risk documented for Ivarest and other diphenhydramine-based competitors on [[topical-diphenhydramine]]

## Cons
- No dedicated ingredient page exists in this database for benzyl alcohol as a topical analgesic specifically — a real coverage gap, though its role here is a minor, well-established one relative to the ingredient's broader (mostly preservative/cosmetic) use
- Not for use on face, genital areas, or broken/blistered skin, or in children under 6 (Super spray) — a notably higher pediatric age floor than most competitors in this category (Caladryl, Ivarest, CeraVe Itch Relief all permit use from age 2 with a doctor's consultation for younger children)
- No Ivy-Dry-brand-specific clinical trial was located

## Use Cases
Temporary relief of itching associated with insect bites, minor skin irritations, and poison ivy/oak/sumac rashes.

## Allergies / Warnings
Per label: for external use only; do not use on face, genital areas, or broken/blistered skin; avoid eye contact (flush with water if exposed); not for children under 6 (Super) or without physician approval under 2 (Cream); stop use and consult a doctor if condition worsens or symptoms persist beyond 7 days; consult a doctor if pregnant or nursing.

## Symptoms Treated
Itching from insect bites, minor skin irritations, and poison ivy/oak/sumac rashes.
