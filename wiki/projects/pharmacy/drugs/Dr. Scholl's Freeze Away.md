## Appears In

## Overview
**Regulatory-category discrepancy, confirmed via FDA 510(k) documentation:** Dr. Scholl's Freeze Away is not a drug at all — it is an **FDA-cleared Class II medical DEVICE** (510(k) K031697, cleared 2003; a related model K052259 cleared 2005), a cryosurgical system that dispenses a **dimethyl ether + propane cryogen** to freeze warts, reaching an average applicator-surface temperature of **-42°C**. This is filed and pharmacist-recommended (28% share, second-largest in the category) within "Wart Removers" alongside Compound W (a genuine salicylic-acid OTC drug) — a real regulatory-category mismatch of the same kind already documented elsewhere in this database (ThermaCare/Beyond BodiHeat devices filed alongside chemical counterirritant drugs in Thermal Relief Products).

Per the Cochrane review already summarized on [[salicylic-acid]] (Kwok et al. 2012, 85 trials/8,815 participants): **cryotherapy vs. placebo showed no statistically significant benefit (RR 1.45, 95% CI 0.65-3.23)**, and cryotherapy vs. salicylic acid head-to-head showed **no significant difference (RR 1.23, 95% CI 0.88-1.71)** — meaning the two mechanisms perform comparably overall, contrary to a common assumption that freezing is the "stronger" option. Aggressive cryotherapy did beat gentle cryotherapy (RR 1.90, 95% CI 1.15-3.15) but with more adverse effects — a real efficacy/tolerability tradeoff relevant to how forcefully/frequently a consumer applies this device. A related Dr. Scholl's SKU, "Dual Action Freeze Away," adds a separate salicylic acid 17% liquid as a post-cryo treatment — combining both mechanisms in one product line, consistent with the Cochrane finding that SA + cryotherapy combined outperforms either alone (RR 1.24, 95% CI 1.07-1.43).

FDA MAUDE adverse-event reports exist for this device line (including a report specifically for the "Freeze Away Max" plantar variant) — consistent with normal post-market surveillance for a Class II device, not independently characterized as a systemic safety signal in this pass beyond noting the reports exist.

## Pros
- Genuine FDA 510(k)-cleared medical device with defined performance specifications (-42°C applicator temperature), not merely an unregulated cosmetic claim
- Per the Cochrane data on [[salicylic-acid]], cryotherapy performs comparably to salicylic acid overall — a real, evidence-supported alternative mechanism, not a weaker "placebo" option despite the cryotherapy-vs-placebo comparison itself not reaching significance
- Faster treatment course than salicylic acid's up-to-12-week regimen — freezing sessions are typically brief and infrequent rather than daily
- "Dual Action" SKU combines cryotherapy with salicylic acid, matching the Cochrane-supported combination-therapy advantage (RR 1.24 over either alone)

## Cons
- **Regulatory-category mismatch: this is an FDA-cleared device, not an FDA-monograph OTC drug**, unlike Compound W and (per the ingredient-level evidence at least) most competitors in this category — a distinction the "Wart Removers" drug-category framing does not surface
- Cryotherapy alone vs. placebo did NOT reach statistical significance in the Cochrane meta-analysis (RR 1.45, 95% CI 0.65-3.23, crossing 1) — a genuinely underappreciated finding given how "medical-grade freezing" is often assumed to be clearly effective
- Same diabetes/poor-circulation contraindication as salicylic acid products, though via a different mechanism (frozen tissue may not heal as quickly or completely, per manufacturer safety guidance) — not from a shared toxicological pathway, but converging on the same at-risk population
- FDA MAUDE adverse-event reports exist for this device line — the specific nature/frequency of reported events was not independently characterized in this pass
- No independent trial specific to Dr. Scholl's own device (as opposed to the general cryotherapy-mechanism Cochrane data) was located

## Use Cases
Freezing/cryosurgical removal of common and plantar warts on hands and feet.

## Allergies / Warnings
Per manufacturer safety guidance: do not use if diabetic or with poor blood circulation, as frozen tissue may not heal as quickly or completely; common side effects include minor skin irritation, rash, itching, stinging, or dryness at the treatment site.

## Symptoms Treated
Common warts and plantar (foot) warts.
