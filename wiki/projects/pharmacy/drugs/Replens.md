## Overview
Replens is confirmed via FDA 510(k) clearance records (K101098, K101241) and manufacturer ingredient disclosure to be a polycarbophil/carbomer-homopolymer-based bioadhesive gel — purified water, glycerin, mineral oil, polycarbophil, carbomer homopolymer type B, hydrogenated palm oil glyceride, and pH-adjusting/preservative ingredients. It is FDA-cleared as a **Class II medical device**, not a drug, for vaginal moisturizing/lubrication. Per [[vaginal-lubricants-moisturizers]], Replens measured 2,143 mOsm/kg in the Edwards & Panay 2015 review — moderately hyperosmotic, above the WHO's 380 mOsm/kg ceiling, causing mild-to-moderate irritation in the cited slug-mucosal-irritation testing model. Replens shares its core bioadhesive mechanism with [[RepHresh]], a real, confirmed functional overlap between two brands tracked separately in this database.

## Pros
- FDA 510(k)-cleared medical device status provides at least a baseline regulatory review pathway, distinct from the fully unregulated cosmetic status of several other products in this category (e.g., [[Balneol]], [[Summer's Eve]])
- Polycarbophil/carbomer bioadhesive mechanism is a genuine, functioning approach for sustained-release vaginal moisturizing, appropriate for regular (not just per-use) application per its intended GSM-comfort use case
- Second-largest share (16%) in this category, reflecting substantial real-world use history

## Cons
- **Confirmed hyperosmolar (2,143 mOsm/kg)**, above the WHO's recommended 380 mOsm/kg ceiling, causing mild-to-moderate irritation in independent testing — one of three top-selling products in this category (alongside [[K-Y]] and [[Astroglide]]) confirmed hyperosmolar in the same source
- Functionally near-identical to [[RepHresh]] (same polycarbophil/carbomer mechanism) despite being tracked as a separate competing brand — no meaningful formulation differentiation was found in this pass
- A separate Pre-Seed-related fertility study found Replens performed worse than Pre-Seed specifically on sperm-quality/motility measures in an in vitro model — a real, brand-specific negative finding relevant to anyone using this product while trying to conceive, distinct from its general GSM-comfort use case

## Use Cases
Regular-use (not just per-sexual-activity) vaginal moisturizing for dryness, particularly genitourinary syndrome of menopause (GSM) — per [[vaginal-lubricants-moisturizers]], a first-line nonhormonal comfort option, though local vaginal estrogen achieves substantially higher (80-90%) symptomatic success where medically appropriate.

## Allergies / Warnings
Hyperosmolar-formulation caution applies (see [[vaginal-lubricants-moisturizers]] for the epithelial-irritation/infection-susceptibility mechanism, most concretely documented for [[K-Y]]). Not established as fertility-friendly — a fertility study found reduced sperm quality/motility with Replens exposure relative to Pre-Seed specifically.

## Symptoms Treated
Vaginal dryness, GSM-related discomfort (comfort/moisturizing use, not a cure).
