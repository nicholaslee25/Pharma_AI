## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed: the base **BC Powder / BC Cherry / BC Arthritis** lines list **aspirin (NSAID) + caffeine** as active ingredients (aspirin as pain reliever/fever reducer, caffeine as "pain reliever aid") — no acetaminophen in this base formulation. A separate sub-line, **BC Max**, adds acetaminophen 500 mg to the same aspirin 500mg + caffeine 65mg base, making it a triple-combination product structurally identical in ingredient category to Goody's Powder and Excedrin. So "BC Powder" spans both a 2-ingredient (aspirin + caffeine) and a 3-ingredient (aspirin + acetaminophen + caffeine) formulation depending on sub-line — the same multi-formulation-under-one-brand pattern documented elsewhere in this database. BC/Goody's/Stanback powders originated in and remain particularly popular in the American South, a real regional-brand-history note distinct from most other brands in this database.

## Pros
- Confirmed FDA Drug Facts label with well-established active ingredients (aspirin ± acetaminophen, plus caffeine as an analgesic adjuvant)
- Powder format dissolves quickly, a genuine format-level difference from tablet/caplet competitors that some consumers prefer for faster perceived onset (not independently verified via pharmacokinetic comparison in this pass)
- BC Max's triple-combination formulation carries the same general combination-analgesic rationale as Excedrin (per `drugs/Excedrin.md`), though no BC-specific RCT (as opposed to Excedrin's dedicated Lipton 1998 trial) was identified

## Cons
- **Real documented safety concern found in this pass, specific to chronic use of this category of high-dose combination powder:** published case reports describe serious adverse outcomes from prolonged BC Powder use, including a case report of chronic salicylate toxicity progressing to acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS), and a separate case of kidney failure after ~4 years of regular use combined with other medications — consistent with the broader "analgesic nephropathy" literature associated with chronic combination-analgesic use (historically most strongly linked to older phenacetin-containing combinations, but caution is still warranted with modern aspirin/acetaminophen/caffeine combination powders used chronically)
- High per-dose active-ingredient content (aspirin 500-845mg + caffeine per packet depending on source/SKU) means the combined GI-bleed, Reye's-syndrome, and caffeine-dependence/withdrawal risks are all present simultaneously
- Caffeine dependence/rebound-headache potential is a real, documented concern with frequent use of caffeine-containing analgesic combinations — the label itself warns to limit other caffeine sources while using this product
- "BC Powder" is not one fixed formulation — base BC/Cherry/Arthritis lines are aspirin + caffeine only, while BC Max adds acetaminophen — a consumer should check the specific packet

## Use Cases
Headache and minor arthritis pain relief via aspirin (± acetaminophen in BC Max) plus caffeine as an analgesic adjuvant.

## Allergies / Warnings
Per label: limit other caffeine-containing medications/foods/beverages while using this product, since excess caffeine may cause nervousness, irritability, sleeplessness, and occasionally rapid heartbeat. Standard aspirin warnings apply (Reye's syndrome risk in children/teens with viral illness, GI bleeding risk, ask a doctor before use if taking a blood thinner) per [[aspirin]]. BC Max additionally carries acetaminophen's hepatotoxicity risk per [[acetaminophen]] — avoid combining with other acetaminophen-containing products.

## Symptoms Treated
Headache, minor arthritis pain, general minor aches.
