## Overview
K-Y (Reckitt) is this database's category-leading lubricant brand (39% share), and carries the single most serious independent safety finding located across this database's entire Women's Health research pass. Per [[vaginal-lubricants-moisturizers]]: K-Y Jelly measured 2,463 mOsm/kg in the Edwards & Panay 2015 review — well above the WHO's 380 mOsm/kg safety ceiling. **Separately, Johns Hopkins researchers found K-Y Warming Jelly specifically (reported osmolality over 30x normal body fluid) increased herpes simplex virus transmission more than NINEFOLD in a mouse model** compared to no lubricant at all, and broader research on hyperosmolar lubricants as a class (K-Y included) documents epithelial-barrier disruption and toxicity to protective vaginal lactobacilli — a real, biologically-grounded mechanism connecting hyperosmolality to increased infection susceptibility, not merely a comfort/irritation issue.

## Pros
- Long-established, widely available, and trusted brand with decades of market presence
- Multiple product lines exist (this database did not exhaustively verify every K-Y SKU's individual osmolality — the Warming Jelly and standard Jelly formulations are the specifically tested products cited above)

## Cons
- **Real, serious, independently published safety finding: K-Y Warming Jelly increased herpes transmission ninefold in a controlled animal study** — this is the most significant single safety finding located for any brand across this entire Women's Health/vaginal-lubricant research pass, and it applies to the category's single largest-share product (39% of all recommendations)
- Both standard K-Y Jelly (2,463 mOsm/kg) and (per the Johns Hopkins research) K-Y Warming Jelly are confirmed hyperosmolar, well above the WHO's 380 mOsm/kg recommended ceiling
- Hyperosmolar lubricants are documented to damage the vaginal epithelial barrier and reduce protective lactobacilli — a plausible mechanism for the herpes-susceptibility finding above, not an isolated one-off result
- Real disconnect between market popularity (this category's single largest share) and independently measured/tested tissue-safety data — the most-recommended product in this category carries the most serious documented safety concern

## Use Cases
Personal lubrication to reduce friction during sexual activity (per label) — general symptomatic/comfort use, not a treatment for GSM or any diagnosed condition.

## Allergies / Warnings
**Real, evidence-based caution:** hyperosmolar formulations (including K-Y Warming Jelly specifically) are associated with increased susceptibility to sexually transmitted infections including HSV-2 in controlled research, and with disruption of protective vaginal flora — individuals at elevated STI-exposure risk, or those prone to bacterial vaginosis/recurrent vaginal infections, may wish to discuss lower-osmolality alternatives (e.g., Good Clean Love, per [[vaginal-lubricants-moisturizers]]) with a healthcare provider.

## Symptoms Treated
Vaginal dryness/friction during sexual activity (comfort use only).
