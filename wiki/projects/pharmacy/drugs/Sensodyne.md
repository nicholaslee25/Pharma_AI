## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed: the core Sensodyne toothpaste formula lists **potassium nitrate 5%** (antihypersensitivity) + **sodium fluoride 0.24% (0.15% w/v fluoride ion)** (anticavity) as active ingredients — matching [[potassium-nitrate]]'s existing confirmed entry and Sensodyne's category-dominant 65% share of Toothpaste, Sensitive Gums/Teeth. **Discrepancy flagged:** despite Sensodyne's brand identity being built almost entirely around potassium nitrate, at least one confirmed sub-line — **Sensodyne Sensitivity, Gum & Enamel** — uses a completely different active ingredient, **stannous fluoride 0.454%**, for a triple anticavity/antigingivitis/antihypersensitivity purpose (the same ingredient and mechanism already documented for Crest Pro-Health and Crest Gum & Sensitivity). This is the same "one master brand, multiple non-interchangeable chemistries" pattern already seen with Crest and Colgate, applied here to the brand this database had previously treated as the clean, singular "potassium nitrate brand."

## Pros
- Confirmed FDA Drug Facts label for the flagship formula, matching [[potassium-nitrate]]'s and [[fluoride-toothpaste]]'s existing documentation
- Real brand-specific RCT evidence beyond the general ingredient-class literature: a 10-day randomized, controlled, double-blind trial using repeated citric-acid erosive challenges found Sensodyne Pronamel (potassium nitrate 5% + sodium fluoride) statistically outperformed Colgate Sensitive Enamel Protect on enamel surface-softening and lesion-rehardening measures
- The Sensitivity, Gum & Enamel sub-line's stannous-fluoride formulation carries the same triple-purpose mechanism (anticavity + antigingivitis + antihypersensitivity) independently documented on [[stannous-fluoride]] for Crest Pro-Health, giving Sensodyne buyers a second, differently-mechanized option under the same brand

## Cons
- **Brand-identity mismatch:** a consumer choosing "Sensodyne" specifically for its well-known potassium-nitrate mechanism could unknowingly buy the Sensitivity, Gum & Enamel stannous-fluoride variant instead, which carries a different evidence base, different staining tradeoff (per [[stannous-fluoride]]), and a different mechanism of action entirely
- Per [[potassium-nitrate]]'s Cochrane evidence (Poulsen 2006), the potassium-nitrate ingredient class shows a real, statistically significant effect on objective (air blast/tactile) sensitivity measures, but patient-reported (subjective) benefit failed to reach statistical significance — the authors' own conclusion was "no clear evidence," a real caveat on Sensodyne's flagship mechanism despite its market dominance
- The Pronamel-vs-Colgate comparative trial and the "66% micro-damage repair" figure circulated in Sensodyne/Pronamel's own marketing (pronamel.us) are manufacturer-sourced claims — treated here as leads, not independently verified effect sizes
- No fully independent (non-manufacturer-funded) head-to-head brand trial was identified in this pass

## Use Cases
Dentin hypersensitivity relief and cavity prevention (flagship potassium-nitrate + sodium-fluoride formula, and Pronamel's enamel-erosion-focused variant); separately, anticavity + antigingivitis + antihypersensitivity via the Sensitivity, Gum & Enamel stannous-fluoride sub-line.

## Allergies / Warnings
Per label: stop use and consult a dentist if sensitivity persists/worsens after 4 weeks, or (Sensitivity Gum & Enamel specifically) if gingivitis/bleeding/redness lasts beyond 2 weeks, or if painful/swollen gums, pus, loose teeth, or increased tooth spacing occur (possible periodontitis signs). Keep out of reach of children; seek immediate medical help if swallowed beyond brushing amounts.

## Symptoms Treated
Tooth sensitivity (cold/heat/acid/sweet/contact triggers), cavity prevention, and — for the stannous-fluoride sub-line only — gingivitis/plaque control.
