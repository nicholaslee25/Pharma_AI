## Overview
**Real, current brand-name ambiguity, flagged rather than resolved by assumption.** "MG217 Psoriasis" (Wisconsin Pharmacal Company) is confirmed via multiple DailyMed labels to cover **at least two entirely different active ingredients** under one umbrella product name, with this database's category file ("MG217 Psoriasis — 1%") not specifying which:

- **Coal tar 3%** — "MG217 Psoriasis Medicated Conditioning Shampoo" and "MG217 Psoriasis Therapeutic Scalp Treatment," per [[coal-tar]]. Directions/mechanism per label: coal tar slows excessive skin-cell reproduction, reducing inflammation, itching, and scaling.
- **Salicylic acid** (concentration confirmed at 3% per manufacturer/retail sources, not independently re-verified against every specific DailyMed SKU) — "MG217 Psoriasis Therapeutic Conditioner" and plain "MG217 Psoriasis" salicylic-acid shampoo, per [[salicylic-acid]].

These are genuinely non-interchangeable mechanisms — coal tar's cytostatic action vs. salicylic acid's keratolytic action — sold under the identical "MG217 Psoriasis" brand name, distinguished only by SKU-level sub-naming ("Medicated Conditioning" vs. "Therapeutic Conditioner," etc.) that a consumer or this database's own category-share data does not clearly disambiguate.

## Pros
- Both confirmed actives (coal tar 3%, salicylic acid ~3%) fall within their respective FDA-monograph ranges, per [[coal-tar]] and [[salicylic-acid]]
- Offering both mechanisms under one brand gives a psoriasis/dandruff sufferer a same-shelf alternative if one active doesn't suit them
- Marketed specifically toward psoriasis (via the Psoriasis.org "Seal of Recognition" cited in manufacturer materials), a more targeted positioning than most other brands in this category

## Cons
- **This database's own category-share entry ("MG217 Psoriasis — 1%") cannot be mapped to a single confirmed active ingredient** — it could refer to either the coal-tar or salicylic-acid product line, a genuine data-attribution gap inherited from the Pharmacy Times source data's brand-name-only listing
- Coal tar's photosensitivity warning (increased sunburn risk) applies to the coal-tar SKUs specifically and would NOT necessarily apply to the salicylic-acid SKUs — a consumer needs to know which MG217 product they have to know which warnings actually apply
- No MG217-brand-specific clinical trial was located for either active in this pass
- Coal-tar SKUs at 3% are subject to California Prop 65's cancer-warning requirement (per [[coal-tar]]), a consideration that would not apply to the salicylic-acid SKUs under the same brand name

## Use Cases
Psoriasis, seborrheic dermatitis, and dandruff scalp symptom relief — via coal tar's cytostatic action (Medicated Conditioning/Therapeutic Scalp Treatment SKUs) or salicylic acid's keratolytic action (Therapeutic Conditioner/plain shampoo SKUs), mechanism depending entirely on which specific product is purchased.
