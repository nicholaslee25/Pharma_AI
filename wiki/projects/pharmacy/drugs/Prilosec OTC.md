## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed: Prilosec OTC's own FDA Drug Facts label (omeprazole magnesium delayed-release tablet, 20.6mg equivalent to 20mg omeprazole) matches [[proton-pump-inhibitors]]'s representative-agent data exactly — this ingredient page was already anchored on Prilosec OTC's own label, so this brand-specific pass mainly confirms rather than corrects. Category-dominant in both Acid Reducers (37%) and Proton Pump Inhibitors (54%), the two largest shares of any brand across either category in this database's Gastrointestinal section.

## Pros
- Confirmed FDA OTC label matches [[proton-pump-inhibitors]]'s existing representative-agent sourcing exactly — no discrepancy found
- Category-dominant share in both Acid Reducers (37%) and Proton Pump Inhibitors (54%)
- Delayed-release (enteric-coated) formulation protects the omeprazole from stomach acid until it reaches the small intestine — the mechanistic basis for once-daily dosing, distinct from Zegerid OTC's immediate-release/bicarbonate approach to the same molecule (see [[Zegerid OTC]])

## Cons
- Per [[proton-pump-inhibitors]]'s own Efficacy section: roughly 50% of patients using a PPI for non-erosive GERD do not achieve complete symptom resolution — a real, substantial real-world efficacy gap that applies to this specific brand as the class's representative agent
- Not for immediate relief (label: may take 1–4 days for full effect) — a caregiver or patient expecting antacid-like immediate relief would be mistaken; this is a fundamentally different use pattern than Tums or Pepcid
- No Prilosec-OTC-specific RCT beyond the general omeprazole/PPI evidence base was independently located in this pass
- Shares the full PPI-class long-term-use safety-signal list from [[proton-pump-inhibitors]] (hypomagnesemia, C. diff/infection risk, rebound acid hypersecretion, B12/iron deficiency) despite being sold OTC for short 14-day courses — worth noting since OTC packaging/marketing emphasizes short-term use while some consumers self-extend use beyond the labeled 14-day/4-month-max-repeat schedule

## Use Cases
Treatment of frequent heartburn occurring 2 or more days per week, via a full 14-day course — not for on-demand/occasional heartburn relief (that use case is better served by an antacid or H2RA per the label's own framing).

## Allergies / Warnings
Per label: do not use if trouble/pain swallowing, vomiting blood, or bloody/black stools — see a doctor. Omeprazole may cause severe skin reactions (reddening, blisters, rash). Pregnant/breastfeeding: ask a health professional. Do not exceed 1 tablet/day; do not use more than 14 days or repeat a 14-day course more often than every 4 months without doctor guidance.

## Symptoms Treated
Frequent heartburn / acid indigestion via the PPI mechanism described in [[proton-pump-inhibitors]] — most potent OTC acid-suppression option, but not appropriate for immediate/occasional symptom relief.
