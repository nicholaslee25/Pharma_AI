## Appears In
Hydration Support - 18%

## Overview
Gatorade is confirmed to be a standard sports drink (~21g sugar per 12 fl oz, ~300mg sodium/L in standard formula) — genuinely well-evidenced for its actual designed purpose (carbohydrate/electrolyte delivery to sustain athletic performance during prolonged exercise), but its sodium content sits well below the WHO oral rehydration solution (ORS) standard documented in [[oral-rehydration-electrolytes]] (~1,035mg sodium/L for [[Pedialyte]]), and its sugar content is substantially higher than a clinical ORS. This is a real, meaningful formulation distinction relevant to this database's Hydration Support category placement: Gatorade is optimized for athletic performance/palatability, not for the rehydration-therapy efficiency the WHO ORS ratio specifically targets (via the sodium-glucose cotransport mechanism). Hydration Support (18%) is covered by [[oral-rehydration-electrolytes]].

## Pros
- Real, long-established evidence base for its actual designed use case: sustaining performance and electrolyte/carbohydrate balance during prolonged exercise (well-documented in sports-science literature generally, not itemized with a specific primary source in this pass)
- Widely available, palatable, and well-tolerated for its intended athletic-performance use case

## Cons
- **Real formulation mismatch with rehydration-therapy use**: sodium content (~300mg/L) is well below the WHO ORS standard (~1,035mg/L) documented in [[oral-rehydration-electrolytes]], while sugar content (~21g/12 fl oz) is substantially higher than a clinical ORS — meaning Gatorade is not formulated to the same rehydration-efficiency standard as [[Pedialyte]] or [[DripDrop]], even though it is tracked in the same "Hydration Support" category in this database
- Not recommended as a rehydration therapy for pediatric gastroenteritis-related dehydration (excess sugar can worsen diarrhea osmotically, inadequate sodium for therapeutic rehydration) — a real, clinically-relevant distinction from its category-mates that a general "Hydration Support" listing doesn't convey
- High sugar content is a real consideration for diabetic or sugar-restricted users using this product routinely rather than during actual prolonged exertion

## Use Cases
Electrolyte/carbohydrate support during prolonged athletic exercise — see [[oral-rehydration-electrolytes]] for the contrast with clinical-grade oral rehydration therapy, which this product's formulation does not match.

## Allergies / Warnings
High sugar content per serving — a real consideration for diabetic/sugar-restricted users. Not an appropriate substitute for a clinical ORS in cases of actual illness-related dehydration (e.g., pediatric gastroenteritis) per [[oral-rehydration-electrolytes]].

## Symptoms Treated
Not indicated for dehydration treatment in the clinical sense — see [[oral-rehydration-electrolytes]] for the category-wide distinction between sports drinks and clinical ORS.

## Brand-Specific Alignment Check
**Real, worth-flagging category-placement nuance:** Gatorade is well-evidenced for athletic performance but is formulated quite differently from — and is not a substitute for — the clinical-grade oral rehydration solutions ([[Pedialyte]], [[DripDrop]]) that dominate the top of this same "Hydration Support" category, despite being tracked alongside them. This is a real, common point of consumer confusion this project's protocol is designed to surface.
