## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed: the classic pink "CALADRYL" lotion uses **calamine 8% (skin protectant) + pramoxine HCl 1% (topical analgesic)**, for temporary relief of pain/itching from poison ivy/oak/sumac, insect bites, and minor irritations, and to dry oozing/weeping from the poison-plant reactions. This is the category leader by a wide margin (21% share, more than 2x the next brand). A separate SKU, **"Caladryl Clear,"** replaces the pink calamine (ferric oxide-based tint) with **zinc acetate** as the skin-protectant/astringent component, keeping the same pramoxine HCl 1% anesthetic base — a cosmetic/tint variation rather than a mechanism change, since both formats share the core pramoxine anesthetic.

Pramoxine HCl is a topical anesthetic chemically distinct from both benzocaine (ester) and lidocaine (amide) — see [[topical-anesthetics]]'s "coverage gap" note on pramoxine, which this brand adds a second confirmed example to (alongside Bacitraycin Plus and Preparation H). No Caladryl-brand-specific clinical trial was located in this pass; evidence rests on the general calamine/pramoxine ingredient literature.

## Pros
- Confirmed, consistent core anesthetic (pramoxine HCl 1%) across both the classic and "Clear" SKUs
- Dual-mechanism formula: calamine provides a genuine skin-protectant/drying effect for oozing poison-ivy lesions in addition to pramoxine's anesthetic action
- Category-leading share (21%) suggests broad, long-standing pharmacist and consumer familiarity
- "Clear" variant offers a cosmetic alternative for users who dislike calamine's pink residue, without changing the active anesthetic

## Cons
- No dedicated ingredient evidence page exists in this database for pramoxine specifically (a real coverage gap already flagged on [[topical-anesthetics]]) — its safety profile (including whether it shares any of benzocaine/lidocaine's known risks) is not independently documented here
- No Caladryl-brand-specific clinical trial was located; evidence is ingredient-class-level only
- Calamine-based products can stain skin/clothing (the very reason the "Clear" alternative exists) — a real, if minor, usability limitation for the classic formula

## Use Cases
Temporary relief of pain and itching from poison ivy, poison oak, and poison sumac rashes, insect bites, and minor skin irritations; dries oozing/weeping poison-plant lesions.

## Allergies / Warnings
Per label: external use only; avoid eye contact; stop use and consult a doctor if condition worsens or does not improve within 7 days, or if symptoms persist/recur; keep away from children, contact Poison Control if swallowed; children under 2 require doctor consultation.

## Symptoms Treated
Pain, itching, and oozing/weeping skin reactions from poison ivy/oak/sumac; insect bites; minor skin irritations and cuts.
