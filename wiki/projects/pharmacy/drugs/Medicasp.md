## Appears In
Dandruff Shampoo - 1%

## Overview
Medicasp is confirmed via DailyMed as **coal tar 0.5%**, per [[coal-tar]] — the lowest confirmed coal-tar concentration in this category (vs. [[Neutrogena T-Gel]]'s 0.5% at the same nominal figure, and [[MG217 Psoriasis]]'s 3% coal-tar SKUs). Purpose: controls symptoms of psoriasis, dandruff, and seborrheic dermatitis. Per manufacturer description: coal tar "helps normalize fast scalp cell turnover and calm irritation."

**Regulatory note (real, current):** Medicasp's 0.5% concentration sits **exactly at the threshold** above which California's Proposition 65 requires a cancer warning on OTC coal-tar products, per [[coal-tar]]'s central regulatory-tension finding (FDA: no evidence of increased cancer risk at monograph concentrations; California: warning required above 0.5%). Whether Medicasp's specific formulation falls at, just below, or just above that exact threshold in practice was not independently resolved in this pass — flagged as a genuine boundary case rather than assumed either way.

## Pros
- Lowest confirmed coal-tar concentration among the coal-tar brands in this category, which — while not independently trial-confirmed as "gentler" in this pass — is at least consistent with a lower-strength positioning for consumers wanting to start conservatively
- Falls within the FDA-monograph allowable range (0.5-5%) for coal tar, per [[coal-tar]]
- Same broad indication set (psoriasis, dandruff, seborrheic dermatitis) as the other coal-tar brands in this category

## Cons
- Sitting at the exact Prop 65 warning-threshold concentration is a genuinely ambiguous position — neither clearly below nor clearly above the line requiring a California cancer warning, a real regulatory edge case this pass could not resolve with certainty
- Same photosensitivity warning applies as every coal-tar brand in this pass (increased sunburn risk for 24 hours after use)
- No Medicasp-brand-specific clinical trial was located in this pass — evidence is entirely ingredient-level, and per [[coal-tar]], the ingredient-level evidence itself is thinner (FDA monograph determination rather than quantified RCT data) than for several other actives in this database
- Smallest recommendation share (1%, tied with MG217) in this category, suggesting limited real-world pharmacist preference relative to the higher-share coal-tar competitor Neutrogena T/Gel (9%)

## Use Cases
Dandruff, seborrheic dermatitis, and psoriasis scalp-symptom control via coal tar's cytostatic slowing of epidermal cell turnover, at the lower end of the FDA-monograph concentration range.

## Allergies / Warnings
Do not get into eyes (rinse thoroughly with water if contact occurs); use caution with sun exposure for up to 24 hours after application; do not use for prolonged periods without consulting a doctor; do not combine with other psoriasis therapies (UV/phototherapy or prescription drugs) unless directed by a doctor.

## Symptoms Treated
Scalp itching, flaking, and scaling associated with dandruff, seborrheic dermatitis, and psoriasis.
