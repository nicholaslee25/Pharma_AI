## Appears In

## Overview
**Third and most explicit example of the category-level finding on [[camphor-menthol-eucalyptus]]:** Frida Baby No-Mess Vapor Rub is marketed by the manufacturer itself as **"Non-Medicated"** with **no active ingredients at all** — a water-based formula (Caprylic/Capric Triglyceride, Glycerin, Water, Hydrogenated Vegetable Oil, and other emollients) scented with eucalyptus and lavender, dispensed via a hands-free roller applicator. Unlike Zarbee's Naturals Soothing Baby Chest Rub and Maty's All Natural Baby Chest Rub (which at least contain concentrated essential oils as the sensory-active component), this product's own manufacturer branding foregrounds "non-medicated" as a selling point rather than something this database had to independently discover — the most transparent of the three non-drug products in this category about its own regulatory status.

## Pros
- Manufacturer is explicit and upfront that the product is "non-medicated," unlike the other two non-active-ingredient competitors in this category, which required checking ingredient lists/FDA disclaimers to establish the same fact
- No camphor, menthol, or concentrated essential-oil exposure — avoids both the camphor-toxicity risk on [[camphor-menthol-eucalyptus]] and the more concentrated essential-oil contact-sensitization risk of Zarbee's/Maty's balm-style products
- Hands-free applicator design reduces direct skin contact during application, a genuine usability/safety feature distinct from the ingredient question
- Labeled safe for infants 2 months+

## Cons
- **No active ingredient of any kind — explicitly non-medicated per the manufacturer's own marketing** — the lowest-share (2%) but most transparent example of this category's recurring finding that several top-ranked "cough suppressant" products contain no FDA-recognized cough-suppressant ingredient
- No RCT or clinical efficacy evidence located, consistent with a product that makes no medicated claim to begin with
- Still carries a general essential-oil (eucalyptus, lavender fragrance) contact-sensitization risk, though at a lower concentration than the concentrated-oil balm products (Zarbee's, Maty's) in this same category

## Use Cases
Marketed as a comfort/sensory aid for cold-related discomfort in infants, explicitly not as a medicated cough or congestion treatment.

## Allergies / Warnings
For external use only. Contains fragrance/essential-oil components (eucalyptus, lavender) with a general, uncommon contact-sensitization risk. No FDA drug warnings apply since no active ingredient is present.

## Symptoms Treated
None via established pharmacological mechanism — explicitly marketed as non-medicated; any perceived benefit is sensory/comfort-based rather than a treatment claim.

## Brand-Specific Alignment Check
