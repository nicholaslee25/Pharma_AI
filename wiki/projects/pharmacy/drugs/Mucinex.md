## Overview
The plain "Mucinex" (Expectorants, 74% share) is already fully confirmed on [[guaifenesin]]: **guaifenesin 600mg extended-release** per its own DailyMed label, and that page's headline RCT (Bennett et al. 2015, PMID 26462765) tested **Mucinex 1200mg specifically** (2×600mg ER tablets) — a genuinely brand-specific trial, not just molecule-level evidence, already captured on the ingredient page. This confirms and upgrades the earlier "formulation not verified" hedge for the plain product. However, Mucinex's appearance in the two Cough/Cold/Flu Combination categories (18%/14%) almost certainly refers to **Mucinex DM** (guaifenesin + dextromethorphan) or **Mucinex Fast-Max** combination lines, not the single-ingredient tablet — per DailyMed, Mucinex DM = guaifenesin + dextromethorphan HBr (both regular and Maximum Strength 1200mg/60mg extended-release), and Mucinex D = guaifenesin + pseudoephedrine (Maximum Strength: 1200mg/120mg). This database's category data doesn't specify which exact combination SKU, so the two combination-category entries are treated as most-likely-DM/D but not confirmed to an exact single SKU.

## Pros
- The plain guaifenesin tablet has genuine brand-specific placebo-controlled RCT evidence at the exact dose sold (1200mg) — see [[guaifenesin]] for the actual result (worth reading before assuming "expectorant" claims are strongly evidence-backed either way)
- Extended-release (12-hour) dosing reduces pill burden vs. immediate-release guaifenesin products
- DM/D combination lines let users add cough suppression or decongestion without switching brands/tracking a second product's guaifenesin content separately

## Cons
- Combination products (DM, D, Fast-Max) inherit the safety considerations of their added ingredient — dextromethorphan's abuse-potential and serotonin-syndrome-interaction profile ([[dextromethorphan]]) or pseudoephedrine's cardiovascular-stimulant profile — on top of guaifenesin's own generally mild safety profile
- Guaifenesin extended-release products including Mucinex Maximum Strength must be limited to 2,400mg/day — easy to exceed if a user takes both a plain Mucinex and a Mucinex-combination product without realizing both contain guaifenesin
- Not for children <12y per the Mucinex label — FDA/AAP guidance also cautions against guaifenesin generally in children under 4

## Use Cases
Plain Mucinex: chest congestion, thinning/loosening mucus. Mucinex DM: adds cough suppression. Mucinex D: adds nasal decongestion.

## Allergies / Warnings
Not for children <12y (label). Do not exceed 2,400mg/day guaifenesin across all guaifenesin-containing products combined. DM/D variants carry their added ingredient's own warnings (see [[dextromethorphan]] / pseudoephedrine page if present).

## Symptoms Treated
Chest congestion, cough (DM), nasal congestion (D).
