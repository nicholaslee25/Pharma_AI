## Overview
Abbott's "FreeStyle" brand spans two genuinely different measurement technologies — a real, worth-naming distinction not resolved by the single house-brand name: (1) classic fingerstick meters (FreeStyle Lite / Freedom Lite, using capillary blood + a lancet, the type most directly relevant to the paired Blood Sampling Devices/Lancets category share), and (2) the **FreeStyle Libre** continuous glucose monitor (CGM) line, which measures interstitial fluid glucose via a wearable sensor rather than fingersticks. Brand-specific accuracy data located for the Libre CGM line shows real, substantial variability by study population and sensor age: Mean Absolute Relative Difference (MARD) ranged from **7.8% (sensor days 5-7) to 18.33% (outpatient setting)**, and ISO 15197:2013 pass rates ranged from **64.9% (critically ill patients) to 97-99.5% (general studies)** — a wide spread illustrating [[blood-glucose-monitors]]'s own point that meter accuracy varies by context, not just by brand. No brand-specific accuracy figure for the classic fingerstick FreeStyle meters (as distinct from Libre) was located in this pass. Largest share (34%) of the Blood Glucose Monitors category and second-largest (23%) of the Blood Sampling Devices/Lancets category.

## Pros
- FreeStyle Libre CGM eliminates most routine fingersticks, a genuine usability/adherence advantage over fingerstick-only competitors for patients using that specific product line
- Multiple independent, published accuracy studies exist for the Libre line — more independent scrutiny located than for several competitors in this category
- Largest market share in the primary Blood Glucose Monitors category, indicating broad pharmacist familiarity

## Cons
- Real, quantified accuracy variability for the Libre CGM: MARD more than doubled (7.8% to 18.33%) and ISO pass rate ranged from 64.9% to 99.5% across different studies/populations — critically ill patients and healthy adolescents both showed notably worse performance than general-population studies
- No accuracy data specific to the classic fingerstick FreeStyle meters (as opposed to Libre) was located — a real gap for the product most directly tied to this brand's Blood Sampling Devices/Lancets category share
- CGM interstitial-fluid lag time (5-15 minutes behind true blood glucose, per [[blood-glucose-monitors]]'s general CGM discussion) applies to the Libre line specifically
- Per King et al. 2018 on [[blood-glucose-monitors]], manufacturer-supported studies show dramatically higher "pass" odds than independent studies for glucose meters generally — worth keeping in mind when weighing any single accuracy figure for this or any brand

## Use Cases
Blood glucose self-monitoring for diabetes management — fingerstick meters for spot-check readings, Libre CGM for continuous/trend monitoring without routine fingersticks.
