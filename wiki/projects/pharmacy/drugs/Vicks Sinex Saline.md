## Appears In

## Overview
Confirmed: Vicks Sinex Saline (Extra Strength) is a **hypertonic saline solution "3X concentrated" vs. isotonic saline**, per the manufacturer's own labeling — salt and purified water only, no added preservatives. Per [[saline-nasal-sprays]]'s cited meta-analyses, hypertonic saline shows a real, measurable congestion-reduction advantage over isotonic saline via an osmotic mechanism (drawing fluid out of swollen nasal mucosa), distinct from the plain moisturizing/rinsing action of isotonic products like [[Simply Saline]]. **Naming-family caution:** other "Vicks Sinex" SKUs (Sinex Severe, Sinex D) contain oxymetazoline HCl, a genuine vasoconstrictor decongestant drug — consumers must confirm they have the saline-only SKU specifically, the same naming-collision pattern flagged on [[Mucinex Sinus]]. No brand-specific clinical trial was identified beyond the general hypertonic-saline evidence.

## Pros
- Hypertonic formulation has real meta-analysis-level evidence for a congestion-specific benefit beyond plain isotonic saline, per [[saline-nasal-sprays]]
- Drug-free, non-habit-forming, safe for daily use — no rebound-congestion risk unlike the oxymetazoline-containing Sinex products in the same brand family
- Simple two-ingredient formulation (salt + purified water), no added preservatives

## Cons
- **Naming-family collision risk**, same pattern as [[Mucinex Sinus]]: "Vicks Sinex" branding spans this drug-free saline product and separate oxymetazoline-containing decongestant sprays — real potential for consumer confusion
- Hypertonic saline can cause more stinging/irritation than isotonic in sensitive users, per [[saline-nasal-sprays]]'s general safety data
- Smallest share among this category's hypertonic option relative to isotonic leaders, despite favorable congestion-specific evidence — recommendation share doesn't track this evidence advantage
- No brand-specific outcome trial exists beyond the general hypertonic-saline class evidence

## Use Cases
Nasal congestion relief via osmotic decongestant-like mechanism; daily sinus/allergy congestion management.

## Allergies / Warnings
No active ingredient, not FDA-evaluated as a drug. **Verify the specific Vicks Sinex SKU** — separate "Sinex Severe"/"Sinex D" products in the same family contain oxymetazoline and carry the standard vasoconstrictor 3-day-use/rebound-congestion warning this saline product does not.

## Symptoms Treated
Nasal congestion (via osmotic mechanism), dryness, mucus clearing.
