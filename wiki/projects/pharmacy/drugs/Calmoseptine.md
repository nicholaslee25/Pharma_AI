## Overview
**Real category-placement finding.** Confirmed via DailyMed: Calmoseptine Ointment's own FDA Drug Facts label — **lanolin 15.7% (anorectal protectant), menthol 0.44% (anorectal analgesic), petrolatum 24% (anorectal protectant), zinc oxide 20.6% (anorectal protectant/astringent)** — is explicitly indicated as an **anorectal ointment** for "temporary relief of local itching and discomfort in the perianal area; pain or burning; skin irritations." **Diaper rash is not mentioned anywhere in the label.** This aligns closely with its much higher share (21%) in Incontinence-Related Skin Protectants — a population and body region (perianal/incontinence-associated dermatitis in adults) the label's own wording directly addresses — but its smaller 4% Diaper Rash Products share reflects real-world off-label pediatric use of a product whose own FDA indication doesn't mention infants or diapers at all. This is a genuine, if minor, category/label mismatch distinct from (but structurally similar to) the A&D/A+D findings elsewhere in this database, where a real product is used clinically for a purpose beyond its literal labeled indication.

## Pros
- Confirmed FDA Drug Facts label with FOUR active ingredients (lanolin, menthol, petrolatum, zinc oxide) rather than the single-ingredient zinc-oxide formulations of Desitin/Boudreaux's/Balmex/Triple Paste — menthol adds a topical analgesic (cooling/itch-relief) component the other zinc-oxide-only competitors in this category lack
- Zinc oxide 20.6% is a mid-high concentration relative to the other brands confirmed in this pass (Balmex 11.3%, Desitin 13-40%, Boudreaux's 16-40%, Triple Paste 12.8-40%)
- Per [[zinc-oxide]]'s own Pharmacy Times Context note, Calmoseptine was reportedly compared favorably to Desitin Maximum Strength in a structured hospitalized-patient care regimen — a genuinely different, more clinically-supervised context than routine home infant diaper-rash use, and not independently re-verified in this pass
- Directly labeled for its actual highest-share use case (perianal/incontinence-associated irritation), unlike several diaper-rash-category products whose labels are diaper-rash-specific but whose real-world adult-incontinence use extends beyond that label

## Cons
- **Its own FDA label does not mention diaper rash at all** — its Diaper Rash Products category placement (4% share) reflects real-world pediatric off-label use of a product FDA-labeled for anorectal/perianal irritation, not a diaper-rash-specific indication
- The menthol component (0.44%, "anorectal analgesic") is a topical cooling/analgesic ingredient not typically expected in infant diaper-rash products — caregivers should verify this is appropriate for an infant's sensitive skin, since menthol-containing anorectal products are formulated with an adult perianal-use population in mind
- Per [[zinc-oxide]]'s own evidence review: overall evidence quality for zinc-oxide barrier preparations is graded low-level (Grade C) — applies here regardless of the additional lanolin/petrolatum/menthol ingredients
- No independent verification in this pass of the specific hospitalized-patient RCT comparing Calmoseptine to Desitin Maximum Strength referenced on [[zinc-oxide]]

## Use Cases
Per its own FDA label: temporary relief of local itching, discomfort, pain, or burning in the perianal area, and general skin irritation protection — primarily an adult/incontinence-context product per its labeling, with secondary off-label pediatric diaper-rash use reflected in this database's smaller Diaper Rash Products share.

## Allergies / Warnings
Per label: for external use only; discontinue and consult a doctor if condition worsens or does not improve. Lanolin carries its own known (if uncommon) contact-allergy risk, consistent with the same finding for [[A&D]]/[[A+D Original Ointment]] elsewhere in this database. Menthol-containing products should generally be used with caution on infant skin given menthol's broader safety profile concerns in young children (see [[camphor-menthol-eucalyptus]] for related menthol-safety context, though that page addresses respiratory/topical-vapor use rather than this anorectal-ointment context specifically).

## Symptoms Treated
Perianal/anorectal itching, discomfort, pain, burning, and general skin irritation (per its actual FDA label) — with real-world off-label extension to infant diaper-rash discomfort via the same zinc-oxide/petrolatum/lanolin barrier mechanism.
