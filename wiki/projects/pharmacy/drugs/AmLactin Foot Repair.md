## Overview
**Real concentration/formulation discrepancy flagged, not resolved.** AmLactin Foot Repair Cream is marketed as containing **"15% Lactic Acid,"** with an ingredient list built around an "ULTRAPLEX" blend of ammonium lactate, potassium lactate, and sodium lactate (plus glycerin, petrolatum, mineral oil, and standard emulsifiers). No independently confirmed FDA Drug Facts panel, distinct active-ingredient percentage callout, or NDC/DailyMed listing specific to this SKU was located in this pass — a genuine gap between this being drugs.com-indexed (which generally lists it as a topical medication) and the product's own marketing page, which presents an INCI-style cosmetic ingredient list rather than a formal Drug Facts box.

**This matters because it is a real departure from the well-established evidence base for AmLactin's own core chemistry.** Multiple controlled trials exist specifically for **ammonium lactate 12%** — including a double-blind RCT specifically on **foot xerosis** (ammonium lactate 12% vs. pure lanolin), plus separate trials against 5% lactic acid lotion and 40% urea, and an FDA-recognized indication for ichthyosis vulgaris and xerosis at that 12% concentration. AmLactin Foot Repair's "15% lactic acid" framing does not map cleanly onto this trial base: the tested/FDA-precedented active is ammonium lactate at 12%, expressed as the neutralized ammonium salt, not "15% lactic acid" as a free-acid marketing figure — and this pass could not confirm whether the two are equivalent in delivered keratolytic activity at the concentrations actually used in this specific product.

No AmLactin-Foot-Repair-brand-specific clinical trial was located; the trial evidence above concerns the general ammonium-lactate-12% formulation used in AmLactin's classic (non-foot-specific) product line and generic equivalents, not independently confirmed against this exact foot-cream SKU's actual formulation and concentration.

## Use Cases
Softening calluses and rough, thickened skin; helping heal cracked heels, splits, and dry patches on the feet — via an alpha-hydroxy-acid (lactate/ammonium-lactate) keratolytic and humectant mechanism.

## Allergies / Warnings
Per manufacturer: external use only; may cause mild stinging or irritation on sensitive or broken skin (discontinue if persistent); AHA sun-sensitivity warning — use sunscreen/protective clothing during use and for one week after; keep away from children and eyes. Per drugs.com's general ammonium-lactate profile: contraindicated in those allergic to ammonium lactate, glycerin, mineral oil, propylene glycol, or parabens; seek emergency care for signs of allergic reaction (hives, difficulty breathing, facial/throat swelling); more likely to irritate fair or sensitive skin.
