## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed: **Bayer Genuine Aspirin** is aspirin 325 mg (already documented on [[aspirin]]), used for general pain/fever relief — most plausibly the product behind the 6% Headache Products share. **Bayer Aspirin Regimen Low Dose** is a materially different product: aspirin **81 mg**, enteric-coated, marketed specifically for secondary prevention of cardiovascular disease under a doctor's direction — most plausibly the product behind the dominant 61% share of Aspirin, Recurrent Heart Attack/Stroke Prevention. **This is an important, safety-relevant dose distinction under one brand name, not a minor formulation variance:** 325mg Genuine Aspirin is dosed for acute pain/fever (per [[aspirin]]'s OTC label: 1–2 tablets q4h, max 3900mg/24h), while 81mg Low Dose Regimen is dosed once daily for chronic cardioprotection — these are not interchangeable, and a consumer should not assume "Bayer Aspirin" means one fixed dose or one fixed purpose.

## Pros
- Confirmed FDA Drug Facts labels for both doses, each appropriate to its specific indication — general pain relief (325mg) vs. secondary cardiovascular prevention (81mg)
- The Low Dose Regimen product's enteric coating is specifically intended to reduce gastric irritation/GI bleeding risk from chronic daily use, addressing a real, documented aspirin safety concern (per [[aspirin]]) with a formulation-level mitigation
- By far the largest single-brand share of Aspirin, Recurrent Heart Attack/Stroke Prevention (61%) in this database, reflecting strong physician/pharmacist alignment with using aspirin specifically (rather than another NSAID) for this indication — consistent with [[aspirin]]'s own irreversible-COX-1/antiplatelet mechanism being uniquely suited to this use, unlike reversible-COX NSAIDs

## Cons
- **The two Bayer Aspirin products serve fundamentally different purposes at different doses under the same brand name** — a consumer confusing the two (e.g., taking Genuine Aspirin 325mg daily thinking it's equivalent to a doctor-directed low-dose regimen, or taking only 81mg expecting acute headache relief) would get the wrong therapeutic effect
- Chronic aspirin use for cardioprotection carries real, well-documented bleeding risk (per [[aspirin]]'s antiplatelet mechanism) that must be weighed against cardiovascular benefit — explicitly a "talk to your doctor first" product per its own marketing, not a self-directed OTC decision the way Genuine Aspirin's acute-pain use is
- Aspirin's Reye's syndrome risk in children/teenagers with viral illness applies to both formulations
- No brand-specific (as opposed to general aspirin ingredient-class) RCT was independently verified in this pass for the specific "31% reduction in second heart attack risk" figure found in Bayer's own marketing — flagged as a manufacturer-cited statistic, not independently re-verified against a primary trial here

## Use Cases
Genuine Aspirin (325mg): acute headache, minor aches/pains, fever reduction. Aspirin Regimen Low Dose (81mg): doctor-directed secondary prevention of heart attack and clot-related (ischemic) stroke in patients with prior cardiovascular events.

## Allergies / Warnings
Per label (both formulations): Reye's syndrome risk in children/teenagers with chickenpox or flu symptoms; NSAID-class GI bleeding risk; ask a doctor before use if taking a blood thinner. Low Dose Regimen specifically: not appropriate for everyone — consult a doctor before beginning a daily aspirin regimen, since the bleeding-risk/cardiovascular-benefit balance is patient-specific.

## Symptoms Treated
Genuine Aspirin: headache, minor aches/pains, fever. Aspirin Regimen Low Dose: secondary prevention (not acute symptom treatment) of heart attack and ischemic stroke recurrence.
