## Overview
**Discrepancy flagged:** "SunBurnt" spans two starkly different products confirmed via DailyMed — **SunBurnt PLUS**, a conventional drug with **lidocaine hydrochloride 4.0%** (external analgesic, lower-methemoglobinemia-risk side of [[topical-anesthetics]]'s divide), and **SunBurnt Advanced After-Sun Gel**, a **HOMEOPATHIC product** whose label carries the standard FDA disclaimer verbatim: *"This homeopathic product has not been evaluated by the Food and Drug Administration for safety or efficacy."* The homeopathic gel's active ingredients are **cantharis vesicatoria 3X HPUS**, **calendula officinalis TINC HPUS**, and **echinacea angustifolia TINC HPUS** — homeopathic dilutions per the label's own HPUS/TINC/3X notation, exactly the pattern this database's [[homeopathic-cold-products]] page frames as lacking convincing evidence at claimed dilutions. **A genuinely interesting wrinkle: cantharis vesicatoria is the source of cantharidin, a real pharmacological vesicant** (used medically, at actual therapeutic concentrations, to deliberately blister skin for wart/molluscum removal) — and 3X is a comparatively LOW homeopathic potency (1:1,000 dilution) versus the ultra-high potencies (e.g., 30C, 10^-60) typically discussed in homeopathy's "nothing measurable remains" framing. No specific adverse-event report or FDA action against this exact product was located, so this is flagged as a point of genuine interest rather than a confirmed safety incident.

## Pros
- SunBurnt PLUS: confirmed FDA Drug Facts label with a clearly disclosed, conventional, evidence-supported active ingredient (lidocaine)
- Brand offers both a conventional-medicine option (PLUS) and an alternative/homeopathic option (Advanced After-Sun) for consumers with different preferences

## Cons
- **The homeopathic SKU's own label states the FDA has not evaluated it for safety or efficacy** — per this database's [[homeopathic-cold-products]] page, the best available systematic-review evidence for homeopathic preparations generally shows no convincing benefit beyond placebo, with a "better trials find nothing" quality gradient
- A consumer seeing only the "SunBurnt" name cannot tell from the brand alone whether they are buying a conventional lidocaine product or an unevaluated homeopathic one — the "PLUS" vs. "Advanced After-Sun" naming distinction is the only differentiator
- Cantharis vesicatoria's active compound (cantharidin) is a genuine vesicant at active concentrations; while 3X is a homeopathic dilution and no specific safety incident was found for this product, it is a meaningfully lower dilution than typical ultra-high-potency homeopathic remedies, worth noting for transparency
- No brand-specific clinical trial for either SunBurnt product was identified

## Use Cases
SunBurnt PLUS: temporary relief of pain and itching from sunburn, minor thermal injuries, insect bites, minor skin irritations, cuts, and scrapes. SunBurnt Advanced After-Sun Gel: marketed relief of mild-to-moderate sunburn symptoms (overheated skin, redness, rawness, itching, dryness) via homeopathic mechanism, not independently evaluated by the FDA.

## Allergies / Warnings
SunBurnt PLUS: external use only; do not apply large quantities over raw/blistered areas or if allergic to any component; consult a physician for severe sunburn or compromised skin; avoid eyes; discontinue if condition worsens or symptoms persist beyond 7 days or recur. SunBurnt Advanced After-Sun Gel: external use only; avoid severe sunburns, broken skin, existing rashes; avoid eyes/mucous membranes; discontinue if symptoms persist beyond 7 days; consult a healthcare provider if pregnant/breastfeeding.

## Symptoms Treated
Pain and itching from sunburn (PLUS, via conventional lidocaine mechanism); marketed relief of sunburn-related redness/rawness/itching/dryness (Advanced After-Sun, via unevaluated homeopathic mechanism).
