## Pros
- Confirmed FDA label matches [[antacids]]'s existing sourcing exactly — no discrepancy found
- Category-dominant share (52%) in Antacids
- Fast-acting (chew-and-swallow, local gastric neutralization) — genuinely different onset profile than the H2RA/PPI categories elsewhere in this section, appropriate for as-needed occasional heartburn

## Cons
- Per [[antacids]]'s own Key Safety Data: calcium carbonate carries a real milk-alkali syndrome risk with excessive/chronic use, plus constipation, acid rebound, and hypercalcemia — this applies directly to this brand as the class's representative agent
- Multiple Tums SKUs exist in the real market (Regular Strength, Extra Strength/EX, Ultra Strength) at different calcium carbonate doses; this database's 52% recommendation share does not specify which SKU it refers to — consistent with the multi-formulation-ambiguity pattern flagged on other brands in this database
- No Tums-specific RCT beyond the general calcium-carbonate/antacid evidence base was independently located in this pass
- [[antacids]]'s own Contraindications/Special Population notes (renal failure, hyperparathyroidism caution; pregnancy dose cap of 10 vs. 15 tablets/24h) apply directly to this brand

## Use Cases
Fast relief of heartburn, acid indigestion, sour/upset stomach via local gastric acid neutralization — an as-needed, occasional-symptom use case, distinct from the PPI class's fixed 14-day-course use pattern.

## Allergies / Warnings
Per label: chew or crush completely before swallowing; do not use maximum dosage (up to 15 tablets/24h, 10 if pregnant) for more than 2 weeks without medical supervision. Per [[antacids]]: caution in renal failure, heart failure, hyperparathyroidism, renal calculus; ask a doctor/pharmacist before use if taking any prescription drug, given the antacid class's broad absorption-interaction pattern.

## Symptoms Treated
Heartburn, acid indigestion, sour stomach, upset stomach — via the calcium-carbonate mechanism detailed in [[antacids]].
