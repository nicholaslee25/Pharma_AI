## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed: "Childrens Dimetapp Cold and Cough" is a **triple combination — brompheniramine maleate (first-generation antihistamine) + dextromethorphan HBr (cough suppressant) + phenylephrine HCl (nasal decongestant)** — labeled for ages 6 and up, relieving stuffy/runny nose, itchy/watery eyes, sneezing, and cough. This is the SAME three-ingredient class (antihistamine + antitussive + decongestant) already documented on `drugs/Children's Dimetapp Cold and Allergy.md` (6% share, Children's Allergy category), except that product swaps dextromethorphan for no cough ingredient at all and is a two-ingredient antihistamine+decongestant combo — **"Dimetapp" as a brand name spans at least two distinct formulations across different Pharmacy Times categories**, consistent with the pattern already flagged on that page. No dedicated ingredient page exists in this database for brompheniramine specifically (a first-generation antihistamine related to but distinct from diphenhydramine) — noted as a gap, same as on the Cold and Allergy page.

## Pros
- Single product addresses three symptom classes (allergy/cold histamine response, congestion, cough) instead of requiring separate medications
- Manufactured under a long-established, widely recognized OTC brand

## Cons
- **Combination cough/cold products for children carry real, documented regulatory history**: following the FDA's January 2008 Public Health Advisory (reports of convulsions, rapid heart rate, and death associated with pediatric OTC cough/cold products) and the industry's November 2008 voluntary CHPA relabeling, these products carry "do not use under 4" as an industry floor — this specific Dimetapp SKU sets an even more conservative "ages 6+" label. This history applies to this product just as it does to Children's Dimetapp Cold and Allergy, Children's Tylenol Cold+Cough+Runny Nose, and every other combination product in this batch — flagged explicitly per this database's standing pediatric-cough-cold research protocol.
- Combines three active-ingredient risk profiles at once (antihistamine sedation/anticholinergic effects, dextromethorphan's dissociative/serotonergic risk at high doses, phenylephrine's stimulant/cardiovascular-adjacent profile) — more complex than single-ingredient competitors like Children's Delsym (DXM only) or Children's Robitussin Long-Acting
- No brand-specific pediatric RCT located for this exact triple combination beyond the general per-ingredient evidence already on [[dextromethorphan]] and [[oral-phenylephrine]]
- No dedicated brompheniramine ingredient page exists in this database — a real gap, same one already flagged on the Cold and Allergy variant

## Use Cases
Combined relief of runny/stuffy nose, itchy/watery eyes, sneezing, and cough due to the common cold or allergies, in children ages 6+.

## Allergies / Warnings
Per label: not for children under 6; do not exceed labeled dose; do not use with any other product containing an antihistamine, decongestant, or cough suppressant, since a child could otherwise receive duplicate active ingredients across products; ask a doctor before use if the child has heart disease, high blood pressure, thyroid disease, diabetes, or trouble urinating due to an enlarged prostate (phenylephrine-related cautions).
