## Overview
Confirmed: Hello Bello Bug Spray is an **essential-oil blend** — cedarwood oil, citronella oil, coconut, lemongrass oil, soybean oil, peppermint oil, quillaja saponaria (soapbark), jojoba seed oil, thyme oil, vitamin E, water, plus additional undisclosed ingredients — marketed as "100% USDA Certified Organic" and "Lab-Tested to Repel Mosquitoes, Flies, Ticks, & More." Every active ingredient identified is drawn from the standard FIFRA Section 25(b) "minimum risk" exempt-pesticide ingredient list, making it **plausibly, though not independently confirmed in this pass, EPA-registration-exempt** — the same regulatory category as [[Babyganics]]'s essential-oil repellent, and a meaningfully lower bar than the EPA-registered DEET/picaridin products ([[Off!]], [[Cutter]], [[Natrapel]]) that dominate this category. Per [[insect-repellents-deet-picaridin]]'s cited Consumer Reports testing, "natural" essential-oil repellents of this type "earned dismal scores" relative to EPA-registered actives — a real, relevant efficacy caution for this specific formulation class, not independently re-tested in this pass.

**Same allergen finding as [[Babyganics]]:** soybean oil appears again as a carrier/inactive ingredient — a top-9 major food allergen under US labeling law, a real consideration for a product marketed toward families/children via the "Hello Bello" baby-and-kids brand identity.

No brand-specific "lab-tested" claim was independently verified against a located, accessible study in this pass — "Lab-Tested to Repel Mosquitoes, Flies, Ticks & More" is manufacturer marketing language without an accompanying citation.

## Pros
- USDA Certified Organic status is a real, independently-verifiable third-party certification (unlike many unsubstantiated "natural" claims)
- Essential-oil formulation avoids DEET's documented (if EPA-uncausally-linked) pediatric seizure case-report history entirely
- Marketed specifically for family/child use, consistent with the "Hello Bello" brand's broader baby-care product identity

## Cons
- **Plausible EPA-registration-exempt status (minimum-risk pesticide), consistent with Consumer Reports' finding that "natural" essential-oil repellents of this type perform poorly relative to EPA-registered DEET/picaridin products** — a real, unresolved efficacy question for a product marketed specifically toward families/children who may be relying on it for meaningful bite/tick-borne-disease prevention
- Soybean oil carrier is a top-9 major allergen, not prominently flagged in "natural"/organic marketing — the same finding as [[Babyganics]]
- "Lab-Tested" marketing claim was not matched to any independently locatable study or citation in this pass
- No brand-specific clinical/efficacy trial exists; smallest recommendation share (6%) among this category's six tracked brands

## Use Cases
Prevention of insect bites (mosquitoes, flies, ticks) via essential-oil scent-masking, per manufacturer claims not independently re-tested in this pass.

## Allergies / Warnings
Standard essential-oil-product caution: external use, avoid eyes, discontinue if irritation occurs. Soybean oil carrier is a real, undisclosed-as-prominent allergen risk for soy-allergic individuals, particularly relevant given the brand's family/child marketing focus.
