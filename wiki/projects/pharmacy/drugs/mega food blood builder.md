## Overview
MegaFood Blood Builder is confirmed to use **iron bisglycinate** (a chelated iron form, "FoodState Iron," combined with vitamin C, folic acid, and B12), which the manufacturer and independent sourcing describe as better absorbed than non-chelated forms like ferrous sulfate. This pass located a genuinely notable brand-specific finding: an eight-week clinical study conducted by researchers at the University of Maryland School of Medicine found increases in iron stores, decreases in fatigue severity/frequency, and **no reported gastrointestinal adverse events (no nausea or constipation)** — a real, favorable brand-specific tolerability result, though this pass could not confirm the study's funding source/independence from the manufacturer, a relevant caveat given this database's recurring pattern of manufacturer-funded supplement trials. Iron Supplements (3% share, smallest tracked) is covered by [[iron]].

## Pros
- **Genuine brand-specific clinical trial data**: an 8-week university-affiliated study found real iron-store increases, reduced fatigue, and zero reported GI adverse events — a real positive differentiator versus most iron brands in this pass, which rely only on general ingredient-level evidence
- Iron bisglycinate (chelated iron) is independently described as better-absorbed and generally better-tolerated than non-chelated ferrous salts (ferrous sulfate, ferrous fumarate) used by most competitors in this category ([[Slow Fe]], [[Feosol]], [[Ferro-Sequels]], [[Vitron-C]])
- Vitamin C, folic acid, and B12 co-formulation supports absorption and addresses complementary nutritional needs relevant to anemia
- "FoodState"/fermented, beet-juice-processed positioning targets consumers seeking a gentler, more "whole-food" iron source

## Cons
- This pass could not confirm whether the cited University of Maryland study was independently funded or manufacturer-sponsored — a relevant transparency gap given how often this database has found supplement brand studies to be industry-funded
- Smallest tracked share (3%) in this category, meaning less real-world pharmacist familiarity/volume than the ferrous-salt-based category leaders
- Chelated iron forms are generally more expensive per elemental-iron-mg than standard ferrous sulfate — a real practical/cost tradeoff for better tolerability
- No independent third-party quality certification (USP Verified, NSF) was located for MegaFood Blood Builder in this pass

## Use Cases
Iron-deficiency anemia treatment/prevention, particularly for users who have experienced GI intolerance to standard ferrous-salt iron supplements — see [[iron]] for the general evidence base.

**Real pediatric poisoning risk from iron overdose applies to all iron supplements, including chelated forms** — keep out of reach of children regardless of formulation. See [[iron]] for drug interactions.
