## Appears In

## Overview
No FDA Drug Facts label was found for Epsom-It Nerve Pain Relief (also sold as "Soothing Nerve" lotion) — the manufacturer explicitly markets it as **"drug-free,"** consistent with a cosmetic classification rather than an OTC monograph drug. The formula is built around a super-concentrated (~25%) magnesium sulfate (Epsom salt) base, plus arnica montana extract, aloe vera, and — notably — **capsaicin**, though at an undisclosed concentration with no accompanying drug-facts percentage or "active ingredient" claim. This is a genuinely odd combination: capsaicin is elsewhere in this same Pharmacy Times category ([[Capzasin]]) sold as a labeled, FDA-monographed 0.1% active ingredient, while here it appears as an undisclosed-concentration component of a product explicitly marketed as "drug-free" — worth naming plainly as an inconsistency in how the same molecule is regulated/marketed across brands in the identical product category, rather than assuming either framing is simply wrong. No dedicated ingredient page exists for topical magnesium sulfate (see also [[Theraworx]] for the same gap, in that case for a homeopathic-dilution product rather than this full-concentration one). No brand-specific clinical trial was found.

## Pros
- Transparent "drug-free" positioning at least signals to consumers that no FDA drug claim is being made, unlike products that use drug-adjacent marketing language without a confirmable label (contrast [[Flexitol Heel Balm]])
- Combines several plausible-mechanism ingredients (magnesium sulfate, arnica, aloe, capsaicin) in one product
- Fragrance-free, paraben-free formulation per manufacturer

## Cons
- Contains capsaicin — a TRPV1 agonist with real burn-risk and adherence considerations per [[capsaicin]] — at an undisclosed concentration and with no FDA drug-facts labeling, meaning consumers cannot compare its dose to the labeled, monographed Capzasin product in the same category
- "Drug-free" marketing may create a false impression of inertness for a product that in fact contains a pharmacologically active TRPV1 agonist
- No independent trial evidence located for this specific formulation
- No dedicated ingredient page exists for topical magnesium sulfate to formally grade the Epsom-salt component's own evidence base

## Use Cases
Marketed for nerve pain, restless legs, and tired-muscle discomfort, including diabetic neuropathy symptom relief, via a magnesium/arnica/capsaicin/aloe combination.

## Allergies / Warnings
No FDA-mandated warnings identified (no confirmed drug label, "drug-free" self-classification). General caution given undisclosed capsaicin content: avoid application to broken/irritated skin, avoid tight bandaging or heat sources (standard capsaicin-class burn-risk precautions per [[capsaicin]]), even though this product does not carry those warnings on an FDA label.

## Symptoms Treated
Nerve pain, restless legs, tired/sore muscles — marketed broadly rather than tied to a specific FDA-labeled indication.

## Brand-Specific Alignment Check

This is preserved as a real labeling inconsistency across brands rather than resolved — a consumer cannot compare doses between the two products.
