Same confirmed formulation as [[A&D]] and [[A+D First Aid Ointment]] (both already researched in this database): **lanolin 15.5% + petrolatum 53.4%**, both classified as FDA OTC skin protectants. This bare "A+D Ointment" brand-file entry in **Incontinence-Related Skin Protectants (17% share) is a CORRECT category placement** — unlike [[A&D]]'s and [[A+D First Aid Ointment]]'s flagged mismatch under "Topical Antibiotics/Antiseptics" and "Burn Treatments" respectively, this product's actual occlusive moisture-barrier mechanism (see [[petrolatum]]) is exactly what a "skin protectant" category calls for. This is the product's own primary FDA-labeled indication (diaper rash/incontinence-associated dermatitis prevention and protection), so no discrepancy applies here — worth noting explicitly as a contrast to the two mismatched category appearances of the same underlying formulation elsewhere in this database.

No brand-specific clinical trial for this use context was identified; evidence is at the [[petrolatum]] ingredient level (Smack et al. 1996 RCT, though that trial used plain petrolatum, not this lanolin-petrolatum combination).

## Pros
- Confirmed FDA Drug Facts label with a simple, long-established two-ingredient formulation (lanolin + petrolatum)
- **Correctly filed** in this category — a genuine skin-protectant product appropriately matched to a skin-protectant category, in contrast to its mismatched appearances elsewhere in this database
- Petrolatum's occlusive mechanism has real supporting RCT evidence for uncomplicated wound/skin-barrier care (see [[petrolatum]])
- Long safety track record, widely used for incontinence-associated dermatitis and diaper rash prevention

## Cons
- No brand-specific clinical trial exists for this exact combination product; evidence is at the ingredient level, and the pivotal petrolatum RCT used plain (not lanolin-combined) petrolatum
- Lanolin carries its own known (if uncommon) contact-allergy risk — a real consideration for repeated, prolonged use on already-irritated incontinence-associated skin
- Purely a barrier/protectant mechanism — does not address any underlying infection or inflammation if incontinence-associated dermatitis has progressed beyond simple moisture-barrier breakdown

## Use Cases
Skin protectant/moisture barrier for incontinence-associated skin irritation and dermatitis prevention/treatment, via lanolin and petrolatum's combined occlusive mechanism.

## Allergies / Warnings
Per label (consistent with [[A&D]]): for external use only; discontinue if condition worsens or does not improve; some users may have lanolin contact sensitivity.

## Symptoms Treated
Skin irritation and breakdown associated with incontinence, via a protective moisture barrier — not an antimicrobial or anti-inflammatory mechanism.
