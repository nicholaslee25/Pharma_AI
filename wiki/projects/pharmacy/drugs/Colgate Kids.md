## Appears In
Children's Mouthwashes - 24%

## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed: Colgate Kids mouthwash (Minions, Unicorn Alcohol Free, and related varieties) is **sodium fluoride 0.02% (0.01% w/v fluoride ion)**, matching [[fluoride-toothpaste]]'s general fluoride evidence base. **Notable ingredient-list finding:** the formulation also contains **cetylpyridinium chloride (CPC)**, but it is listed among the INACTIVE ingredients despite CPC's real, independently-documented antimicrobial properties — the same "real-activity ingredient relegated to inactive status" pattern already flagged in this database for CloSYS (chlorine dioxide) and Arm & Hammer (baking soda), though here CPC is not marketed as the product's defining technology the way those other ingredients were, so the discrepancy is more minor. **Discrepancy/comparison worth flagging directly:** this concentration is roughly HALF the fluoride-ion content of the category leader, ACT Kids Anticavity (sodium fluoride 0.05%, 0.02% w/v fluoride ion — see `drugs/ACT Kids Anticavity.md`).

## Pros
- Confirmed FDA Drug Facts label with a well-established anticavity active ingredient, backed by [[fluoride-toothpaste]]'s general fluoride evidence base
- Contains cetylpyridinium chloride as a supporting antimicrobial ingredient (inactive-labeled, but with real documented antibacterial properties per general pharmacology), potentially contributing a plaque-control benefit alongside the fluoride's anticavity action, though not independently trial-verified for this specific combination
- Second-largest share (24%) among Children's Mouthwashes in this database

## Cons
- Lower fluoride-ion concentration (0.01% w/v) than the category-leading ACT Kids product (0.02% w/v) — a real, quantified potency difference a caregiver comparing products should be aware of, though this database did not independently confirm whether the lower concentration is clinically meaningfully less effective at typical rinse-use patterns
- The CPC-as-inactive-ingredient labeling could understate a real supporting antimicrobial benefit to a consumer reading only the "Active Ingredient" line, the same labeling-transparency nuance already flagged for CloSYS and Arm & Hammer
- Labeled for ages 6+ with supervision for under 12 — not appropriate for younger children given fluorosis risk during tooth development
- No independent, Colgate-brand-specific rinse efficacy RCT was identified in this pass

## Use Cases
Cavity prevention via daily fluoride mouth rinse, ages 6+, with a supporting antimicrobial ingredient (CPC) alongside the fluoride.

## Allergies / Warnings
Per label: keep away from children; if more than used for rinsing is accidentally swallowed, get medical help or contact a Poison Control Center right away.
