## Appears In
Ear Wax Removal - 3%

## Overview
**Discrepancy/complexity flagged:** Clinere is actually two distinct product types under one brand. (1) A **mechanical ear-cleaning tool** (medical-grade silicone scoop-and-fin design, reusable or disposable) — this is a device with no active ingredient, not a drug at all, designed to clean only the entrance to the ear canal via a built-in comfort-stop guide rather than probing deeper like a cotton swab. (2) A separate **"Ear Wax Removal Kit" liquid**, confirmed via its own DailyMed label to be carbamide-peroxide-based — the same active ingredient class as [[Debrox]], covered by [[cerumenolytics]]. The sources reviewed in this pass could not confirm which SKU drives Pharmacy Times' tracked 3% recommendation share. Notably, [[cerumenolytics]]'s cited StatPearls source explicitly warns against cotton swabs for wax removal (risk of worsening impaction or perforating the eardrum) — Clinere's tool markets itself as a safer alternative to cotton swabs specifically because of its stop-guide design, a real design mitigation of that documented risk, though no independent injury-rate data comparing Clinere to cotton swabs was located.

## Pros
- The mechanical tool's stop-guide design is a genuine, documented mitigation of the cotton-swab perforation/impaction risk that [[cerumenolytics]]'s StatPearls source warns about generally
- The liquid kit SKU uses the same well-established carbamide peroxide active as Debrox
- Offers a non-chemical option (the tool alone) for users who prefer not to use any cerumenolytic liquid

## Cons
- **Brand identity is genuinely split** between a device (tool) and a drug (liquid kit) — a consumer or pharmacist recommendation of "Clinere" doesn't specify which, and this database's sources could not resolve which SKU underlies its Pharmacy Times share
- The tool still requires insertion into the outer ear/canal entrance; manufacturer's own instructions to "use lightly, especially on first use" imply a real, if apparently low, injury risk if used too aggressively despite the stop-guide
- The liquid kit SKU carries the same [[cerumenolytics]]-documented caveat as Debrox: no Cochrane-level evidence that carbamide peroxide outperforms plain water/saline
- No brand-specific clinical/injury-rate trial exists for either SKU

## Use Cases
Tool: manual removal of visible/entrance-level earwax and outer-ear itch relief. Liquid kit: occasional softening/loosening of excessive earwax (same indication as [[cerumenolytics]]-class products generally).

## Allergies / Warnings
Tool: do not use with a perforated eardrum, ear pain, or history of ear surgery; use gently, especially on first use; do not insert past the stop-guide. Liquid kit: standard cerumenolytic-class cautions (avoid with perforated eardrum, ear pain/discharge, or recent ear surgery) per [[cerumenolytics]].

## Symptoms Treated
Visible/entrance-level earwax buildup and outer-ear itch (tool); excessive/impacted earwax (liquid kit).
