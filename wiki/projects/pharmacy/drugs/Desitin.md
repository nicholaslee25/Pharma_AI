## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed: Desitin is a **multi-formulation zinc-oxide brand with a real, quantified 3x concentration spread** — Daily Defense Cream (formerly "Rapid Relief") is **13% zinc oxide**, positioned for daily prevention/mild rash, while Maximum Strength Original Paste is **40% zinc oxide** (the highest concentration available without a prescription), positioned for severe/persistent rash and overnight use. This is a more extreme version of the one-brand-multiple-formulations pattern already documented across this database's multivitamin and cough-cold brands — here the formulations aren't just different products but a nearly 3x difference in active-ingredient concentration under one brand name, filed under Diaper Rash Products (31% share) and, separately, Incontinence-Related Skin Protectants (14% share). Confirms and extends [[zinc-oxide]]'s existing brand list, which had listed Desitin as "plausibly zinc-oxide-based by real-world market identity" without a confirmed concentration.

## Pros
- Confirmed FDA Drug Facts labels for both formulations, matching [[zinc-oxide]]'s barrier-protectant evidence base (Alkhamis et al. 2026 systematic review)
- Category-dominant share in both listed categories (31% Diaper Rash Products, 14% Incontinence-Related Skin Protectants)
- Two-tier concentration system (13% vs. 40%) allows matching product strength to rash severity, consistent with [[zinc-oxide]]'s own note that different studies show different zinc-oxide efficacy depending on severity/comparator — a caregiver or caregiver-of-an-incontinent-adult can plausibly step up from Daily Defense to Maximum Strength as needed

## Cons
- **This database's category-mapping data ("Desitin," 31%/14% share) does not specify which formulation (13% vs. 40% zinc oxide) the recommendation share refers to** — a caregiver assuming "Desitin" is a single fixed-concentration product would be mistaken, and the two formulations' relative real-world efficacy for a given rash severity was not independently confirmed by brand-specific RCT in this pass
- Per [[zinc-oxide]]'s own evidence review: overall evidence quality for zinc oxide barrier preparations is graded low-level (Grade C) due to missing methodological information across the underlying studies — a caveat that applies to Desitin's mechanism generally, regardless of which concentration is used
- No Desitin-specific RCT was independently located in this pass beyond the general zinc-oxide evidence base — the [[zinc-oxide]] page's cited comparative RCT (Desitin Maximum Strength vs. Calmoseptine in a hospitalized-patient context) was itself not independently re-verified in this pass
- Same-brand use across two different Pharmacy Times categories (Diaper Rash Products and Incontinence-Related Skin Protectants) means the underlying pathophysiology being addressed differs (infant diaper dermatitis vs. adult incontinence-associated dermatitis) even though the barrier-protectant mechanism and zinc-oxide evidence base is shared

## Use Cases
Prevention and treatment of diaper rash (irritant diaper dermatitis) in infants; prevention/treatment of incontinence-associated skin irritation in adults — both via the same zinc-oxide barrier-protectant mechanism, at either 13% or 40% concentration depending on product line and severity.

## Allergies / Warnings
Per label: for external use only; discontinue and consult a doctor if condition worsens or does not improve within 7 days, or if a rash is accompanied by fever. Generally regarded as very low-risk for topical use per [[zinc-oxide]]'s Contraindications section — no specific Desitin allergen finding identified in this pass.

## Symptoms Treated
Diaper rash / irritant diaper dermatitis (infants); incontinence-associated dermatitis (adults) — both via zinc oxide's moisture-barrier mechanism, per [[zinc-oxide]].
