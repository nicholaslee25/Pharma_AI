## Overview
Lipozene's active ingredient is **glucomannan**, a konjac-root-derived soluble fiber — a genuinely different fiber type than the psyllium/pectin combination [[fiber]]'s own cited meta-analysis found significantly effective (for constipation, not weight loss; glucomannan was not one of the two significant types in that review, and that review covered constipation, not weight loss, a different indication entirely). This pass confirmed **real, serious, repeated regulatory and legal action** against Lipozene's manufacturer, Obesity Research Institute (ORI): a 2005 FTC consent order and $1.5 million payment over deceptive rapid-weight-loss claims for prior products (FiberThin, Propolene); confirmed 2014 findings that Lipozene was violating that same 2005 consent order by continuing to claim rapid/substantial weight loss "for all users" and disease-treatment/"clinically proven" claims; and a 2013 class-action lawsuit (settled for up to $4.6 million without admitted wrongdoing) alleging Lipozene was falsely marketed as a "weight-loss breakthrough," with plaintiffs specifically alleging **glucomannan is not absorbed by the human body** and the product is neither safe nor effective as marketed. This is a real, repeated, multi-decade pattern of regulatory/legal action specifically over unsupported efficacy claims — one of the most heavily-documented false-advertising histories found in this entire database. Diet Aids (4% share) has no dedicated ingredient page in this database; glucomannan is a different fiber type than what [[fiber]] covers.

## Pros
- Glucomannan, as a soluble fiber, has a real, plausible general mechanism (fiber-related satiety/bulking) shared with other soluble fibers, even though this pass did not locate a Lipozene-specific trial independently confirming clinically meaningful weight-loss efficacy

## Cons
- **Real, repeated, multi-decade regulatory/legal history over false advertising**: a 2005 FTC consent order and $1.5M payment (for prior ORI products), a 2014 finding of continued consent-order violations specifically for Lipozene's own marketing, and a 2013 class-action lawsuit settled for up to $4.6 million — the single most extensive documented false-advertising pattern found for any brand in this Vitamins/Supplements research pass
- **The 2013 lawsuit's core allegation — that glucomannan is not meaningfully absorbed/effective as marketed — was not independently resolved by an efficacy trial in this pass**, and the repeated regulatory findings suggest the underlying efficacy claims have a real, documented history of being found unsupported by regulators
- No independent third-party quality certification (USP Verified, NSF) was located for Lipozene in this pass
- No dedicated glucomannan ingredient page exists yet in this database — a real research gap, since this pass could not independently evidence-grade the ingredient's actual weight-loss efficacy separate from the brand's own contested marketing claims

## Use Cases

## Allergies / Warnings
Soluble fiber supplements should be taken with adequate water to avoid choking/blockage risk (a general fiber-supplement caution, consistent with [[fiber]]'s own psyllium-specific warning, though not independently confirmed for glucomannan specifically in this pass).
