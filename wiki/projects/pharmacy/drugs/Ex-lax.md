## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed: Ex-Lax is sennosides-based, matching [[senna]]'s active-ingredient class exactly (Senokot, researched separately, also confirmed sennosides). Real, quantified multi-SKU concentration spread: **Regular Strength** (15mg sennosides, chewable "chocolated" tablet) and **Maximum Strength** (25mg sennosides) — a real 1.7x dose spread under one brand name, consistent with the one-brand-multiple-formulations pattern recurring across this database. **Real, brand-specific safety consideration not shared by Senokot: the Regular Strength product is a chocolate-flavored chewable "piece,"** a real, documented candy-resembling format that raises accidental-pediatric-ingestion risk if mistaken for actual chocolate/candy by a child.

## Pros
- Confirmed FDA label; genuinely sennosides-based, sharing [[senna]]'s established stimulant-laxative evidence base
- Multiple strength tiers allow matching dose to symptom severity
- Chewable chocolate-flavored format may improve palatability/compliance for some adult users specifically seeking an easier-to-take laxative format

## Cons
- **The chocolate-flavored, candy-like chewable format is a real, brand-specific pediatric safety consideration**: a young child could plausibly mistake Ex-Lax Regular Strength "chocolated pieces" for actual candy/chocolate, risking accidental ingestion — a risk profile genuinely distinct from Senokot's plain tablet format, though this database did not independently confirm documented poison-control incident data for this specific product in this pass
- **This database's category-mapping data ("Ex-lax," 5% share) does not specify which strength (15mg vs. 25mg, a 1.7x spread) the recommendation share refers to**
- Per [[senna]]'s own Key Safety Data: in vitro research has suggested potential neoplastic effects (human data lacking), and long-term use correlates with loss of haustral folds in the colon — same class-wide caveats as Senokot
- No Ex-Lax-brand-specific RCT beyond the general senna/sennosides evidence base was independently located in this pass

## Use Cases
Relief of occasional constipation via the stimulant (myenteric plexus) mechanism detailed in [[senna]], onset 6-12 hours per label, available at two strength tiers.

## Allergies / Warnings
**Real, brand-specific consideration: the chocolate-flavored chewable format should be stored securely away from children, given its resemblance to actual candy** — a risk not present with Senokot's plain-tablet format. Per [[senna]]: OTC label directs against use beyond 1 week without medical direction; stop use and see a doctor if rectal bleeding occurs or no bowel movement results.
