## Overview
The bare "Vaseline" brand-file entry (distinct from [[Vaseline Petroleum Jelly]] and [[Vaseline Lip Therapy]], tracked separately elsewhere in this database) corresponds to Unilever's **Vaseline Intensive Care** lotion/cream line — confirmed via DailyMed (labeler: Conopco Inc. d/b/a Unilever, product "Vaseline Intensive Care Deep Moisture"). Active ingredient: **white petrolatum USP 30%**, purpose "skin protectant." This is a genuine, confirmed **actual FDA OTC drug**, not a cosmetic — a real contrast to [[Vanicream]]'s Moisturizing Cream in the same two categories, which carries no FDA-recognized active ingredient at all.

**Confirmed concentration difference within the same brand:** Vaseline Intensive Care's 30% petrolatum is a real formulation step down from [[Vaseline Petroleum Jelly]]'s near-100% pure petrolatum (per [[petrolatum]]'s existing brand entry) — the Intensive Care lotion blends petrolatum with water, glycerin, and additional emollients/preservatives into a spreadable lotion, while Petroleum Jelly is the classic occlusive-only product. Both are marketed under the "Vaseline" name but are meaningfully different formulations for different use cases (lotion vs. thick occlusive balm).

**Separate SKU-line finding, not part of this brand-file's tracked categories:** "Vaseline Intensive Care Aloe Fresh Protecting Body Lotion SPF 15" is a chemical-filter sunscreen (octinoxate 7.5%, oxybenzone 3.0%, octisalate 1.0%) — a completely different active-ingredient class from the petrolatum-based Deep Moisture line, illustrating the same "one brand name, multiple mechanisms" pattern seen elsewhere in this database, though this specific SKU is not the one driving this brand-file's Eczema Care/Moisturizers share.

No Vaseline-Intensive-Care-brand-specific clinical trial was located; see [[petrolatum]] for the Smack 1996 RCT (922 patients, plain white petrolatum vs. bacitracin for postprocedure wound care) — that trial used plain petrolatum, not this specific 30%-petrolatum lotion formulation, so its results should not be assumed to transfer at full strength.

## Pros
- Confirmed actual FDA OTC Drug Facts label (white petrolatum 30%, skin protectant) — a genuine drug product, not merely cosmetic marketing, unlike some competing "moisturizer" entries in the same categories (see [[Vanicream]])
- Petrolatum's occlusive mechanism has strong general safety and efficacy support at the ingredient level (see [[petrolatum]], including a very clean allergy profile in its pivotal RCT)
- Lower petrolatum concentration (30%) than pure Petroleum Jelly makes for a lighter, more spreadable lotion, likely improving cosmetic acceptability/adherence for daily all-over-body moisturizing use versus thick petroleum jelly

## Cons
- No brand-specific (Vaseline Intensive Care) clinical trial was located — evidence is at the [[petrolatum]] ingredient level, and even that pivotal trial used a different (higher-concentration, plain) petrolatum formulation
- Contains preservatives (methylparaben, propylparaben) and fragrance-adjacent ingredients (cedrol) not present in plain petroleum jelly — a real, if generally low-risk, difference in contact-sensitization potential between this lotion and [[Vaseline Petroleum Jelly]]
- Lowest recommendation share (1%) in both tracked categories, well behind category leaders like [[CeraVe]]/[[Aveeno Eczema Therapy]]
- The brand's "Aloe Fresh SPF 15" chemical-sunscreen SKU (octinoxate/oxybenzone/octisalate) is a real formulation departure from the plain-petrolatum identity most consumers associate with "Vaseline" — a potential source of consumer confusion about what "Vaseline" products actually contain from SKU to SKU

## Use Cases
Temporary protection and relief of chapped/cracked skin and environmental drying, plus temporary protection of minor cuts/scrapes/burns, via white petrolatum's occlusive skin-protectant mechanism (see [[petrolatum]]).

## Allergies / Warnings
Per label: external use only, avoid eye contact; discontinue use if condition worsens or symptoms persist beyond 7 days; do not use on deep wounds, animal bites, or serious burns; keep away from children, contact Poison Control if swallowed. Per [[petrolatum]]: generally very clean allergy profile at the ingredient level, though this specific lotion's added preservatives/fragrance-adjacent ingredients are not covered by that ingredient-level allergy data.

## Symptoms Treated
Chapped/cracked/dry skin, minor cuts/scrapes/burns (temporary protection), via petrolatum's occlusive barrier mechanism.
