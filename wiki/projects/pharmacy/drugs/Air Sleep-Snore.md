## Overview
This entry matches **AirSnore**, a dual-component anti-snoring kit combining (1) a boil-and-bite **mandibular advancement mouthpiece** (hard outer plastic layer + soft moldable thermoplastic inner layer, both medical-grade and BPA-free) with (2) **AirSnore Drops**, an essential-oil blend (sunflower seed oil, eucalyptus leaf oil, lavender oil, peppermint leaf oil, Scots pine leaf oil) applied topically, marketed to relieve congestion and promote relaxation. Per [[snore-aid-devices]], the mouthpiece component belongs to the MAD subtype with the strongest RCT-level support in the broader sleep-medicine literature for actual OSA-severity reduction — though, consistent with [[SnoreRx]]'s FDA clearance scope, an OTC boil-and-bite MAD is typically cleared for snoring reduction specifically, not diagnosed OSA treatment. The Drops component has no independent snoring-efficacy trial located in this pass — an aromatherapy-level addition, not a mechanism responsible for any airway-opening effect.

## Pros
- The mouthpiece component uses the MAD mechanism, the device subtype with the strongest evidence base in this category per [[snore-aid-devices]]
- Adjustable boil-and-bite fitting allows some degree of personalization without a dental visit
- Dual-component design (mouthpiece + drops) may appeal to users wanting a congestion-relief addition alongside the mechanical device

## Cons
- **The Drops component's essential-oil blend has no independent snoring-efficacy evidence** — its inclusion in a "dual-action" marketing claim could overstate what the aromatic oils alone contribute beyond the mouthpiece's mechanical effect
- Per [[snore-aid-devices]], MADs carry a real long-term risk of dental/skeletal side effects (bite changes, tooth movement, jaw discomfort) with regular use — a genuine consideration not shared by nasal-based devices
- Not a substitute for diagnosed-OSA treatment even though the general MAD class has real OSA-relevant literature — no brand-specific trial confirms this exact product performs at that level

## Use Cases
Snoring reduction via jaw repositioning (mouthpiece); congestion/relaxation support via topical essential oils (drops) — not a diagnosed-OSA treatment.

## Allergies / Warnings
Mouthpiece: general oral-appliance cautions, dental/jaw evaluation advisable before regular use. Drops: essential-oil sensitivity is a general consideration (eucalyptus, peppermint, and pine oils can occasionally irritate skin or mucous membranes in sensitive users).

## Symptoms Treated
Snoring (jaw-repositioning mechanism); nasal/sinus congestion sensation and relaxation (aromatic oils, unproven for snoring specifically).
