## Overview
Confirmed: Retaine's (OCuSOFT) flagship MGD (meibomian gland dysfunction) formula has an FDA label (DailyMed) listing **light mineral oil 0.5% and mineral oil 0.5%** as active ingredients, formulated as a milky-white oil-in-water emulsion — the same oil-emulsion, lipid-layer-targeted mechanism category as [[Soothe]] XP, distinct from the aqueous CMC/PEG/hypromellose products that make up most of this category. Retaine's name and MGD-specific branding directly signal its targeted use case: meibomian gland dysfunction causes evaporative dry eye through lipid-layer insufficiency, and an oil-emulsion drop is a mechanistically appropriate (if not independently outcome-trial-tested in the sources reviewed) match for that specific etiology, more so than a purely aqueous lubricant would be. A separate "Retaine CMC" product exists under the same brand umbrella using carboxymethylcellulose instead, and "Retaine PM" is a nighttime ointment formulation — meaning, similar to several other brands in this category, "Retaine" spans more than one active-ingredient profile depending on the specific named sub-product. No Retaine-brand-specific outcome trial beyond general mineral-oil-emulsion and MGD pathophysiology evidence was identified.

## Pros
- Oil-emulsion mechanism is a genuinely differentiated, MGD-appropriate approach relative to most other brands in this category, which skew toward aqueous-deficiency-focused lubricants
- Preservative system (cetalkonium chloride) and formulation details are clearly documented on the FDA label, giving good transparency for users with preservative sensitivities to evaluate
- Product-line naming (MGD, CMC, PM) is relatively explicit about which sub-product uses which mechanism, reducing (though not eliminating) the cross-SKU confusion seen in some competing multi-formulation brands

## Cons
- Smallest recommendation share in the category (1%), likely reflecting a smaller, more specialty-positioned (MGD-specific) market niche rather than a quality signal
- No independent brand-specific outcome trial (as distinct from general MGD/oil-emulsion pathophysiology literature) was located to confirm real-world symptom improvement for this specific product
- As with other multi-SKU brands in this category, "Retaine" alone does not specify which active ingredient a given recommendation refers to (MGD's mineral oil vs. CMC's cellulose-based formula) — a consumer should confirm the specific named sub-product

## Use Cases
Retaine MGD: evaporative dry eye associated with meibomian gland dysfunction specifically, via lipid-layer oil-emulsion supplementation. (Other Retaine sub-products, e.g. CMC and PM, use different mechanisms not covered in depth in this pass.)

## Allergies / Warnings
Per label: for external use only; discontinue and see a doctor for eye pain, vision changes, continued redness/irritation, or symptoms persisting beyond 72 hours. Oil-based formulation may cause transient blurred vision immediately after instillation, a formulation-inherent effect.

## Symptoms Treated
Evaporative dry eye specifically associated with meibomian gland dysfunction (MGD formula); general dry-eye symptoms depending on sub-product.
