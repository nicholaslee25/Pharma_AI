## Appears In

## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed: PanOxyl is a **single-mechanism, multi-concentration** brand — every SKU located uses **benzoyl peroxide**, matching this database's existing [[benzoyl-peroxide]] evidence page exactly, but spans a wide concentration range across formats: 4% Creamy Wash, 5% Regular Strength Cleansing Bar, and 10% Foaming Wash/Acne Cleanser Cream (marketed "maximum strength," available without a prescription). Unlike [[Cetaphil]], [[Clearasil]], and [[AcneFree]] elsewhere in this pass, PanOxyl does not split across benzoyl peroxide AND salicylic acid — it is genuinely a single-ingredient brand, just at three different strengths. This concentration spread (4-10%, a 2.5x range) is real and consumer-relevant: PanOxyl's own DailyMed labels for the cleansing-bar SKU cite the same benzoyl-peroxide efficacy data StatPearls describes generally (10% applied daily for two weeks reduces *P. acnes* by 98% and skin free fatty acids by 50%, per [[benzoyl-peroxide]]).

## Pros
- Single, confirmed active ingredient across all SKUs checked — genuinely simpler brand-name-to-mechanism mapping than most other acne brands in this pass
- Matches [[benzoyl-peroxide]]'s FDA-monograph-approved concentration range (2.5-10%) at every tier checked
- Wash-off formats (creamy wash, foaming wash, cleansing bar) allow shorter contact time than leave-on gels, potentially reducing irritation while still delivering the Cochrane-documented efficacy signal for BPO (see [[benzoyl-peroxide]]'s Yang et al. 2020 review, RR 1.27 for participant-reported improvement)
- "Maximum strength" 10% tier gives consumers access to the top of the FDA-approved OTC range without a prescription

## Cons
- Per [[benzoyl-peroxide]]'s own Cochrane evidence: withdrawal due to adverse effects (erythema, pruritus, burning) is significantly HIGHER with BPO than placebo (RR 2.13) — a real, non-trivial tolerability tradeoff that applies at any PanOxyl concentration, and more so at the 10% "maximum strength" tier
- A consumer selecting "PanOxyl" without checking the concentration (4% vs. 5% vs. 10%) could unintentionally jump between a mild and a maximum-strength product — same brand-name-spans-a-concentration-range pattern already flagged elsewhere in this database (Desitin, Rolaids)
- No PanOxyl-brand-specific RCT beyond the general benzoyl-peroxide evidence base was independently located in this pass
- Wash-off format's shorter skin-contact time, while gentler, has not been independently shown in PanOxyl-specific trials to match leave-on BPO gel efficacy — a plausible but unconfirmed assumption

## Use Cases
Treatment of mild-to-moderate acne vulgaris via antibacterial/keratolytic benzoyl peroxide action, with a consumer choice of 4% (daily control), 5% (regular strength), or 10% (maximum strength) depending on tolerance and severity.

## Allergies / Warnings
Per [[benzoyl-peroxide]] label conventions: for external use only; avoid eyes, lips, and mouth; may bleach hair or colored fabric; skin irritation (redness, dryness, peeling) more likely at higher concentrations or with sun exposure; discontinue if excessive irritation occurs; do not use with other topical acne medications at the same time unless directed by a doctor.

## Symptoms Treated
Acne vulgaris — via the same *Cutibacterium acnes*-targeting, keratolytic/sebostatic mechanism documented on [[benzoyl-peroxide]].
