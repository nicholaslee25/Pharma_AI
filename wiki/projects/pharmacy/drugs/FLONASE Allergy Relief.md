## Overview

Note: Flonase Sensimist is a *different* fluticasone salt (fluticasone furoate) — a separate product not to be confused with plain Flonase despite the shared brand name, per the ingredient page's own existing caveat.

## Pros
- [[intranasal-corticosteroids]]'s evidence grade is "High — recommended first-line for allergic rhinitis," backed by a large 151-study meta-analysis; fluticasone propionate specifically is among the top-performing single agents for nasal symptoms
- Per that page's mechanism section, intranasal corticosteroids are superior to antihistamines specifically at reducing nasal inflammation and mucosal pathology, and more effective than antihistamines at relieving sneezing, rhinorrhea, and nasal blockage
- By far the largest recommendation share (77%/82%) of any product in either of its categories in this database — consistent with strong evidence backing this class and this specific brand

## Cons
- Full onset of anti-inflammatory benefit for intranasal corticosteroids typically takes days of consistent use, not immediate relief like an antihistamine or decongestant — a real usability trade-off vs. as-needed products
- Local irritation, nosebleeds, and (rarely) septal perforation with prolonged high-dose use are known class risks (general corticosteroid-class knowledge; specific incidence data would need to be checked against the ingredient page's Key Safety Data section)

## Use Cases
First-line treatment for allergic rhinitis: nasal congestion, itchy nose, runny nose, itchy/watery eyes, sneezing.
