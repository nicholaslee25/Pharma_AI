## Overview
**Real mismatched-mechanism finding.** [[proton-pump-inhibitors]]'s existing text described Zegerid OTC only as an "omeprazole/sodium bicarbonate combination, per common real-world market identity," without confirming the formulation matters mechanistically. Confirmed via DailyMed: Zegerid OTC is genuinely different from Prilosec OTC, Nexium 24HR, and Prevacid 24HR despite sharing the same PPI drug class and (for the omeprazole component) the exact same molecule as Prilosec — it is an **immediate-release** capsule (omeprazole 20mg + sodium bicarbonate 1100mg), not a delayed-release enteric-coated tablet/capsule like its three category-mates. The sodium bicarbonate's role is not just an inactive buffer: it protects the acid-labile omeprazole from being destroyed by stomach acid before absorption, allowing immediate release instead of requiring an enteric coating — a real, distinct formulation strategy for delivering the same drug class. **Separate, clinically important finding: each capsule delivers approximately 303mg of sodium** (from the 1100mg sodium bicarbonate) — a real, non-trivial sodium load not present in any other PPI brand in this category, with the manufacturer's own label specifically warning that patients on a low-sodium diet may not be able to use this product, and that it should be used with caution in congestive heart failure or hypertension.

## Pros
- Confirmed FDA OTC label; omeprazole is the same molecule as Prilosec OTC, so shares that evidence base for the acid-suppression mechanism itself
- Immediate-release formulation means, per label, onset "may take 1 to 14 days" is bracketed differently than delayed-release competitors — some sources note faster initial acid-suppression onset than enteric-coated PPIs due to the bicarbonate buffering mechanism, though this database did not independently verify a head-to-head onset-speed RCT in this pass
- No enteric coating to accidentally destroy by chewing/crushing (a risk flagged for enteric-coated competitors and for bisacodyl-class stimulant laxatives elsewhere in this database) — though the label does not indicate the capsule is meant to be chewed either

## Cons
- **~303mg of sodium per capsule is a real, quantified, and clinically relevant difference from every other PPI brand in this category** — the manufacturer's own label warns patients on a low-sodium/salt-restricted diet, or with heart failure/hypertension, to consult a doctor before use. This is a genuine safety consideration this database's [[proton-pump-inhibitors]] page's general Contraindications section does not itemize (that section discusses severe hepatic disease, not sodium load), because it is specific to this one brand's bicarbonate-based delivery mechanism, not the PPI class generally
- Same PPI-class long-term safety-signal list applies (hypomagnesemia, C. diff/infection risk, rebound acid hypersecretion) to the omeprazole component
- Smallest recommendation share of any PPI brand in this database (2–3%) — no red flag by itself, but means less real-world usage data/pharmacist familiarity relative to Prilosec/Nexium/Prevacid
- No Zegerid-specific RCT beyond the general omeprazole/PPI evidence base was independently located in this pass

## Use Cases
Treatment of frequent heartburn (≥2 days/week) via a 14-day course, once daily — same general PPI use pattern as [[Prilosec OTC]], but via an immediate-release/bicarbonate delivery mechanism rather than delayed-release enteric coating.

## Allergies / Warnings
**Real, brand-specific warning not shared by other PPI brands in this category: contains ~303mg sodium per capsule — patients on a low-sodium diet, or with congestive heart failure or hypertension, should consult a doctor before use**, per manufacturer labeling; chronic sodium bicarbonate use can also risk systemic alkalosis with excessive/prolonged use. Standard PPI-class warnings also apply: do not use with trouble/pain swallowing, vomiting blood, or bloody/black stools; omeprazole may cause severe skin reactions; pregnant/breastfeeding — ask a health professional.

## Symptoms Treated
Frequent heartburn / acid indigestion via the same omeprazole PPI mechanism as [[Prilosec OTC]], delivered through an immediate-release/sodium-bicarbonate formulation rather than delayed-release enteric coating.

## Brand-Specific Alignment Check
**Real finding, added to both this page and [[proton-pump-inhibitors]]:** Zegerid OTC is mechanistically distinct in delivery method (immediate-release + sodium bicarbonate buffer vs. delayed-release enteric coating) from every other PPI brand in this database's Gastrointestinal category, and carries a genuine, quantified sodium-load warning (~303mg/capsule) that does not apply to Prilosec OTC, Nexium 24HR, or Prevacid 24HR. A consumer with a sodium-restricted diet substituting Zegerid for another PPI brand assuming interchangeability would be making a real, clinically relevant mistake.
