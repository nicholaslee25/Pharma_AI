## Overview
Confirmed: NeilMed NasaMist is sold in both **isotonic (9 mg/mL sodium chloride, 0.9%)** and **hypertonic Extra Strength (27 mg/mL, 2.7%, marketed as a "drug-free nasal decongestant")** formulations — one of the few brands in this category offering both concentration types under one name. Per [[saline-nasal-sprays]], hypertonic saline has real meta-analysis-level evidence for a congestion-specific benefit beyond what isotonic saline provides, via an osmotic mechanism that draws fluid out of swollen nasal mucosa. Third-largest share (15%). No NeilMed-brand-specific clinical trial was identified beyond the general saline-class evidence.

## Pros
- Offers both isotonic and hypertonic options under one brand, letting users choose based on need (general moisturizing vs. congestion-specific relief)
- The hypertonic Extra Strength SKU is backed by real meta-analysis evidence for a congestion benefit beyond plain isotonic saline, per [[saline-nasal-sprays]]
- Sterile, preservative-free, pH-balanced formulation

## Cons
- Two different concentrations under the same brand name means a consumer must check the specific SKU to know which mechanism (pure moisturizing vs. osmotic decongestant effect) they're getting
- No brand-specific outcome trial beyond the general saline-class evidence exists
- Hypertonic saline can cause more stinging/irritation than isotonic in sensitive users, per [[saline-nasal-sprays]]'s general safety data

## Use Cases
Isotonic: general nasal moisturizing, allergy/sinus relief. Extra Strength/hypertonic: congestion relief via osmotic mechanism, marketed as a drug-free decongestant alternative.

## Allergies / Warnings
Not FDA-evaluated as a drug (no active ingredient); general saline-class cautions apply — mild stinging possible, more likely with the hypertonic formulation.
