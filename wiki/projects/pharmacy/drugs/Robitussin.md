## Overview
Robitussin is a large multi-SKU line, confirmed via DailyMed to span several genuinely different combinations: **Robitussin Maximum Strength Cough + Chest Congestion DM** = dextromethorphan HBr 20mg + guaifenesin 400mg per 20mL (touches both [[dextromethorphan]] and [[guaifenesin]]); **Robitussin CF** = dextromethorphan + guaifenesin + pseudoephedrine (adds decongestant); **Robitussin Maximum Strength 12 Hour Cough and Mucus Relief** = guaifenesin/dextromethorphan extended-release tablet (1200mg/60mg); **Robitussin Day and Night** kits split dosing with doxylamine added at night. This confirms and refines the earlier hedge ("guaifenesin... or dextromethorphan... not verified") — the answer for most core Robitussin SKUs is actually **both**, combined in one product, not an either/or choice as the old hedge implied. ## Pros
- The DM combination directly pairs a cough suppressant with an expectorant in one product, addressing both a dry/unproductive cough (dextromethorphan) and mucus thinning (guaifenesin) simultaneously
- 12-Hour extended-release option reduces dosing frequency
- Sugar-free/dye-free variants exist for those with dietary restrictions (relevant given its "Diabetic Cough Products" category listing at 28% share)
- Children's Robitussin Cough and Chest Congestion DM formulation available (ages 4+) for pediatric use, separate from adult-strength products

## Cons
- Because the brand spans single-ingredient-adjacent products (plain expectorant-focused SKUs) and multi-ingredient combinations (CF, DM, Day/Night), a consumer grabbing "Robitussin" off a shelf cannot assume a specific ingredient profile without reading the exact box — real risk of unintentional double-dosing dextromethorphan or guaifenesin if combining two different Robitussin products, or a Robitussin product with a separate cold medicine
- Dextromethorphan carries real interaction risk with MAOIs (serotonin syndrome) — explicitly warned about on Robitussin's own labels per search results — and has documented recreational-misuse potential at supratherapeutic doses (relevant to [[dextromethorphan]] if that's covered there)
- Its 28% share in "Diabetic Cough Products" reflects being formulated without sugar in that specific SKU, not any special diabetes-specific pharmacological benefit — worth being explicit that this is a formulation/excipient distinction, not an efficacy one

## Use Cases
Cough suppression, chest congestion/mucus thinning, combined cough+congestion+decongestant relief (CF), nighttime cough relief with sleep aid (Day/Night kits), diabetic-friendly (sugar-free) cough relief.

## Allergies / Warnings
Do not use with MAOIs or within 2 weeks of stopping one (dextromethorphan interaction). Ask a doctor before use if cough is accompanied by too much phlegm (mucus), persists >7 days, or recurs (possible sign of a condition needing different treatment). Children <4y: ask a doctor for cough/cold products generally per current pediatric OTC guidance.

## Symptoms Treated
Cough (dry and productive), chest congestion, nasal congestion (CF variant), sleeplessness during illness (Day/Night variant).
