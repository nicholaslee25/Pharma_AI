## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed (labeler: Bayer Healthcare LLC): "Lotrimin" spans **two chemically distinct antifungal actives** under one brand name — a genuine "one brand, multiple mechanisms" case, though unlike some other examples in this database, both mechanisms here are well-evidenced:

- **Lotrimin AF (base line): clotrimazole 1%** — an azole antifungal, fungistatic mechanism (inhibits ergosterol synthesis via lanosterol 14-alpha-demethylase). Ages 2+. See [[clotrimazole]] for full evidence — high-grade for tinea infections at labeled doses, though explicitly NOT effective for nail or scalp fungal infections.
- **Lotrimin Ultra: butenafine hydrochloride 1%** — a benzylamine antifungal, fungicidal mechanism (inhibits squalene epoxidase). Ages 12+, a notably higher pediatric threshold than the clotrimazole line. See [[butenafine]] (new ingredient page built this pass) — genuine head-to-head RCT evidence shows butenafine achieving substantially higher clinical cure (26.5% vs. 2.9% at 1 week) and mycological cure (61.7% vs. 17.6%) than clotrimazole in tinea cruris/corporis, plus a strong placebo-controlled tinea pedis trial (88% vs. 33% mycologic cure).

**Real correction made this pass:** this database's [[terbinafine]] and [[clotrimazole]] ingredient pages previously stated Lotrimin Ultra uses terbinafine — this was incorrect and has been fixed on both pages. Lotrimin Ultra is butenafine, a different (if mechanistically related) molecule from [[Lamisil AT]]'s terbinafine.

Combined 49% (Jock Itch) and 39% (Toe/Foot) share does not distinguish which sub-line (clotrimazole AF vs. butenafine Ultra) drives most of the recommendation volume — the Pharmacy Times source data tracks "Lotrimin" as a single brand row across both mechanisms.

## Pros
- Both confirmed active ingredients (clotrimazole and butenafine) have genuine FDA Drug Facts labels and real trial-level evidence — clotrimazole's broad tinea-infection evidence base and butenafine's superior head-to-head RCT performance
- Offering both a fungistatic (clotrimazole) and fungicidal (butenafine) mechanism under one brand gives consumers/pharmacists a same-shelf upgrade path if the milder mechanism proves insufficient
- Category-leading share in both tracked categories (49%, 39%) reflects long-standing brand trust and efficacy

## Cons
- **Two distinct, non-interchangeable antifungal actives sold under one brand name** with different age restrictions (2+ vs. 12+) — a consumer or caregiver must read the specific product name ("AF" vs. "Ultra"), not just "Lotrimin," to know which active and age restriction applies
- Per [[clotrimazole]]: NOT effective for onychomycosis (nail fungus) or scalp infections — a real limitation on consumer expectations for the base AF line, likely relevant given this brand also appears in Toe/Foot Antifungal Products
- No Lotrimin-brand-specific (as opposed to general clotrimazole/butenafine ingredient-level) clinical trial was located; the strongest trial evidence (butenafine RCTs) concerns the active ingredient generically, not independently confirmed to be Lotrimin-brand-sponsored research
- The genuine efficacy advantage of butenafine (Ultra) over clotrimazole (AF) in head-to-head trials is not obviously surfaced to a consumer choosing between the two Lotrimin sub-lines by name alone

## Use Cases
Treatment of athlete's foot (tinea pedis), jock itch (tinea cruris), and ringworm (tinea corporis) via either clotrimazole's fungistatic azole mechanism (AF line, ages 2+, 2-4 week courses depending on indication) or butenafine's fungicidal benzylamine mechanism (Ultra line, ages 12+).

## Allergies / Warnings
Clotrimazole (AF) line: per [[clotrimazole]] — external use only; avoid eyes; not effective for nail/scalp infections; azole hypersensitivity; seek medical care if no improvement within 2 weeks (jock itch) or 4 weeks (athlete's foot/ringworm). Butenafine (Ultra) line: per [[butenafine]] — external use only; not for nails, scalp, mouth, eyes, or vaginal use; ages 12+ only.

## Symptoms Treated
Itching, burning, cracking, and scaling from athlete's foot, jock itch, and ringworm — mechanism (fungistatic vs. fungicidal) and pediatric eligibility depending on which specific Lotrimin sub-line is chosen.
