## Appears In

## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed: Alavert Allergy and Congestion D-12 Hour = **loratadine 5mg + pseudoephedrine sulfate 120mg**, extended-release, dosed every 12 hours. This confirms the earlier "widely known as... not independently confirmed" hedge exactly as guessed. Note the loratadine dose here (5mg) is half the plain-Alavert dose (10mg) — the same pattern already observed for Zyrtec-D's reduced cetirizine dose, suggesting this may be a common design convention across "-D" antihistamine/decongestant combinations rather than a one-off. Touches [[second-generation-antihistamines]] (loratadine dosing not specifically covered there — see the plain Alavert/Claritin brand pages) and [[pseudoephedrine]].

## Pros
- Evidence-backed decongestant component (pseudoephedrine, "moderate-to-high" per [[pseudoephedrine]])
- Likely the lowest-cost pseudoephedrine + antihistamine combination option in this batch, consistent with Alavert's general market positioning as the value-priced loratadine brand

## Cons
- Behind-the-counter purchase restriction applies due to the pseudoephedrine component
- Smallest recommendation share (2%) of any product in the Decongestants, Oral category in this database — likely reflects lower brand recognition rather than an efficacy or safety difference, since the ingredients themselves are well-evidenced
- The reduced loratadine dose (5mg vs. 10mg) means this combination may deliver less antihistamine effect than plain Alavert/Claritin — worth being explicit about for a reader assuming equivalence

## Use Cases
Non-drowsy relief of nasal/sinus congestion plus allergy symptoms (sneezing, runny nose, itchy/watery eyes, itchy throat/nose) for indoor/outdoor allergies.

## Allergies / Warnings
Ask a doctor before use with liver/kidney disease (loratadine), high blood pressure, heart disease, thyroid disease, or diabetes (pseudoephedrine).

## Symptoms Treated
Nasal/sinus congestion, sneezing, runny nose, itchy/watery eyes, itchy throat/nose.
