## Overview
**Real category-mismatch finding, same pattern as [[Band-Aid Acne Patches]] elsewhere in this pass.** Mighty Patch (Hero Cosmetics) is a hydrocolloid acne patch, explicitly marketed as "drug-free" — medical-grade hydrocolloid (fluid-absorbing agents including pectin) that mechanically absorbs fluid/gunk from a whitehead over several hours, with no FDA Drug Facts panel and no active pharmaceutical ingredient. This is the same non-drug/device category as [[Band-Aid Acne Patches]], and works best specifically on whiteheads rather than other acne lesion types (cystic, comedonal, or inflammatory papules) — a real, if minor, scope limitation not disclosed by "acne treatment" framing generally.

## Pros
- No drug-interaction or chemical-irritation risk since no active pharmaceutical ingredient is present, a genuine advantage for users sensitive to benzoyl peroxide/salicylic acid/adapalene
- Marketed and manufacturer-stated as "US FDA approved" as a device (not the same regulatory pathway or standard as an FDA-approved drug — device clearance and drug approval are different regulatory processes, worth distinguishing rather than conflating)
- Latex-free, addressing one common hydrocolloid-material allergen concern directly

## Cons
- **Non-drug device, not a treatment for acne's underlying causes** — same finding as [[Band-Aid Acne Patches]]: does nothing for comedone formation, bacterial load (*C. acnes*), or inflammation the way [[benzoyl-peroxide]], [[salicylic-acid]], or [[adapalene]] do
- Manufacturer's own positioning states it "works best on whiteheads" — a real, disclosed scope limitation not shared by the drug-based treatments in this category, which are formulated to work across broader acne-lesion types
- Pectin/rubber-sensitive individuals should avoid, per manufacturer caution (same hydrocolloid-material consideration as [[Band-Aid Acne Patches]])
- No clinical trial evidence (RCT or otherwise) for hydrocolloid patches treating acne was located in this pass — claims are mechanistic/observational (visible absorption), not outcome-trial-based
- "US FDA approved" marketing language for a device-classified, non-drug product could be read by a consumer as implying the same regulatory rigor as an FDA-approved drug, when device clearance and drug approval follow different standards

## Use Cases
Physical absorption of fluid from an existing whitehead specifically, plus physical protection from picking — narrower in scope than a general acne treatment.

## Allergies / Warnings
Manufacturer caution: may not be suitable for individuals allergic to pectin or rubber; latex-free. No FDA Drug Facts warnings exist since this is a non-drug device.

## Symptoms Treated
None in the pharmacological sense — mechanically manages fluid from an existing whitehead; does not treat acne's underlying inflammatory or bacterial process, and is not positioned by its own manufacturer as effective for other acne-lesion types.
