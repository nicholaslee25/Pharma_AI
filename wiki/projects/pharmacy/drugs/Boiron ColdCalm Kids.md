## Appears In
Children's Homeopathic Cough Products - 9%

## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed: ColdCalm Kids spans **two formulations with different ingredient sets from each other AND from the adult `drugs/Boiron ColdCalm.md` product** (9-remedy combination: Allium cepa, Apis mellifica, Belladonna, Eupatorium perfoliatum, Gelsemium sempervirens, Kali bichromicum, Nux vomica, Phytolacca decandra, Pulsatilla). ColdCalm Kids **Liquid Doses**: Allium Cepa 3C, Apis Mellifica 15C, Gelsemium Sempervirens 6C, Kali Bichromicum 6C (4 remedies). ColdCalm Kids **Pellets**: Allium cepa 3C, Apis mellifica 6C, Belladonna 6C (3 remedies) — a different potency for Apis mellifica (6C vs. 15C) between the two kids' SKUs themselves. Labeled for ages 4+. This means "ColdCalm" as a brand spans at least three distinct homeopathic formulations (adult 9-remedy, kids' liquid 4-remedy, kids' pellet 3-remedy) — not simply a smaller dose of the same adult formula, which a caregiver might otherwise reasonably assume from the shared brand name.

## Pros
- Same manufacturer (Boiron) as Oscillococcinum and the adult ColdCalm, with consistent labeling transparency about homeopathic status
- Fewer combined remedies (3-4) than the adult 9-remedy formulation may simplify any attempt to reason about mechanism, even under a homeopathic evidence framework
- No serious adverse events reported for general homeopathic cold products per the Cochrane review on [[homeopathic-cold-products]]

## Cons
- **Same class-wide evidence limitation as [[homeopathic-cold-products]] documents**: no convincing evidence of clinically meaningful benefit at these dilutions; the Hawke 2018 Cochrane review's quality-vs-benefit inverse relationship (better-designed trials found nothing, weaker trials found an effect) applies here
- **Genuine formulation inconsistency worth flagging**: the Kids Liquid and Kids Pellet SKUs use different ingredient sets and different potencies for their one shared ingredient (Apis mellifica 6C vs. 15C) — a caregiver switching between the two kids' formats, or assuming either is simply "ColdCalm for kids" in a consistent sense, would not actually be getting the same formulation
- No product-specific pediatric RCT for either ColdCalm Kids formulation was located in this pass — general homeopathic-class evidence (or lack thereof) is being extrapolated to these specific formulations
- Ages 4+ label is more restrictive than some pediatric cough/cold competitors in adjacent categories, though this reflects standard homeopathic-product convention rather than a documented specific safety concern

## Use Cases
Marketed to temporarily relieve cold symptoms in children: sneezing, runny nose, sinus/nasal congestion (per label claim, not established by high-quality evidence).

## Allergies / Warnings
Not for children under 4. Standard FDA homeopathic-product disclaimer applies (not evaluated for safety/efficacy in the same pathway as monograph OTC drugs).

## Symptoms Treated
Sneezing, runny nose, nasal/sinus congestion (unproven beyond placebo per current evidence).

## Brand-Specific Alignment Check
**New finding, added to both this page and [[homeopathic-cold-products]]:** ColdCalm's three-way formulation split (adult 9-remedy vs. kids' liquid 4-remedy vs. kids' pellet 3-remedy, with inconsistent potency even between the two kids' SKUs) is a real, previously undocumented formulation-inconsistency pattern for this specific brand family — distinct from, but related to, the "one brand spans multiple formulations" pattern already flagged repeatedly for conventional-drug brands (Dimetapp, Mucinex, PediaCare) elsewhere in this Pediatrics research pass.
