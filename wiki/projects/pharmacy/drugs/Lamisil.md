## Overview
This is the same brand/active-ingredient family as [[Lamisil AT]] (tracked separately by Pharmacy Times under the Jock Itch/Antifungal Products category, 28% share) — **terbinafine HCl 1%**, an allylamine antifungal (fungicidal via squalene-epoxidase inhibition; see [[terbinafine]]). The Pharmacy Times source data tracks "Lamisil" (Toe/Foot) and "Lamisil AT" (Jock Itch) as two separate named rows despite almost certainly referring to the same core terbinafine 1% cream/spray product line marketed for both indications — this database preserves that distinction as tracked in source rather than merging the two rows, consistent with the project's "never merge" convention for source data, but notes the underlying product identity is very likely the same.

See [[Lamisil AT]] for the full researched overview, including a genuine brand-named placebo-controlled RCT (Budimulja et al. 2001, 84.2% vs. 23.3% mycological cure) and a real head-to-head finding that butenafine (this category's leading brand [[Lotrimin]] Ultra's active) significantly outperformed terbinafine for tinea cruris (94.87% vs. 62.16% mycological cure at day 42) — directly relevant here since Lamisil (32%) and Lotrimin (39%, spanning both its clotrimazole and butenafine sub-lines) are this Toe/Foot subcategory's two largest brands.

## Pros
See [[Lamisil AT]] — confirmed FDA Drug Facts label, real brand-named RCT evidence (84.2% vs. 23.3% mycological cure), and a notably shorter treatment course (1-2 weeks for athlete's foot) than [[clotrimazole]]'s 2-4 week regimens.

## Cons
See [[Lamisil AT]] — the real head-to-head trial showing butenafine outperforming terbinafine for tinea cruris is directly relevant to this brand's #2 position (32%) behind [[Lotrimin]] (39%) in this specific subcategory, a genuine, evidence-based efficacy gap rather than a marketing distinction. No independent trial confirming this comparative finding extends specifically to tinea pedis (athlete's foot, the primary indication for this subcategory) rather than tinea cruris (jock itch) was located in this pass.

## Use Cases
Treatment of athlete's foot (tinea pedis) via terbinafine's fungicidal squalene-epoxidase-inhibiting mechanism, typically a 1-2 week course depending on the specific formulation/foot-fungus subtype per label.

## Allergies / Warnings
Per [[terbinafine]]/[[Lamisil AT]]: external use only; avoid eyes, nose, mouth; not for nail or scalp fungal infections; discontinue and consult a doctor if irritation develops or no improvement within the labeled treatment window.

## Symptoms Treated
Itching, burning, cracking, and scaling from athlete's foot.
