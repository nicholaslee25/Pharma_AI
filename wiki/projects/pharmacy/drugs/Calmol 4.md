## Appears In

## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed (labeler: ResiCal, Inc.): Calmol 4 is a rectal suppository with **two active ingredients — zinc oxide 10% and cocoa butter 76%** — both classified as protectants, per uspharmacist.com's OTC hemorrhoid-category breakdown (zinc oxide/cocoa butter are named examples of the "protectant" mechanism class, alongside lanolin and mineral oil). This is the only brand in this category with **no vasoconstrictor, anesthetic, or corticosteroid active ingredient at all** — a genuinely different, milder mechanism than [[Preparation H]] (phenylephrine/pramoxine/hydrocortisone), [[RectiCare]] (lidocaine ± phenylephrine), or [[Tucks]] (witch hazel astringent / lidocaine). Zinc oxide functions here as an astringent/healing agent per the manufacturer; cocoa butter provides lubrication and a protective coating. See [[zinc-oxide]] for the ingredient's broader evidence base (built primarily around diaper-rash/skin-protectant use, now cross-referenced here for its anorectal application alongside Calmoseptine elsewhere in this database). No dedicated cocoa butter ingredient page exists in this database; it is treated here as a standard emollient/lubricant base rather than a pharmacologically active agent requiring its own evidence page.

No Calmol-4-brand-specific clinical trial was located; evidence is at the general protectant-mechanism level described in the uspharmacist.com OTC hemorrhoid-counseling reference, which does not provide comparative efficacy data between protectant-class ingredients and the vasoconstrictor/anesthetic/corticosteroid classes used elsewhere in this category.

## Pros
- Confirmed FDA Drug Facts label with two clearly identified active ingredients — genuine regulatory transparency
- No vasoconstrictor, anesthetic, or corticosteroid means none of [[rectal-phenylephrine]]'s cardiovascular/thyroid/diabetes/BPH contraindications, [[topical-anesthetics]]'s contact-dermatitis/systemic-absorption considerations, or [[hydrocortisone]]'s skin-atrophy/infection-masking risks apply — a genuinely gentler risk profile for the mildest-mechanism brand in this category
- Suppository format may be preferred by some users over external cream/pad application for internal hemorrhoid symptoms
- Protectant-only mechanism is appropriate for the "coating/lubrication to make bowel movements less painful" use case specifically, independent of any vasoconstrictive or anesthetic action

## Cons
- Purely protectant mechanism — does not address swelling (no vasoconstrictor), pain (no anesthetic), or inflammation (no corticosteroid) the way competing brands in this category do; symptom relief is limited to lubrication/coating and mild astringent action
- No Calmol-4-brand-specific clinical trial was located; general protectant-class evidence in the sources reviewed is descriptive/consensus-based rather than RCT-quantified
- No dedicated cocoa butter ingredient page exists in this database — its specific protectant efficacy was not independently cross-checked against a dedicated evidence page, though it is a long-established, low-risk lubricant
- Lowest recommendation share (7%) among the four brands in this category

## Use Cases
Temporary relief of burning, irritation, itching, and discomfort associated with hemorrhoids; provides a protective coating for anorectal discomfort; helps make bowel movements less painful by protecting inflamed, irritated anorectal tissue — via zinc oxide/cocoa butter's protectant mechanism, no vasoconstrictive, anesthetic, or anti-inflammatory action.

## Allergies / Warnings
Standard OTC anorectal-product conventions apply per the category's general guidance (uspharmacist.com): not for use with prolapse, rectal bleeding, in patients under 12, or for symptoms persisting beyond 7 days. No specific zinc-oxide or cocoa-butter allergy warnings were located in the sources reviewed in this pass beyond general protectant-class low-risk profile.

## Symptoms Treated
Burning, irritation, itching, and discomfort from hemorrhoids; painful bowel movements associated with anorectal irritation.
