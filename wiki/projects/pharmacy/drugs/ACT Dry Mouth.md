## Overview
**Discrepancy flagged:** Confirmed via DailyMed: "ACT Total Care Dry Mouth Anticavity Mouthwash" lists **sodium fluoride 0.02% (0.009% w/v fluoride ion)** as its sole FDA-recognized active ingredient — an **anticavity** claim, per the same mechanism documented on [[fluoride-toothpaste]]. Despite the "Dry Mouth" branding suggesting a xerostomia-targeted therapeutic mechanism (saliva substitution, moisturizing, or salivary stimulation), the product's only approved active ingredient targets cavity prevention, not dryness itself — any dry-mouth comfort benefit would come from unlisted inactive ingredients (e.g., glycerin/humectant bases) rather than an FDA-reviewed therapeutic claim for xerostomia. This is a real, if subtle, category-placement nuance: ACT Dry Mouth is genuinely a fluoride anticavity drug wearing a dry-mouth-comfort marketing label.

## Pros
- Unlike several competitors in this category (Biotène, TheraBreath, SmartMouth, Oral-B), ACT Dry Mouth DOES carry a confirmed FDA Drug Facts panel with a real, well-established active ingredient (fluoride) and genuine anticaries evidence base (see [[fluoride-toothpaste]]'s 42,300-child Cochrane meta-analysis)
- Combines a real anticavity benefit with dry-mouth-oriented marketing/formulation, potentially useful for patients with medication-induced xerostomia who are also at elevated cavity risk (a genuinely common combined clinical picture, since reduced saliva itself raises caries risk)
- Manufactured by Church & Dwight, an established oral-care company with other confirmed fluoride products in this database (see [[fluoride-toothpaste]])

## Cons
- **The product's FDA-recognized active ingredient (fluoride) does not target dry mouth/xerostomia symptoms at all** — a consumer specifically seeking relief FROM dry mouth discomfort (rather than cavity prevention) may be better served by a genuine saliva substitute, understanding that this product's primary evidence-backed benefit is anticavity, not moisturizing
- No brand-specific clinical trial evidence was identified for this product's dry-mouth comfort claims specifically (as opposed to fluoride's general anticaries evidence)
- Reduced saliva flow (the underlying condition this product markets toward) is itself a caries risk factor per [[saliva-substitutes]]'s StatPearls sourcing — so the fluoride content is clinically sensible, but should be understood as addressing a downstream consequence of dry mouth, not the dryness itself

## Use Cases
Cavity prevention (confirmed fluoride mechanism) marketed toward, and potentially particularly relevant for, patients with dry-mouth-related elevated caries risk; general dry-mouth comfort not independently confirmed as an FDA-reviewed claim.

## Allergies / Warnings
Standard fluoride precautions per [[fluoride-toothpaste]]: do not swallow, supervise children, discontinue and consult a dentist/doctor if irritation or other adverse reaction occurs.
