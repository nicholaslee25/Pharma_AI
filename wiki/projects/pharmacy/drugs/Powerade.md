## Appears In
Hydration Support - 5%

## Overview
Powerade is confirmed to be a standard sports drink with a sugar content nearly identical to [[Gatorade]] (~21g/12 fl oz equivalent) but a somewhat higher sodium and potassium content in comparative sourcing (480mg sodium/150mg potassium per 710mL bottle vs. Gatorade's 300mg/75mg), plus added vitamin C and B12 not present in Gatorade's standard formula. Despite the modestly higher electrolyte content, Powerade's sodium level still sits well below the WHO oral rehydration solution (ORS) standard documented in [[oral-rehydration-electrolytes]] (~1,035mg sodium/L for [[Pedialyte]]) — the same category-placement caveat that applies to Gatorade applies here. Hydration Support (5%) is covered by [[oral-rehydration-electrolytes]].

## Pros
- Modestly higher sodium/potassium content than [[Gatorade]] in comparative sourcing, plus added vitamin C and B12 — real, if incremental, formulation differences
- Well-established for its designed use case (athletic performance/electrolyte replacement during exercise)

## Cons
- **Same real formulation mismatch as [[Gatorade]] for rehydration-therapy use**: sodium content, while somewhat higher than Gatorade's, remains well below the WHO ORS standard, and sugar content remains high — not a substitute for a clinical ORS like [[Pedialyte]] for actual illness-related dehydration
- No Powerade-specific clinical trial (beyond general sports-drink literature) was located in this pass
- High sugar content is a real consideration for diabetic/sugar-restricted users using this product outside of actual prolonged athletic exertion
- Smallest tracked share (5%) in this category

## Use Cases
Electrolyte/carbohydrate support during prolonged athletic exercise — see [[oral-rehydration-electrolytes]] for the contrast with clinical-grade oral rehydration therapy.

## Allergies / Warnings
High sugar content per serving. Not an appropriate substitute for a clinical ORS in cases of actual illness-related dehydration. See [[oral-rehydration-electrolytes]].

## Symptoms Treated
Not indicated for dehydration treatment in the clinical sense — see [[oral-rehydration-electrolytes]].

## Brand-Specific Alignment Check
Same worth-flagging category-placement nuance as [[Gatorade]]: Powerade is a standard sports drink, not formulated to the WHO ORS ratio that makes [[Pedialyte]] and [[DripDrop]] genuine rehydration-therapy products, despite sharing this database's "Hydration Support" category.
