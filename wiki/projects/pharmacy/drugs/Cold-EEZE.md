## Pros
- Directly confirmed, pharmacologically real zinc dose (13.3mg/lozenge) despite the "2X" homeopathic-style label notation — see the ingredient page's Regulatory Note explaining why this differs from ultra-dilute homeopathic products like Oscillococcinum
- Started within 24-48h of symptom onset, zinc lozenges show a real (if modest) reduction in cold duration per the Cochrane evidence on [[zinc]]
- Long-established brand with a specific, single, consistent core formulation (unlike Zicam's multi-SKU spread)

## Cons
- Labeled specifically NOT for influenza or allergies — a real limitation on what it treats
- Must avoid citrus fruit/juice within 30 minutes before or after dosing (label-specified interaction with product effectiveness)
- Interaction warning: consult a doctor/pharmacist if taking minocycline, doxycycline, tetracycline, or warfarin
- Carries the standard "not evaluated by FDA for safety or efficacy" homeopathic disclaimer despite delivering a real pharmacological zinc dose — a labeling quirk that could mislead a consumer into under- or over-trusting the product depending on how they read it głów

## Use Cases
Reducing the duration and severity of common cold symptoms (cough, sore throat, nasal congestion, post-nasal drip, hoarseness) when started early in the illness.

## Allergies / Warnings
Not for influenza or allergy symptoms. Avoid citrus around dosing. Check for interaction with tetracycline-class antibiotics or warfarin. Zinc doses above the 40mg/day adult tolerable upper limit are a safety concern per [[zinc]] if a user combines multiple zinc products/lozenges beyond label directions.

## Symptoms Treated
