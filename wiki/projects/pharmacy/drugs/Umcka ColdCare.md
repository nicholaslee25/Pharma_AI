## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed: Umcka ColdCare's active ingredient is labeled **"Pelargonium sidoides 1X"**, directions: adults/children ≥12y take 1.5mL by mouth 3x daily, starting at first symptoms and continuing 48h after symptoms clear. This matches [[pelargonium]] on the underlying botanical, but the label's "1X" potency notation is worth flagging directly: [[pelargonium]]'s own header explicitly states this ingredient is "**NOT a homeopathic dilution**" and is instead a pharmacologically active standardized extract (EPs 7630) with real RCT evidence behind it — yet the actual FDA-registered label uses homeopathic-style "1X" potency notation (1:10 dilution), which is the same regulatory pattern seen with Cold-EEZE's "2X" zinc labeling. This is a real discrepancy in framing (ingredient page says "not homeopathic," product label uses homeopathic dilution notation) worth resolving rather than silently picking one — noted here and added to the ingredient page below.

## Pros
- One of the few OTC cold botanicals in this database with actual placebo-controlled RCT and meta-analysis support (see [[pelargonium]]): 2022 meta-analysis of 2195 participants found meaningful reductions in cough/symptom scores vs. placebo across pediatric and adult acute bronchitis and adult common cold
- Liquid dosing format may suit those who prefer not to swallow tablets/lozenges

## Cons
- The largest, most recent, most methodologically rigorous trial (PHYTOBRONCH 2026, 332 adults) found **no significant difference** vs. usual care on the primary symptom-reduction endpoint, and **significantly more adverse events** (32.3% vs. 21.5%) — this directly tempers the older positive-trial evidence and should not be ignored in favor of it, per the ingredient page's own framing
- "1X" label potency notation may confuse consumers into either dismissing it as an inert homeopathic remedy (when it isn't) or not realizing it's regulated/labeled under a homeopathic-adjacent framework — see the alignment note below
- Not FDA-evaluated for safety/efficacy as a drug (herbal/homeopathic-adjacent product category)

## Use Cases
Reducing duration/severity of common cold and acute bronchitis symptoms: cough, congestion, hoarseness, sore throat, nasal/throat/bronchial irritation.

## Allergies / Warnings
No FDA drug-label warnings panel of the standard OTC-monograph type was found; general herbal-product caution applies (discuss with a doctor if pregnant, breastfeeding, or on other medications, per standard supplement-safety practice — not independently sourced to a specific citation here).

## Symptoms Treated

## Brand-Specific Alignment Check
**Discrepancy flagged**: Umcka ColdCare's own FDA-registered label lists its active ingredient as "Pelargonium sidoides **1X**" — homeopathic-style dilution notation — while [[pelargonium]]'s header explicitly frames this ingredient as "NOT a homeopathic dilution... a standardized root extract (EPs 7630) with proposed antiviral... activity," distinguishing it from ultra-dilute homeopathic products. Both things can be simultaneously true (as with Cold-EEZE's "2X" zinc — a real, non-trivial dilution/potency notation that still carries meaningful active compound), but this wasn't previously spelled out on the ingredient page for pelargonium the way it was for zinc. Added a Brand-Specific Notes section to [[pelargonium]] to carry this forward rather than silently resolving it here only.
