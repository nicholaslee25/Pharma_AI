## Overview
Band-Aid (Johnson & Johnson/Kenvue) is the category-leading brand by a wide margin (74% share). No FDA drug label exists — Band-Aid adhesive bandages are regulated as a **Class I device** under 21 CFR 880.5240 (FDA product code KGX), 510(k)-exempt, with no active drug ingredient. Full evidence and regulatory detail is on [[bandages-covers-and-gauze]]. The one genuinely notable, brand-specific finding from a targeted search: a 2024 EPA-certified lab study (Mamavation/Environmental Health News) testing 40 bandage products from 18 brands for PFAS ("forever chemicals") found **Band-Aid's OURTONE Flexible Fabric BR65 line had the single highest PFAS reading in the entire study** (260 ppm on the absorbent pad, 374 ppm on the sticky flaps), while a different Band-Aid SKU (Hello Kitty Assorted Adhesive Bandages) tested non-detect (<10 ppm) in the same study — meaning PFAS presence varies by specific product line within the Band-Aid brand, not uniformly across it.

## Pros
- Largest recommendation share in the category (74%) by a wide margin, reflecting broad retail availability and long-standing pharmacist/consumer familiarity
- Class I device status reflects a long, well-established safety track record for the basic adhesive-bandage design
- At least one tested SKU (Hello Kitty Assorted) came back non-detect for PFAS in the 2024 independent lab study, showing the brand's full lineup is not uniformly PFAS-positive

## Cons
- The single highest PFAS reading of any product in the 2024 Mamavation/EHN 40-product study was a Band-Aid SKU (OURTONE Flexible Fabric BR65, marketed for use on deeper skin tones) — a real, source-confirmed, brand-specific finding, not a category-wide generality
- No brand-specific clinical or comparative-performance trial exists (expected for this Class I device category, not a gap unique to Band-Aid)
- "Band-Aid" as a brand spans many distinct SKUs (fabric, plastic, waterproof, tone-matched, character/novelty lines) with different material compositions — a PFAS or material finding for one SKU should not be assumed to apply to another

## Use Cases
Covering and protecting minor cuts, scrapes, and abrasions; holding together skin edges of a minor wound; general first-aid wound coverage.

## Allergies / Warnings
Not a drug — no ingredient/allergy profile in the pharmacological sense. Standard device cautions: discontinue use and consult a doctor if a wound shows signs of infection or does not heal; some users may have adhesive contact sensitivity. See [[bandages-covers-and-gauze]] for the PFAS testing context — an environmental-chemical-exposure consideration distinct from wound-care efficacy.

## Symptoms Treated
Not applicable in the drug sense — provides physical wound coverage/protection, not a pharmacological treatment.
