## Appears In
Sunscreen - 9%

## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed: Blue Lizard's entire product range checked in this pass (Sensitive, Sensitive Face, Sport, Sheer Face, Baby, Kids — all SPF 50) uses **titanium dioxide and zinc oxide** as the sole active ingredients — a **consistent, all-mineral-filter brand**, unlike several competitors in this subcategory ([[Sun Bum]], [[Coppertone]], COOLA's Classic line) that mix chemical and mineral SKUs under one name. See [[mineral-sunscreen-filters]] for the ingredient-class evidence (FDA GRASE-affirmed status, nanoparticle-penetration safety data). Manufacturer marketing states the formulation blocks "98% of UVB rays" and is "free from harsh chemical active ingredients" — consistent with the confirmed all-mineral formulation, not an overstated claim in this case.

No independent Blue Lizard-brand-specific clinical trial was located in this pass; the relevant evidence is the general zinc oxide/titanium dioxide mineral-filter literature.

## Pros
- **Confirmed, consistent all-mineral-filter formulation across every SKU checked** — the only brand in this subcategory found to be this internally consistent, avoiding the "same brand, different filter types depending on SKU" pattern found elsewhere ([[Sun Bum]], [[Coppertone]])
- Uses the two FDA-GRASE-affirmed sunscreen actives (zinc oxide, titanium dioxide) exclusively — the strongest regulatory-confidence position available in this ingredient category per [[mineral-sunscreen-filters]]
- No chemical-filter systemic-absorption concerns (per the JAMA plasma-concentration findings documented on [[chemical-sunscreen-filters]]) apply to this brand
- Dedicated Baby and Kids SKUs suit sensitive-skin/pediatric use, consistent with the brand's "Sensitive"-forward positioning

## Cons
- No independent Blue Lizard-brand-specific clinical trial was located; general mineral-filter literature applies (see [[mineral-sunscreen-filters]])
- Mineral sunscreens are generally associated with a heavier/whiter cosmetic finish than chemical filters (though modern nanoparticle formulations, as used here, substantially reduce this) — not independently verified for this specific brand's formulations in this pass
- Smaller share (9%) than chemical-filter-line competitors like [[Neutrogena]] (27%) and [[Coppertone]] (11%), consistent with mineral sunscreens' generally smaller market share overall

## Use Cases
Broad-spectrum sunburn prevention and, with regular use and other sun-protection measures, reduced skin cancer/early-aging risk, via zinc oxide and titanium dioxide physical/mineral UV filters.

## Allergies / Warnings
Standard mineral-sunscreen precautions apply (see [[mineral-sunscreen-filters]]): external use only, avoid eye contact, discontinue if irritation develops.

## Symptoms Treated
Sunburn prevention (not a treatment for existing sunburn).
