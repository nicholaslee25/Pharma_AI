## Appears In
Toothpaste, Sensitive Gums/Teeth - 12%

## Overview
**Discrepancy flagged:** Confirmed via DailyMed — the product line behind this listing (marketed as "Crest Pro-Health Sensitive and Gum," "Crest Pro-Health Gum and Sensitivity," etc.) lists **stannous fluoride 0.454% (0.15% w/v fluoride ion)** as its active ingredient, with purposes stated as anticavity, antigingivitis, AND antisensitivity — all three benefits from one ingredient. This is **NOT potassium nitrate**, the desensitizing agent used by the category leader Sensodyne and presumed (per [[potassium-nitrate]]'s prior "plausibly potassium-nitrate-based... not individually confirmed" placeholder) for the other brands in this same Pharmacy Times subcategory. Crest's entry in "Toothpaste, Sensitive Gums/Teeth" achieves its desensitizing effect through a completely different mechanism than most of the category: stannous ions precipitate onto and physically occlude exposed dentin tubules (blocking fluid movement that triggers nerve pain), versus potassium nitrate's proposed nerve-excitability-reduction mechanism. This corrects the ingredient page's prior assumption and is flagged there.

## Pros
- Genuine brand-specific RCT evidence: Schiff et al., an 8-week randomized study, found 0.454% stannous fluoride dentifrice (Crest Pro-Health) produced a statistically significant reduction in hypersensitivity (Schiff Air Index) vs. a 0.243% sodium-fluoride control at both week 4 and week 8 (P<.0001) — stronger, brand-specific evidence than exists for several competitors' generic ingredient-class claims
- Triple-purpose formulation (anticavity + antigingivitis + antisensitivity) from a single active ingredient, consistent with the same stannous-fluoride mechanism already documented for Crest Pro-Health's other sub-lines
- Avoids the "no clear evidence" caveat that Cochrane's Poulsen 2006 review attaches specifically to potassium-nitrate desensitizers (see [[potassium-nitrate]]) — stannous fluoride's sensitivity evidence here comes from a different, brand-specific trial design (objective Schiff Air Index) rather than the subjective-outcome gap Cochrane flagged for potassium nitrate

## Cons
- The Pharmacy Times category name ("Toothpaste, Sensitive Gums/Teeth") groups Crest's stannous-fluoride product together with competitors' potassium-nitrate products as if they were interchangeable — they are not the same mechanism, evidence base, or side-effect profile, and a consumer switching between them should not expect identical results
- Stannous fluoride's own systematic-review evidence base (Paraskevas 2006, Johannsen 2019, per [[stannous-fluoride]]) is characterized as "moderate," not "strong," for its gingivitis/plaque claims specifically, and carries a documented tooth-staining tradeoff not shared by potassium-nitrate formulations
- The Schiff et al. trial (like most brand-sponsored dentifrice trials) was Procter & Gamble-affiliated research; no fully independent replication was identified in this pass

## Use Cases
Combined cavity prevention, gingivitis/plaque reduction, and dentin-hypersensitivity relief (cold/heat/acid/sweet/contact triggers) via stannous fluoride — same underlying ingredient as Crest Pro-Health, marketed under a sensitivity-forward name for this category.

## Allergies / Warnings
Per label: do not use to treat sensitivity for more than 4 weeks without consulting a dentist; stop use and consult a dentist if sensitivity persists or worsens (may indicate a more serious condition); keep out of reach of children; seek immediate medical help if swallowed in excess of brushing amounts. Staining tradeoff per [[stannous-fluoride]] is cosmetic, not a safety warning.

## Symptoms Treated
Dentin hypersensitivity (cold/heat/acid/sweet/contact-triggered tooth pain), gingivitis, plaque, and cavity prevention — a broader symptom set from one ingredient than the potassium-nitrate-based competitors in the same category typically claim.
