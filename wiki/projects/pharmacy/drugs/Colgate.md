## Appears In
Toothpaste, General Use - 26%

## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed: **Colgate Cavity Protection** (the flagship line most plausibly driving this 26% General Use share) lists **sodium monofluorophosphate (MFP) 0.76% (0.15% w/v fluoride ion)** as its active ingredient in the "Great Regular Flavor" paste, purpose "anticavity agent" — helps protect against cavities, a standard [[fluoride-toothpaste]] formulation, just a different fluoride salt than sodium fluoride. **Formulation nuance:** the Cavity Protection GEL variant instead uses **sodium fluoride 0.24% (0.15% w/v fluoride ion)** — same delivered fluoride-ion concentration, different chemical salt, depending on paste vs. gel format. Both are legitimate, FDA-recognized anticaries active ingredients delivering the same fluoride-ion dose; this is a normal industry formulation variance, not a safety concern, but means "Colgate Cavity Protection" is not one single fixed chemical formula across its own SKUs. Separately, **Colgate Total** (a related but distinct product line, e.g. "Total Gum Protect Whitening") is confirmed **stannous fluoride**-based per DailyMed — the same multi-formulation-under-one-master-brand pattern already documented for Crest (sodium fluoride Cavity Protection vs. stannous fluoride Pro-Health).

## Pros
- Confirmed FDA Drug Facts label with a well-established anticavity active ingredient (fluoride, delivered as either sodium fluoride or sodium monofluorophosphate depending on SKU) for the core Cavity Protection line
- A real comparative RCT exists: Colgate Total vs. Parodontax vs. Colgate Cavity Protection Toothpaste, a randomized, double-blind, parallel-group plaque/gingivitis efficacy study — brand-specific comparative evidence beyond the general ingredient-class literature
- By far the second-largest share in Toothpaste, General Use (26%, close behind Crest's 28%), reflecting broad consumer/pharmacist familiarity

## Cons
- "Colgate Cavity Protection" itself is not one fixed chemical formula — paste uses sodium monofluorophosphate 0.76%, gel uses sodium fluoride 0.24% — and the related "Colgate Total" line uses a third, materially different ingredient (stannous fluoride) entirely; a consumer should check the specific SKU rather than assume uniform composition from the brand name
- No independent (non-Colgate-sponsored) brand-specific RCT for the Cavity Protection formulation specifically was identified in this pass, beyond the general fluoride/MFP ingredient-class Cochrane evidence
- Per [[fluoride-toothpaste]]'s own caveat, effect size depends on brushing frequency/technique/supervision — brand choice alone does not guarantee the ingredient-class benefit

## Use Cases
Cavity prevention via daily brushing (sodium monofluorophosphate or sodium fluoride, depending on Cavity Protection SKU); Colgate Total (stannous fluoride) separately adds antigingivitis/antiplaque benefit, structurally analogous to Crest Pro-Health.

## Allergies / Warnings
Per label: keep away from children under 6 years; if swallowed in excess of brushing amounts, get medical help or contact a Poison Control Center right away. Standard fluoride precautions per [[fluoride-toothpaste]] (pea-sized amount for ages 3–6, supervise brushing, dentist consultation under age 2).

## Symptoms Treated
Cavity prevention (all Cavity Protection SKUs, regardless of which fluoride salt). Colgate Total additionally targets gingivitis/plaque via its separate stannous-fluoride formulation — not part of the base Cavity Protection product.
