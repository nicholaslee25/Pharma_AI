## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed (brand also appears as "SafeTussin," one word, on some labels): **dextromethorphan HBr 10mg + guaifenesin 100mg** per 5mL, explicitly formulated **without decongestants, acetaminophen, sugar, alcohol, sorbitol, fructose, gluten, or dyes**, and marketed as safe for adults with high blood pressure and diabetes. This confirms and expands the earlier hedge — it's a guaifenesin+dextromethorphan combination, not dextromethorphan alone, similar branding logic to Coricidin HBP's decongestant-free positioning. Touches [[dextromethorphan]] and [[guaifenesin]]. Label saved at `raw/primary-sources/brands/safe-tussin-dm/dailymed-safe-tussin-dm.md`.

## Pros
- **Genuinely brand-distinguishing, confirmed feature**: explicitly free of sugar, dyes, alcohol, and common sweetener alternatives, plus no decongestant or acetaminophen — a real option for diabetic patients (no sugar) and hypertensive patients (no decongestant) simultaneously, addressing two different at-risk populations at once, more comprehensively than the single-axis Coricidin HBP (decongestant-free only) or diabetic-cough-specific Robitussin sugar-free variants discussed elsewhere in this batch
- Lower guaifenesin dose (100mg) than Mucinex-family products, which may suit someone wanting a milder expectorant effect

## Cons
- Lower guaifenesin dose (100mg vs. 600-1200mg in Mucinex products) means the Bennett 2015 RCT evidence on [[guaifenesin]] (tested at 1200mg) does not directly transfer to this product's dose
- Still carries dextromethorphan's MAOI interaction risk and the "low-to-moderate," partly-uncertain efficacy evidence discussed on [[dextromethorphan]]
- Smaller market share (4%) than most competitors in its category, for reasons not established in this pass (could reflect brand recognition rather than any efficacy/safety difference)

## Use Cases
Cough suppression and mucus thinning for patients specifically wanting to avoid sugar, dyes, and decongestants — e.g. diabetic and/or hypertensive patients.

## Allergies / Warnings
Do not use with MAOIs or within 2 weeks of stopping one. Ask a doctor/pharmacist if unsure whether a prescription drug contains an MAOI.

## Symptoms Treated
Cough, chest congestion/mucus.

No discrepancy with [[dextromethorphan]] or [[guaifenesin]] on dose/warnings — both are standard OTC monograph amounts. The brand's free-from formulation claims are a genuine, confirmed differentiator worth highlighting rather than assuming from the name alone.
