## Appears In

## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed: Listerine's classic antiseptic mouthwash line (Original, Cool Mint) lists **eucalyptol, menthol, methyl salicylate (0.060%), and thymol** — a four-essential-oil combination — as active ingredients, labeled antiseptic/antigingivitis/antiplaque. This is genuinely one of the stronger evidence bases in this database's mouthwash coverage: per a JADA-published meta-analysis of 32 long-term RCTs (5,000+ participants with gingivitis), essential-oil mouthrinse provided a clinically significant additional benefit in reducing plaque and gingival bleeding versus brushing/flossing alone, and one 6-month study found it outperformed both a CPC rinse and placebo at all post-baseline timepoints. A separate "Total Care Zero Alcohol Anticavity" SKU adds fluoride while removing alcohol. The toothpaste-line share (2%) is a separate, presumably fluoride-based product not independently re-confirmed in this pass.

## Pros
- One of the largest, most consistent RCT evidence bases in this database's entire mouthwash coverage (32 trials, 5,000+ participants) for real plaque/gingivitis reduction — comparable in scale to chlorhexidine's Cochrane evidence
- Unlike chlorhexidine (per [[chlorhexidine]]'s Cochrane data: large tooth-staining increase), essential-oil mouthwash is generally understood in the dental literature to carry a substantially lower staining burden — a meaningful practical advantage for long-term daily use
- Available in an alcohol-free, fluoride-containing variant (Total Care Zero Alcohol Anticavity) for users who want to avoid alcohol specifically

## Cons
- Classic formulations contain a substantial alcohol concentration (historically ~21-26% in Original/Cool Mint Antiseptic), which has been the subject of a long-running, still-debated scientific controversy about a possible association between alcohol-containing mouthwash and oral cancer risk — this database did not independently adjudicate that debate in this pass, but flags it as a real, disputed safety question rather than a settled non-issue
- Methyl salicylate content (0.060%) is far lower than the counterirritant-strength concentrations documented on [[topical-counterirritants]] (which run 10-30%+ for topical pain-relief rubs), so it should not be assumed to carry the same NSAID-class systemic risk profile at mouthwash-level exposure — but this database did not independently confirm a specific safety threshold distinguishing the two uses
- Burning/stinging sensation on use is a commonly reported tolerability complaint for the alcohol-containing formulations, a real adherence barrier for some users

## Use Cases
Antiseptic reduction of plaque, gingivitis, and bad-breath-causing bacteria as a daily adjunct to brushing and flossing; the alcohol-free/fluoride variant adds cavity prevention.

## Allergies / Warnings
Per label: alcohol content contraindicated for children under 12 (per general OTC mouthwash convention) and those avoiding alcohol for medical/personal reasons; stop use and consult a dentist if gum problems (bleeding, redness, swelling) persist beyond 2 weeks or if soreness/irritation/redness persists more than 7 days.

## Symptoms Treated
Plaque accumulation, gingivitis (gum inflammation/bleeding), and oral malodor via essential-oil antibacterial action; cavity prevention in the alcohol-free/fluoride SKU.
