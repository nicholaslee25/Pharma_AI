## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed: "Blistex" spans at least **three mechanistically different formulations**, a wider internal spread than most brands in this database. (1) **Blistex Cold Sore Treatment** uses **docosanol 10%** — the same antiviral active ingredient as category leader Abreva (see [[docosanol]]), meaning Blistex directly competes with its own category's #1 brand using an identical mechanism, not a differentiated one. (2) **Blistex Cold Sore Fast-Acting Pain Relief** uses **glycerin + lidocaine** — an anesthetic gel for symptomatic numbing, mechanistically unrelated to the docosanol SKU. (3) **Blistex Medicated Lip Ointment** (the more general lip-balm-line product, plausibly the SKU behind the Lip Balms 15% share) uses **dimethicone, camphor (synthetic), menthol, and phenol** — a skin-protectant/counterirritant combination for chapped-lip relief, a third distinct mechanism from both cold-sore SKUs. A fourth SKU, **Blistex Cold & Allergy Lip Soother**, is a non-medicated dimethicone stick with added vitamins/botanicals.

## Pros
- The docosanol-based Cold Sore Treatment SKU carries the same real, quantified pivotal-trial evidence documented on [[docosanol]] (Sacks 2001, ~18 hours faster healing vs. placebo) — genuinely evidence-backed, not just brand-name recognition
- Offers a choice of mechanism (antiviral docosanol vs. anesthetic lidocaine) under one brand, letting a consumer pick based on whether they want to target the virus or just numb symptoms
- The Lip Balm-line product(s) address a different, more common need (general chapped-lip protection) than the dedicated cold-sore SKUs, useful for daily preventive lip care

## Cons
- Extreme internal formulation diversity (docosanol antiviral / lidocaine anesthetic / camphor-menthol-phenol counterirritant / non-medicated dimethicone) means "Blistex" as a brand name provides very little information about which active ingredient — or whether any drug ingredient at all — a specific product contains
- The lidocaine-based Fast-Acting Pain Relief SKU provides only symptomatic numbing, not the antiviral effect of the company's own docosanol SKU — a consumer reaching for "Blistex" for a cold sore could get either mechanism depending on which specific product they pick up
- No brand-specific trial evidence was identified for the camphor/menthol/phenol Medicated Lip Ointment SKU's effect on cold sores/chapped lips specifically, beyond the general safety/use profile of those individual ingredients

## Use Cases
Cold sore/fever blister treatment (docosanol SKU, antiviral) or temporary pain/itch relief (lidocaine SKU, anesthetic); general chapped/cracked lip protection and relief (Medicated Lip Ointment, camphor/menthol/phenol; Cold & Allergy Lip Soother, non-medicated).

## Allergies / Warnings
Docosanol SKU: external use only, not for eyes/intraoral use, discontinue if not healed within 10 days (see [[docosanol]]). Lidocaine SKU: standard topical-anesthetic precautions (see [[topical-anesthetics]], though lidocaine carries a far lower methemoglobinemia risk than benzocaine per the Guay 2009 data there). Camphor/menthol/phenol SKU: external use only, avoid eyes, discontinue if irritation develops.

## Symptoms Treated
Cold sore/fever blister healing time and viral activity (docosanol SKU); cold sore pain/itching (lidocaine SKU); chapped/cracked/dry lip discomfort (Medicated Lip Ointment and Lip Soother SKUs).

## Brand-Specific Alignment Check
New finding for this database: Blistex is now the second brand (after Neosporin's antibiotic-family spread and Salonpas's five-SKU spread) shown to compete against the SAME category's leading brand (Abreva) using an IDENTICAL active ingredient (docosanol) in one SKU, while simultaneously offering a completely different mechanism (lidocaine) in another SKU under the same name. Flagged on [[docosanol]] and [[topical-anesthetics]].
