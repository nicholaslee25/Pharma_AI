## Overview
**Major finding, confirmed via DailyMed — corrects an assumption this database's own ingredient page made about this brand before verification:** Licefreee! Head Lice Treatment Spray's active ingredient is listed as **"Natrum muriaticum 1X (Sodium chloride, USP)"**, and the product's **FDA marketing category is "unapproved homeopathic"** — carrying the standard disclaimer, "This homeopathic product has not been evaluated by the Food and Drug Administration for safety or efficacy." Labeler: Tec Laboratories, Inc. Directions: spray on dry hair, saturate fully, air dry (do not rinse), repeat as often as desired; ages 6 months and up.

This is a real, verified regulatory-status fact, not a database error being alleged against Tec Laboratories — homeopathic X-potency labeling (1X = one decimal serial dilution from a mother tincture) is a legitimate FDA marketing pathway distinct from the standard OTC drug monograph that governs [[Nix]] and [[RID]]'s classic line. The mechanism is genuinely mundane (sodium chloride, a desiccant) and the ingredient page [[permethrin-pyrethrin-lice]] cites a real randomized trial (Pharmacology & Pharmacy, 2013; n=42) finding **1% sodium chloride spray significantly outperformed 1% permethrin crème rinse** — but **that trial's plain "1%" formulation is not confirmed to be the same product, strength, or regulatory category as Licefreee!'s homeopathically-labeled "1X" commercial product.** The underlying chemical is identical; the evidence base and the marketed product may not be.

No Licefreee!-brand-specific clinical trial was located in this pass.

## Pros
- Genuinely resistance-independent mechanism in principle (desiccation is not a pyrethroid sodium-channel target, so kdr mutations documented on [[permethrin-pyrethrin-lice]] do not apply to sodium chloride's mode of action)
- No-rinse, reapply-as-needed directions are simple and low-irritation-risk relative to pesticide-class actives
- Approved down to 6 months of age
- A real supportive RCT exists for the sodium-chloride-desiccation mechanism in general (see caveat above about whether it maps precisely onto this specific product)

## Cons
- **Filed with FDA as "unapproved homeopathic," not a standard OTC drug monograph product** — FDA has explicitly not evaluated this product for safety or efficacy, the same regulatory category this database has already flagged as a real efficacy-evidence gap elsewhere (see [[homeopathic-arnica]] precedent)
- **The favorable RCT cited on [[permethrin-pyrethrin-lice]] is not confirmed to correspond to this specific homeopathically-labeled product** — a real evidence-to-product mapping gap, not a clean "here's proof this brand works" story
- No Licefreee!-brand-specific clinical trial exists
- Homeopathic X-potency dilution means the marketed strength is not stated as a plain concentration, making it harder for a consumer to compare directly against the cited trial's "1%" formulation

## Use Cases
Marketed treatment of head lice, pubic (crab) lice, and body lice via a sodium-chloride desiccation mechanism, spray-on/air-dry, reapply as often as desired.

## Allergies / Warnings
Per label: external use only; do not use in or near eyes; avoid mucous membranes; tingling in first 10 minutes is expected/normal; discontinue if skin irritation develops; not for children under 6 months (consult a doctor).

## Symptoms Treated
Active head lice, pubic lice, and body lice infestation.

## Brand-Specific Alignment Check
**Real discrepancy found and flagged on both this page and [[permethrin-pyrethrin-lice]]:** this database's ingredient page originally described Licefreee! as a straightforwardly evidence-backed "resistance-independent alternative" to permethrin/pyrethrin, implicitly treating it as an OTC monograph desiccant product. DailyMed verification instead shows it is filed as **"unapproved homeopathic"** — the sodium chloride active ingredient is real and the desiccation mechanism is mechanistically sound and resistance-independent, but the product's actual FDA regulatory status is the same weak-evidence category (no FDA safety/efficacy review) already flagged elsewhere in this database for homeopathic topical products. Both pages have been corrected to state this plainly rather than resolve it silently.
