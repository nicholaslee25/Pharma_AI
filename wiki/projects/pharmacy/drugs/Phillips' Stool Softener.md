## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed: Phillips' Stool Softener is docusate sodium 100mg — identical active ingredient and dose to Colace (61% share, this category's dominant brand, researched separately) and to Dulcolax Stool Softener (part of the Dulcolax brand family's own two-mechanism split, also researched separately). A real "identical formulation under multiple competing brand names" instance. Also a genuine mechanism differentiator within the "Phillips'" family itself: this is the THIRD distinct mechanism confirmed under the "Phillips'" brand name in this database's research pass, alongside plain Phillips' (magnesium hydroxide/Milk of Magnesia) and Phillips' Fiber Good (inulin dietary-supplement gummy) — meaning "Phillips'" alone spans three different laxative/softener mechanisms.

## Pros
- Confirmed FDA label; genuinely docusate-based, sharing [[docusate]]'s established (if weakly-evidenced) mechanism
- Liquid gel capsule format, per DailyMed labeling, may improve swallowability for some users

## Cons
- **Per [[docusate]]'s own Efficacy Controversy: docusate has no FDA-approved indication, and a 2021 review of 7 RCTs found no significant difference from placebo** — the same weak-evidence finding applying to Colace applies identically here, since the active ingredient and dose are the same
- **A consumer paying a different price for "Phillips'" vs. "Colace" vs. "Dulcolax Stool Softener" is paying for brand/marketing, not a different drug** — all three are confirmed identical (docusate sodium 100mg)
- Contains synthetic dyes (FD&C Blue #1, Red #40, Yellow #6) per its own DailyMed inactive-ingredient list — a real, brand-specific allergen/sensitivity consideration not necessarily shared by every competing docusate brand (not independently confirmed for Colace/Dulcolax's own dye content in this pass)
- Real brand-family confusion risk: "Phillips'" now spans three different mechanisms (magnesium hydroxide, docusate, inulin) across its product line, per this database's research in this same pass
- No Phillips'-Stool-Softener-brand-specific RCT beyond the general docusate evidence base was independently located in this pass

## Use Cases
Relief of occasional constipation, easing difficult/painful defecation — identical use case and mechanism to Colace, per [[docusate]].

## Allergies / Warnings
**Contains synthetic dyes (FD&C Blue #1, Red #40, Yellow #6)** — a real, brand-specific consideration for dye-sensitive individuals. Per [[docusate]]: do not use with mineral oil unless directed by a doctor; contraindicated with nausea/vomiting, intestinal obstruction; risk of dependency beyond 7 days of use.
