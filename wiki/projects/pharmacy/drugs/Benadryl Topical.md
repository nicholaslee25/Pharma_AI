## Appears In

## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed: "Benadryl Topical" (as distinct from oral Benadryl) spans at least three SKUs, all diphenhydramine-based but at different strengths and with a second ingredient in the creams: **Benadryl Extra Strength Itch Stopping Cream** (diphenhydramine HCl 2% + zinc acetate 0.1% skin protectant), **Benadryl Original Strength Itch Stopping Cream** (diphenhydramine HCl 1% + zinc acetate 0.1%), and **Benadryl Extra Strength Itch Stopping Gel** (diphenhydramine HCl only, no zinc acetate). No new ingredient page existed for topical diphenhydramine prior to this research pass — a new [[topical-diphenhydramine]] page was created covering both this brand and [[After Bite]]'s diphenhydramine-based "Xtra" SKU. The zinc acetate component adds a genuine secondary function (drying oozing/weeping poison-plant reactions) not present in the gel SKU or in competing insect-bite products.

## Pros
- Confirmed FDA Drug Facts labels for all identified SKUs, with clear per-SKU strength labeling (1% "Original" vs. 2% "Extra Strength")
- Zinc acetate addition in the cream SKUs provides a documented secondary skin-protectant/drying function for poison-plant reactions, beyond the itch/pain relief
- Trusted, long-standing brand name; single consistent active ingredient (diphenhydramine) across all topical SKUs, a narrower and more predictable formulation spread than [[After Bite]]'s five-plus different active ingredients

## Cons
- Diphenhydramine's topical route carries a real, case-report-documented systemic-absorption toxicity risk if applied to large body areas, broken/eczematous skin, or combined with other diphenhydramine products (oral or topical) — see [[topical-diphenhydramine]] for a pediatric fatality case report (PMID 19901814) directly relevant to this ingredient
- The brand name "Benadryl" is far better known for its oral antihistamine use; a consumer could plausibly conflate topical and oral products and inadvertently double up on diphenhydramine exposure — the label explicitly warns against this, underscoring it is a real, anticipated risk rather than a hypothetical one
- No brand-specific clinical trial for Benadryl's topical itch-stopping products specifically was identified beyond the general diphenhydramine ingredient-level evidence
- Symptom relief only — does not address the underlying bite/sting reaction mechanism, consistent with [[insect-bites]]'s general framing

## Use Cases
Temporary relief of pain and itching from insect bites, minor burns, sunburn, minor skin irritations, minor cuts/scrapes, and poison ivy/oak/sumac rashes; cream SKUs additionally dry the oozing/weeping of poison-plant reactions via zinc acetate.

## Allergies / Warnings
Per label: external use only; do not apply to large body areas; do not use with other diphenhydramine-containing products, including oral ones; ask a doctor before use on chicken pox or measles; avoid eyes; stop use and consult a doctor if condition worsens, persists beyond 7 days, or recurs within days of clearing; keep out of reach of children, contact Poison Control if ingested. See [[topical-diphenhydramine]] for the quantified systemic-toxicity case-report data underlying these warnings.

## Symptoms Treated
Itching and pain from insect bites; also broadly labeled for minor burns, sunburn, cuts/scrapes, and poison ivy/oak/sumac rashes (with drying action on oozing/weeping in the zinc-acetate cream SKUs).
