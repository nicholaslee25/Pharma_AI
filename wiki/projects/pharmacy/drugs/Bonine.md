## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed: Bonine is meclizine HCl 25mg chewable — see [[meclizine]] for the full evidence base. Bonine is the second dedicated meclizine brand documented in this database (alongside Dramamine Nausea), directly competing on an identical active ingredient/dose. Bonine's own label carries the comparative marketing claim **"Less drowsy vs. Dramamine® dimenhydrinate"** — a real, printed-on-label comparison, but one comparing meclizine (a different molecule) against dimenhydrinate specifically, not against Dramamine Nausea's own meclizine-based product.

## Pros
- Once-daily dosing (up to 24-hour relief per label) versus dimenhydrinate's every-4-8-hour regimen — a real, meaningful convenience advantage, consistent with meclizine's longer duration of action documented on [[meclizine]]
- Meclizine is generally considered less sedating than dimenhydrinate/diphenhydramine per the general pharmacologic literature already cited on [[meclizine]] — directionally supports (though does not independently prove) the label's comparative claim

## Cons
- **The "less drowsy vs. Dramamine dimenhydrinate" claim was not independently verified against a dedicated head-to-head RCT in this pass** — it is a class-level pharmacologic inference (meclizine vs. dimenhydrinate as different molecules with different general sedation profiles) presented on-label as a direct comparative claim; this database flags the claim as directionally plausible but not independently confirmed by a cited trial
- Not labeled for use under age 12, a narrower pediatric applicability than dimenhydrinate-based Dramamine products (labeled down to age 2)
- Same core meclizine safety profile applies per [[meclizine]]: anticholinergic side effects, caution in glaucoma/breathing problems/enlarged prostate per label, and a historically-noted (not independently re-confirmed here) pregnancy caution in some older monograph sources

## Use Cases
Prevention and treatment of motion sickness: nausea, vomiting, dizziness, and "cyber sickness" (per newer label language) — once-daily dosing.

## Allergies / Warnings
Do not use unless directed by a doctor if glaucoma, breathing problems (emphysema/chronic bronchitis), or difficulty urinating due to enlarged prostate. Not for children under 12 without doctor guidance.

## Symptoms Treated
Motion-sickness-related nausea, vomiting, dizziness.
