## Overview
Confirmed (manufacturer: Spectrum Brands, Inc.): Cutter, like [[Off!]], is an **EPA-regulated pesticide/repellent, not an FDA drug** — no DailyMed/FDA Drug Facts label exists. The brand spans multiple active-ingredient concentrations and classes across its product line: **DEET** at several strengths (Cutter Insect Repellent 30% DEET; #25 ST 10% DEET; #30CR 25% DEET), **picaridin** (#10GE 20% picaridin; Advanced Outdoorsmen line 7% and 15% picaridin), and **oil of lemon eucalyptus/PMD** (Cutter Lemon Eucalyptus line, DEET-free, lasting up to 6 hours, approved for kids 3+). See [[insect-repellents-deet-picaridin]] for the comparative DEET/picaridin efficacy and safety evidence base.

Cutter's marketing explicitly notes that oil of lemon eucalyptus is "the only plant-based active ingredient recommended by the CDC for mosquito protection" — a genuine, verifiable CDC endorsement distinguishing it from less-rigorously-studied essential-oil repellents (citronella, etc.) marketed by other brands in this category.

## Pros
- Confirmed EPA-registered actives across DEET, picaridin, and oil of lemon eucalyptus/PMD — the same three "conventional"/CDC-recognized repellent classes as [[Off!]], giving consumers real mechanistic choice under one brand
- Oil of lemon eucalyptus line is specifically the CDC-recommended plant-based option, a genuine evidence-backed alternative for DEET-averse consumers, unlike less-studied essential-oil-only competitors
- Wide concentration range (7-30%+ across actives) allows matching protection duration to specific outdoor-exposure needs

## Cons
- Same DEET pediatric-neurotoxicity case-report history documented on [[insect-repellents-deet-picaridin]] applies to Cutter's DEET-containing SKUs — real seizure/encephalopathy case reports exist in the literature, though EPA's own review could not confirm DEET caused them
- Oil of lemon eucalyptus line is not approved for children under 3 — a real, specific pediatric age restriction distinct from DEET/picaridin's no-age-restriction EPA status
- Spans a wide DEET concentration range (10-30%+) under one brand name without a unifying "Cutter = X% DEET" identity — a consumer must check the specific SKU
- No brand-specific clinical trial was sought, consistent with this database's FDA-label-centric methodology; efficacy evidence is at the ingredient-class level

## Use Cases
Prevention of insect bites/stings (mosquitoes, ticks) by repelling insects from skin/clothing before contact — via DEET, picaridin, or oil of lemon eucalyptus depending on the specific SKU chosen.

## Allergies / Warnings
Standard EPA label guidance for DEET/picaridin-containing products: do not apply over cuts, wounds, or irritated skin; avoid children's eyes, mouth, and hands; wash treated skin after returning indoors. Oil of lemon eucalyptus line: not for children under 3 years.

## Symptoms Treated
None — a preventive repellent, not a symptom-treatment product.
