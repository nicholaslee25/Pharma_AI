## Overview
Confirmed via Gold Bond's own product page (Drug Facts panel): **Gold Bond Ultimate Diabetics' Dry Skin Relief Foot Cream** is an actual OTC monograph drug, not a cosmetic — active ingredients **Dimethicone 3%** and **White Petrolatum 30%**, both classified "Skin protectant." This is a real, unambiguous distinction from category-mates like [[CeraVe]] and Eucerin, which carry no active-ingredient claim for their equivalent diabetic-line SKUs. Petrolatum is covered at [[petrolatum]] — that page's formulation caveat (its efficacy evidence is for plain white petrolatum in wound care, not petrolatum-dimethicone skin-protectant blends) applies here: this product's use case (moisturizing/protecting intact dry skin) is different from the wound-care RCT context on that page, so the two shouldn't be conflated even though petrolatum is a shared ingredient. Dimethicone has no dedicated ingredient page in this database — noted as a gap. Label saved in full at `raw/primary-sources/brands/gold-bond-ultimate/goldbond-diabetics-dry-skin-relief-foot-cream.md`. No brand- or diabetic-population-specific clinical trial was found; see [[diabetic-foot-care]] for the general evidence context (ulceration/amputation risk reduction is driven by education/screening/footwear, not cream choice).

## Pros
- Genuine dual-skin-protectant formulation (dimethicone + high-concentration 30% white petrolatum) with an actual FDA Drug Facts label — more transparent than several cosmetic-classified competitors in this same category
- 30% petrolatum is a substantial occlusive concentration, consistent with the strong barrier/moisture-retention mechanism described on [[petrolatum]]
- Marketed and formulated specifically for diabetics' dry skin, not a repurposed general moisturizer

## Cons
- No dimethicone-specific or combination-specific efficacy trial was located — the "skin protectant" monograph classification reflects ingredient-category precedent, not a study of this exact formulation
- Like all products in this category, no evidence that it reduces diabetic ulceration or amputation risk specifically — see [[diabetic-foot-care]]
- Label directs discontinuing use if symptoms persist beyond 7 days, meaning it is not intended as an indefinite standalone therapy for chronic diabetic skin issues without reassessment

## Use Cases
Temporary protection and relief of cracked, dry skin on the feet, particularly for diabetic patients, as part of (not a replacement for) a broader foot-care and screening routine.

## Allergies / Warnings
For external use only; avoid eye contact. Do not apply to deep wounds, puncture wounds, animal bites, or serious burns. Stop and consult a doctor if the condition worsens or symptoms persist beyond 7 days or recur within days. Keep away from children; contact Poison Control if swallowed.

## Symptoms Treated
Cracked, dry, rough skin on the feet.
