## Appears In
Insect Bite and Sting Management - 4%

## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed: BiteMD (Cutter brand) is a **triple-active** liquid — **benzocaine 5%** (analgesic/anesthetic/antipruritic), **camphor 3%** (topical analgesic), and **phenol 1.35%** (first aid antiseptic). The benzocaine component links to [[topical-anesthetics]]'s well-documented methemoglobinemia risk (disproportionately a benzocaine issue relative to lidocaine), though at 5% concentration in a small pen-applicator format rather than a broad spray. Camphor and phenol have no dedicated ingredient evidence page in this database yet — flagged as a gap rather than fabricated; a broader counterirritant/topical-analgesic ingredient page (covering camphor, menthol, methyl salicylate) is anticipated when this database's research reaches the Thermal Relief Products subcategory, where these ingredients appear far more heavily.

## Pros
- Confirmed FDA Drug Facts label with three clearly disclosed active ingredients and their distinct purposes
- Combines anesthetic (benzocaine), counterirritant (camphor), and antiseptic (phenol) action in one product — a broader mechanism combination than single-ingredient competitors
- Manufactured by Cutter, an established insect-repellent brand family, giving it a plausible adjacent-category trust signal (though repellency and bite-treatment are different functions)

## Cons
- Contains benzocaine, carrying the disproportionate methemoglobinemia risk documented on [[topical-anesthetics]] (benzocaine involved in ~66% of 242 reviewed cases vs. ~5% for lidocaine) — a real consideration despite the small-format pen applicator
- Camphor and phenol lack dedicated evidence pages in this database — their independent safety/efficacy data is not documented here yet (gap, not fabricated)
- Label explicitly warns against use on large body areas or under a bandage, and limits use to under one week without medical direction — meaningful use restrictions beyond a simple "apply and reapply" framing
- No brand-specific clinical trial for BiteMD was identified; a secondary retail source (manufacturer's own product page) omits camphor from its ingredient list, an inconsistency with the DailyMed FDA label used as the authoritative source here — worth being aware of if cross-referencing marketing materials against the actual label

## Use Cases
First aid for temporary relief of pain and itching from insect bites/stings (bees, wasps, mosquitoes, ticks, hornets, ants, chiggers, per manufacturer marketing), plus antiseptic action to help prevent infection.

## Allergies / Warnings
Per label: external use only; avoid eyes and large body areas; do not bandage after application; consult a doctor for deep/puncture wounds, animal bites, or serious burns; discontinue if condition worsens or persists beyond 7 days; do not use longer than 1 week without medical direction; flush eyes with water for 15 minutes if product enters eyes; keep away from children, contact Poison Control if swallowed. See [[topical-anesthetics]] for the benzocaine-specific methemoglobinemia risk data.

## Symptoms Treated
Pain, itching, and infection-risk reduction from insect bites and stings.
