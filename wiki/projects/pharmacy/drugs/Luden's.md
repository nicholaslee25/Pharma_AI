## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed: Luden's active ingredient is **pectin**, labeled as an **oral demulcent** — a genuinely different mechanism from the menthol-based lozenges dominating most of this category (Halls, Ricola, Fisherman's Friend, Honees, Burt's Bees, Vicks VapoCOOL). Pectin works by coating/soothing irritated mucous membranes rather than through menthol's cooling/anesthetic sensory action or benzocaine's true local anesthesia — labeled for "temporary relief of minor discomfort and protection of irritated areas in sore mouth and sore throat." No ingredient page exists in this database for pectin as an oral demulcent (a "peg-3350" and "docusate" exist for GI use, unrelated). ## Pros
- Genuinely different, non-menthol/non-anesthetic mechanism (physical coating/protection) — may suit users who find menthol's cooling sensation unpleasant or who want a gentler product
- No labeled cough-suppressant or oral-anesthetic claim to worry about interacting with other medications, unlike benzocaine- or dextromethorphan-containing competitors in this category
- Wide flavor variety for compliance

## Cons
- Being a demulcent rather than an active cough suppressant or anesthetic, it makes no claim to actually suppress cough or numb pain — purely a soothing/protective coating action, a real functional difference from most of its category-mates that a consumer should understand before choosing it for cough suppression specifically
- No dedicated ingredient page exists to provide a fuller evidence/mechanism discussion for pectin as an oral demulcent

## Use Cases
