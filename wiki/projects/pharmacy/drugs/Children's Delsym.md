## Overview
Per [[dextromethorphan]]'s existing confirmed DailyMed research, "Children's Delsym" (plain, no suffix) = **dextromethorphan polistirex extended-release**, a single-active-ingredient antitussive, dosed by age per the table on that ingredient page (7.5mg q12h for 4-5y up to 30mg q12h for 12+/adults; not for under 4). **Category-placement discrepancy worth flagging:** this same "Children's Delsym" brand name carries an 11% share in Children's Cough/Cold Combinations — a category whose name implies a multi-ingredient combination product — despite the confirmed base-product formulation being single-ingredient. The real-world Delsym line does sell a genuine combination SKU, "Children's Delsym Cough Plus Cold Night Time" (confirmed via DailyMed to contain acetaminophen + diphenhydramine + phenylephrine, a completely different three-ingredient set with no dextromethorphan at all), filed separately in this database as `drugs/Delsym Cough + Cold Night Time.md`. It is plausible the Cough/Cold Combinations category entry actually refers to this Night Time SKU rather than the plain single-ingredient product, but this database's category-mapping file lists it simply as "Children's Delsym" without SKU-level specificity — flagged here rather than silently assumed either way.

## Pros
- Extended-release (q12h) dosing may improve nighttime cough control and reduce dosing-schedule burden compared to shorter-acting q4h cough syrups, if the plain single-ingredient formulation is what's actually being recommended in both categories
- Confirmed FDA OTC label with explicit pediatric age-banded dosing down to age 4

## Cons
- **The brand-name/category mismatch above is a real, unresolved ambiguity**: a parent recommended "Children's Delsym" for a cold/combination need could receive either the plain dextromethorphan-only product or the acetaminophen+diphenhydramine+phenylephrine Night Time combination depending on which SKU they buy — these have materially different active ingredients, risk profiles, and dosing, yet share a parent brand name closely enough that this database's own category-mapping data could not disambiguate them with confidence
- Per [[dextromethorphan]]'s own evidence grade (low-to-moderate): the best-designed placebo-controlled trial located (Ramsay 2008) found dextromethorphan raised objective cough-reflex threshold but showed no significant subjective symptom benefit over placebo — the same caveat applies to this pediatric product's core active ingredient
- **Standing 2008 FDA/CHPA pediatric cough-cold labeling history** (detailed on `drugs/Children's Dimetapp Cold and Allergy.md`) applies to any combination-product SKU sold under this brand name; the plain single-ingredient extended-release syrup carries its own age-4 floor per [[dextromethorphan]]'s dosing table, independent of that combination-product history

## Use Cases
Temporary relief of cough due to minor throat/bronchial irritation associated with the common cold, ages 4+ (single-ingredient SKU); broader cold/flu symptom relief if the Night Time combination SKU is what's actually being recommended under this name (see Overview).

## Allergies / Warnings
Per [[dextromethorphan]]'s label data: not for children under 4; do not use with any other product containing dextromethorphan; ask a doctor if the child takes an MAOI. If the Night Time combination SKU applies instead, see `drugs/Delsym Cough + Cold Night Time.md` for its distinct acetaminophen/diphenhydramine/phenylephrine warning set.

## Symptoms Treated
Cough due to minor throat/bronchial irritation (single-ingredient SKU); potentially broader cold symptoms (runny nose, sneezing, fever) if the combination SKU applies.

## Brand-Specific Alignment Check
**New finding, flagged on both this page and [[dextromethorphan]]:** the brand-name ambiguity between plain "Children's Delsym" (single-ingredient DXM, confirmed) and "Children's Delsym Cough Plus Cold Night Time" (a completely different acetaminophen/diphenhydramine/phenylephrine combination, no DXM) means this database's Children's Cough/Cold Combinations category entry for "Children's Delsym" cannot be confirmed against a specific label with full certainty — a genuine, transparently-flagged data gap rather than a silently resolved assumption.
