## Appears In

## Overview
Neutrogena T/Sal (Kenvue Brands LLC) is confirmed via DailyMed as **salicylic acid 3%**, per [[salicylic-acid]] — notably a higher concentration than any acne-line salicylic-acid product documented elsewhere in this pass (e.g., [[Stridex]]'s 0.5-2% or [[OXY]]'s 0.5-2%), for a completely different indication set: dandruff, seborrheic dermatitis, and psoriasis-related scalp scaling. Related SKUs confirmed via DailyMed title search: "T/Sal Therapeutic," "T/Sal Plus 3% Salicylic Acid Anti-Dandruff for Dry, Itchy Scalp," and "T/Sal 3% Salicylic Acid Anti-Dandruff for Scalp Build Up Control" — all at the same 3% concentration, differing mainly in secondary marketing claims (dry/itchy scalp vs. buildup control) rather than active-ingredient strength.

This is a distinct product line from [[Neutrogena T-Gel]] (coal tar) within the same Dandruff Shampoo category, and from [[Neutrogena]]'s acne-line salicylic acid products documented elsewhere in this database — three different mechanisms/concentrations under the "Neutrogena" house-brand name across just these few product lines.

## Pros
- Confirmed 3% concentration is a real, disclosed, monograph-consistent salicylic-acid strength for the scalp/dandruff indication specifically
- Salicylic acid's keratolytic mechanism is well-suited to scalp-buildup/scaling symptoms specifically, a genuinely different symptom profile than acne
- Broad labeled indication set (dandruff, seborrheic dermatitis, psoriasis) matches [[Neutrogena T-Gel]]'s equally broad claims, giving a consumer a same-brand choice between a salicylic-acid mechanism and a coal-tar mechanism

## Cons
- **3% concentration is meaningfully higher than any Neutrogena acne-line salicylic-acid product** — per [[salicylic-acid]]'s own Formulation Caveat, concentration and body-surface-area/site matter significantly for systemic-salicylate-toxicity risk; a consumer using both an acne-line Neutrogena salicylic-acid product AND T/Sal simultaneously (e.g., face + scalp) would be combining two salicylic-acid exposures without an obvious single-product warning addressing that combination
- No Neutrogena-T/Sal-brand-specific clinical trial was located in this pass — evidence is entirely ingredient-level, and the salicylic-acid ingredient page's own strongest evidence base (Cochrane wart-removal data) does not directly transfer to the dandruff/scalp indication or this concentration, per [[salicylic-acid]]'s existing framing
- "Ask a doctor before use if you have a condition that covers a large area of the body" — a real, specific label warning relevant to systemic-absorption risk at this higher 3% concentration, more pointed than warnings seen on lower-concentration salicylic-acid acne products in this pass

## Use Cases
Scalp itching, irritation, redness, flaking, and scaling associated with dandruff, seborrheic dermatitis, and psoriasis, via salicylic acid's keratolytic action at 3% concentration.

## Allergies / Warnings
For external use only; ask a doctor before use if you have a condition covering a large body area; avoid eye contact (rinse thoroughly with water if contact occurs); stop use and ask a doctor if condition worsens or does not improve; keep out of reach of children, contact Poison Control if swallowed. General systemic-salicylate-toxicity caution applies at higher concentrations/larger application areas, per [[salicylic-acid]].

## Symptoms Treated
Scalp itching, irritation, redness, flaking, and scaling from dandruff, seborrheic dermatitis, or psoriasis.
