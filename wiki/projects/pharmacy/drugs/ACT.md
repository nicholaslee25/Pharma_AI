## Appears In
Therapeutic Mouthwashes/Oral Rinses - 11%

## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed: ACT's core "Anticavity Fluoride" mouthwash line (Mint, Cinnamon, Total Care Fresh Mint) lists **sodium fluoride 0.02%** as its active ingredient — the same well-established anticaries mechanism documented on [[fluoride-toothpaste]], applied here as a rinse rather than a paste. This is a distinct, simpler formulation than the same brand's separate "ACT Dry Mouth" sub-line (also sodium fluoride 0.02%, but marketed toward dry-mouth comfort rather than general therapeutic use — see `drugs/ACT Dry Mouth.md`).

## Pros
- Carries fluoride's genuinely strong, Cochrane-backed anticaries evidence base (Marinho et al. 2003: 24% pooled prevented fraction across 42,300 children) — one of the stronger evidence positions in this Oral Care category pass
- Available in alcohol-free variants (e.g., Cinnamon), useful for users who want to avoid alcohol-containing rinses like classic Listerine
- Simple, single-active-ingredient formulation makes its mechanism and evidence base straightforward to characterize, unlike several brands in this category with multiple sub-lines of differing composition

## Cons
- Fluoride rinse alone does not address gingivitis/plaque/bad-breath bacteria the way essential-oil (Listerine), CPC (Cepacol Antibacterial, Crest Scope), or stannous fluoride (Crest Pro-Health) formulations do — a consumer specifically wanting antigingivitis benefit would need a different ACT sub-line or a different brand
- No brand-specific clinical trial evidence was identified for ACT's rinse specifically, beyond the general fluoride ingredient-class evidence
- As with all fluoride rinses, not appropriate for children under 6 due to swallowing/fluorosis risk

## Use Cases
Cavity prevention via a fluoride rinse, used as a daily adjunct to brushing.

## Allergies / Warnings
Per label: do not swallow; children under 6 should not use unless directed by a dentist/doctor; supervise use in children 6-12; discontinue and consult a dentist if irritation occurs.

## Symptoms Treated
Cavity prevention via topical fluoride exposure — no confirmed antigingivitis/antiplaque or breath-freshening therapeutic claim for this specific sub-line.
