## Appears In
Laxatives, Stimulant - 15%

## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed: Colace 2-IN-1 is docusate sodium 50mg + sennosides 8.6mg — resolving [[docusate]]'s prior hedge ("exact formulation not independently pulled/verified"). This is a genuine dual-mechanism combination (stool softener + stimulant laxative in one tablet), distinct from plain Colace (docusate only, researched separately) — a real, deliberate combination product rather than a rebrand, explaining its placement in the Laxatives, Stimulant category despite the parent "Colace" brand being primarily associated with stool softening.

## Pros
- Confirmed FDA label; combines two complementary mechanisms (docusate's stool-softening + sennosides' stimulant peristalsis) in one tablet — addresses both stool consistency and motility in a single product
- The added sennosides component brings genuine, FDA-approved stimulant-laxative evidence per [[senna]], potentially offsetting docusate's own weak standalone evidence (see Cons)

## Cons
- Per [[docusate]]'s own Efficacy Controversy: docusate alone has **no FDA-approved indication** and a 2021 review of 7 RCTs found no significant difference from placebo — meaning half of this combination product's two active ingredients carries this database's weakest evidence grade for any laxative mechanism reviewed
- Per [[senna]]'s own Key Safety Data: the sennosides component carries the same in-vitro (not human-confirmed) neoplastic-effect question and long-term colonic structural-change association as Senokot/Ex-Lax
- A consumer already taking plain Colace (docusate only) who adds Colace 2-IN-1 without realizing it also contains sennosides risks unintentionally combining two different laxative mechanisms rather than simply increasing their docusate dose
- No Colace-2-IN-1-brand-specific RCT beyond the general docusate/senna evidence bases was independently located in this pass

## Use Cases
Relief of occasional constipation via a combined stool-softening (docusate) and stimulant (sennosides) mechanism — onset 6-12 hours per label, faster than plain Colace's docusate-only 12-72 hour window, likely reflecting the added stimulant component's faster action.

## Allergies / Warnings
Per [[docusate]]: do not use with mineral oil unless directed by a doctor; contraindicated with nausea/vomiting, intestinal obstruction. Per [[senna]]: OTC label directs against use beyond 1 week without medical direction. Combines both ingredients' contraindication profiles.

## Symptoms Treated
Occasional constipation — via combined docusate (stool-softening, per [[docusate]]) and sennosides (stimulant, per [[senna]]) mechanisms.
