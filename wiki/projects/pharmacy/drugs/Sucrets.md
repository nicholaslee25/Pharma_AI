## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed: Sucrets' core active ingredient is **dyclonine HCl** (an oral anesthetic, different from Cepacol's/Orajel's benzocaine), combined with menthol and pectin in various SKUs. This is a new, previously-unlinked ingredient confirmation — no ingredient page in this database is dedicated to dyclonine specifically ([[topical-anesthetics]] covers benzocaine and lidocaine, not dyclonine), which is flagged as a data gap below rather than force-fitted into that page. "Sucrets Complete Vapor Cherry" adds vitamin C and zinc to some variants per search results, meaning at least one Sucrets SKU also touches [[vitamin-c]]/[[zinc]]. Label saved at `raw/primary-sources/brands/sucrets/dailymed-sucrets.md`.

## Pros
- Dyclonine is a distinct local-anesthetic chemical class from benzocaine/lidocaine — this may matter for a consumer who has had a reaction to benzocaine-class anesthetics (e.g. Cepacol) and needs an alternative, though cross-sensitivity specifics weren't established in this pass
- Menthol adds a cooling/soothing sensory effect alongside the anesthetic action
- Some SKUs add vitamin C/zinc for combined symptomatic + nutritional support

## Cons
- Dyclonine's own safety/efficacy evidence (methemoglobinemia risk profile, dosing limits, contraindications) was **not independently pulled** in this pass — since no ingredient page exists for it, this brand page cannot currently cross-check its label against a deeper evidence base the way Cepacol could against [[topical-anesthetics]]. This is a genuine research gap worth flagging for a future pass (a `ingredients/dyclonine.md` page would be a reasonable addition).
- No brand-specific clinical trial was found for Sucrets
