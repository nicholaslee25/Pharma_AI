## Overview
**Real, current category-placement mismatch, actively flagged rather than resolved by inference.** pHisoderm (manufactured today by The Mentholatum Company — the same manufacturer confirmed for [[OXY]] elsewhere in this pass) does **not currently sell an antibacterial soap**. Confirmed via DailyMed: pHisoderm's current product line (Anti-Blemish Body Wash, "Clean" and "Clinical" facial cleansers) is built entirely around **salicylic acid 2%** as an acne-treatment active — purpose "acne treatment," not "antibacterial." No current pHisoderm SKU with a benzalkonium-chloride or any other antibacterial Drug Facts active was located in this pass.

**Historical context (real, significant, and distinct from the current-formulation finding above):** pHisoderm and its sister brand Phisohex were both major antibacterial/antiseptic skin cleansers through the 1960s, formulated with **hexachlorophene**. In September 1972, the FDA restricted hexachlorophene concentrations above 1% following documented **neurotoxicity — including 39 infant deaths in France and 15 in the United States** linked to hexachlorophene exposure — a genuine, serious historical FDA safety action, not a minor reformulation. The brand survived under new ownership (Winthrop Laboratories/Sterling Drug) with a reformulated, non-hexachlorophene product, and has since drifted further from any antibacterial-soap identity into today's salicylic-acid-based acne-cleanser positioning.

**Net finding:** this database's Pharmacy Times-sourced "Antibacterial Soaps" category (4% share) reflects either outdated brand categorization or a discontinued/legacy pHisoderm antibacterial-soap SKU not located in this pass — the currently-marketed pHisoderm product line is an acne cleanser, not an antibacterial soap, and should not be assumed to share any formulation, evidence base, or safety profile with [[benzalkonium-chloride]] or [[chlorhexidine]]-based products elsewhere in this category.
