## Overview
Name-confirmed as a boric-acid vaginal suppository product; see [[boric-acid]] for the full evidence base (Van Slyke 1981 RCT: 92% cure at 7-10 days vs. 64% for nystatin, positioned specifically for recurrent/azole-resistant candidiasis, not general first-line use). ## Pros
- Sits within a real, cited clinical evidence base (Van Slyke 1981; Iavazzo 2011 systematic review) for boric acid as a genuine second-line option for recurrent/resistant yeast infections
- AZO's general brand recognition/availability may improve access relative to less widely-stocked boric-acid brands

## Cons
- **Specific per-suppository mg dose not independently confirmed against AZO's own label in this pass** — per [[boric-acid]], not every product marketed as "boric acid suppository" contains the studied 600mg pharmacological dose (a homeopathic-dilution example, "V-BELLA," is documented on that page as a real contrast case); confirm the Active Ingredient line on the actual purchased product
- Boric acid's own evidence base positions it as a second-line/alternative treatment for recurrent or azole-resistant cases specifically — not a general first-line yeast-infection treatment the way miconazole/tioconazole products are; a consumer selecting this product for a first, uncomplicated yeast infection may be reaching for a less-appropriate-tier option than the category's miconazole-based competitors
- **Toxic if swallowed** — a genuine, serious safety consideration distinct from its topical/vaginal safety profile, requiring careful storage away from children per [[boric-acid]]
- Same brand-family mechanism-spread caution already documented on [[AZO]] applies: consumers should confirm which specific AZO product (boric acid vs. phenazopyridine vs. homeopathic Yeast Plus vs. probiotic) they are selecting, since the shared brand name spans genuinely different mechanisms

## Use Cases
Vaginal yeast infection symptoms (itching, burning, irritation, discharge, odor) — per [[boric-acid]], best-evidenced specifically for recurrent or azole-resistant cases.

## Allergies / Warnings
Toxic if swallowed — contact Poison Control immediately if ingested. Consult a doctor before use if pregnant, nursing, or experiencing vaginal bleeding, sores, fever, or pelvic pain. May compromise latex condoms/diaphragms during treatment.
