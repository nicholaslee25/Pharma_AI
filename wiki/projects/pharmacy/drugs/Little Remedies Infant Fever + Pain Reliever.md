## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed: Little Remedies Infant Fever/Pain Reliever is **acetaminophen 160 mg per 5 mL** — the same standardized concentration as Children's Tylenol, Genexa, and DrKids, matching [[acetaminophen]]. Despite being named "Infant" (suggesting the pre-2011 higher-concentration infant drops), this product is confirmed at the current, standardized post-2011 concentration — a reassuring finding given the real historical confusion between "infant" and "children's" acetaminophen concentrations documented on [[acetaminophen]]. A related "Little Remedies Children's Fever/Pain Reliever" product exists under the same brand family at the same 160mg/5mL concentration.

## Pros
- Confirmed at the current standardized concentration despite the "Infant" naming — directly relevant reassurance given [[acetaminophen]]'s documented history of infant/children's concentration confusion
- Same well-established acetaminophen evidence base as Children's Tylenol

## Cons
- The "Infant" branding on a product that is actually the SAME concentration as "Children's" products (rather than a different, infant-specific concentration) could, in principle, cause a caregiver to assume a dosing difference that doesn't exist — worth noting given how much of [[acetaminophen]]'s pediatric safety history centers on exactly this kind of infant/children's naming and concentration confusion, even though in THIS specific case the concentration is confirmed uniform
- Same full acetaminophen hepatotoxicity risk profile as any other 160mg/5mL product
- No independent brand-specific RCT was identified for this pediatric formulation specifically in this pass
