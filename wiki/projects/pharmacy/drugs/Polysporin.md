## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed: Polysporin First Aid Antibiotic's own FDA OTC Drug Facts label lists **bacitracin zinc (500 units) and polymyxin B sulfate (10,000 units)** as active ingredients — a 2-agent antibiotic combination, **neomycin-free**, consistent with [[topical-antibiotics]]'s existing note distinguishing it from Neosporin's 3-agent formula. This neomycin-free profile means Polysporin avoids neomycin's specific, well-documented contact-allergy/anaphylaxis risk (per the NACDG data on [[topical-antibiotics]]) while retaining bacitracin's own separate, also well-documented allergy risk (~6.9% patch-test prevalence). One notable brand-family finding: Polysporin's current US product page is hosted under the Neosporin.com domain (both are Kenvue/J&J-family topical-antibiotic brands), suggesting shared corporate ownership/positioning rather than fully independent competing companies — worth noting since the Pharmacy Times category treats them as separate recommendation-share entries.

## Pros
- Confirmed FDA Drug Facts label; neomycin-free formula avoids the single most flagged contact-allergen among the three common triple-antibiotic ingredients
- Same core evidence base as bacitracin generally (Smack et al. 1996 RCT on [[petrolatum]]/[[topical-antibiotics]]) — comparable overall infection-rate performance to plain petrolatum, with the added polymyxin B component providing broader gram-negative coverage than bacitracin alone
- Present in both Burn Treatments (5%) and Topical Antibiotics/Antiseptics (10%) with a single consistent formula, unlike some other multi-category brands in this database with formulation drift across categories

## Cons
- Bacitracin retains its own well-documented contact-allergy risk even without neomycin present — "neomycin-free" is not the same as "allergy-risk-free"
- Per the pivotal RCT evidence, this class of product (bacitracin ± polymyxin B) is not proven superior to plain petrolatum on OVERALL infection rate for uncomplicated minor wounds — its specific advantage is preventing staph infections specifically, a narrower benefit
- No brand-specific (as opposed to ingredient-class) clinical trial for Polysporin's specific 2-agent combination was identified
- Smaller share than Neosporin in both categories it appears in, despite the more favorable (neomycin-free) allergy profile — recommendation share does not track this evidence distinction

## Use Cases
First aid to help prevent infection in minor cuts, scrapes, and burns.

## Allergies / Warnings
Per label: for external use only; discontinue and consult a doctor if condition persists/worsens or a rash/allergic reaction develops. See [[topical-antibiotics]] for bacitracin's documented contact-allergy data (applies here; neomycin-specific risk does not, since this formula excludes neomycin).

## Symptoms Treated
Prevention/treatment of minor localized skin infection risk in cuts, scrapes, and burns; no analgesic ingredient (no pain relief, unlike Bacitraycin Plus or Bactine MAX's ointment SKU).
