## Overview
Ascensia Diabetes Care's (formerly Bayer's) **Contour Next** line has among the strongest brand-specific accuracy figures located in this category: a study of the Contour Next Link 2.4 model found **100% of results met ISO 15197:2013 accuracy criteria** (≥95% of results required within ±15 mg/dL or ±15% of reference), and a separate evaluation of the related Contour Plus Elite model likewise found 100% of trained-operator fingertip results within ISO 15197:2013 limits with 100% of measurements falling in Zone A of the consensus error grid (the best-possible clinical-risk category). These are the cleanest ISO-compliance figures found among the four Blood Glucose Monitors brands researched in this pass, though — consistent with [[blood-glucose-monitors]]'s standing caveat — these are largely industry/university-collaboration studies (University of Ulm's Institut für Diabetes-Technologie, a frequent evaluator of multiple competing brands) rather than fully independent of all manufacturer involvement. Smallest share (12%/13%) among the four paired-category glucose brands in this database, despite the strong accuracy data found.

## Pros
- 100% ISO 15197:2013 compliance and 100% Zone A error-grid performance in the studies located — the strongest single-brand accuracy figures found in this category
- Multiple models (Contour Next Link 2.4, Contour Plus Elite) show convergent strong performance rather than a single isolated result
- Evaluated by an academic diabetes-technology research institute (University of Ulm) across multiple studies, which also evaluates competing brands, lending some comparative credibility to the methodology

## Cons
- Smallest market share (12-13%) among the four directly comparable Blood Glucose Monitors/Lancets brands despite strong accuracy data — worth noting as a real disconnect between measured performance and pharmacist-recommendation share, though many factors besides accuracy (cost, insurance coverage, brand familiarity) likely drive recommendation share
- The evaluating institute, while academically based, is not fully independent of industry given its business model evaluating manufacturer-supplied devices — the general King et al. 2018 manufacturer-funding-bias caveat from [[blood-glucose-monitors]] should still be kept in mind
- Subject to the same general interference/hematocrit/strip-lot failure modes documented on [[blood-glucose-monitors]]
