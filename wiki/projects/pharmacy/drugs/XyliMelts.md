## Appears In
Dry Mouth Therapy - 6%

## Overview
XyliMelts (OraCoat/OraHealth Corp.) is an oral-adhering disc that sticks to the teeth or gum line and slowly releases **xylitol (approximately 500-550mg per disc, per differing source figures) plus cellulose gum** as a lubricant/humectant, designed to be worn overnight for sustained relief of nighttime oral dryness. Unlike a rinse or spray, this is a genuinely different DELIVERY mechanism (a slow-dissolving adhesive disc) from every other product in this category. Xylitol itself is FDA GRAS (Generally Recognized As Safe) — a food-additive-level safety designation, not necessarily an FDA OTC monograph "active ingredient" review, though its proposed mechanism (osmotic salivary stimulation plus local reflex stimulation) has real physiologic plausibility beyond simple lubrication.

## Pros
- One of the few products in this weak-evidence category with a dedicated, published clinical study: **Burgess & Lee (PMID 22040224, International Journal of Dental Hygiene)** — 15 subjects with self-identified morning oral dryness, evaluated without treatment then with XyliMelts used overnight, found perceived oral wetness scores increased more than threefold and morning discomfort significantly improved with use
- Xylitol is nonfermentable by oral bacteria, meaning it does not itself contribute to tooth decay the way a sugar-based lozenge would — a genuine advantage for a product used repeatedly, especially overnight
- The adhering-disc format provides sustained, hands-free overnight relief without needing to wake up to reapply a spray or rinse

## Cons
- The pivotal study is small (N=15) and used a before/after (self-controlled) design rather than a randomized, placebo-controlled trial — a real limitation on how strong the evidence can be considered relative to, e.g., fluoride's Cochrane-level evidence base elsewhere in this database
- Xylitol's classification as FDA GRAS is a food-safety designation, not equivalent to formal OTC monograph drug review — this product's regulatory status sits closer to a food-derived comfort aid than a reviewed therapeutic
- No independently located trial compares XyliMelts against rinse-based competitors (Biotène, ACT, SmartMouth) head-to-head for relative effectiveness
- Adhesive-disc format may not suit all users (e.g., those who find oral foreign objects uncomfortable, or who have difficulty positioning a disc precisely at the gum line)

## Use Cases
Sustained (multi-hour, often overnight) relief of dry mouth discomfort via a slow-dissolving adhesive disc combining xylitol and a cellulose-gum lubricant.

## Allergies / Warnings
No FDA drug-specific warnings identified; general xylitol caution: xylitol is toxic to dogs, a real household safety consideration for any xylitol-containing oral product left within a pet's reach. Standard precaution to consult a dentist/doctor if dry mouth persists or has an unaddressed underlying cause (see [[saliva-substitutes]]).

## Symptoms Treated
Nighttime/overnight oral dryness and associated morning discomfort — the one product in this category with dedicated (if small-scale) supporting trial data specifically for this use case.
