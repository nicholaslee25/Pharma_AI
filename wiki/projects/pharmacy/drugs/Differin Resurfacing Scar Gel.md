## Overview
**Major brand-name discrepancy, confirmed via manufacturer (Galderma) product description:** despite carrying the "Differin" name — famous for **adapalene** 0.1%, a genuine retinoid drug (Rx-to-OTC switched, see [[adapalene]]) — **Differin Resurfacing Scar Gel contains no adapalene at all.** Its actual formula is built around **bakuchiol** (marketed as "nature's Retinol," a plant-derived compound chemically unrelated to true retinoids), a peptide blend, and centella asiatica extract. No FDA Drug Facts panel or DailyMed listing was located for this specific product, consistent with it being a **cosmetic**, not an OTC drug — a completely different regulatory and pharmacological category from the adapalene-based Differin Gel that built this brand's reputation and drives its acne-category presence elsewhere in this database.

This is a materially more significant version of the "one brand, multiple mechanisms" pattern already documented repeatedly in this database (Lotrimin, Blistex, Carmex, RID): here, the brand name's single best-known, most clinically established active ingredient (adapalene) is **entirely absent** from this specific product, and the replacement (bakuchiol) has a much thinner, more preliminary evidence base and no FDA drug status at all. A consumer familiar with Differin's reputation for genuine retinoid efficacy could reasonably but incorrectly assume this "Resurfacing Scar Gel" delivers a comparable, FDA-evaluated retinoid effect for scarring — it does not.

No independent Differin Resurfacing Scar Gel-brand-specific clinical trial was located; bakuchiol's general evidence base (small trials suggesting retinol-like effects with less irritation) was not independently re-verified in this pass.

## Pros
- Contains centella asiatica extract, an ingredient with some general dermatologic wound-healing interest in the broader literature (not independently verified in this pass)
- Bakuchiol is generally reported as less irritating than true retinoids in the preliminary literature, which may suit sensitive-skin users specifically avoiding adapalene/tretinoin-type irritation
- Manufactured by Galderma, the same company with genuine, well-evidenced adapalene expertise (elsewhere in its product line)

## Cons
- **No adapalene — despite the "Differin" brand name's core association with that ingredient** — this is the most significant active-ingredient/brand-name mismatch found in this database's Topicals research to date, more pronounced than the Lotrimin Ultra/butenafine or Vamousse/homeopathic corrections, because here the flagship active ingredient the brand name is known for is entirely absent from this specific labeled product, not merely a differently-concentrated or differently-branded relative
- **No FDA Drug Facts panel or drug status found** — this is a cosmetic, not an OTC drug, despite appearing in this database's "Scar Treatments" OTC drug category
- Bakuchiol's evidence base is meaningfully thinner and more preliminary than adapalene's — not independently re-verified in this pass, but understood to be a much newer, less-studied compound
- No independent clinical trial specific to this product was located

## Use Cases
Cosmetic reduction in appearance of post-acne scarring and skin texture, via bakuchiol, peptides, and centella asiatica — not an adapalene-based retinoid treatment, and not an FDA-evaluated drug claim.

## Allergies / Warnings
No FDA Drug Facts label exists to cite. Standard cosmetic-ingredient precautions apply; bakuchiol is generally better tolerated than true retinoids per preliminary literature, but individual sensitivity varies.
