## Overview
**Real category-mismatch finding.** Per manufacturer disclosure, Band-Aid Brand Hydro Seal Acne Blemish Patches are explicitly **non-medicated** — no FDA Drug Facts panel, no active pharmaceutical ingredient of any kind. They are a hydrocolloid device: a gel-forming dressing (sodium carboxymethyl cellulose, gelatin, pectin, hydrophobic pressure-sensitive polymers like polyisobutylene) that absorbs wound/blemish exudate mechanically, forming a visible white bubble as it works. This is the same non-drug/device finding already documented for [[Mighty Patch]] elsewhere in this pass — this database's "Acne Products" category, at least at its lower-share end, includes products with literally no active ingredient alongside benzoyl-peroxide and salicylic-acid drug products carrying up to 10% and 2% concentrations respectively.

## Pros
- Mechanically absorbs fluid from an already-open or draining blemish without any drug-interaction or irritation risk, since no active pharmaceutical ingredient is present
- Manufacturer states "dermatologist-tested" and suitable for sensitive skin, consistent with having no chemical active to trigger irritation or allergic contact dermatitis from a drug ingredient (though the hydrocolloid material itself carries its own allergen profile, see Cons)
- Simple single-step application (patch, leave on 8 hours/overnight) requires no dosing/frequency decisions unlike drug-based acne treatments

## Cons
- **Non-drug device, not a treatment for the underlying acne process** — it manages a single already-formed, draining lesion mechanically; it does nothing for comedone formation, bacterial load, or inflammation the way benzoyl peroxide, salicylic acid, or adapalene do (see [[benzoyl-peroxide]], [[salicylic-acid]], [[adapalene]])
- A consumer selecting "Band-Aid Acne Patches" expecting a medicated acne treatment (as this database's Acne Products category label might imply) would be getting a fundamentally different product category — a real category-vs-mechanism mismatch, similar in kind to the CeraVe diabetic-foot-cream and Neutrogena Sun Rescue non-drug-SKU findings elsewhere in this database
- Hydrocolloid material (pectin-based) is contraindicated for individuals with pectin or latex/rubber sensitivities per manufacturer warning
- No clinical trial evidence (RCT or otherwise) for hydrocolloid patches specifically treating acne blemishes was located in this pass — efficacy claims are mechanistic/observational (visible fluid absorption), not outcome-trial-based

## Use Cases
Physical absorption of fluid/exudate from an already-formed, draining pimple or blemish, and physical protection of the area from picking/friction — not a treatment for acne's underlying causes.

## Allergies / Warnings
Manufacturer warning: may not be suitable for individuals allergic to pectin or rubber/latex; latex-free per current formulation. No FDA Drug Facts warnings exist since this is a non-drug device.

## Symptoms Treated
None in the pharmacological sense — mechanically manages fluid from an existing blemish; does not treat acne's underlying inflammatory or bacterial process.
