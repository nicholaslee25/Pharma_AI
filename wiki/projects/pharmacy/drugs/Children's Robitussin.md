## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed/Pfizer labeling: the "Children's Robitussin" name covers at least two distinct formulations — **"Children's Robitussin Cough & Cold CF"** (dextromethorphan HBr + guaifenesin 100mg + phenylephrine HCl 5mg, an expectorant/antitussive/decongestant triple combination) and **"Children's Robitussin Cough & Cold Long-Acting"** (chlorpheniramine maleate 2mg + dextromethorphan HBr 15mg, an antihistamine/antitussive combination with NO guaifenesin, ages 6+). This is the same brand-name-spans-multiple-formulations pattern already documented for Dimetapp, Mucinex, Delsym, and Tylenol above — this database's category-mapping data lists a single "Children's Robitussin" entry for both Children's Cough (12%) and Children's Cough/Cold Combinations (10%) without SKU-level specificity, so which exact formulation underlies each share cannot be confirmed with certainty. Combines [[dextromethorphan]] and [[guaifenesin]] (both already in this database) with phenylephrine (see [[oral-phenylephrine]]'s FDA-flagged efficacy problem) and/or chlorpheniramine (no dedicated ingredient page in this database — same gap flagged for `drugs/Children's Tylenol Cold + Cough + Runny Nose.md`).

## Pros
- Both confirmed formulations use ingredients with existing evidence pages in this database (dextromethorphan, guaifenesin), allowing direct cross-reference rather than treating this as an unstudied combination
- "Long-Acting" SKU's chlorpheniramine+dextromethorphan combination avoids phenylephrine entirely, sidestepping that ingredient's FDA-flagged efficacy problem — a genuine formulation-dependent advantage if that's the specific SKU being recommended

## Cons
- **Same brand-name/SKU ambiguity problem flagged for Children's Delsym**: without SKU-level data in this database's category mapping, it cannot be confirmed whether the "Children's Robitussin" share reflects the CF (guaifenesin+phenylephrine+DXM) or Long-Acting (chlorpheniramine+DXM) formulation, or some mix — these have different active-ingredient sets and different risk profiles
- If the CF formulation applies: inherits [[oral-phenylephrine]]'s FDA-flagged efficacy problem (2023 advisory panel found oral phenylephrine not GRASE at OTC doses) plus [[guaifenesin]]'s own null objective-outcome RCT (Bennett 2015)
- **Standing 2008 FDA/CHPA pediatric cough-cold labeling history applies** — both confirmed Robitussin combination SKUs are labeled for ages 6+ specifically, more conservative than the age-4 floor used by some competitors (Children's Mucinex)
- No dedicated ingredient page exists for chlorpheniramine in this database if the Long-Acting SKU applies — a gap, consistent with prior chlorpheniramine/brompheniramine gaps flagged elsewhere in this batch
- No brand-specific pediatric RCT located for either combination beyond individual-ingredient evidence

## Use Cases
CF formulation: relief of cough, chest congestion, and stuffy/runny nose. Long-Acting formulation: relief of cough plus antihistamine-responsive symptoms (runny nose, sneezing, itchy/watery eyes), ages 6+.

## Allergies / Warnings
Per label (both SKUs): not for children under 6 (Long-Acting) or per age-banded dosing (CF); do not use with any other product containing dextromethorphan, guaifenesin, a decongestant, or an antihistamine; ask a doctor before use if the child has heart disease, high blood pressure, thyroid disease, diabetes, or a breathing problem.

## Symptoms Treated
Cough, chest congestion (CF); cough, runny nose, sneezing, itchy/watery eyes (Long-Acting).
