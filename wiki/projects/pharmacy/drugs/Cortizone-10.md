## Overview
Same brand, same confirmed formulation as [[Cortizone-10 Itch Relief]] (researched earlier this pass under Insect Bite and Sting Management, 43% share) — DailyMed confirms **hydrocortisone 1%** as the sole active ingredient across every identified SKU (Cooling Relief Gel, Cream, Intensive Healing Cream, Ointment, Plus Cream, Soothing Aloe Itch Relief Cream, Water Resistant Ointment, Ultra Soothing Creme, Sensitive Skin Creme, Easy Relief liquid). This single bare "Cortizone-10" brand file is the plain-brand entry appearing under two more categories in this database: Eczema Care/Relief Products (9%, third-largest hydrocortisone-mechanism entrant behind [[CeraVe]]/[[Aveeno Eczema Therapy]]) and Topical Poison Ivy/Oak Remedies (19%, second only to Caladryl's calamine-based line). Per [[hydrocortisone]], no dedicated Cortizone-10-brand RCT was located for either indication — the ingredient's broad anti-inflammatory/anti-itch evidence base (corticosteroid mechanism, well-established across dermatoses) applies, not a brand-specific trial.

**Mechanism note relevant to both categories on this page:** hydrocortisone is a genuinely different mechanism from the moisturizer/emollient approach ([[moisturizers-ceramides]], [[colloidal-oatmeal]]) used by the other top Eczema Care brands, and different again from the antihistamine/astringent/solvent approaches used by other Poison Ivy/Oak brands (Benadryl = diphenhydramine, Tecnu = solvent-based urushiol removal, Domeboro = astringent). A consumer comparing "% share" across brands in either category is comparing genuinely different mechanisms, not competing versions of the same drug.

## Pros
- Confirmed consistent FDA Drug Facts label (hydrocortisone 1%) across all identified SKUs, independently verified via DailyMed
- Well-established corticosteroid anti-inflammatory/anti-itch mechanism (see [[hydrocortisone]]) applicable to both eczema flares and poison ivy/oak contact dermatitis
- Directly addresses the itch/inflammation of poison ivy/oak reactions (unlike Tecnu, which addresses urushiol removal/prevention rather than active symptoms)
- Category leader across three separate Pharmacy Times categories in this database (Insect Bites 43%, Poison Ivy/Oak 19%, Eczema 9%) — the widest cross-category recommendation footprint of any hydrocortisone brand found in this pass

## Cons
- No Cortizone-10-brand-specific clinical trial located for either eczema or poison ivy/oak — evidence is entirely at the [[hydrocortisone]] ingredient level
- Per [[hydrocortisone]]: not for use beyond 7 days without medical guidance, and should not be used on broken skin, bacterial, or fungal infections — poison ivy/oak blisters that have opened or become secondarily infected fall outside appropriate use
- Treats itch/inflammation only — does not remove residual urushiol oil from skin the way Tecnu or a dedicated poison-ivy wash does, so it does not prevent the rash from spreading via residual contact
- For eczema specifically, a topical corticosteroid used chronically carries the skin-atrophy/tachyphylaxis risks detailed on [[hydrocortisone]] — a real consideration for a condition (eczema) that is often chronic/recurring, versus poison ivy/oak's typically self-limited course

## Use Cases
Temporary relief of itching and inflammation from: eczema flares, poison ivy/oak/sumac contact dermatitis, insect bites, and other minor skin irritations — via hydrocortisone's corticosteroid mechanism, applied topically per label directions.

## Allergies / Warnings
Per label: external use only; avoid eyes; not for diaper rash; not for genital use with vaginal discharge; do not exceed recommended dosage; stop use and consult a doctor if condition worsens or symptoms persist beyond 7 days or recur within a few days. See [[hydrocortisone]] for full contraindication and safety data (bacterial/fungal infection masking, skin atrophy with prolonged use, pediatric HPA-axis sensitivity, systemic absorption variability by skin site).

## Symptoms Treated
Itching and inflammation from eczema, poison ivy/oak/sumac contact dermatitis, insect bites, psoriasis, seborrheic dermatitis, and other minor skin irritation.
