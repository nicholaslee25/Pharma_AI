## Appears In
Topical Anesthetics - 29%

## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed: "Salonpas Lidocaine" is a distinct sub-line from the methyl-salicylate-based "Salonpas" products already documented on [[Salonpas]] — this line uses **lidocaine HCl 4%** (maximum OTC strength) across multiple formats (Lidocaine Plus cream/liquid, which adds benzyl alcohol 10% as a second labeled "topical anesthetic"; FLEX Patch, wearable up to 8 hours; Gel-Patch for back/neck/shoulders/knees/elbows). Per [[topical-anesthetics]], lidocaine carries the substantially lower methemoglobinemia risk relative to benzocaine (~5% vs. ~66% of 242 reviewed case-report episodes). Since this line contains NO methyl salicylate, none of the NSAID-class systemic risks (stomach bleeding, salicylate allergy, pregnancy warning at 20+ weeks) documented on [[topical-counterirritants]] for the separate "Salonpas" methyl-salicylate line apply here — a genuinely different, non-overlapping safety profile despite the shared "Salonpas" brand name.

## Pros
- Confirmed FDA Drug Facts labels across multiple formats, all at the maximum OTC lidocaine strength (4%)
- Lidocaine-based (lower methemoglobinemia risk per [[topical-anesthetics]]) rather than benzocaine
- No methyl salicylate — avoids the NSAID-class systemic risks that apply to the separate methyl-salicylate "Salonpas" line, making this a genuinely safer option for aspirin/salicylate-sensitive users who still want a Salonpas product
- Category leader by a wide margin (29% share) in Topical Anesthetics
- Multiple formats (cream, liquid, patch, gel-patch) support different application preferences and body locations

## Cons
- Benzyl alcohol (in the Plus cream/liquid) is also labeled a "topical anesthetic" alongside lidocaine — its own independent safety/efficacy evidence is not separately documented in this database; flagged as a minor gap rather than a specific concern
- Label explicitly warns against use with a heating pad and on wounds/damaged skin — real limitations for treating already-compromised skin
- No brand-specific clinical trial for Salonpas Lidocaine specifically was identified beyond the general lidocaine ingredient-level evidence on [[topical-anesthetics]]
- A consumer should not confuse this line with the separate methyl-salicylate-based "Salonpas" products sharing the same core brand name — genuinely different active ingredients and risk profiles

## Use Cases
Temporary relief of pain (general, plus specifically marketed for back, neck, shoulders, knees, and elbows in the Gel-Patch format).

## Allergies / Warnings
Per label: do not use on wounds/damaged skin, in large quantities, with a heating pad, or if allergic to any ingredient; avoid eyes/mucous membranes/rashes; do not bandage tightly; stop use and ask a doctor if skin reactions occur (rash, itching, redness, irritation, pain, swelling, blistering), condition worsens, symptoms persist beyond 7 days, or recur; ask a health professional if pregnant/breastfeeding; keep from children, contact Poison Control if swallowed. Adults/children 12+ (Lidocaine Plus); under 12 consult a doctor.

## Symptoms Treated
Pain relief via lidocaine's sodium-channel-blocking local anesthetic mechanism — a numbing effect distinct from the counterirritant (cooling/warming sensory-masking) mechanism of the separate methyl-salicylate "Salonpas" line.
