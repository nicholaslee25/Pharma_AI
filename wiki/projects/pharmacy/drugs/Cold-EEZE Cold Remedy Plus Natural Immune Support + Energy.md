## Overview
Cold-EEZE (zinc gluconate glycine lozenges) has a genuinely mixed, real evidence picture — not a simple positive or negative. Multiple studies (including a retrospective chart review and an open-label phase IV trial) found real, statistically meaningful cold-duration reductions (e.g., 6.9 vs. 9.0 days in one cited comparison). However, this pass also found: (1) other independent research cited in the same search results found Cold-EEZE "had no effect on the duration or severity of symptoms in either the experimental or natural study model" — a direct, real contradiction of the positive studies; and (2) a real, confirmed class-action lawsuit (Gibbs v. ProPhase Labs, N.D. Cal., Case No. 3:15-cv-00865) alleging the product is "totally ineffective" despite being used as directed. This mixed picture is broadly consistent with [[zinc]]'s own evidence page, which — per this database's prior research — found zinc's cold-duration benefit is real but modest and highly dependent on timing (most effective only if started within 24-48 hours of symptom onset) and dose/formulation, meaning study-to-study variability in results is expected rather than surprising. Immune Support (6%) is covered by [[zinc]].

## Pros
- Real, positive clinical trial evidence exists (open-label phase IV trial, retrospective chart review) showing statistically significant cold-duration reduction with directed use
- Zinc gluconate is a specific, disclosed active ingredient with a real DailyMed-listed label — more label transparency than many "immune support" products in this database
- Zinc's cold-duration benefit (when effective) is consistent with [[zinc]]'s own evidence page, giving this product a real, if conditional, mechanistic basis

## Cons
- **Real, directly contradictory research exists**: at least one study found no effect on cold duration or severity versus control — this is not merely "some studies show more than others," it's a genuine positive/negative split in the evidence base for the same product
- **Real, confirmed class-action lawsuit alleging the product is "totally ineffective" as directed** — a serious, brand-specific efficacy-claim challenge, not a hypothetical concern
- Per [[zinc]]'s general evidence, timing is critical (must start within 24-48h of symptom onset) — a real, easy-to-miss usage requirement that could explain some of the negative-trial results if participants didn't dose early enough
- This pass did not resolve why some trials found benefit and others did not (e.g., differing zinc dose, lozenge dissolution rate, or population) — a genuine, unresolved evidence inconsistency
