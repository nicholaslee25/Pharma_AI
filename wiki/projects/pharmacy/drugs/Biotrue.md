## Overview
**Biotrue** (Bausch + Lomb) spans two genuinely different product families under one brand name, each relevant to a different EENT subcategory. **Biotrue Hydration Boost eye drops** (Artificial Tears/Ophthalmic Lubricants, 5% share): active ingredient **glycerin**, an FDA-recognized OTC ophthalmic lubricant, plus hyaluronan (HA) as a supporting moisturizer — per [[artificial-tears]]'s framing, glycerin is one of the recognized lubricant actives in this class, though it was not one of the specific ingredients individually outcome-compared in the ingredient page's cited Semp et al. 2023 review (which focused on PEG, CMC, HPMC, and liposome formulations). **Biotrue Multi-Purpose Solution** (Contact Lens Solutions, Saline, 30% share, second to [[OPTI-FREE Puremoist]]'s 45%): this is a Class II device lens-care disinfecting/cleaning/storage solution (21 CFR 886.5928), not an ophthalmic drug in the same sense — its function relies on an antimicrobial preservative/disinfectant system (not an "active ingredient" framed as therapeutic in the same regulatory sense as an eye drop) plus hyaluronan for lens wetting/comfort and a pH matched to natural tears. See [[contact-lens-solutions-saline]] for the category-level evidence, including the documented class-wide limitation that standard multipurpose solutions (Biotrue included, no brand-specific exemption identified) are not effective against Acanthamoeba despite passing standard ISO 14729 bacterial/fungal panel testing. No Biotrue-brand-specific clinical trial was found for either product line beyond general glycerin-lubricant and lens-solution-comfort literature.

## Pros
- **Eye drops:** glycerin is a recognized, FDA-monograph OTC ophthalmic lubricant active ingredient with an established safety record
- **Lens solution:** pH matched to natural tears and hyaluronan content are real, verifiable formulation features aimed at lens-wearer comfort, distinguishing it from purely disinfectant-focused competing solutions
- Single trusted brand name across both eye-drop and lens-care needs may simplify purchasing for contact lens wearers specifically

## Cons
- **Two unrelated product categories under one brand name** — a consumer must not assume the eye-drop line's ingredient profile (glycerin, a therapeutic lubricant) has anything to do with the lens-solution line's function (disinfection/cleaning, a device-adjacent product), and vice versa
- Biotrue eye drops' active ingredient (glycerin) was not one of the specific formulations individually outcome-ranked in [[artificial-tears]]'s cited systematic review (which covered PEG, CMC, HPMC, liposome types specifically) — so no direct comparative-evidence ranking is available for this particular active ingredient from that source
- No dedicated ingredient-evidence page exists yet in this project for contact lens care/disinfecting solutions as a category — a real research gap, not a Biotrue-specific one

## Use Cases
Eye drops: temporary relief of dry-eye discomfort for contact lens wearers and others. Lens solution: daily cleaning, disinfecting, rinsing, and storage of soft contact lenses.

## Allergies / Warnings
Eye drops: standard OTC lubricant cautions (discontinue and see a doctor for eye pain, vision changes, or symptoms persisting beyond 72 hours). Lens solution: never use lens solution as an eye drop or vice versa — the two Biotrue product lines are not interchangeable despite the shared brand name; follow lens-care instructions exactly to avoid microbial keratitis risk from improper lens hygiene (a general contact-lens-solution safety principle, not specific to this brand).

## Symptoms Treated
Eye drops: dry eye, burning, irritation from wind/sun/reduced tear production. Lens solution: not a symptom treatment — a lens hygiene/disinfection product.
