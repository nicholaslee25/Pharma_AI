## Appears In
Toe/Foot Antifungal Products - 4%

## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed: Desenex Antifungal Foot Powder and Antifungal Foot Cream both use **miconazole nitrate 2%**, an azole antifungal — see existing ingredient page [[miconazole-nitrate-topical]] (built in this database's Jock Itch/Antifungal Products research pass, citing a 129-trial/18,086-participant Cochrane review). This is a consistent single-active formulation across both checked SKUs, unlike several multi-mechanism brands elsewhere in this category ([[Lotrimin]], [[FUNGICURE]]). The powder format adds moisture-absorption/odor-control marketing claims (Triple Action Powder); the cream format adds "patented odor control technology" (Dual Action Cream) — both framed as supporting, non-active marketing features alongside the core miconazole active.

No independent Desenex-brand-specific clinical trial was located in this pass; the relevant evidence is the general miconazole nitrate literature on [[miconazole-nitrate-topical]].

## Pros
- Confirmed, consistent miconazole nitrate 2% formulation across both powder and cream SKUs checked in this pass
- Backed by a large Cochrane review (129 trials, 18,086 participants) at the ingredient-class level, per [[miconazole-nitrate-topical]]
- Powder format offers a genuine, distinct moisture-management use case alongside the antifungal action, relevant for tinea pedis where moisture control aids symptom management

## Cons
- No independent Desenex-brand-specific clinical trial was located; evidence is at the general miconazole-nitrate ingredient level, not independently confirmed for this specific brand's formulation
- Per the general azole-antifungal literature already documented elsewhere in this database: not effective for onychomycosis (nail fungus) or scalp infections
- Smaller share (4%) than this category's top azole/allylamine/benzylamine competitors ([[Lotrimin]] 39%, [[Lamisil]] 32%)
- "Patented odor control technology" and similar marketing claims were not independently evaluated for any efficacy benefit beyond the confirmed miconazole active

## Use Cases
Treatment of athlete's foot via miconazole nitrate's azole antifungal mechanism, with added moisture-absorption (powder) or odor-control (cream) supporting features.

## Allergies / Warnings
Per [[miconazole-nitrate-topical]]: external use only; avoid eyes and mucous membranes; discontinue and consult a doctor if irritation develops or no improvement within the labeled treatment duration; azole hypersensitivity consideration.

## Symptoms Treated
Itching, burning, cracking, and scaling from athlete's foot; moisture/odor management as a supporting (non-antifungal) benefit.
