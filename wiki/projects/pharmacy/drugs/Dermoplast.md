## Overview
Confirmed via multiple DailyMed listings: "Dermoplast" spans **at least two distinct formulations**, both benzocaine-based but combined with a different second ingredient depending on SKU: **Dermoplast Pain Relieving** (benzocaine 20% + menthol 0.5%/levomenthol — anesthetic + cooling counterirritant, no antimicrobial action) is the likely driver of its shares in Burn Treatments, Insect Bite and Sting Management, Sun Burn Relief, and Topical Anesthetics (four categories centered on pain/itch relief); **Dermoplast First Aid** (benzethonium chloride + benzocaine — antiseptic + anesthetic, explicitly labeled to "help prevent infection") is the likely driver of its separate Topical Antibiotics/Antiseptics share (4%). Both formulas use **benzocaine**, which per [[topical-anesthetics]] carries a real, well-documented, disproportionate methemoglobinemia risk relative to lidocaine (benzocaine involved in ~66% of 242 reviewed methemoglobinemia cases vs. ~5% for lidocaine) — meaning every Dermoplast SKU across all five categories it appears in carries this same class-level risk, unlike competitors such as [[Aspercreme with Lidocaine]] or [[Alocane]] that use lidocaine instead. Both formulations include an allergy-alert warning against use in patients with a history of allergy to "-caine" anesthetics.

## Pros
- Confirmed FDA Drug Facts labels for both identified formulations, with active ingredients clearly disclosed per SKU
- Fast, broad symptomatic relief (pain + itch, and in the First Aid SKU, infection-risk reduction) across five different minor-skin-injury use cases under one recognized brand
- Menthol addition (Pain Relieving SKU) provides a genuine secondary cooling-counterirritant mechanism alongside benzocaine's numbing effect, similar in concept to [[Biofreeze]]'s counterirritant action

## Cons
- **Every Dermoplast SKU identified uses benzocaine**, the anesthetic with the disproportionate, well-documented methemoglobinemia risk per [[topical-anesthetics]] — this risk applies broadly across all five of Dermoplast's category appearances, a meaningfully different safety profile than lidocaine-based competitors in the same categories (e.g., Alocane, Aspercreme with Lidocaine)
- "Dermoplast" spans at least two different active-ingredient combinations (Pain Relieving vs. First Aid) under one brand name — a consumer should check the specific product, not assume uniform ingredients across all five category appearances
- Per the methemoglobinemia review cited on [[topical-anesthetics]], "a single spray of benzocaine may induce methemoglobinemia" — not necessarily a misuse/overdose phenomenon — a real risk consideration for a spray-format product used repeatedly across multiple body sites/conditions
- No brand-specific clinical trial for Dermoplast specifically was identified beyond the general benzocaine/menthol ingredient-level evidence

## Use Cases
Temporary relief of pain and itching from sunburn, insect bites, minor cuts, minor burns, and minor skin irritation (Pain Relieving formula); first aid antiseptic plus pain/itch relief to help prevent infection in minor cuts, scrapes, and burns (First Aid formula).

## Allergies / Warnings
Per label: do not use if allergic to procaine, butacaine, benzocaine, or other "-caine" local anesthetics; for external use only; discontinue and consult a doctor if condition worsens or symptoms persist beyond 7 days. See [[topical-anesthetics]] for the full, quantified benzocaine methemoglobinemia risk data — this applies to every Dermoplast SKU.

## Symptoms Treated
Pain, burning, and itching from sunburn, insect bites, minor cuts/scrapes/burns; the First Aid SKU additionally targets infection-risk reduction via benzethonium chloride antisepsis.
