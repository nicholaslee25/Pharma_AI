## Overview
Astroglide Liquid is confirmed via the Edwards & Panay 2015 review to be **severely hyperosmotic at 5,848 mOsm/kg** — the highest osmolality of any product independently tested in that source, causing SEVERE irritation and tissue damage in the cited slug-mucosal-irritation assay, well above the WHO's 380 mOsm/kg ceiling and even above the generally-accepted 1,200 mOsm/kg practical upper limit. The manufacturer's own social-media statement (cited in secondary reporting) puts current Astroglide Liquid osmolality at 4,005 mmol/kg — still severely hyperosmotic by any standard, though somewhat lower than the 5,848/8,064 figures reported in independent testing, a real discrepancy between manufacturer-stated and independently-measured values worth noting rather than resolving. The company's own statement also confirms a reformulated Astroglide Ultra Gentle Gel at a substantially lower 1,010 mmol/kg — a real, if still-above-WHO-ceiling, improvement in that specific product line.

## Pros
- Astroglide Ultra Gentle Gel (per manufacturer's own reformulation statement) measures ~1,010 mmol/kg — notably lower than the standard Liquid formulation, a real within-brand improvement for consumers who select this specific variant
- Long market history and wide availability

## Cons
- **Astroglide Liquid is the single most severely hyperosmotic product independently tested among this category's brands** — 5,848 mOsm/kg (Edwards & Panay 2015), causing severe irritation/tissue damage in the cited testing, a materially worse result than even K-Y (2,463) or Replens (2,143)
- **A 2014 Fertility and Sterility study (SUNY Upstate) found Astroglide products had significant deleterious effects on sperm motility/health**, in direct contrast to Pre-Seed (tested in the same study), which showed no significant adverse effect — a real, brand-specific negative finding for anyone using this product while trying to conceive
- Real discrepancy between the manufacturer's own more recent stated osmolality figure (4,005 mmol/kg) and the independently published 5,848–8,064 mOsm/kg figures from other sources — this database cannot resolve which reflects the current retail formulation, and flags this as an open question rather than picking one number as authoritative
- Contains propylene glycol; research on hyperosmolar lubricants generally (with high propylene glycol content specifically implicated) links this combination to reduced vaginal epithelial barrier integrity and histological tissue damage in laboratory models

## Use Cases
Personal lubrication for sexual activity (per label); the "TTC" (trying to conceive) sub-line specifically markets a fertility-supportive use, which the cited 2014 study's findings directly contradict for Astroglide products tested.

## Allergies / Warnings
Hyperosmolar-formulation caution applies at its most severe level among this category's tested brands — see [[vaginal-lubricants-moisturizers]] for the epithelial-damage/infection-susceptibility mechanism. Not confirmed sperm-friendly; the cited fertility study found the opposite for products tested under the Astroglide name.

## Symptoms Treated
Vaginal dryness/friction during sexual activity (comfort use only) — not confirmed compatible with a fertility-supportive use case per the cited sperm-motility study.
