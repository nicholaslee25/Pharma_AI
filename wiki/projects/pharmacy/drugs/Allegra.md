## Overview
Confirmed: Allegra Allergy's DailyMed OTC label lists **fexofenadine HCl** as active ingredient — 60mg (12-hour tablet) or 180mg (24-hour tablet); a separate "Allegra Hives 24HR" SKU (180mg) is specifically labeled for hives/urticaria itching. This replaces the earlier hedged pointer. See [[second-generation-antihistamines]] for ingredient-level evidence (that page uses cetirizine as its representative agent, noting fexofenadine has its own separate StatPearls entry not independently pulled). Label saved at `raw/primary-sources/brands/allegra/dailymed-allegra.md`.

## Pros
- Available in both 12-hour (60mg BID) and 24-hour (180mg once daily) dosing options — more flexibility than single-dose-only competitors
- Separately FDA-labeled for hives/urticaria itch relief (Allegra Hives 24HR), not just hay fever symptoms
- Large safety database: FDA review cites ~18,361 subjects exposed to fexofenadine across 124 studies with a favorable safety profile

## Cons
- Must be taken with water, not fruit juice — apple, orange, and grapefruit juice significantly reduce fexofenadine absorption (an OATP transporter interaction), a dosing pitfall distinct from other antihistamines in this class
- No fexofenadine-specific head-to-head efficacy data against cetirizine/loratadine was found beyond the general class-level network meta-analysis on the ingredient page

## Use Cases
Seasonal allergic rhinitis (sneezing, runny nose, itchy watery eyes, nose/throat itching); chronic urticaria/hives itching (Allegra Hives 24HR specifically); pediatric perennial allergic rhinitis and atopic dermatitis per Sanofi-sponsored trials (NCT01244217, NCT01244230).

## Allergies / Warnings
Ask a doctor before use with kidney disease (fexofenadine is renally cleared). Avoid dosing with fruit juices. Contact Poison Control for overdose.

## Symptoms Treated
Sneezing, runny nose, itchy/watery eyes, nose/throat itching, hives/urticaria itching.
