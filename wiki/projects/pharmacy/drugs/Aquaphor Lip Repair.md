## Appears In
Lip Balms - 19%

## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed: "Aquaphor Lip Repair" spans **at least three distinct formulations** depending on SKU, all made by Beiersdorf. (1) **Aquaphor Lip Protectant and Sunscreen** — a full six-active combination: **petrolatum 31%** (skin protectant) plus a five-chemical sunscreen filter set, **avobenzone 3%, octinoxate 6.75%, octisalate 4.5%, octocrylene 2%, oxybenzone 5.4%**. (2) **Aquaphor Lip Repair Stick and Sunscreen** — a *different* sunscreen filter combination (avobenzone, homosalate, octisalate, octocrylene — no octinoxate or oxybenzone; percentages not confirmed in this pass), meaning two products both called "Lip Repair...and Sunscreen" use non-identical active-ingredient sets. (3) **Aquaphor Healing Balm Stick** — petrolatum ointment only, no sunscreen actives, consistent with the long-standing non-lip-specific Aquaphor Healing Ointment line.

This is category leader at 19% share; the source data does not distinguish which of these three formulations (or what mix) drives that share. Dedicated evidence pages for the five chemical sunscreen filter actives (avobenzone, octinoxate, octisalate, octocrylene, oxybenzone) have not yet been built in this database — that work is deferred to the dedicated Sunscreen subcategory research pass; see [[petrolatum]] for the skin-protectant component shared across all three SKUs.

No Aquaphor Lip Repair-brand-specific clinical trial was located in this pass.

## Pros
- Petrolatum-based skin-protectant component has strong general evidence (see [[petrolatum]] — comparable to topical antibiotic ointment for barrier/occlusive wound care, ~99% reduction in transepidermal water loss)
- SPF-containing SKUs add broad-spectrum sun protection to lip care, relevant since lips are a common, often-overlooked sunburn site
- Multiple format options (lotion, stick) let a consumer choose SPF vs. non-SPF depending on need

## Cons
- **Two different sunscreen-actives combinations exist under highly similar product names** ("Lip Protectant and Sunscreen" vs. "Lip Repair Stick and Sunscreen") — a real risk of consumer confusion about which specific chemical filters (and thus which allergens/photostability profile) a given tube actually contains
- Oxybenzone, present in one of the two SPF formulations, has drawn separate environmental/systemic-absorption scrutiny in the broader sunscreen literature (not evaluated in depth in this pass — reserved for the dedicated Sunscreen subcategory page)
- No Aquaphor Lip Repair-brand-specific clinical trial was located
- Source data's single 19% share figure cannot be broken out across the three distinct SKUs

## Use Cases
Skin protectant for chapped/cracked lips (petrolatum, all SKUs) and, on the two SPF-labeled SKUs, broad-spectrum sun protection for the lips (chemical sunscreen filters, formulation varies by specific product — see Overview).

## Allergies / Warnings
Per label(s): external use only; avoid eye contact; discontinue if irritation occurs. SPF SKUs carry standard chemical-sunscreen warnings and the FDA-mandated skin-cancer/aging risk-reduction language tied to consistent use with other sun-protection measures.

## Symptoms Treated
Chapped, cracked, or dry lips; sunburn prevention on the lips (SPF SKUs only).

## Brand-Specific Alignment Check
No wrong-ingredient error found, but a real internal-consistency flag: two same-brand "...and Sunscreen" products use different sunscreen filter combinations, which this database has not seen documented elsewhere and is noting here rather than assuming either is more "correct."
