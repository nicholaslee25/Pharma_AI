## Overview
**Major real finding: "Dulcolax" spans TWO mechanistically unrelated laxative types under one brand name.** Confirmed via DailyMed: the core Dulcolax product is bisacodyl 5mg (a stimulant laxative, matching [[bisacodyl]]'s existing sourcing exactly) — but the brand family ALSO includes "Dulcolax Stool Softener" and "Dulcolax Pink Stool Softener," both confirmed as **docusate sodium 100mg** (a surfactant stool softener, the same active ingredient as Colace, per [[docusate]]), with NO bisacodyl at all. This single brand name genuinely explains its own three-category appearance in this database: Laxatives, Stimulant (42%, the bisacodyl tablet), Laxatives, Nonfiber (27%, likely also bisacodyl-line SKUs), and Stool Softeners (17%, the docusate-based line) — a real, confirmed instance of the one-brand-multiple-mechanisms pattern, more consequential here than the same-brand-different-concentration pattern found elsewhere (Desitin, Rolaids), since bisacodyl and docusate are entirely different pharmacological classes with different evidence bases, onset times, and safety profiles.

## Pros
- Confirmed FDA labels for both the bisacodyl and docusate-sodium product lines; each individually matches its respective ingredient page's sourcing exactly ([[bisacodyl]], [[docusate]])
- Category-dominant share in Laxatives, Stimulant (42%) via the bisacodyl line — an FDA-approved, ACG/AGA-guideline-endorsed first-line stimulant laxative per [[bisacodyl]]
- Brand-family breadth allows a consumer familiar with "Dulcolax" to find a stimulant laxative, softener, or (per other market SKUs) suppository/enema format all under one trusted name

## Cons
- **A consumer picking "Dulcolax" off a shelf without checking whether it says "Stool Softener" would get a fundamentally different drug** — bisacodyl (stimulant, stimulates colonic nerve endings, per [[bisacodyl]]) vs. docusate (surfactant, softens stool via water/lipid penetration, per [[docusate]]) have different onset times (6-12h vs. 12-72h), different contraindications, and — per [[docusate]]'s own Efficacy Controversy section — genuinely different evidence quality: docusate has **no FDA-approved indication** and a 2021 review of 7 RCTs found no significant difference from placebo, while bisacodyl is FDA-approved and 2023 ACG/AGA-guideline-endorsed
- Per [[bisacodyl]]'s own Key Safety Data: the stimulant-laxative line carries StatPearls' own stated "potential carcinogenic risk" concern with use beyond 4 weeks, plus real risk of chronic colonic structural changes with regular overuse — risks that do NOT apply to the separate docusate-based Dulcolax Stool Softener line
- Per [[bisacodyl]]'s Drug Interactions: avoid taking bisacodyl within 1 hour of antacids/milk (premature enteric-coating dissolution) — a real, easily-missed administration-timing rule specific to the bisacodyl line only
- No Dulcolax-brand-specific RCT beyond the general bisacodyl/docusate evidence bases was independently located in this pass

## Use Cases
**Bisacodyl line:** relief of occasional constipation via a stimulant mechanism, onset 6-12h, FDA-approved first-line. **Stool Softener line (docusate):** easing difficult/painful defecation, an off-label-indication use per [[docusate]]'s own "no FDA-approved indication" finding, onset 12-72h — genuinely different use cases requiring the consumer to select the correct product for their actual need.

## Allergies / Warnings
**Bisacodyl line** per [[bisacodyl]]: do not chew/crush tablets (destroys enteric coating); avoid within 1h of antacids/milk; not recommended beyond 4 weeks of use; contraindicated in acute GI diseases, bowel obstruction, severe dehydration/electrolyte imbalance. **Docusate line** per [[docusate]]: do not use with mineral oil unless directed by a doctor; contraindicated with nausea/vomiting, intestinal obstruction, acute abdominal pain.

## Symptoms Treated
Occasional constipation — via TWO distinct mechanisms depending on which Dulcolax product is selected: stimulant (bisacodyl, per [[bisacodyl]]) or surfactant/softener (docusate, per [[docusate]]).
