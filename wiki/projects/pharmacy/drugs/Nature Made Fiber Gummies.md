## Overview
Confirmed via manufacturer product information: Nature Made's fiber gummy line uses **inulin (chicory root fiber)** as its fiber source (the "Prebiotic Fiber Gummies" line provides 6g fiber/serving for adults; a separate "Probiotic + Prebiotic" variant adds Bacillus coagulans). Per [[fiber]]'s own reviewed 2022 meta-analysis, inulin is **not one of the two fiber types (psyllium, pectin) shown to have a statistically significant effect on constipation** — this is the same non-significant-fiber-type pattern this database's research already confirmed for Benefiber, Citrucel, FiberCon, Mirafiber, Vitafusion Fiber Well, and Phillips' Fiber Good. Inulin does have its own separate, real evidence base as a prebiotic (fermenting in the colon to feed beneficial bacteria and produce short-chain fatty acids) distinct from the bulk-laxative mechanism the meta-analysis tested — meaning this product may have genuine prebiotic/gut-microbiome value even though it isn't the specific fiber type shown to significantly relieve constipation in [[fiber]]'s cited review. Fiber Supplements (7%) is covered by [[fiber]].

## Pros
- Gummy format may improve adherence for users who dislike fiber powders/psyllium's gel-forming texture
- Inulin has a real, separate prebiotic evidence base (feeding beneficial gut bacteria, short-chain fatty acid production) distinct from — and not covered by — [[fiber]]'s constipation-specific meta-analysis
- Backed by [[Nature Made]]'s otherwise strong USP-Verified quality infrastructure (see that page), though the mark's product-specific coverage for this exact SKU was not confirmed

## Cons
- **Confirmed to use inulin, not psyllium or pectin — the two fiber types [[fiber]]'s own cited 2022 meta-analysis found to have a statistically significant constipation-relief effect** — the same non-significant-type pattern found across the majority of brands in this category (Benefiber, Citrucel, FiberCon, Mirafiber, Vitafusion Fiber Well, Phillips' Fiber Good all confirmed different, non-significant types in this database's prior research)
- Gummy format delivers less total fiber per serving-unit than powder forms typically require multiple gummies to reach a meaningful dose, and inulin/prebiotic fibers are separately associated with more gas/bloating at higher intakes, a real practical tradeoff
- No Nature-Made-Fiber-Gummies-specific clinical trial was located — efficacy evidence is at the general-ingredient level, and that evidence specifically does not favor this product's fiber type for the constipation-relief use case its category placement implies

## Use Cases
Prebiotic fiber/gut-microbiome support (inulin's own separate evidence base) — not confirmed effective for constipation relief specifically per [[fiber]]'s cited meta-analysis, which found only psyllium and pectin (not inulin) statistically significant for that indication.

## Allergies / Warnings
Inulin/prebiotic fibers commonly cause gas, bloating, and cramping, especially at higher doses or with sudden introduction — a real, common tolerability issue distinct from psyllium's own flatulence finding in [[fiber]]. Check the product's full ingredient list for gelatin/gluten-free claims.

## Symptoms Treated
