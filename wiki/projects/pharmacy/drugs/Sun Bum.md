## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed: Sun Bum's lip-balm line spans **at least three different active-ingredient combinations across flavor/format variants** — a real, confirmed inconsistency, not an assumption. **Sunscreen Lip Balm SPF 30 (Coconut)**: avobenzone 3%, homosalate 10%, octocrylene 8%, octisalate 5%. A **different Sun Bum lip balm SKU** (flavor/version not fully distinguished in this pass): avobenzone 2%, homosalate 15%, octinoxate 7.5%, octisalate 5% — a materially different filter set and concentration profile from the Coconut version despite both being labeled "Sunscreen Lip Balm Broad Spectrum SPF 30." **Sun Bum Signature Mineral Sunscreen Lip Balm**: **zinc oxide 8%** as the sole active — a physical/mineral filter, mechanistically unrelated to the chemical-filter SKUs. This is the same "same product name, different chemical filter combinations" pattern already flagged for [[Aquaphor Lip Repair]] in this subcategory, but with a third, mineral-mechanism variant added on top.

**Sunscreen category (6% share) — researched 2026-08-27, new ingredient pages [[chemical-sunscreen-filters]] and [[mineral-sunscreen-filters]] built for this pass:** Sun Bum's core body/face sunscreen line (SPF 30 Premium Moisturizing Sunscreen Lotion/Spray, Glow 30, Daily Sunscreen Moisturizer, Daily Sunscreen Face Mist) uses chemical filters — confirmed formulations include **avobenzone 3%, homosalate 5-10%, octisalate 3-5%, octocrylene 10%** (exact percentages vary slightly by SKU). **A real, confirmed brand-specific discrepancy: "Sun Bum Daily Mineral Sunscreen Moisturizer SPF30" contains NO zinc oxide or titanium dioxide at all** — its Drug Facts panel lists the identical chemical-filter combination as the brand's non-mineral products (avobenzone 3%, homosalate 10%, octisalate 5%, octocrylene 10%). This is a genuine product-naming discrepancy, not an assumption: a consumer specifically seeking a mineral/physical sunscreen (for chemical-filter sensitivity, pregnancy caution, or reef-safety reasons — see [[chemical-sunscreen-filters]]) would be misled by this specific product's name into buying an all-chemical-filter formulation.

No Sun Bum-brand-specific clinical trial was located in this pass; see [[chemical-sunscreen-filters]] and [[mineral-sunscreen-filters]] for the general ingredient-class evidence (systemic absorption data, GRASE status, skin-cancer-prevention RCT evidence).

## Pros
- Offers both chemical-filter and genuine mineral (zinc-oxide-based, on the lip-balm Signature line) sunscreen mechanisms across its broader product range, giving consumers a choice for chemical-filter sensitivity — when the labeling is accurate (see Cons)
- All checked SKUs across both Lip Balms and Sunscreen are broad-spectrum SPF 30, a solid, consistent SPF level across the line
- Body/face sunscreen line's chemical-filter combination is a standard, FDA-monograph-compliant formulation with no unusual or non-standard actives

## Cons
- **Confirmed same-name, different-actives inconsistency in Lip Balms**: two SKUs both labeled "Sunscreen Lip Balm Broad Spectrum SPF 30" use different filter combinations and concentrations
- **Confirmed, more significant discrepancy in Sunscreen**: "Sun Bum Daily Mineral Sunscreen Moisturizer SPF30" contains no zinc oxide or titanium dioxide — it is chemically identical to the brand's non-mineral chemical-filter products despite the "Mineral" name, a real, brand-specific labeling-accuracy concern
- The core chemical-filter combination (avobenzone, homosalate, octisalate, octocrylene) carries the class-wide systemic-absorption findings and FDA GRASE data-gap status documented on [[chemical-sunscreen-filters]]
- No Sun Bum-brand-specific clinical trial was located for either the lip-balm or body/face sunscreen lines

## Use Cases
Lip Balms: broad-spectrum SPF 30 sun protection for lips (chemical-filter combination varies by SKU, or a genuine zinc-oxide-8% mineral formulation on the Signature line). Sunscreen: broad-spectrum SPF 30 body/face sun protection via chemical UV filters (avobenzone/homosalate/octisalate/octocrylene) across all checked SKUs, including the misleadingly-named "Daily Mineral" product.

## Allergies / Warnings
Standard chemical-sunscreen-filter warnings apply (see [[chemical-sunscreen-filters]] for the ingredient-class safety/absorption picture). The lip-balm Signature mineral (zinc oxide) variant carries standard mineral-sunscreen precautions instead (see [[mineral-sunscreen-filters]]).

## Symptoms Treated
Sunburn prevention on lips and body/face.

## Brand-Specific Alignment Check
**Two real discrepancies found and flagged**: (1) identically-branded "Sunscreen Lip Balm Broad Spectrum SPF 30" products use different active-ingredient combinations depending on flavor/SKU; (2) more significantly, "Sun Bum Daily Mineral Sunscreen Moisturizer SPF30" is not actually a mineral sunscreen at all — its Drug Facts panel confirms only chemical filters, identical to the brand's standard (non-"Mineral") sunscreen moisturizer.
