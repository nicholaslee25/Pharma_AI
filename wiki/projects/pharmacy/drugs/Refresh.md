## Overview
Confirmed: Refresh Tears' (Allergan/AbbVie) own FDA OTC Drug Facts label (DailyMed) lists **carboxymethylcellulose sodium 0.5%** as the sole active ingredient — this is in fact the exact representative label already pulled and cited on [[artificial-tears]] (raw/primary-sources/artificial-tears/dailymed-refresh-tears.md), so this brand page confirms rather than newly discovers that link. A preservative-free variant, Refresh Tears PF, adds glycerin 0.9% as a second active ingredient. Per the Semp et al. 2023 systematic review cited on [[artificial-tears]], CMC/carmellose-based tears (Refresh's core formulation) were found to underperform PEG-containing tears (like [[Systane]]'s) in comparative outcomes — a real, source-supported formulation-tier distinction between the two largest brands in this category, not a criticism invented here. No Refresh-brand-specific clinical trial beyond the general CMC-formulation evidence already on [[artificial-tears]] was identified in a targeted search, though a company-sponsored trial registry entry (NCT01294384, "Safety and Efficacy of New Eye Drop Formulations With Refresh Tears") exists comparing newer formulations against Refresh Tears as an active comparator — its results were not retrieved in this pass.

## Pros
- Single, well-characterized active ingredient (CMC sodium 0.5%) with a long market history and a directly-pulled FDA label (no formulation ambiguity for the core Refresh Tears product, unlike some competitors with multiple divergent sub-formulations)
- Preservative-free option (Refresh Tears PF) available for patients needing to avoid preservative-related ocular surface irritation
- Second-largest recommendation share in the category (23%), reflecting long-standing brand recognition

## Cons
- Per [[artificial-tears]]'s cited systematic review, CMC/carmellose-based single-ingredient tears (Refresh's core formulation) underperformed PEG-containing and combination-ingredient tears in the underlying comparative evidence — a real formulation-tier disadvantage relative to [[Systane]], not a fabricated one
- Symptom improvement generally requires about a month of regular, consistent use per the ingredient-page evidence — not an instant-relief product
- No independent brand-specific outcome trial isolates Refresh's real-world performance from the general CMC-ingredient evidence

## Use Cases
Temporary relief of burning, irritation, and discomfort due to dryness of the eye or exposure to wind or sun; general dry-eye lubrication.

## Allergies / Warnings
Per label: no specific contraindication beyond general external-use-only precautions; discontinue and see a doctor for eye pain, vision changes, continued redness/irritation, or symptoms persisting beyond 72 hours.

## Symptoms Treated
Dry eye burning, irritation, discomfort from wind/sun exposure or reduced tear production.
