## Appears In

## Overview
**Discrepancy resolved and flagged:** Confirmed via DailyMed — **Excedrin Extra Strength lists the identical active-ingredient formulation as Excedrin Migraine**: acetaminophen 250 mg + aspirin 250 mg (NSAID) + caffeine 65 mg per caplet. This is not a coincidence or approximation — DailyMed's own filing groups "Excedrin Extra Strength and Excedrin Tension Headache" together as a combined "kit" listing, and separately lists a chemically identical "Excedrin Migraine Relief." The FDA-approved "for migraine" indication specifically (the only OTC product with this indication, granted 1998) is a labeling/marketing distinction layered onto what is, per active ingredients, the same triple-combination product sold under at least three different Excedrin sub-brand names (Extra Strength, Tension Headache, Migraine). This resolves the prior hedge on this page ("presumed to share the... formulation... not independently confirmed") into a confirmed finding, and is cross-referenced on [[acetaminophen]] and [[aspirin]] as a real "same formulation, multiple brand names" instance.

## Pros
- Confirmed FDA Drug Facts label combining three independently well-evidenced active ingredients — acetaminophen and aspirin each carry substantial ingredient-class Cochrane evidence per [[acetaminophen]] and [[aspirin]], and the specific triple combination has its own dedicated brand-level RCT evidence (see Excedrin Migraine page)
- Confirming the formulation is identical across Extra Strength/Tension Headache/Migraine sub-brands means a consumer isn't missing out on a "stronger" formulation by choosing the cheaper non-migraine-labeled version for a headache — same active ingredients, same doses
- Largest single-brand share of Headache Products in this database's data after Tylenol and Advil (19%)

## Cons
- **The Extra Strength/Tension Headache/Migraine naming implies differentiated products for different severity/type of headache, when the underlying medicine is chemically identical** — a real, brand-specific instance of this database's recurring "one formulation, multiple marketed names" pattern (same family as Parodontax's toothpaste-vs-mouthwash split, in the opposite direction: here it's one formulation marketed as many products, not one brand with many formulations)
- Carries combined risk profile of THREE active ingredients: acetaminophen's hepatotoxicity risk, aspirin's GI-bleed/Reye's-syndrome/antiplatelet risk, and caffeine's own stimulant effects (each dose contains roughly as much caffeine as a cup of coffee, per label) — a more complex safety profile than a single-ingredient product
- Severe liver damage warning applies at >8 caplets/24h per label — combination-product acetaminophen overlap risk (per [[acetaminophen]]) is especially relevant here since a consumer might not realize this contains acetaminophen at all if focused on the "Excedrin" brand name alone

## Use Cases
Headache pain relief (tension-type and general), leveraging the same acetaminophen + aspirin + caffeine combination FDA-approved for migraine specifically under the Excedrin Migraine name — see that page for the dedicated migraine trial evidence.

## Allergies / Warnings
Per label: contains an NSAID (aspirin) — may cause severe stomach bleeding; severe liver damage may occur with more than 8 caplets in 24 hours, use with other acetaminophen-containing products, or 3+ alcoholic drinks/day; reduce caffeine intake from other sources while using (each dose ≈ one cup of coffee's worth of caffeine); Reye's syndrome risk in children/teenagers with viral illness (aspirin-specific, per [[aspirin]]).

## Symptoms Treated
Headache (tension-type and general), and — per the identical formulation sold as Excedrin Migraine — migraine pain, nausea, photophobia, and phonophobia.
