## Appears In
Antibacterial Soaps - 25%

## Overview
Dial (Henkel Corporation) is confirmed via DailyMed as spanning **two distinct antibacterial actives plus one discontinued historical active**:

- **Bar soaps** (Dial Advanced Clean Antibacterial Gold, Dial Antibacterial and Sensitive Hypoallergenic Bar, Dial Antibacterial with Vitamins) — confirmed **benzalkonium chloride 0.10%**, per [[benzalkonium-chloride]].
- **Liquid hand wash** (Dial Antibacterial Hand Wash) — confirmed **benzethonium chloride 0.1%**, a different (though related, quaternary-ammonium-class) active from the bar-soap line — a genuine within-brand mechanism split.
- **Historical/discontinued**: "Dial Gold Bar Antibacterial Deodorant Soap" used **triclocarban 0.60%**. Confirmed via DailyMed label metadata that this SKU's NDC is now **inactivated** ("This SPL contains inactivated NDCs listed by the FDA initiated compliance action"), consistent with triclocarban's inclusion in the FDA's September 2016 ban of 19 antiseptic-wash active ingredients — see [[benzalkonium-chloride]] for the full regulatory-history discussion.

## Pros
- Reformulated away from triclocarban following the FDA's 2016 ban — the current bar-soap line uses one of the three ingredients the FDA specifically deferred (not banned) rather than one it banned outright
- Confirmed concentrations (0.10% bar / 0.1% liquid) are consistent with what other brands in this category use
- Simple, uniform directions across the confirmed bar-soap SKUs (wet, lather, rinse)

## Cons
- **Benzalkonium chloride carries no final FDA GRASE determination** for antiseptic-wash use, per [[benzalkonium-chloride]] — Dial's flagship bar-soap active remains in the same unresolved regulatory category nearly a decade after the 2016 deferral, a fact easy for a consumer to miss given the product is openly marketed and sold as "antibacterial"
- **Two different actives (benzalkonium chloride vs. benzethonium chloride) across bar vs. liquid formats** under one brand name — not flagged anywhere in consumer-facing marketing located this pass
- No Dial-brand-specific clinical trial was located in this pass — evidence is entirely ingredient-level (and for benzalkonium chloride specifically, that ingredient-level evidence is itself thin for wash-off formulations, see [[benzalkonium-chloride]])
- The historical triclocarban formulation's discontinuation was confirmed only via DailyMed NDC-inactivation metadata, not an explicit public Henkel recall announcement — treated as a regulatory-compliance-driven reformulation, not a customer-facing recall, though the underlying reason (2016 ban) is well documented

## Use Cases
Daily hand/body washing intended to reduce surface bacteria, per Drug Facts wording ("for washing to decrease the bacteria on the skin") — general hygiene use, not a substitute for medical antisepsis.

## Allergies / Warnings
Per label (bar soap): for external use only; avoid contact with eyes (flush with water if contact occurs); stop use and ask a doctor if irritation or redness develops; keep out of reach of children; contact Poison Control if swallowed. Liquid hand wash (benzethonium chloride) not independently re-checked for warning-language differences in this pass, but is expected to carry a similar profile per [[benzalkonium-chloride]].

## Symptoms Treated
Not a treatment for an existing condition — a preventive hygiene product intended to reduce bacterial load on skin during normal washing.

## Brand-Specific Alignment Check
**Confirmed multi-active finding:** Dial spans benzalkonium chloride (bar), benzethonium chloride (liquid), and a discontinued triclocarban formulation (historical), all under one brand name. Both currently-marketed actives carry real, documented regulatory-status caveats — benzalkonium chloride's unresolved GRASE status is the central, database-wide finding on [[benzalkonium-chloride]]; benzethonium chloride was not independently deep-researched to the same depth in this pass and is flagged as a gap.
