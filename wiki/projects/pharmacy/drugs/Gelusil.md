## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed: Gelusil chewable tablets contain **aluminum hydroxide dried gel 200mg + magnesium hydroxide 200mg + simethicone 25mg** — the same three-ingredient antacid-plus-antigas combination class confirmed for Mylanta in this same research pass, though at lower per-tablet doses than Mylanta's liquid (200mg/200mg/25mg per tablet vs. 800mg/800mg/80mg per 10mL dose). A suspension formulation also exists at higher per-30mL doses (1,200mg/1,200mg/120mg, or a "double strength" 2,400mg/2,400mg/240mg). Like Mylanta, this explains Gelusil's dual-category appearance (Antacids 3%, Antiflatulence Products 3%) — the simethicone component makes it genuinely both an antacid and antigas product, unlike Tums.

## Pros
- Confirmed FDA OTC label; combined antacid ([[antacids]]) plus antigas ([[simethicone]]) mechanism addresses heartburn and gas/bloating together, same rationale as Mylanta
- Chewable tablet format offers a lower, more titratable per-unit dose (200mg/200mg/25mg) than the liquid Mylanta formulation, which may suit users wanting finer dose control

## Cons
- **Not calcium-carbonate-based despite sitting in the same Antacids category as Tums** — the same mismatch pattern confirmed for Gaviscon and Mylanta in this research pass
- Per [[antacids]]: aluminum-hydroxide component carries real toxicity risk in renal failure/infants; magnesium-hydroxide component can bind tetracycline/fluoroquinolone antibiotics
- Per [[simethicone]]: the antigas component's evidence base is a single old (1974), small (n=41) positive trial, with an explicit negative finding for infant colic (not this product's marketed use, but relevant context)
- Real formulation spread across at least three strength tiers (tablet 200/200/25mg, suspension 1,200/1,200/120mg, "double strength" suspension 2,400/2,400/240mg) under one brand name — this database's 3%/3% recommendation-share data does not specify which formulation it refers to, consistent with the one-brand-multiple-formulations pattern flagged repeatedly across this database
- No Gelusil-specific RCT beyond the general antacid/simethicone evidence base was independently located in this pass

## Use Cases
Relief of heartburn, acid indigestion, sour stomach, plus gas/bloating pressure — same dual-mechanism use case as Mylanta, at a real, quantified lower per-unit dose in the tablet formulation.

## Allergies / Warnings
Per label: do not exceed 12 tablets/24h; do not use maximum dosage for more than 2 weeks. Ask a doctor/pharmacist before use if taking a prescription drug. Pregnant/breastfeeding: ask a health professional. Per [[antacids]]: caution in renal failure and with tetracycline/fluoroquinolone antibiotics.

## Symptoms Treated
