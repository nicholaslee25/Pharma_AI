## Appears In

## Overview
DayQuil is a multi-SKU line, not one formulation. Confirmed via DailyMed: **Vicks DayQuil Severe Cold and Flu** (liquid) = acetaminophen 325mg + dextromethorphan HBr 10mg + guaifenesin 200mg + phenylephrine HCl 5mg per 15mL; other DayQuil Cold and Flu SKUs (capsule/liquid) drop guaifenesin and use just acetaminophen + dextromethorphan + phenylephrine. This is a new ingredient confirmation (no prior pointer existed) touching four ingredient pages at once: [[acetaminophen]], [[dextromethorphan]], [[guaifenesin]], and [[oral-phenylephrine]]. Label saved at `raw/primary-sources/brands/dayquil/dailymed-dayquil.md`.

## Pros
- Covers four symptom categories (pain/fever, cough, mucus, congestion) in one non-drowsy daytime product — no antihistamine/sedative component, unlike its NyQuil nighttime counterpart
- Widely recognized brand with long market history and predictable dosing intervals

## Cons
- Acetaminophen dose (325mg per 15mL serving) means a user must track total daily intake carefully against the 4,000mg/day ceiling on [[acetaminophen]], especially if combining with a separate pain reliever
- Oral phenylephrine's efficacy at standard OTC doses has been publicly questioned by an FDA advisory committee (2023) as not statistically better than placebo — see [[oral-phenylephrine]] for the Hatton et al. meta-analysis if it addresses this
- Guaifenesin's own brand-specific RCT evidence on [[guaifenesin]] (Bennett 2015) was conducted at 1200mg (Mucinex dose) — DayQuil's 200mg guaifenesin per serving is a much smaller dose than what that trial tested, so that evidence should not be assumed to transfer directly to DayQuil's formulation
- Multi-ingredient product makes isolating the source of any side effect harder

## Use Cases
Daytime multi-symptom cold/flu relief: fever, body aches, cough, chest congestion (guaifenesin SKU only), nasal congestion.

## Allergies / Warnings
Do not use with other acetaminophen-containing products. Ask a doctor before use with liver disease, high blood pressure, heart disease, thyroid disease, diabetes, or trouble urinating due to enlarged prostate (phenylephrine-related cautions). Contains no antihistamine, so no drowsiness warning specific to this daytime formulation.

## Symptoms Treated
Fever, body aches, cough, chest congestion (select SKUs), nasal/sinus congestion.

## Brand-Specific Alignment Check
No contradiction found — each individual active ingredient's dose and warnings in DayQuil's label are consistent with the corresponding ingredient pages. The main actionable note is that DayQuil's guaifenesin dose (200mg) is well below the dose used in the ingredient page's own cited RCT (1200mg, Mucinex-specific) — worth keeping in mind when reading across from that evidence to this product.
