## Overview
**Discrepancy flagged — "Arthritis" in the product name refers to packaging, not formulation:** confirmed via manufacturer/retailer sources that Advil Easy Open Arthritis Cap is **standard Advil ibuprofen 200 mg coated tablets** — the identical active ingredient, dose, and formulation as regular Advil (per `drugs/Advil.md` and [[ibuprofen]]). The ONLY difference is the bottle cap design: an easier-to-open "faucet" cap that received an Ease-of-Use Commendation from the Arthritis Foundation, specifically intended to help people with arthritis-impaired hand dexterity open the bottle. This is a genuinely different kind of "Arthritis" product claim than `drugs/Tylenol 8 HR Arthritis Pain.md`, which IS a distinct extended-release 650mg formulation — a consumer should not assume "Advil Easy Open Arthritis Cap" contains a different dose, a slower-release mechanism, or any arthritis-specific pharmacological advantage over standard Advil. Notably, per manufacturer marketing, **Aleve also sells its own "Easy Open Arthritis Cap" product** — the same packaging-accessibility approach exists across at least two competing brands in this category.

## Pros
- Confirmed FDA Drug Facts label identical to standard Advil — carries the same well-established NSAID evidence base per [[ibuprofen]] (large-scale Cochrane meta-analytic evidence, NNT 2.5-2.7 for acute pain)
- The accessible cap design is a genuine, real-world usability benefit for arthritis patients with reduced hand dexterity or grip strength — an Arthritis Foundation-commended feature, not an empty marketing claim
- Second-largest share of Oral Arthritis Pain Relievers (18%) in this database, despite being chemically identical to standard Advil rather than a specialized arthritis formulation

## Cons
- **The product name could reasonably mislead a consumer into expecting an arthritis-specific formulation, dose, or extended-release mechanism** — it is none of those; it is standard-dose ibuprofen in an accessible bottle. Contrast directly with Tylenol 8 HR Arthritis Pain, which genuinely is a different formulation (650mg extended-release bi-layer tablet)
- Carries the full ibuprofen risk profile documented on [[ibuprofen]]: boxed warnings for cardiovascular thrombotic events and GI bleeding, contraindicated perioperatively for CABG, aspirin-interaction timing issue — none of these risks are mitigated or altered by the accessible-cap packaging
- No arthritis-specific dosing guidance beyond standard ibuprofen OTC dosing (1 tablet every 4-6 hours, ages 12+) — this is not a higher-strength or longer-acting arthritis product, despite the category placement
- No independent brand-specific RCT was identified for this specific packaging variant, unsurprisingly, since it is not a distinct drug product

## Use Cases
General analgesia and anti-inflammatory relief — headache, muscle aches, backache, menstrual pain, minor arthritis and other joint pain, common cold aches — identical use case to standard Advil, packaged for easier access.

## Allergies / Warnings
Identical to standard Advil per [[ibuprofen]]: NSAID stomach-bleeding risk, severe allergic reaction risk especially in aspirin-allergic individuals, cardiovascular thrombotic risk, CABG contraindication, aspirin-interaction timing.

## Symptoms Treated
Headache, muscle aches, backache, menstrual pain, minor arthritis and joint pain, common cold-associated aches — same as standard Advil, no arthritis-specific symptom targeting beyond what standard ibuprofen already provides.
