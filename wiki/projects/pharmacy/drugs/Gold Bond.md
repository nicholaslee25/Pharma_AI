## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed (labeler: Chattem, Inc., a Sanofi subsidiary): the bare "Gold Bond" brand spans **at least three genuinely different active-ingredient mechanisms** across its four tracked categories — a wide "one brand, multiple mechanisms" spread, even by this database's own recurring-pattern standard:

- **Foot Care Products (15%) and Topical Poison Ivy/Oak Remedies (1%):** driven by the flagship **Medicated Powder** line — **menthol 1%** (Maximum Strength) as an anti-itch/external-analgesic counterirritant (see [[topical-counterirritants]]), talc-free cornstarch-based formula. The Moisturizers (3%) share is driven by **Medicated Lotion** — menthol 0.5% (Extra Strength) or menthol 0.5% + pramoxine hydrochloride 1% (Medicated/Intensive Relief Anti-Itch Lotion, adding a topical anesthetic to the counterirritant).
- **Jock Itch/Antifungal Products (9%): a real, confirmed brand-internal mechanism split.** Gold Bond markets a genuine antifungal **Anti-Fungal Spray (miconazole nitrate 2%)** and Jock Itch Cream/Powder line (also miconazole-nitrate-based per this pass's search), which actually treats the fungal infection — but the same "Gold Bond" name most consumers associate with jock itch relief is also carried by the **flagship Medicated Powder (menthol only, zero antifungal activity)**, which is explicitly non-antifungal and only provides moisture control and itch/comfort masking. **A consumer buying "Gold Bond" for jock itch could get an actual antifungal treatment or a menthol-only comfort product with no antifungal effect whatsoever, depending on which specific SKU they select** — a genuinely serious mechanism gap for an infection-category product, not merely a concentration difference.

No Gold-Bond-brand-specific clinical trial was located for any SKU in this pass; evidence is at the [[topical-counterirritants]] (menthol) and general miconazole-nitrate-antifungal-monograph level.

## Pros
- Menthol-based Foot Care/Moisturizer/Poison-Ivy-Oak lines have a confirmed, consistent FDA Drug Facts label and a real (if sensory-masking, not curative) counterirritant mechanism per [[topical-counterirritants]]
- The dedicated Anti-Fungal Spray/Jock Itch Cream (miconazole nitrate 2%) is a genuine, standard-concentration antifungal — an appropriate, actually-curative choice when that specific SKU is selected
- Pramoxine-containing Intensive Relief lotion adds a genuine topical anesthetic (see [[topical-anesthetics]]) alongside the counterirritant, offering a second, complementary itch-relief mechanism for consumers who need it
- Talc-free cornstarch reformulation of the foot powder addresses a real, independently-documented talc/asbestos-contamination and inhalation-safety concern that has affected the broader powder category historically (not specific to Gold Bond, but a real positive formulation choice)

## Cons
- **Confirmed real mechanism gap in the Jock Itch/Antifungal Products category: the flagship "Gold Bond" Medicated Powder most associated with the brand name contains no antifungal ingredient at all (menthol only)** — a consumer treating an actual fungal infection with the wrong Gold Bond SKU would get itch/moisture comfort but zero antifungal treatment, allowing the underlying infection to persist or worsen. This is a more consequential version of the "one brand, multiple mechanisms" pattern than most other examples in this database, because an actual infection (not just a symptom) is at stake
- No brand-specific clinical trial exists for any SKU; evidence is entirely at the ingredient-class level ([[topical-counterirritants]] for menthol, general OTC monograph for miconazole nitrate)
- Menthol concentrations vary across the line (0.5% lotion to 1% powder) without a unifying "Gold Bond = X%" identity
- Per [[topical-counterirritants]]: FDA's 2012 burn-risk communication flagged menthol above 3% as a risk factor for more severe burns — Gold Bond's menthol concentrations (0.5-1%) are well below this threshold, a genuine safety positive worth noting explicitly given how much higher other menthol-containing brands in this database run (Biofreeze 10-10.5%, BENGAY up to 10%)

## Use Cases
Foot Care/Moisturizers/Poison Ivy-Oak: temporary relief of itching and minor skin irritation via menthol's cooling counterirritant effect (+ pramoxine's topical anesthesia in the Intensive Relief lotion). Jock Itch/Antifungal: either genuine antifungal treatment of tinea cruris (Anti-Fungal Spray/Cream/Powder, miconazole nitrate 2%) or comfort-only moisture/itch control with no antifungal effect (flagship Medicated Powder) — the specific SKU determines which use case actually applies.

## Allergies / Warnings
Menthol lines: external use only; avoid eyes/mucous membranes; do not apply to wounds or damaged skin; do not use with a heating pad; discontinue if condition worsens or symptoms persist beyond 7 days. Miconazole nitrate lines: external use only; avoid eye contact; not for children under 2 without doctor approval; discontinue and consult a doctor if irritation occurs or no improvement within 2 weeks (jock itch)/4 weeks (athlete's foot/ringworm); not effective on scalp or nails.

## Symptoms Treated
Itching, minor skin irritation, and chafing (menthol-based Foot Care/Moisturizer/Poison-Ivy-Oak lines); actual fungal infection (tinea cruris/jock itch) specifically via the miconazole-nitrate-based Anti-Fungal Spray/Cream/Powder SKUs only, not the flagship Medicated Powder.

## Brand-Specific Alignment Check
