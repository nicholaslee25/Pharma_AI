## Overview
St. Joseph Low Dose Aspirin is a conventional 81mg enteric-coated aspirin tablet — the standard cardioprotective/secondary-prevention dose, per [[aspirin]]'s full evidence base. It shares an equal 18% share with [[Ecotrin]] in this category, and no brand-specific trial or formulation difference distinguishing it from standard 81mg enteric-coated aspirin (including Ecotrin's own 81mg product and Bayer's Aspirin Regimen Low Dose) was located in this pass — the three are, on the evidence located here, functionally equivalent generic-class products under different brand names.

## Pros
- Long-established, historically significant brand specifically associated with low-dose aspirin therapy (originally marketed as a children's aspirin brand decades ago, now repositioned around the low-dose cardioprotective use, per general brand history) — carries strong name recognition in this specific use case
- Standard enteric-coated formulation with the same local-gastric-protection rationale documented on [[Ecotrin]]

## Cons
- No St. Joseph-brand-specific RCT distinct from the general low-dose-aspirin secondary-prevention literature was located in this pass — its evidence base is entirely the general aspirin literature on [[aspirin]], not a dedicated brand trial
- Same core aspirin safety profile applies: GI bleeding risk (systemic), Reye syndrome risk in children/teens with viral illness, anticoagulant-interaction bleeding risk
- Enteric coating shares the same absorption-delay/reliability tradeoff already noted on [[Ecotrin]] — not suitable for fast-onset emergency use (e.g., suspected acute MI), where immediate-release chewed aspirin is the standard recommendation

## Use Cases
Doctor-directed secondary prevention of recurrent heart attack/stroke (81mg low-dose aspirin) — per [[aspirin]]'s Special Population Flags, this is an established, evidence-supported use in patients with existing cardiovascular disease, distinct from the unsupported "prophylactic use in healthy/low-risk individuals" question that current general aspirin guidance treats more cautiously.

## Allergies / Warnings
Standard aspirin-class cautions apply per [[aspirin]]: avoid in children/teens with viral illness (Reye syndrome); GI bleeding risk; anticoagulant-interaction caution; consult a doctor before starting low-dose aspirin therapy, since it should be a doctor-directed decision based on individual cardiovascular risk, not a self-directed OTC choice.

## Symptoms Treated
Secondary prevention of recurrent heart attack/stroke in patients with established cardiovascular disease — not a treatment for an acute symptom.
