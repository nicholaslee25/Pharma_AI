## Appears In

## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed: the flagship "Tucks Medicated Cooling Pads" (the product most likely driving this database's 9% share, given the brand's classic pad-format identity) use **witch hazel 50% w/w** as the sole active ingredient, purpose "astringent" — see [[witch-hazel]] (new ingredient page built this pass). The pads are dual-labeled to "cleanse and soothe rectal or vaginal areas," a broader indication than hemorrhoids alone.

**Real brand-internal mechanism split, same pattern as [[Preparation H]] and [[Gold Bond]]:** Tucks separately sells **"Tucks Hemorrhoidal Cream"** — glycerin 15% (protectant) + **lidocaine 5% (local anesthetic)** — a completely different mechanism from the astringent pads, sold together as a "Multi-Care Relief Kit." A consumer buying "Tucks" for hemorrhoid relief could get a witch-hazel astringent pad or a lidocaine anesthetic cream depending on which specific product they select.

**Real evidence-strength finding on the core witch-hazel mechanism:** per [[witch-hazel]]'s dedicated research this pass, witch hazel's hemorrhoid-relief evidence is characterized in the literature reviewed as clinical-use/professional-guideline-level (American Society of Colon and Rectal Surgeons recognition) rather than backed by dedicated randomized controlled trial data — a real, worth-noting evidence gap for this category's third-largest brand, though this does not mean the ingredient is ineffective, only that its evidence base is less rigorously quantified than some other ingredients in this database.

No Tucks-brand-specific clinical trial was located for either SKU.

## Pros
- Confirmed FDA Drug Facts label for both the witch hazel pads and the lidocaine cream — genuine regulatory transparency
- Witch hazel astringent mechanism is a mild, well-tolerated option relative to the vasoconstrictor/corticosteroid options elsewhere in this category, with a long history of clinical use and professional-society recognition
- Pad format offers a genuinely different, arguably gentler application method (wipe/cleanse) than cream/ointment/suppository formats used by every other brand in this category
- The Hemorrhoidal Cream's lidocaine 5% matches [[RectiCare]]'s base concentration, giving consumers a comparable anesthetic option within the Tucks brand family if the astringent pads alone are insufficient

## Cons
- **Witch hazel's hemorrhoid-relief evidence base is guideline/clinical-use-level, not RCT-level** per [[witch-hazel]] — a real, explicitly-flagged limitation for the mechanism driving this brand's flagship, most brand-recognized product
- Real risk of consumer confusion between the two Tucks mechanisms (witch hazel pads vs. lidocaine cream), especially since they are sold together in a single "Multi-Care Relief Kit" without a unifying single-ingredient identity
- No Tucks-brand-specific clinical trial exists for either SKU
- Astringent-only mechanism (pads) does not address swelling the way a vasoconstrictor would, or pain the way an anesthetic would — a narrower symptom-relief scope than combination products elsewhere in this category

## Use Cases
Cleansing and soothing of external anal (and, per label, vaginal) irritation, itching, and burning via witch hazel's astringent mechanism (Medicated Cooling Pads); temporary local pain relief via lidocaine (Hemorrhoidal Cream, separate SKU).

## Allergies / Warnings
Pads: external use only; do not insert; seek medical attention if bleeding or worsening occurs; "do not flush" after use (a real, specific disposal warning distinct from most other products in this category). Cream: external use only; do not insert directly into rectum; discontinue if rectal bleeding occurs or condition doesn't improve within 7 days. See [[topical-anesthetics]] for lidocaine's general safety profile (materially better methemoglobinemia profile than benzocaine).

## Symptoms Treated
Itching, discomfort, and irritation from hemorrhoids (and general rectal/vaginal-area cleansing/soothing, per the pads' broader label) via witch hazel astringent action; local pain via lidocaine anesthesia (separate cream SKU).
