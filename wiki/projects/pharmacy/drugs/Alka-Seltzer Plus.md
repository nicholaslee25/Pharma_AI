## Overview
"Alka-Seltzer Plus" is a large **family of distinct multi-symptom combination products**, not one formulation — DailyMed lists at least 8 different Alka-Seltzer Plus SKUs with different active-ingredient combinations. Confirmed examples: **Severe Cold & Flu** (tablet) = acetaminophen 250mg + chlorpheniramine maleate 2mg + dextromethorphan HBr 10mg + phenylephrine HCl 5mg; **Maximum Strength Day/Night Cold & Flu** = acetaminophen + dextromethorphan + doxylamine succinate + phenylephrine (night) split by day/night dosing; **Cold Medicine Sparkling Original** = aspirin + chlorpheniramine maleate + phenylephrine bitartrate (an aspirin-based, not acetaminophen-based, variant — a real formulation split within the same brand name that matters for aspirin-sensitive or anticoagulated users). No single ingredient page in this database covers this whole combination; the constituent actives map individually to [[acetaminophen]], [[dextromethorphan]], [[pseudoephedrine]]/[[oral-phenylephrine]] (phenylephrine specifically, not pseudoephedrine — see [[oral-phenylephrine]]), and [[aspirin]] depending on SKU. This replaces having no prior ingredient pointer at all. Label saved at `raw/primary-sources/brands/alka-seltzer-plus/dailymed-alka-seltzer-plus.md`.

## Pros
- Broad symptom coverage (pain/fever, cough, congestion, allergy/sleep) in one product, useful for multi-symptom colds/flu
- Day/night split formulations (doxylamine at night only) let users avoid daytime sedation while still getting nighttime sleep support
- Effervescent tablet format may suit those who dislike swallowing pills

## Cons
- **Real risk of ingredient duplication**: because the exact active-ingredient combination varies by specific SKU (aspirin vs. acetaminophen base, phenylephrine dose varies), a user switching between Alka-Seltzer Plus variants — or combining one with a separate standalone pain reliever — risks unknowingly double-dosing acetaminophen or aspirin. This is a genuine, brand-family-specific safety consideration beyond what any single ingredient page can capture.
- The aspirin-based variant (Cold Medicine Sparkling Original) carries aspirin's own risks (GI bleeding, Reye's syndrome risk in children/teens with viral illness, contraindication with certain anticoagulants) — see [[aspirin]] — that the acetaminophen-based variants do not share; consumers may not realize the two formulations differ this significantly
- Phenylephrine's oral efficacy has been publicly questioned by an FDA advisory panel (2023) as not significantly better than placebo at standard OTC doses — see [[oral-phenylephrine]] for that evidence if present in this database
- Multi-ingredient products make it harder to identify which component is causing a given side effect

## Use Cases
Multi-symptom cold/flu relief: pain, fever, cough, nasal congestion, and (in "Night" variants) sleep support during illness.

## Allergies / Warnings
Contains multiple actives — check for acetaminophen or aspirin overlap with any other medication before use. Reye's syndrome warning applies to aspirin-containing variants in children/teenagers with flu-like illness or chickenpox. Ask a doctor before use with liver disease (acetaminophen variants), bleeding disorders or ulcers (aspirin variant), high blood pressure, thyroid disease, diabetes, or trouble urinating due to enlarged prostate (phenylephrine/antihistamine components).

## Symptoms Treated
Fever, body aches, cough, nasal/sinus congestion, runny nose, sneezing (antihistamine variants), sleeplessness during illness (Night variants).
