## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed: **diclofenac sodium topical gel 1%**, a topical NSAID. This is the category leader by a wide margin in both Pharmacy Times subcategories (38% and 42% respectively — more than double the next brand, Biofreeze). See [[topical-diclofenac]] for the full ingredient evidence page, which already treats "Voltaren Gel / Voltaren Arthritis Pain" as a single entity and cites this exact recommendation-share data.

**Naming/regulatory nuance worth noting (not a discrepancy, but easily confused):** the original prescription Voltaren Gel (approved 2007) and the OTC "Voltaren Arthritis Pain" product (FDA Rx-to-OTC switch, February 2020) are confirmed to be the **same 1% diclofenac sodium formula** — only the prescription-vs-OTC availability changed. The prescribing-information dosing (2g per application for elbow/wrist/hand, 4g for knee/ankle/foot, 4x daily, max 32g/day across all joints, max 8g/day on a single upper-extremity joint, no more than 2 body areas at once) matches the consumer OTC label's directions cited on [[topical-diclofenac]]. Pharmacy Times' continued use of the legacy "Voltaren Gel" name in a consumer OTC-recommendation survey most plausibly reflects brand-name recognition carrying over from the Rx era rather than pharmacists recommending an actual prescription product in an OTC guide — worth flagging since a patient searching for "Voltaren Gel" at a pharmacy today will find it shelved/labeled as "Voltaren Arthritis Pain."

A separate 3% diclofenac gel remains prescription-only, for actinic keratosis — a different indication and concentration, not the arthritis product tracked here. No Voltaren-brand-specific clinical trial beyond the class-wide diclofenac evidence already summarized on [[topical-diclofenac]] was located in this pass.

## Pros
- Confirmed, consistent 1% diclofenac sodium formulation across the Rx-to-OTC transition — no formulation dilution or change accompanied the 2020 OTC switch
- Topical route gives materially lower systemic NSAID exposure than oral diclofenac, per [[topical-diclofenac]] — a genuine safety advantage for patients with cardiovascular/GI/renal risk factors who need localized arthritis relief
- Runaway category leader (38-42% share) in both Topical Analgesics subcategories, well ahead of the next-highest brand in either

## Cons
- Not indicated for strains, sprains, bruises, or sports injuries — arthritis-specific only, unlike several lower-share competitors in the same category (Biofreeze, Icy Hot, BENGAY) that are labeled for general musculoskeletal aches
- May take up to 7 days for full effect — not a fast-acting product despite the "Topical Analgesics" category framing alongside instant-cooling counterirritants
- Carries class-wide NSAID label warnings (GI bleeding, cardiovascular, hepatic) even though systemic absorption is low — see [[topical-diclofenac]] for the full safety data
- No Voltaren-specific clinical trial distinct from the broader diclofenac evidence base was located in this pass

## Use Cases
Temporary relief of arthritis pain specifically in the hand, wrist, elbow, foot, ankle, or knee — not a general-purpose muscle/joint analgesic.

## Allergies / Warnings
Per label: aspirin/NSAID allergy is a contraindication; do not use before/after heart surgery; do not apply to more than 2 body areas simultaneously; avoid eyes, nose, mouth, and broken skin; see [[topical-diclofenac]] for full warnings (GI bleeding risk factors, cardiovascular risk, hepatic risk).

## Symptoms Treated
Arthritis pain in hand, wrist, elbow, foot, ankle, or knee.
