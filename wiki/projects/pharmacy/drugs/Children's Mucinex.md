## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed: "Mucinex Childrens Multi-Symptom Cold and Sore Throat" liquid = **dextromethorphan HBr 5mg + guaifenesin 100mg + phenylephrine HCl 2.5mg per 5mL**, labeled for ages 4+ (5mL every 4h for 4-6y, 10mL every 4h for 6-12y, max 6 doses/24h; under 4 — do not use). This combines the [[dextromethorphan]] and [[guaifenesin]] ingredient pages already in this database with phenylephrine, a third active not previously cross-linked from either of those pages for this brand. Separately, a "Childrens Mucinex Night Time Multi-Symptom Cold" SKU adds diphenhydramine and acetaminophen in place of/alongside these — **"Children's Mucinex" as a brand name spans at least two materially different formulations** (daytime DXM/guaifenesin/phenylephrine vs. nighttime acetaminophen/diphenhydramine/phenylephrine ± guaifenesin), the same brand-name-spanning-formulations pattern already flagged repeatedly across this database's cough/cold categories.

## Pros
- Combines an expectorant (guaifenesin) with a cough suppressant (dextromethorphan) and decongestant (phenylephrine), addressing three symptom classes (productive cough, chest congestion, nasal congestion) in one product
- Explicit "under 4 — do not use" label reflects the post-2008 FDA/CHPA pediatric cough-cold labeling standard directly (see Cons)

## Cons
- **Per the 2008 FDA Public Health Advisory and CHPA's industry-wide voluntary relabeling (documented in detail on `drugs/Children's Dimetapp Cold and Allergy.md`), pediatric combination cough/cold products are not recommended under age 4** — this Mucinex SKU's own label directly implements that floor, making it a clean confirming example of that regulatory history rather than an exception to it.
- Neither the dextromethorphan/guaifenesin combination's efficacy (both individual ingredients carry LOW-to-MODERATE evidence grades per [[dextromethorphan]] and [[guaifenesin]] — the best RCTs for each found no clear subjective/objective benefit over placebo) nor phenylephrine's own well-documented efficacy problems (see [[oral-phenylephrine]] — FDA's 2023 advisory panel found oral phenylephrine no better than placebo at OTC doses) were separately re-tested for this specific pediatric combination — a pediatric patient here is receiving three ingredients whose adult evidence base is each individually weak-to-null
- No brand-specific pediatric RCT located for this combination beyond the individual-ingredient evidence
- Two materially different "Children's Mucinex" formulations (daytime vs. night time, different active-ingredient sets) exist under the same parent brand name — a source of real potential parent confusion/dosing error if switching between them without re-checking the label

## Use Cases
Relief of productive cough, chest congestion, stuffy/runny nose, and sore throat associated with the common cold, ages 4+.

## Allergies / Warnings
Per label: do not use under age 4; do not exceed 6 doses/24h; do not use with any other product containing dextromethorphan, guaifenesin, or a decongestant; ask a doctor before use if the child has heart disease, high blood pressure, thyroid disease, diabetes, or breathing problems.

## Symptoms Treated
Cough (productive and non-productive), chest congestion, stuffy/runny nose, sore throat.
