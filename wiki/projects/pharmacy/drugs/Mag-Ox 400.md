## Overview
**Real, significant, category-wide discrepancy found here:** Mag-Ox 400 is the category-leading Magnesium Supplements brand in this database (46% share — more than the next several brands combined) and is confirmed via pharmaceutical-grade product labeling to use **magnesium oxide**, the exact salt form [[magnesium]]'s own evidence page identifies by name as having LOWER bioavailability than the aspartate, citrate, lactate, and chloride forms. Independent pharmacology sourcing confirms magnesium oxide absorption is inversely dose-dependent (~50% at low/dietary-level intake vs. only 15–30% at the higher doses typical of supplementation), and that unabsorbed magnesium oxide reaching the colon exerts a real osmotic laxative effect — magnesium oxide is described in comparative sourcing as "primarily useful as a laxative" rather than as an efficiently-absorbed systemic magnesium source. This means the single most-recommended magnesium brand in this database's entire Magnesium Supplements category uses the specific form its own linked ingredient evidence page flags as comparatively poorly absorbed — directly relevant to the brand's #3-ranked category-mate [[SlowMag Mg]], which uses a better-absorbed chloride salt specifically to reduce this exact laxative/GI-upset tradeoff. Manufacturer guidance to take Mag-Ox 400 with a full meal is consistent with maximizing what absorption the oxide form does offer.

## Cons
- **Real, significant, and category-wide discrepancy: uses magnesium oxide, the specific salt form [[magnesium]]'s own evidence page names as having lower bioavailability than aspartate/citrate/lactate/chloride forms** — and independent sourcing puts oxide-form absorption at only 15–30% at typical supplemental doses, versus ~50% at low/dietary intake levels (absorption is inversely dose-dependent)
- Unabsorbed magnesium oxide reaching the colon produces a real osmotic-laxative effect — meaning a meaningful fraction of what's taken for systemic magnesium repletion (e.g., migraine prevention, per [[magnesium]]'s strongest-evidenced use case) may instead simply move through the GI tract and cause loose stools, without being absorbed for the intended purpose
- This is a real, significant category-wide finding: the #1-share brand (46%) uses a comparatively worse-absorbed form than the #3-share brand ([[SlowMag Mg]], 13%, magnesium chloride) in the very same category — pharmacist recommendation share does not track absorption quality here
- No brand-specific clinical trial was located; efficacy evidence is at the general-ingredient level, and that evidence itself already flags this exact form-dependent limitation
