## Overview
**Real, significant category-placement discrepancy found here.** Diurex is confirmed via FDA label (DailyMed) to be a real OTC monograph drug — **pamabrom** 25-50mg, a diuretic — but its FDA-labeled indication is **temporary relief of water retention, bloating, swelling, and "full feeling" specifically associated with the premenstrual and menstrual periods**, not fat/weight loss. This is a real, meaningful mechanism mismatch with its "Diet Aids" category placement: pamabrom causes the body to excrete excess water/fluid, which can cause a temporary drop on a bathroom scale, but does **not** reduce body fat and has no FDA-reviewed weight-loss indication at all. Diet Aids (26% share, second-largest in this category) has no dedicated ingredient page in this database, and pamabrom's actual indication (PMS-related bloating) is entirely different from what this category's name implies.

## Pros
- Real, FDA-monograph OTC drug with a genuine, well-defined indication (PMS/menstrual bloating and water retention) — more regulatory clarity than several unproven dietary-supplement competitors in this category
- Transparent, single active ingredient (pamabrom) with a clear DailyMed label

## Cons
- **Real, significant category-placement mismatch: this product's actual FDA-reviewed indication is PMS/menstrual water retention, not weight/fat loss** — a consumer using Diurex specifically as a "diet aid" for fat loss is using a product for an indication it was never evidenced or approved for; any weight change from a diuretic is water weight, not fat loss, and is temporary (returns once normal fluid balance resumes)
- No clinical trial evidence located in this pass supports pamabrom for actual body-fat reduction — its evidence base (to the extent independently located) concerns fluid retention specifically
- Diuretic misuse for perceived "weight loss" carries real electrolyte-imbalance risk with excessive/inappropriate use, a genuine safety consideration when a product is used outside its actual indication

## Use Cases
FDA-labeled use: temporary relief of water retention, bloating, and swelling associated with the premenstrual/menstrual period. **Not evidenced or labeled for fat/weight loss** despite this database's "Diet Aids" category placement.

## Allergies / Warnings
Per general pamabrom labeling: not intended for use beyond the labeled PMS/menstrual indication; excessive or inappropriate diuretic use can cause electrolyte imbalance. Consult a doctor if used for a purpose other than its labeled indication.
