## Overview
**Real, historic, landmark false-advertising case found here.** Geritol (launched 1950, iron/vitamin tonic marketed for "tired blood") is the subject of one of the most consequential false-advertising cases in FTC history: an FTC investigation beginning in 1959 led to a 1965 order finding Geritol's advertising falsely implied that most fatigue results from iron-deficiency anemia, when independent evidence showed such anemia affects only 1-3% of the general population; after a 13-year legal battle, a federal court imposed an **$812,000 penalty in 1973 — the largest FTC fine up to that time** (equivalent to roughly $5.89 million in 2025 dollars). Separately, the original liquid formulation contained **12% alcohol**, a real, notable ingredient for a product marketed to relieve fatigue. This is a genuinely historic case study in unsupported health-marketing claims, though it concerns the brand's mid-20th-century formulation/marketing, not necessarily its current product.
