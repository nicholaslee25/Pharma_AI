## Overview
The **Microlet 2 Adjustable Lancing Device** (Bayer, now under Ascensia Diabetes Care, the same company behind [[Contour Next]]) uses **28-gauge lancets** with **5 adjustable depth settings**, allowing users to tune puncture depth to skin thickness/sensitivity. Exact depth-per-setting figures (in mm) for the Microlet 2 specifically were not confirmed in this pass — a related current-generation model, the Microlet NEXT, is documented at 1.25mm (Setting 1) to 2.75mm (Setting 5) in 0.4mm-or-less increments, and likely approximates the Microlet 2's range, but this is not independently confirmed as identical and is flagged here as an inference rather than a fact. No brand-specific pain-comparison or infection-rate study was located for this specific device — consistent with [[blood-sampling-devices-lancets]]'s own statement that no brand-specific data exists for any lancet device in this category, with the strongest available evidence instead being the CDC's reuse/sharing infection-risk data and the Loveland 1999 puncture-site pain RCT, both of which apply across all lancet brands rather than to Microlet 2 specifically.

## Pros
- 5 adjustable depth settings allow real personalization for skin thickness and pain sensitivity, a genuinely useful design feature even without a brand-specific pain-comparison trial to quantify its benefit
- Made by the same manufacturer family (Ascensia) as [[Contour Next]], which showed strong ISO 15197:2013 compliance data — though lancet accuracy/safety and meter accuracy are separate questions and this should not be read as transferring one product's evidence to the other
- 28-gauge needle is a comparatively fine gauge, plausibly reducing puncture pain relative to thicker lancets, though this specific comparison was not independently trial-tested in the sources found

## Cons
- No brand-specific pain, gauge-comparison, or infection-rate data was located — the adjustable-depth feature's real-world benefit is plausible but not independently trial-confirmed for this device
- Like all lancet devices, the single most important safety issue (per [[blood-sampling-devices-lancets]]'s CDC data) is behavioral: device reuse/sharing between patients caused real, documented hepatitis B outbreaks even when devices were disinfected between uses. This applies to the Microlet 2 exactly as much as to any other lancing device — no design feature of this specific product mitigates that risk
- Smallest single-brand share (13%, tied with Contour Next) among the five brands in this category

## Use Cases
Fingerstick capillary blood sampling for blood glucose self-monitoring, with adjustable puncture depth.

## Allergies / Warnings
Not a drug — no allergy profile. **Critical, CDC-documented safety point (per [[blood-sampling-devices-lancets]]):** never share this or any lancing device between people, even with cleaning/disinfection between uses — disinfection alone has been documented to fail to prevent bloodborne pathogen (hepatitis B) transmission in real outbreak investigations. Assign one device to one person only.
