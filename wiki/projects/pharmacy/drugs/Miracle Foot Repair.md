## Overview
This bare "Miracle Foot Repair" brand-file entry (Foot Care Products, 2%) is the same product already researched as [[Miracle Foot Repair Cream]] under Diabetic Foot Cream (4% share). Confirmed via DailyMed: Miracle Foot Repair Cream (Winning Solutions) carries an actual OTC Drug Facts label — active ingredient **Menthol 0.1%**, purpose "external analgesic," on a 60%-aloe-vera-gel cosmetic base. Salicylic acid also appears in the formula, but only as an INACTIVE ingredient with no percentage or drug-facts claim — a meaningfully different regulatory role than [[Kerasal]]'s active-ingredient use of salicylic acid in the same broader foot-care space, and the two should not be conflated. No dedicated menthol ingredient page exists yet in this database (see [[topical-counterirritants]] for a related but distinct higher-concentration menthol context; this product's 0.1% is far below that page's brands, which run 0.5-10%). No brand-specific clinical trial was found.

## Pros
- Confirmed, genuine FDA Drug Facts label (external analgesic) — more regulatory transparency than several cosmetic-classified competitors in this category (e.g. plain moisturizing creams elsewhere in Foot Care Products)
- 60% aloe vera gel base provides a soothing, moisturizing vehicle alongside the menthol's cooling sensation
- Very low (0.1%) menthol concentration is well below the FDA's 3% burn-risk threshold flagged on [[topical-counterirritants]] — a genuine safety positive relative to the much higher-concentration menthol brands in that category (Biofreeze 10%, BENGAY up to 10%)

## Cons
- No dedicated ingredient-evidence page exists yet for menthol in this database, so no formal efficacy cross-check could be performed for the "external analgesic" claim at this specific (very low) concentration
- The listed NDC was inactivated in DailyMed at time of pull (per [[Miracle Foot Repair Cream]]'s existing finding) — worth noting as a possible sign of discontinuation or reformulation
- No diabetic-population-specific outcome data exists — see [[diabetic-foot-care]]
- No brand-specific clinical trial exists beyond the general OTC menthol-as-external-analgesic monograph classification
- Does not address structural foot-care needs (calluses, cracked heels, fungal infection) that several competing Foot Care Products brands (Dr. Scholl's, AmLactin Foot Repair, Kerasal) specifically target — this product's mechanism is limited to chapped/cracked-skin protection plus mild cooling comfort

## Use Cases
Temporary protection and relief of chapped or cracked skin, with a mild cooling/soothing sensory component from low-concentration menthol.

## Allergies / Warnings
For external use only. In case of accidental ingestion, seek medical help or contact Poison Control immediately. Keep away from children under 2 years; children under 2 require physician approval before use.
