## Appears In
Lice Treatments - 3%

## Overview
**Major correction, confirmed via DailyMed — this database's own ingredient page previously misidentified Vamousse's mechanism, and that error has now been fixed:** every current US Vamousse SKU checked (Vamousse Lice Treatment aerosol foam, Vamousse Lice Treatment Complete Kit, Vamousse Pro Lice Treatment, Vamousse Lice Defense shampoo) lists its active ingredient as **Natrum muriaticum 2X (HPUS)** — homeopathic sodium chloride — with the treatment-line kit and defense shampoo also adding **Eucalyptus globulus 2X (HPUS)**. **Marketing status on every SKU: "unapproved homeopathic,"** with the standard FDA disclaimer that the product has not been evaluated for safety or efficacy. Labeler: Alliance Pharma Inc.

**This is not dimethicone/dimeticone**, despite some third-party retail/marketing copy (a UK retailer listing and consumer-facing "how it works" pages) describing Vamousse as a dimethicone-based, physically-suffocating pediculicide — a real, well-evidenced mechanism used by other international products, but not what DailyMed's official US drug listing shows for the Vamousse products actually sold and presumably tracked in this database's US pharmacist-recommendation-share data. **[[permethrin-pyrethrin-lice]] previously cited a genuine 100-patient dimeticone-vs-permethrin RCT as "Vamousse's mechanism" — that attribution was incorrect and has been corrected on that page.** The dimeticone RCT remains real, well-conducted evidence — it is just not evidence for this specific US-marketed brand.

No Vamousse-brand-specific clinical trial was located for the actual homeopathic Natrum muriaticum/Eucalyptus globulus formulation.

## Pros
- Sodium chloride's desiccation mechanism is, in principle, resistance-independent (not a pyrethroid sodium-channel target, so the kdr-mutation resistance documented on [[permethrin-pyrethrin-lice]] does not apply to it mechanistically)
- No pesticide-class active ingredient, generally well-tolerated
- Foam/mousse format with a 15-minute dwell time and included nit comb

## Cons
- **Filed with FDA as "unapproved homeopathic" across every current SKU** — FDA has explicitly not evaluated this product for safety or efficacy, placing it in the same weak-evidence regulatory category already flagged elsewhere in this database (see [[homeopathic-arnica]])
- **This database's own ingredient page previously and incorrectly attributed a genuine, favorable dimethicone RCT to this brand** — that error has been corrected, but it means the strongest evidence once associated with Vamousse in this database does not actually apply to it
- No confirmed clinical trial exists for Vamousse's actual formulation (homeopathic Natrum muriaticum/Eucalyptus globulus)
- Homeopathic 2X potency is a low-strength serial dilution notation, not a stated pharmacological concentration — harder for a consumer to evaluate against monograph-drug alternatives in this same category
- Flammable aerosol formulation (do not smoke or use near open flame, per label) — a real, product-specific safety note not shared by the non-aerosol brands in this category
- Smallest recommendation share in this category (3%)

## Use Cases
Marketed treatment of head lice via a homeopathic sodium chloride (and, in some SKUs, eucalyptus) formulation; foam applied to dry hair, 15-minute dwell time, then combed out.

## Allergies / Warnings
Per label: external use only; flammable, avoid open flame/smoking during use; avoid eyes, nose, mouth, and vaginal contact; keep eyes closed and protected during application; not for children under 2 years without consulting a doctor.

## Symptoms Treated
Active head lice infestation (as marketed; FDA has not evaluated efficacy for this homeopathic filing).

## Brand-Specific Alignment Check
**Real discrepancy found and corrected, on both this page and [[permethrin-pyrethrin-lice]]:** this database's ingredient page previously stated Vamousse uses a dimethicone mechanism, citing a genuine favorable RCT for that mechanism as if it applied to this brand. DailyMed confirms this was wrong — every current US Vamousse SKU is filed as homeopathic Natrum muriaticum ± Eucalyptus globulus, "unapproved homeopathic" marketing status, not dimethicone. This is a materially different (and materially weaker) evidence position than the ingredient page originally implied, parallel to the [[Lotrimin]] Ultra/terbinafine correction made earlier in this database's Topicals research.
