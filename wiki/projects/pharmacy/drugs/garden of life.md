## Overview
Garden of Life markets several product lines with real third-party certifications — NSF Certified for Sport and Non-GMO Project Verified confirmed on its Sport protein line, and USDA Organic on select products — which are genuine, checkable programs, more than most brands in this pass have. However, this database found a **significant, real, brand-specific and recent discrepancy**: a December 2025 class-action lawsuit alleges Garden of Life's Organic Plant-Based Protein contains lead at 2.76 µg per serving — 564% over the plaintiffs' cited safe-limit threshold — while being marketed as "clean and certified" and "rigorously tested." A 2025 Consumer Reports investigation separately found lead levels 4–6x above the product's own safety threshold in Garden of Life products. This follows a prior (2020–2021) Clean Label Project lawsuit over heavy metals in Garden of Life prenatal products — directly relevant to this brand's own Prenatal Vitamins category tracked here (10% share). Organic/Non-GMO certification does not itself guard against heavy-metal contamination, since USDA Organic certifies farming practice (pesticide-free), not soil safety, and heavy metals like lead occur naturally in soil regardless of organic status — a real, non-obvious limitation of the "organic" claim specifically. Prenatal Vitamins (10%) is covered by [[prenatal-vitamins]]; Probiotic Dietary Supplements (3%) by [[probiotics]] (which separately notes strain-level identification, not just genus, is required to assess probiotic evidence — this brand's specific strains were not identified in this pass); Nutritional Supplements has no dedicated ingredient page in this database.

## Cons

- **A second, earlier (2020–2021) heavy-metals lawsuit specifically over prenatal products** — directly relevant to this brand's own Prenatal Vitamins category (10% share) tracked in this database, meaning the heavy-metal concern is not confined to one unrelated product line
- 2025 Consumer Reports investigation independently corroborates elevated lead findings (4–6x above the product's own safety threshold), suggesting this is not solely a plaintiff's-attorney claim
- "Organic" and "Non-GMO" certifications do not test for or guarantee freedom from heavy metals — a real, easily-misunderstood limitation of those specific labels that consumers may reasonably (but incorrectly) assume covers contaminant safety
- No Garden-of-Life-specific clinical trial was located for the Nutritional Supplements or Probiotic Dietary Supplements lines tracked here
