## Overview
Burt's Bees is a large, multi-category personal-care brand. The 2026-08-26 pass covered only its Throat Drops product; this pass (2026-08-27, Topicals work) adds the Acne, Lip Balms, and Therapeutic Skin Care Cleansers appearances. **Toothpaste, General Use remains genuinely unresearched** — that entry belongs to Oral Care, which this database's category tracker marks fully built, so this is a real gap left over from that earlier pass rather than new Topicals scope; noted here rather than silently left unaddressed.

**Throat Drops (Topical Cough Suppressants, Lozenges — 3%):** Confirmed via DailyMed: Burt's Bees Throat Drops (Honey & Lemon) = **menthol 2.0mg per drop**, labeled as an oral anesthetic/cough-relief lozenge, with honey, eucalyptus oil, and rice extract among inactive ingredients. No dedicated ingredient page exists for menthol in this database. Label saved at `raw/primary-sources/brands/burts-bees\dailymed-burts-bees-throat-drops.md`.

**Acne Products (3%):** Burt's Bees Natural Acne Solutions Purifying Gel Cleanser uses **salicylic acid 1.0%**, marketed as "naturally derived" from willow bark (Salix nigra bark extract). Per [[salicylic-acid]], this database's evidence for salicylic acid is strongest for wart removal and comparatively thinner/less specifically documented for acne — a brand-marketing angle ("naturally derived from willow bark") does not change the underlying molecule's evidence base. Manufacturer press material (EurekAlert, AAD 2022) claims a company-sponsored comparison finding this 1% formulation "gentle yet effective" versus a competitor's 0.5% salicylic acid product; this is manufacturer-sponsored consumer-preference/tolerability marketing, not an independently published RCT, and is flagged rather than treated as clinical evidence.

**Lip Balms (15%, Burt's Bees' largest single Topicals share):** The flagship Burt's Bees Beeswax Lip Balm is a **non-drug cosmetic** — beeswax, sunflower oil, coconut oil, castor oil, lanolin, rosemary extract, tocopherol, and fragrance/flavor oils, with **no FDA-recognized active ingredient and no SPF**. This is a real, consumer-relevant contrast with several other brands in the same Lip Balms category that ARE drug products: [[Blistex]] and [[Carmex]] (both menthol/camphor-based OTC drugs, researched 2026-08-26) and [[Neutrogena]] (SPF sunscreen-active lip products, researched 2026-08-27 in this same pass). A consumer choosing "a lip balm" across this one Pharmacy Times category is actually choosing between at least three different regulatory categories: cosmetic-only (Burt's Bees), OTC external-analgesic drug (Blistex/Carmex), and OTC sunscreen drug (Neutrogena) — none of which is disclosed by the shared category label alone.

**Therapeutic Skin Care, Cleansers (2%):** Burt's Bees Sensitive Facial Cleanser (a soap-free cream cleanser with cotton/rice extract and aloe) is, per third-party ingredient listings, also a **non-drug cosmetic** with no located Drug Facts panel — consistent with the brand's Lip Balm SKU in having no active pharmaceutical ingredient, distinct from its own Acne Products SKU (salicylic acid) under the same house brand.

## Pros
- Throat Drops: lowest confirmed menthol dose (2.0mg) among brands checked in that category in the earlier pass — may suit users sensitive to menthol's cooling intensity
- Acne line: confirmed, disclosed 1.0% salicylic acid concentration within the FDA-monograph range, per [[salicylic-acid]]
- Lip Balm and Cleanser lines: fragrance/flavor-oil-based rather than menthol/camphor-based, a genuine option for users who find medicated lip products or cleansers irritating
- "100% natural"/"98.9% natural origin" formulations per manufacturer marketing across multiple SKUs (not independently verified against a specific purity standard in this pass)

## Cons
- **Genuine cross-SKU inconsistency:** under one house brand, Throat Drops and the Acne cleanser are FDA-monograph OTC drugs (menthol, salicylic acid respectively) while the flagship Lip Balm and the Sensitive Facial Cleanser are non-drug cosmetics with no active ingredient at all — a consumer cannot assume any given "Burt's Bees" product treats anything medically
- **Toothpaste, General Use (2%) remains unresearched** — a real, acknowledged gap from the earlier Oral Care pass, not filled in this Topicals pass since it falls outside this pass's category scope
- Acne line's headline "naturally derived from willow bark" positioning is manufacturer marketing around a company-sponsored comparison, not an independently published trial; flagged rather than treated as added efficacy evidence over other 1% salicylic-acid products
- Lip Balm SKU offers no sun protection, unlike some competitors in the same category (e.g. [[Neutrogena]]) — a real functional gap for a user assuming "lip balm" implies SPF
- No Burt's Bees-brand-specific RCT beyond the manufacturer-sponsored acne-cleanser comparison was located for any SKU in this pass

## Use Cases
Cough/throat irritation relief (Throat Drops, menthol); acne treatment (Purifying Gel Cleanser, salicylic acid 1%); lip moisturizing with no medicinal or sun-protective claim (Beeswax Lip Balm); general facial cleansing for sensitive skin with no medicinal claim (Sensitive Facial Cleanser). Toothpaste use case not researched.

## Allergies / Warnings
Throat Drops: standard OTC lozenge age/dose directions. Acne cleanser: per [[salicylic-acid]]-class labeling, avoid eyes/mucous membranes, discontinue if irritation/dryness is excessive, do not use with other topical acne medications without doctor's advice. Lip Balm and Facial Cleanser: no FDA Drug Facts warnings located (non-drug cosmetics) — general cosmetic-allergen caution applies (lanolin and fragrance/essential oils in the Lip Balm are recognized contact-allergen categories, though no Burt's Bees-specific allergy signal was identified in this pass).

## Symptoms Treated
Cough, minor throat/mouth irritation (Throat Drops); acne (Purifying Gel Cleanser); none — cosmetic moisturizing/cleansing only (Lip Balm, Sensitive Facial Cleanser). Toothpaste not researched.

## Brand-Specific Alignment Check
**Confirmed finding, this pass:** Burt's Bees spans both FDA-monograph OTC drug SKUs (menthol lozenges, salicylic-acid acne cleanser) and non-drug cosmetic SKUs (Beeswax Lip Balm, Sensitive Facial Cleanser) under one "natural" branding umbrella — flagged here since the "natural" positioning could imply a consistent gentler-but-still-medicinal identity across the whole line, when in fact roughly half the researched SKUs contain no medication at all. Toothpaste, General Use remains an open, acknowledged research gap.
