## Overview
Cepacol is a multi-SKU brand line, not a single formulation. Confirmed via DailyMed labels: **Cepacol Extra Strength Sore Throat and Cough** = benzocaine 7.5mg + dextromethorphan HBr 5mg per lozenge; other Cepacol Sore Throat lozenge variants = benzocaine 15mg + menthol lozenges. Benzocaine is the same active ingredient already covered at [[topical-anesthetics]] (that page currently only lists Orajel as confirmed — Cepacol should be added as a second confirmed benzocaine brand, done below). The Cosmetic Mouthwash/Oral Rinse SKU (12% share) is a materially different product line — Cepacol mouthwash is historically cetylpyridinium chloride (CPC) based, which was **not independently confirmed via label in this pass** and has no dedicated ingredient page in this database; flagged as a data gap rather than guessed at. Label saved at `raw/primary-sources/brands/cepacol/dailymed-cepacol.md`.

## Pros
- Benzocaine provides genuine, fast local anesthetic relief for sore throat pain (see [[topical-anesthetics]] for the broader evidence base)
- Extra Strength variant combines a local anesthetic with dextromethorphan for combined sore-throat-pain + cough relief in one lozenge
- Multiple flavor/format SKUs (cherry, honey lemon, tangerine) may improve palatability/compliance

## Cons
- **Methemoglobinemia risk** — the Cepacol label itself carries this warning, and [[topical-anesthetics]] documents this as a real, disproportionate benzocaine-specific risk (benzocaine accounted for ~66% of a 242-case methemoglobinemia review despite lidocaine/prilocaine also being common). Risk increases ~20-fold with benzocaine re-exposure within the prior week — relevant for anyone using Cepacol lozenges repeatedly through a cold.
- Benzocaine is FDA Category C in pregnancy and on the pediatric KIDs list for avoidance in certain uses (per ingredient page) — though Cepacol's own lozenge label age cutoff (5+ years) is less restrictive than the "avoid in children" guidance StatPearls gives for teething/pharyngitis use specifically; these are different use cases, not a direct contradiction, but worth the reader's awareness
- The mouthwash SKU's actual active ingredient was not confirmed in this pass — a real data gap

## Use Cases
Sore throat pain relief (benzocaine lozenges); combined sore throat + cough relief (Extra Strength); oral hygiene/cosmetic rinse (mouthwash SKU, ingredient unconfirmed).

## Allergies / Warnings
Do not use if allergic to local anesthetics. Discontinue and seek medical care immediately for signs of methemoglobinemia (pale/gray/blue skin, lips, or nail beds; shortness of breath; fatigue; rapid heart rate). For adults/children ≥5y (lozenges); ask a doctor for use in younger children.

## Symptoms Treated
Sore throat pain, cough (Extra Strength SKU only).
