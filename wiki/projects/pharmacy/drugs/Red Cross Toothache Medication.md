## Appears In
Toothache Products - 2%

## Overview
Confirmed via DailyMed: Red Cross Toothache liquid (The Mentholatum Company) lists **eugenol 85%** as its active ingredient in a **sesame oil** base — a completely different active ingredient and mechanism from every other researched brand in this category (Orajel, Anbesol, Kank-A, DenTek Instant Pain Relief, all benzocaine-based). Eugenol, the primary aromatic compound in clove oil, has a long history of use in dentistry as a topical analgesic/mild local anesthetic. It is labeled specifically for temporary relief of throbbing, persistent toothache due to a cavity — narrower than the benzocaine competitors' broader "toothache, sore gums, canker sores, dentures, braces" labeling.

## Pros
- Genuinely differentiated mechanism from the benzocaine-dominated rest of this category — avoids benzocaine's disproportionate methemoglobinemia risk documented on [[topical-anesthetics]] entirely
- Eugenol/clove oil has a long, established traditional and clinical-dentistry history for toothache relief, predating most OTC benzocaine products
- High (85%) concentration suggests a concentrated, direct-application product intended for a specific cavity-related toothache rather than broader mouth/gum irritation

## Cons
- Narrower labeled indication (specifically cavity-related throbbing toothache) than benzocaine competitors' broader labeled uses (canker sores, dentures, braces, minor dental procedures)
- Label explicitly warns that contact with lips or mucous membranes can cause burning, swelling, or irritation — a real local-tissue-irritation risk specific to eugenol at this high (85%) concentration, a different risk profile than benzocaine's systemic methemoglobinemia concern
- Contraindicated for anyone with a eugenol (clove oil) allergy — a distinct allergy population from benzocaine/"caine"-family allergy sufferers, meaning this product is not automatically a safe alternative for someone avoiding benzocaine for allergy reasons without first confirming they don't also react to eugenol
- No brand-specific clinical trial evidence was identified for this specific product beyond eugenol's general traditional-use history in dentistry
- Smallest share (2%) of the researched Toothache Products brands, though this reflects market/pharmacist-recommendation patterns rather than confirmed inferiority

## Use Cases
Temporary relief of throbbing, persistent toothache due to a cavity, until a dentist can be seen — explicitly framed as a bridge to professional care rather than a definitive treatment.

## Allergies / Warnings
Per label: external use only; do not use if allergic to eugenol (clove oil); avoid contact with lips/mucous membranes (can cause burning, swelling, or irritation); adults and children 12+, apply up to 4 times daily or as directed by a dentist/doctor.

## Symptoms Treated
Cavity-related toothache pain specifically, via eugenol's local analgesic action — a narrower, more targeted indication than the broader benzocaine-based competitors in this category.
