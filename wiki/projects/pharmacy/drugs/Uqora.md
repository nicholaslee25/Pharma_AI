## Appears In
Urinary Health - 2%

## Overview
Uqora is a three-product UTI-prevention line, each with a genuinely different mechanism, confirmed via manufacturer/independent-review data: **Target** (a powder taken after sex or every 3 days) and **Control** (a daily pill) both contain D-mannose, a naturally-occurring sugar that binds E. coli bacteria to help flush them from the urinary tract before infection takes hold; **Promote** is a separate probiotic pill meant to restore vaginal/gut flora after an antibiotic course, see [[probiotics]]. D-mannose's evidence is genuinely mixed but not absent: a 2014 randomized controlled trial (n=308) using the same dose Uqora Target contains found D-mannose "significantly reduced the risk of recurrent UTI" — but the trial is described as small, and the American Urogynecologic Society's own Best Practice Statement characterizes the overall evidence for D-mannose as "limited."

## Pros
- D-mannose has real, if modest, RCT support at the specific dose Uqora Target uses — a genuinely better evidence position than many "UTI prevention" supplements on the market that rest on weaker or no trial data
- Three-product line lets users match mechanism to their situation (acute post-exposure use, daily prevention, or post-antibiotic flora restoration)
- No pending lawsuit was located in this pass (a real, if modest, clean-record finding)

## Cons
- **A 2024 Vice investigative article characterized Uqora and similar "wellness brand" UTI-prevention marketing as using aggressive Instagram advertising that "skirts FDA rules"** on structure/function-claim boundaries — a real, published journalistic criticism of the marketing approach (not a lawsuit or regulatory action, and this database did not independently verify the specific claims cited in that article), worth a user's awareness alongside the product's genuine D-mannose evidence
- AUGS's own characterization of D-mannose evidence as "limited" is a real, more conservative framing than "significantly reduced risk" marketing language might suggest — both are true simultaneously (a real positive trial exists; the overall evidence base is still thin)
- D-mannose is not effective against all UTI-causing bacteria — it specifically targets E. coli via mannose-binding adhesion, so its mechanism does not address non-E.-coli UTI causes, a real mechanistic limitation not always conveyed by "UTI prevention" marketing framing generally

## Use Cases
Target: post-exposure/periodic UTI-risk reduction. Control: daily UTI-prevention. Promote: vaginal/gut flora restoration after antibiotics.

## Allergies / Warnings
No brand-specific recall or lawsuit located in this pass. D-mannose is a sugar — diabetic users should be aware of carbohydrate content, though typical doses are modest.

## Symptoms Treated
UTI prevention (Target, Control, via E. coli anti-adhesion mechanism); vaginal/gut flora support after antibiotics (Promote) — not a treatment for an active, established infection in any of the three products.

## Brand-Specific Alignment Check
**Real, worth-flagging tension:** Uqora's D-mannose products have genuine, if limited, trial support — a real positive relative to many competitors in this space — but the brand's marketing intensity (per the cited Vice reporting) appears to outpace what the "limited" AUGS evidence characterization would support. Presented as a two-sided finding rather than a condemnation: the ingredient science is real but modest, and the marketing tone is more confident than that modesty would suggest.
