# Oral Pain Relievers — Comparison (Ibuprofen / Naproxen / Aspirin / Acetaminophen)

**Type:** Analysis (comparison)
**Verification Status:** AI-compiled from primary sources, unverified — not yet reviewed by a pharmacist/physician
**Last updated:** 2026-08-28
**Sources:** Synthesized from [[ibuprofen]], [[naproxen]], [[aspirin]], and [[acetaminophen]] — no new primary research performed for this page.

---

## Category snapshot

Three of these four ([[ibuprofen]], [[naproxen]], [[aspirin]]) are NSAIDs — they share a mechanism (COX inhibition) and, largely, a risk profile (GI bleeding, cardiovascular warning, kidney risk). [[Acetaminophen]] is mechanistically distinct — it is not an NSAID, has no meaningful peripheral anti-inflammatory effect, and trades the NSAID class's GI/cardiovascular/antiplatelet risks for a different one entirely: liver toxicity. Understanding that split is more useful than memorizing brand names.

| Ingredient | Representative brand | Class | Anti-inflammatory? | Antiplatelet effect? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ibuprofen | Advil, Motrin | NSAID (non-selective COX-1/COX-2) | Yes | Reversible, short-lived |
| Naproxen | Aleve | NSAID (non-selective, slightly COX-1-favoring) | Yes | Reversible, short-lived |
| Aspirin | Bayer | NSAID (irreversible COX-1 inhibitor) | Yes | **Irreversible — lasts the ~7–10 day platelet lifespan**, the basis of its separate low-dose cardioprotective use |
| Acetaminophen | Tylenol | Not an NSAID — distinct, incompletely understood central mechanism | **No** meaningful peripheral effect | No |

## Head-to-head: single-dose acute-pain efficacy (Cochrane data)

All four ingredient pages in this database cite Cochrane single-dose acute (mostly postoperative) pain reviews reporting **NNT for ≥50% pain relief over 4–6 hours** — the most directly comparable number across ingredients:

| Ingredient / dose | NNT (95% CI) | Source review |
|---|---|---|
| Naproxen sodium 550mg | **2.6** (2.2–3.2) | Mason et al. 2004, PMID 15495091 |
| Ibuprofen 400mg | **2.5** (2.4–2.6) | Derry et al. 2009, PMID 19588326 |
| Ibuprofen 200mg | 2.7 (2.5–3.0) | Derry et al. 2009 |
| Aspirin 1200mg | 2.7 (2.0–3.8) | Derry et al. 2012, PMID 22513905 |
| Aspirin 900/1000mg | 3.8 (3.0–5.1) | Derry et al. 2012 |
| Aspirin 600/650mg | 4.2 (3.9–4.8) | Derry et al. 2012 |
| Aspirin <600mg | **Not significantly different from placebo** | Derry et al. 2012 |

**Acetaminophen coverage gap, stated plainly:** this database's [[acetaminophen]] page cites a Cochrane review of acetaminophen for the *common cold* (finding insufficient evidence for cold-symptom relief specifically), not a dedicated single-dose acute-pain NNT review comparable to the three NSAIDs above. The naproxen page does note that its own cited aspirin review's authors describe aspirin's pain relief as "very similar milligram for milligram to acetaminophen" — the closest sourced comparison point available, not a substitute for a dedicated acetaminophen NNT figure.

**Reading the table:** at their respective standard OTC single doses, naproxen sodium 550mg and ibuprofen 400mg outperform aspirin 600/650mg on this specific measure — aspirin needs to reach 1200mg (above the standard 325–650mg OTC single dose) to approach the same NNT. This is single-dose acute-pain data; it does not by itself tell you which is best for chronic arthritis pain, fever, or a specific person's risk profile — see Bottom Lines below.

## Duration of action

| Ingredient | Half-life | Typical dosing interval | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ibuprofen | 1.8–2.0h | q4–6h | Shortest-acting of the three NSAIDs — more doses/day but faster clearance if a dose needs to be stopped |
| Aspirin | Short (first-order at low dose; kinetics saturate at high/repeated dosing) | q4–6h | Antiplatelet effect (7–10 days) drastically outlasts its analgesic half-life — the two effects are not on the same clock |
| Naproxen | **12–17h** | q8–12h | Longest-acting — time to remedication after a single dose was 7.6h vs. 2.6h for placebo in the Cochrane data above, meaningfully longer coverage per dose than ibuprofen |
| Acetaminophen | Not comparably tabulated across sources in this database | q4–6h (up to 1000mg) or q6h | — |

## Key risk differentiator: NSAIDs vs. acetaminophen

| Risk | Ibuprofen / Naproxen / Aspirin (NSAID class) | Acetaminophen |
|---|---|---|
| GI ulcer/bleed | Real, class-wide, can occur "at any time, with or without warning" (ibuprofen FDA label); ~1% at 3–6 months, ~2–4% at one year | Not a mechanism concern (no COX/prostaglandin effect on gastric mucosa) |
| Cardiovascular | **FDA Boxed Warning** (ibuprofen, naproxen): increased MI/stroke risk; aspirin is the historical exception (low-dose aspirin is itself a cardioprotective drug, a genuinely different clinical role from its own OTC pain-relief dose — see [[aspirin]]'s own "one rule" callout) | Not a boxed-warning risk; one cited trial (PATH-BP) found 4g/day raised systolic BP ~5mmHg in a hypertensive/elderly population — a real but smaller-magnitude signal |
| Kidney | Reduced renal blood flow risk, especially with dehydration/CHF/pre-existing renal disease; ibuprofen NNH 5.5 for AKI in dehydrated ultramarathon runners | Not a primary NSAID-style renal risk profile in the sources reviewed; renal impairment still requires dose-interval extension |
| Liver | Not the primary risk (aspirin: avoid in severe hepatic insufficiency) | **The defining risk.** Most prevalent drug-related cause of acute liver failure in the US; ~500 deaths/year, ~50,000 ED admissions/year. Antidote (N-acetylcysteine) is time-critical — see [[red-flags]] |
| Bleeding/antiplatelet | Ibuprofen/naproxen: reversible, short-lived. Aspirin: **irreversible, ~7–10 days** — the basis of its separate cardioprotective use, and a genuine bleeding-risk consideration if combined with anticoagulants or before surgery | None — a real advantage for patients who need pain relief without adding bleeding risk (e.g., on warfarin) |
| Pediatric viral illness (flu, chickenpox) | **Aspirin specifically is contraindicated — Reye syndrome, 30–45% mortality.** Ibuprofen/naproxen do not carry this specific risk | Preferred pediatric antipyretic for exactly this reason — see [[red-flags]] |
| Pregnancy | Ibuprofen/naproxen: avoid ≥30 weeks gestation (ductus arteriosus closure risk); aspirin: OTC label warns against 3rd-trimester use generally, though low-dose (81mg) aspirin specifically is NOT associated with premature ductal closure the way other NSAIDs are | **The only OTC antipyretic/analgesic ACOG considers safe throughout pregnancy** — though a 2025 FDA-initiated label-change process around a possible (explicitly not causally established) ASD/ADHD association is a live, unresolved regulatory question, not a settled risk — see [[acetaminophen]] |
| Asthma + nasal polyps (Samter/aspirin triad) | Real risk of severe, potentially fatal bronchospasm with **any** NSAID, not just aspirin | Not implicated by this mechanism |

## A practical interaction worth knowing: ibuprofen can block aspirin's heart-protective effect

If someone takes daily **low-dose aspirin for cardioprotection** and also needs an occasional pain reliever, dosing matters:
- Ibuprofen taken 2h *before* aspirin → only 53% platelet inhibition (interaction present, aspirin's protection is blunted)
- Ibuprofen taken 8h *before* aspirin → 90.7% inhibition
- Ibuprofen taken 2h *after* aspirin → 99.2% inhibition (no meaningful interaction)

**Practical rule (per [[ibuprofen]]):** dose immediate-release aspirin at least 2 hours *before* ibuprofen, or use acetaminophen instead for occasional pain relief in someone on daily low-dose aspirin — acetaminophen does not compete with aspirin for the platelet COX-1 binding site the way ibuprofen does. Naproxen's own ingredient page does not independently confirm whether it shares ibuprofen's specific timing interaction, though it plausibly does as a same-class effect — flagged as not independently verified for naproxen in this database.

## Bottom lines

1. **On daily low-dose aspirin for heart protection?** Prefer acetaminophen for occasional pain relief, or time ibuprofen carefully (≥2h after aspirin) — see the interaction above.
2. **History of stomach ulcers, GI bleeding, kidney disease, or taking a blood thinner?** Acetaminophen avoids the GI-bleed, antiplatelet, and NSAID-class renal risks entirely — but respect the 4000mg/day liver-safe ceiling strictly, especially with any regular alcohol use.
3. **Need to reduce actual inflammation/swelling (not just pain), e.g., a sprain or arthritis flare?** An NSAID (ibuprofen, naproxen, or aspirin) is mechanistically the right tool — acetaminophen has no meaningful peripheral anti-inflammatory effect regardless of dose.
4. **Want fewer doses per day?** Naproxen's 12–17h half-life supports q8–12h dosing, versus q4–6h for the other three.
5. **Child or teenager with a current or recent viral illness (flu, chickenpox)?** Use acetaminophen or ibuprofen — **never aspirin**, and never a bismuth-subsalicylate product like Pepto-Bismol/Kaopectate either (same salicylate mechanism) — see [[red-flags]] for the Reye syndrome detail.
6. **Pregnant?** Acetaminophen is the only one of the four ACOG considers safe across all trimesters; NSAIDs are contraindicated ≥30 weeks and aspirin's OTC label advises against 3rd-trimester use generally (with the low-dose cardioprotective exception noted above).
7. **Fastest single-dose relief per the Cochrane NNT data above?** Naproxen sodium 550mg and ibuprofen 400mg edge out standard-dose aspirin (600/650mg) — aspirin needs 1200mg to reach a comparable NNT.
