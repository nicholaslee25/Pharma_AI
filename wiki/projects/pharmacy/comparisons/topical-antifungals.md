## Category snapshot

Five active ingredients treat the same three conditions — athlete's foot (tinea pedis), jock itch (tinea cruris), ringworm (tinea corporis) — but split into two mechanism families with a real, trial-confirmed efficacy gradient between them. Pharmacy Times Jock Itch/Antifungal Products (916,387/month): Lotrimin 49% (clotrimazole + a separate butenafine sub-line), Lamisil AT 28% (terbinafine), Gold Bond 9% (miconazole), Tinactin 6% (tolnaftate), FUNGICURE 5% (clotrimazole + undecylenic acid), Zeasorb 3% (miconazole). Toe/Foot Antifungal Products (1,277,521/month): Lotrimin 39%, Lamisil 32%, Fungi-Nail 8%, Fungi Cure 7%, Desenex 4% (miconazole), Tinactin 3%, Zeasorb 3%, ProClearz 2% (tolnaftate), Tineacide 2%.

## The real, trial-confirmed efficacy hierarchy

This is one of the best head-to-head-trial-documented comparisons in this entire database — the ingredient pages cite genuine RCTs putting these five agents in a real order, not just different marketing:

1. **Butenafine (Lotrimin Ultra)** — the strongest performer in every head-to-head trial cited across this database's sources. Vs. clotrimazole at 1 week: 61.7% mycological cure vs. 17.6%. Vs. terbinafine at day 42 (tinea cruris, n=76): 94.87% vs. 62.16% mycological cure, with the gap already significant by day 7 (P<0.01). Also reported superior to tolnaftate and bifonazole in the same trial literature.
2. **Terbinafine (Lamisil AT)** — a real placebo-controlled RCT (Budimulja 2001) found once-daily terbinafine 1% cream for 7 days achieved 84.2% mycological cure vs. 23.3% placebo, and a separate trial found ~94% mycological cure vs. ketoconazole 2%. Strong, but the butenafine head-to-head above shows it's not the top performer in this class.
3. **Miconazole (Gold Bond, Zeasorb AF, Desenex)** — a large Cochrane review (El-Gohary 2014, 129 trials, 18,086 participants) found all topical antifungal classes effective, but direct comparisons generally favored allylamines (terbinafine) over azoles (miconazole, clotrimazole). Miconazole specifically outperformed tolnaftate head-to-head (68% vs. 50% cure at 28 days for tinea pedis) and beat placebo (75.5% clinical clearing).
4. **Clotrimazole (Lotrimin AF)** — the category's largest single share (49%/39%) despite sitting behind butenafine, terbinafine, and miconazole in the direct comparative trials cited above. Still a real, FDA-labeled, effective option — just not the trial-strongest one.
5. **Tolnaftate (Tinactin)** — the oldest evidence base (93% mycologic eradication in small older studies, roughly equivalent to clotrimazole in older comparisons) but consistently the weakest performer in every modern head-to-head cited (behind butenafine, terbinafine, and miconazole). Per [[tolnaftate]], clinical dermatology practice has genuinely moved away from it as newer agents outperformed it in trials — stated plainly in the pharmacology literature reviewed, not just inferred here.

**A genuine irony worth naming:** the category's top-recommended brand by share (Lotrimin, 49%/39% combined across both categories) sells clotrimazole under its main "Lotrimin AF" line — a real, effective but comparatively lower-performing azole — while its own "Lotrimin Ultra" sub-line uses the trial-strongest agent in this entire comparison (butenafine). Checking which specific "Lotrimin" product is in hand matters more than the brand name alone.

## What none of these treat

All five ingredients share one important limitation: **none is effective for onychomycosis (nail fungus) or scalp infections** — clotrimazole's and tolnaftate's pages state this explicitly (nail-plate penetration failure), and it applies across the mechanism class generally. A nail or scalp fungal infection needs a different approach (oral terbinafine by prescription, per [[terbinafine]]'s oral-form data included for contrast) — not a stronger topical cream from this same list.

## Duration and dosing differences

| Ingredient | Regimen | Duration (athlete's foot / jock itch) |
|---|---|---|
| Clotrimazole | Twice daily | 4 weeks / 2 weeks |
| Terbinafine | Twice daily (between toes) or once daily (jock itch/ringworm) | 1–2 weeks / 1 week — notably shorter than clotrimazole |
| Butenafine | Per FDA OTC monograph, similar structure to clotrimazole/terbinafine | Not separately tabulated in the source reviewed beyond the trial timepoints above |
| Tolnaftate | Twice daily | 3–4 weeks |
| Miconazole | Twice daily | 2 weeks (per its own placebo trial regimen) |

Terbinafine's and butenafine's fungicidal, squalene-epoxidase mechanism gives both a real pharmacological reason for shorter courses and "residual activity after stopping" (explicitly noted on [[butenafine]]'s page, where the placebo-trial gap was largest 4 weeks *after* treatment ended) — a genuine mechanistic edge over the fungistatic azoles (clotrimazole, miconazole), which need to remain present long enough for the fungus to stop growing on its own.

## Safety and special populations

All five are topical, minimally systemically absorbed, and share a broadly similar mild local-irritation safety profile (redness, burning, itching, peeling) — no ingredient in this comparison carries a boxed warning or a serious systemic risk at labeled topical use. The real differences are narrower:
- **Pediatric age cutoffs differ by brand, not just ingredient:** clotrimazole (Lotrimin AF) is labeled for ages 2+, while butenafine (Lotrimin Ultra) — same parent brand — requires ages 12+, a real access difference within one brand family. Tolnaftate (Tinactin) and miconazole (Zeasorb AF) both restrict unsupervised use under age 2.
- **Oral clotrimazole** (a different product entirely from the topical cream, used for thrush) carries a real signal: >10% of patients on the oral troche show abnormal liver function tests — not relevant to the topical antifungal use in this comparison, but worth not confusing the two forms.
- **Drug interactions** are minimal for all five at topical use given negligible systemic absorption; clotrimazole's tacrolimus and progesterone interactions are most relevant to its oral/vaginal forms, not the topical cream compared here.

## Bottom lines

1. **Want the strongest trial-backed option?** Butenafine (Lotrimin Ultra) has the best head-to-head performance against clotrimazole, tolnaftate, and terbinafine in the trial literature reviewed — but check the specific "Lotrimin" sub-line, since the base "Lotrimin AF" is clotrimazole, a different (though also effective) ingredient.
2. **Terbinafine (Lamisil AT)** is a strong, well-evidenced second choice with a real placebo-controlled RCT behind it and shorter treatment courses than clotrimazole.
3. **Clotrimazole and miconazole** (Lotrimin AF, Gold Bond, Zeasorb AF, Desenex) remain real, FDA-effective, and by far the most-recommended options by volume — just not the trial-strongest per the comparative data cited here.
4. **Tolnaftate (Tinactin)** is the oldest, weakest-performing agent in every modern head-to-head cited, though still a real, FDA-monograph-effective option, not a placebo.
5. **None of these treat nail or scalp fungal infections** — that needs a different product/approach entirely.
6. **Check the sub-line, not just the brand.** Lotrimin, Gold Bond, and Zeasorb all sell more than one active ingredient (or, for Gold Bond/Zeasorb, a non-antifungal plain-powder line) under one brand name — see [[substitutions-and-duplicates]].
