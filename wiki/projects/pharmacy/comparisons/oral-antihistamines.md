## Category snapshot

Pharmacy Times 2026 OTC Guide, Antihistamines/Oral category (~1,700 pharmacists surveyed, 2,399,370 monthly recommendations): **Zyrtec 34%, Claritin 30%, Allegra 17%, Benadryl 8%, Xyzal 6%, Alavert 5%.** These are pharmacist *recommendation shares*, not efficacy rankings — per this database's core rule, they must never be read as a clinical response rate.

## The one comparison that actually matters: first-generation vs. second-generation

This is the single biggest, best-evidenced difference in this category — bigger than any difference between the individual second-generation agents.

| | First-generation (Benadryl / diphenhydramine) | Second-generation (Zyrtec, Claritin, Allegra, Xyzal, Alavert) |
|---|---|---|
| Blood-brain-barrier penetration | Crosses readily | Minimal |
| Sedation | Significant — this is diphenhydramine's dominant real-world OTC use as a sleep aid (see [[oral-diphenhydramine-sleep-aid]]) | Substantially less; cetirizine still carries dose-related somnolence as its most common adverse effect, but at a materially lower rate than first-generation agents |
| Anticholinergic burden | Significant — dry mouth, blurred vision, urinary retention; cumulative exposure linked to falls/delirium/dementia risk | Minimal |
| AGS Beers Criteria (older-adult prescribing guideline) | **Lists diphenhydramine by name for avoidance in most/all older adults** — allows an exception only for acute treatment of severe allergic reactions, not routine/daily allergy control | No comparable class-wide avoidance flag in the sources reviewed |
| Typical dosing interval | Every 4–6 hours (shorter-acting) | Once daily (cetirizine, loratadine, levocetirizine); fexofenadine children's formulations may be twice daily |
| Onset | Not independently sourced for the allergy indication in this database (only sourced for diphenhydramine's separate sleep-aid use — flagged as a real gap rather than assumed) | Cetirizine: 20–60 min, persists ≥24h (supports once-daily dosing) |

**Bottom line #1:** for routine, ongoing allergy-symptom control, second-generation agents are the better default — not because Benadryl doesn't work, but because its sedation and anticholinergic burden are real, well-documented costs that the second-generation agents mostly avoid. Benadryl's genuine remaining niche per the Beers Criteria's own carve-out is fast-acting treatment of an acute severe allergic reaction, not daily maintenance — and it should be avoided altogether in older adults for anything other than that acute use.

## Within second-generation agents: does the brand actually matter?

**Vieira et al. 2026 network meta-analysis (PMID 41707943)** — 74 RCTs (21 perennial allergic rhinitis, 53 seasonal), comparing oral H1-antihistamines head-to-head and against placebo:
- **Cetirizine, ebastine, bilastine, and rupatadine** trended as the individual agents with the highest efficacy for nasal symptoms specifically (note: bilastine, ebastine, and rupatadine are not sold under the Pharmacy Times-tracked brands in this table).
- The most-efficacious agent varied by outcome (nasal symptoms vs. ocular symptoms vs. quality of life) — no single drug won on every measure.
- Adverse-event frequency was similar across second-generation agents; serious adverse events were rare across the board.
- **The authors' own conclusion: differences between individual second-generation antihistamines are mostly trivial or small, with no relevant safety-profile differences found.** Certainty of evidence for most head-to-head comparisons was rated low or very low.

**Bottom line #2:** within the second-generation class, brand/molecule choice matters far less than commonly assumed. Cetirizine (Zyrtec, and its enantiomer levocetirizine in Xyzal) trends slightly favorably for nasal symptoms specifically, but this database has not independently pulled loratadine (Claritin/Alavert)- or fexofenadine (Allegra)-specific StatPearls/FDA-label data beyond what's summarized here — see the Coverage Gap note below.

## Practical differentiators, where they exist

- **Renal/hepatic impairment:** cetirizine (Zyrtec/Xyzal, since levocetirizine is cetirizine's active enantiomer) has an explicit dose-reduction schedule (5mg/day at ≥12y for hepatic impairment or CrCl 11–31 mL/min) — this database does not have equivalent sourced dose-adjustment data for loratadine or fexofenadine.
- **Drug interaction potential:** cetirizine is not a CYP450 substrate (lower interaction potential than many drugs) but is a P-glycoprotein substrate — verapamil and erdafitinib can increase its CNS penetration. No equivalent interaction data is sourced in this database for loratadine or fexofenadine specifically.
- **Pregnancy:** ACOG/ACAAI support cetirizine when antihistamine treatment is genuinely needed in pregnancy, though some older guidance still considers first-generation antihistamines the historically "safest" option in pregnancy specifically — a real, preserved tension rather than a single settled answer (see [[second-generation-antihistamines]]).
- **Breastfeeding:** occasional/small cetirizine doses are considered acceptable; the OTC label itself says "not recommended" while breastfeeding — both statements are preserved in the source ingredient page rather than resolved into one.
- **Xyzal (levocetirizine)** is chemically the active enantiomer of cetirizine (Zyrtec) — not an independent molecule, though this database has not independently sourced whether this yields any clinically meaningful difference in side-effect rate at equivalent dosing.
- **Zyrtec-D** (cetirizine + pseudoephedrine) is a separate combination product holding 5% of the Decongestants, Oral category — see [[pseudoephedrine]] for that component's own efficacy/safety profile and MAOI contraindication.

## Coverage gap, stated plainly

This database's [[second-generation-antihistamines]] ingredient page was built using **cetirizine (Zyrtec) as the representative agent** for StatPearls/FDA-label sourcing, since it holds the largest Pharmacy Times share in this class. Loratadine (Claritin, Alavert) and fexofenadine (Allegra) have their own separate real-world pharmacology (e.g., fexofenadine's distinct absorption/food-interaction profile is well-known in the broader medical literature) that has **not yet been independently pulled into this database**. This comparison page reflects that limitation rather than inventing brand-specific data — the class-level "second-gen beats first-gen for routine use" conclusion is solid, but a reader wanting a rigorous Claritin-vs-Allegra-vs-Zyrtec efficacy comparison specifically should treat the Vieira 2026 network-meta-analysis finding above (differences are mostly trivial) as the best currently-sourced answer.
