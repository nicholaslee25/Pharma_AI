## Category snapshot

Every FDA OTC sunscreen sold in the US uses one or both of two fundamentally different UV-filtering approaches. Pharmacy Times Sunscreen category (1,197,479/month): Neutrogena 27%, CeraVe 11%, Coppertone 11%, Blue Lizard Australian Sunscreen 9%, La Roche-Posay 7%, Aveeno 6%, Banana Boat 6%, Hawaiian Tropic 6%, Sun Bum 6%, Cetaphil 4%, COOLA 4%, Babyganics 2%, Vanicream 1%.

## The FDA's own 2019 verdict, stated plainly

Per [[chemical-sunscreen-filters]], the FDA's February 2019 proposed rule reviewed all 16 sunscreen actives then on the US market and split them three ways: zinc oxide/titanium dioxide affirmed GRASE (Generally Recognized As Safe and Effective); PABA and trolamine salicylate found **not** GRASE (no longer used); and the 12 remaining, including all six chemical filters compared here, left in **regulatory limbo** — not unsafe, not ineffective, just insufficiently studied on systemic-absorption, carcinogenicity, and developmental/reproductive toxicity grounds. This is a real, FDA-documented data gap, not a settled safety verdict against chemical filters, and it's stated in these terms because overstating it either direction (declaring chemical filters "dangerous" or dismissing the gap entirely) would misrepresent the source.

The Matta et al. 2020 JAMA trial gives the concrete number behind that data gap: all six chemical filters tested, in all four commercial products, produced blood plasma concentrations exceeding the FDA's own safety-study trigger threshold after a single application — oxybenzone reaching the highest levels (up to 258.1 ng/mL). The study's own authors state the clinical significance of this is undetermined — exceeding a conservative threshold designed to trigger further study is not the same as demonstrated harm — but it's a real, quantified finding, not a hypothetical one.

Mineral filters have a materially more reassuring systemic-exposure picture: modern nanoparticle-sized zinc oxide/titanium dioxide show minimal skin penetration (<0.03% of applied zinc reaching the epidermis in the cited study) and current evidence assessment finds neither likely to cause harm as directed. [[Mineral-sunscreen-filters]] notes this is not a perfectly apples-to-apples comparison — skin-penetration studies for minerals vs. direct blood-plasma RCTs for chemical filters measure different things — but the directional contrast is real.

## Skin cancer prevention — genuinely mixed evidence, stated honestly

Both filter classes share the same efficacy evidence base since the trials generally didn't separate chemical from mineral formulations: the Nambour Trial (the only RCT of sunscreen for melanoma prevention) found a statistically significant protective effect for invasive melanoma specifically (3 vs. 11 cases) but not for all melanomas combined; a 2015 Cochrane review found support for reduced squamous cell carcinoma with regular use but a pooled case-control meta-analysis did **not** confirm that sunscreen reduces melanoma risk at the population level. Per [[chemical-sunscreen-filters]]: "the human evidence base for melanoma specifically is smaller and more mixed than commonly assumed" — worth knowing precisely because "sunscreen prevents skin cancer" is often treated as fully settled when the RCT evidence is real but narrower than that.

## Practical differences that actually drive a choice

- **Reef/environmental concern:** mineral filters (zinc oxide, titanium dioxide) are not subject to Hawaii's ban on oxybenzone/octinoxate and are commonly marketed "reef-safe" on that basis.
- **UVA coverage:** among chemical filters, avobenzone is the only one providing meaningful UVA1 (long-UVA) coverage, but it's photounstable and needs a stabilizer (usually octocrylene) to avoid degrading in sunlight. Zinc oxide alone provides the broadest single-ingredient UVA coverage of any FDA-monograph active, chemical or mineral — a real edge for anyone wanting one ingredient to do the whole job.
- **Contact irritation:** oxybenzone specifically carries a documented, if uncommon, contact-allergy risk noted on [[chemical-sunscreen-filters]].
- **Systemic-absorption caution (pregnancy, infants, chronic daily use):** the reassuring nanoparticle-penetration data makes mineral filters the more conservative choice where minimizing systemic exposure is a priority, though neither ingredient page in this database states an outright contraindication for chemical filters in these populations — this is a risk-minimization preference, not a documented restriction.

## A real brand-label trap in this exact category

Per [[pharmacy overview]]'s own build notes: **Sun Bum's "Daily Mineral Sunscreen" was confirmed to contain zero mineral filters** — only the brand's standard chemical-filter combination — despite the product name. This is a genuine, confirmed brand-specific mismatch, not a hypothetical risk; if the presence of mineral vs. chemical filters is the deciding factor, check the Drug Facts active-ingredients panel, not the product name, on any SKU.

## Bottom lines

1. **Want the FDA's most safety-data-confident actives?** Zinc oxide and titanium dioxide are the only two of 16 reviewed ingredients affirmed GRASE — a real, meaningful regulatory distinction worth looking for on the label.
2. **Chemical filters are not declared unsafe** — they remain legally marketed and effective — but they do carry a real, unresolved FDA data gap and a documented systemic-absorption finding that mineral filters do not share to the same degree.
3. **Reef-conscious or minimizing systemic exposure (pregnancy, young children, daily long-term use)?** Mineral filters (zinc oxide, titanium dioxide) are the more conservative choice on both counts.
4. **Either class works for basic sunburn prevention and photodamage reduction** — the RCT evidence for cancer prevention specifically is real but stronger for squamous cell carcinoma than for melanoma, regardless of filter type.
5. **Check the ingredients panel, not the product name** — a "mineral" or "reef-safe"-branded product is not guaranteed to be mineral-only, as the confirmed Sun Bum finding above shows.
