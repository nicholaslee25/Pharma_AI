## Category snapshot

Five distinct mechanisms get marketed as "laxatives," and they are not interchangeable — some have real trial evidence behind them, and one of the most commonly used has none. Pharmacy Times monthly recommendations across the four tracked categories: Laxatives Nonfiber 1,881,590 (MiraLAX 63%, Dulcolax 27%, Fleet 5%, Phillips' 5%), Stool Softeners 1,585,084 (Colace 61%, MiraLAX 18%, Dulcolax 17%, Phillips' Stool Softener 4%), Laxatives Stimulant 1,368,277 (Dulcolax 42%, Senokot 33%, Colace 2-IN-1 15%, Ex-Lax 5%, Fleet 5%), Laxatives Bulk/Fiber 1,285,714 (Metamucil 56%, Benefiber 20%, Citrucel 8%, FiberCon 7%, Konsyl 3%, Mirafiber 3%, Vitafusion Fiber Well 2%, Phillips' Fiber Good 1%).

|---|---|---|---|
| Bulk/fiber ([[fiber]]) | Metamucil (psyllium) | Days (needs ≥4 weeks for full effect per the cited meta-analysis) | Real, quantified: RR 1.48 for psyllium/pectin >10g/day ≥4 weeks — but NOT a class-wide fiber effect |
| Stimulant — senna ([[senna]]) | Senokot | 6–12h | Established mechanism, moderate evidence; long-term structural colonic changes flagged |

## The single most important finding in this category: docusate's evidence gap

Colace holds **61% of the entire Stool Softeners category** — the largest single-brand share of any laxative mechanism in this comparison — yet per [[docusate]], StatPearls states plainly that "numerous systematic reviews examining the efficacy of docusate have yielded no significant evidence to support its use," including a 2021 review of 7 RCTs finding no significant difference from placebo, and that professional organizations have recommended discontinuing its routine use in favor of better-evidenced alternatives. Docusate also has **no FDA-approved indication at all** — it is used per product monograph, not FDA approval. This is a genuine, sourced disconnect between recommendation volume and trial evidence, not a hedge — stated here as plainly as [[docusate]]'s own page states it.

By contrast, [[peg-3350]] (MiraLAX) has a real placebo-controlled RCT behind it (n=203, p<0.0001 for reduced stool hardness/exertion) and is the preferred agent for chronic constipation per StatPearls, and [[fiber]]'s psyllium/pectin-specific meta-analysis (RR 1.48, 16 RCTs, n=1,251) is likewise a real, quantified positive finding — but note that finding is specific to **psyllium and pectin**, not fiber generally: most of the Bulk/Fiber category's actual volume (Benefiber/wheat dextrin, Citrucel/methylcellulose, FiberCon/calcium polycarbophil, Mirafiber/inulin — together the majority of that category's share) goes to fiber types the cited meta-analysis did NOT show significant benefit for.

## Onset-of-action — matching the mechanism to the need

- **Need relief within hours (occasional, acute constipation)?** Magnesium hydroxide (Milk of Magnesia, ½–6h) or bisacodyl (6–12h oral, 15–60min rectal) act fastest.
- **Chronic/recurring constipation, want a sustainable daily approach?** PEG 3350 (MiraLAX) or psyllium fiber (Metamucil) — both slower to start (1–3 days / needs ≥4 weeks respectively) but built for ongoing use, with real trial support behind each.
- **Painful defecation to avoid straining (post-surgery, hemorrhoids, cardiac patients), not actual constipation relief?** This is docusate's specific rationale niche per its own page — softening stool consistency for comfort, a different goal than resolving true constipation, and worth separating from "does it cure constipation" given the efficacy controversy above.

## Safety differentiators

| Risk | Fiber | Senna/Bisacodyl (stimulant) | PEG 3350 (osmotic) | Magnesium hydroxide (saline) | Docusate (softener) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Most-cited adverse effect | Flatulence (significantly more than control, SMD 0.80) | Abdominal pain; senna's long-term use linked to loss of colonic haustral folds; bisacodyl: 45% of frequent (>3x/week, ≥1yr) users showed radiographic colonic redundancy/dilation | Flatulence, nausea, cramps, diarrhea | Diarrhea/dehydration at excess dose | Anorexia, diarrhea, vomiting at excess dose |
| Serious/rare risk | Not detailed beyond flatulence in the source reviewed | In-vitro neoplastic signal (human data lacking, not established); bisacodyl: electrolyte imbalance, colonic ischemia (mechanism unclear) | Pediatric neuropsychiatric FDA signal with prolonged use (seizures, tics, mood/behavior changes); rare metabolic acidosis/renal tubular necrosis | **Hypermagnesemia — real, documented, sometimes fatal case reports**, especially in renal impairment/elderly: CNS depression, respiratory depression, cardiac arrest in severe cases | Laxative dependency with chronic/excessive use (hypokalemia, hypomagnesemia, metabolic acidosis) |
| Key contraindication | None itemized in source | Not itemized senna-specifically; bisacodyl: bowel obstruction, ileus, appendicitis, severe dehydration/electrolyte imbalance | Known/suspected bowel obstruction, IBD, perforated bowel | **Renal impairment/CKD/dialysis — should avoid unless physician-directed**, per case-report literature | Nausea/vomiting, intestinal obstruction, fecal impaction; caution combined with mineral oil (increased systemic absorption risk) |
| Max duration without medical guidance | Not specified in source | 1 week (Senokot label) | 7 days OTC (label); up to 2 weeks per StatPearls | Not detailed with a specific cap in the source reviewed | 1 week (Colace label) |
| Pregnancy | Not addressed with population-specific data in source | Ask a health professional (label); one non-independently-verified LactMed note suggests usual doses acceptable in breastfeeding | **AAFP's drug of choice for chronic constipation in pregnancy** | Ask a health professional (label); not independently detailed | Limited data; one case report of neonatal hypomagnesemia from chronic maternal use |

## Brand-name traps specific to this category

[[Substitutions-and-duplicates]] covers this in full, but the laxative aisle is one of the worst offenders for one-brand-multiple-mechanisms:
- **Dulcolax** sells both a bisacodyl stimulant line and a "Dulcolax Stool Softener"/"Dulcolax Pink" docusate line — same brand, opposite ends of the evidence spectrum (bisacodyl is guideline-endorsed; docusate has no FDA-approved indication).
- **Phillips'** spans magnesium hydroxide (Milk of Magnesia, a fast saline laxative) and "Phillips' Stool Softener" (plain docusate) and "Phillips' Fiber Good" (inulin fiber gummies) — three different mechanisms under one name.
- **Colace** itself is docusate, but "Colace 2-IN-1" adds sennosides — a real, deliberate dual-mechanism combination, not the same product as plain Colace.
- **MiraLAX vs. MiraFIBER**: PEG 3350 (real RCT evidence) vs. an inulin fiber gummy — mechanistically unrelated products sharing a brand prefix.

## Bottom lines

1. **Widely-used does not equal well-evidenced here.** Docusate (Colace, 61% share of Stool Softeners) is the clearest example in this entire database of high recommendation volume paired with a stated lack of trial evidence — a 2021 review of 7 RCTs found no significant difference from placebo, and it carries no FDA-approved indication at all.
2. **For real, quantified efficacy, PEG 3350 (MiraLAX) or psyllium fiber (Metamucil) specifically** are this category's best-evidenced choices per the sources reviewed — but expect a days-to-weeks timeline, not immediate relief.
3. **For fast relief of an acute, occasional episode,** magnesium hydroxide or bisacodyl act within hours — but check kidney function first with magnesium hydroxide specifically, given its real, sometimes-fatal hypermagnesemia case-report history in renal impairment.
4. **Check the label, not just the brand name,** before buying anything in this category — Dulcolax, Phillips', and Colace 2-IN-1 all span genuinely different mechanisms under one recognizable name.
5. **None of these are meant for open-ended daily use without medical guidance** — every ingredient reviewed here caps OTC self-use at roughly 1–2 weeks, and several (senna, bisacodyl, PEG 3350 in children) carry specific long-term-use safety signals.
