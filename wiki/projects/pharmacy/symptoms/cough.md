## Start here: [[cough-suppressant-vs-expectorant]]

That comparison page carries the full detail on dextromethorphan, guaifenesin, and honey — including the single most important honest finding for this symptom: **neither dextromethorphan's nor guaifenesin's best-cited RCT in this database found a clear subjective/real-world symptom benefit over placebo**, despite both being FDA-approved, widely marketed OTC actives. Honey, by contrast, has the most favorable placebo-controlled Cochrane evidence of the three — but only for short-term (≤3 days) use in children ≥1 year.

---

## Adult vs. pediatric decision, in brief

- **Adult, dry/disruptive cough:** dextromethorphan (Delsym, 54% of Cough Suppressants category) has a real, measurable effect on cough-reflex sensitivity even though its best trial didn't show a clear edge over placebo on what patients actually notice.
- **Adult, productive/chest-congestion cough:** guaifenesin (Mucinex, 74% of Expectorants category) is FDA-approved for this, though the most rigorous objective trial cited found no significant effect vs. placebo on mucociliary/cough clearance.
- **Child ≥1 year:** honey has the strongest placebo-controlled evidence of the three ingredients on this page for short-term nighttime cough relief — genuinely worth considering as a first option before an OTC drug product, subject to the 3-day effectiveness window.
- **Infant <12 months:** neither an OTC cough drug (dextromethorphan not for <4y; guaifenesin not for <12y per the Mucinex label, FDA/AAP against use <4y generally) nor honey (absolutely contraindicated — infantile botulism risk) is appropriate. See a pediatrician instead.

---

## Safety flags specific to this symptom

- **MAOIs:** avoid dextromethorphan-containing products — real serotonin syndrome risk.
- **Kidney stones:** avoid guaifenesin specifically — it's contraindicated with a kidney-stone history.
- **Under 12 months:** honey is an absolute, non-dose-dependent contraindication (infantile botulism), categorically different from the other two ingredients' dose-related pediatric age cutoffs.
- **Brand-name ambiguity:** "Robitussin" spans both plain dextromethorphan and guaifenesin+dextromethorphan (Robitussin DM) formulations — check the specific SKU.

## When a cough points somewhere else

A cough accompanied by sinus pressure/congestion may respond better to treating the congestion — see [[nasal-congestion]]. A cough with a headache is covered from the headache side on [[headache]]'s sinus-pressure section.

## Seek care if — pointer to Layer 5

From this database's own sourced content (see [[red-flags]] for full detail):
- Cough with high fever, difficulty breathing, or chest pain — beyond simple cold-related cough, warrants medical evaluation.
- A cough persisting well beyond a typical cold's course, or one that produces blood.
- Infant under 12 months with any cough — do not self-treat with an OTC drug or honey; see a pediatrician.
- Any rash, hives, facial/throat swelling, or breathing difficulty after a cough medication — possible allergic reaction — emergency.
