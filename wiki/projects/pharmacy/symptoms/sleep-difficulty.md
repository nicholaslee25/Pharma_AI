## Start here: [[sleep-aids]]

That comparison page carries the full detail on diphenhydramine, doxylamine, and melatonin. The short version: a systematic review found first-generation antihistamine sleep aids (diphenhydramine, doxylamine) "generally lack robust clinical evidence supporting efficacy and safety" for occasional sleeplessness, despite FDA recognition as safe/effective OTC options. Melatonin has the most quantified positive trial evidence of the three (a 2024 dose-response meta-analysis, 26 RCTs) — but the common real-world dosing pattern (2mg at bedtime) is likely suboptimal versus the evidence-derived ideal (4mg, 3 hours before bed).

---

## The single most important safety flag for this symptom

**Both diphenhydramine and doxylamine are flagged by the AGS Beers Criteria for avoidance in most/all older adults** — highly anticholinergic, reduced clearance with age, tolerance develops with regular hypnotic use, and cumulative exposure is linked to falls, delirium, and dementia risk. This directly implicates "PM" combination products (Tylenol PM, Advil PM, Aleve PM) and dedicated sleep aids (Unisom, ZzzQuil, Sominex, Simply Sleep) commonly used by exactly this population. If sleep difficulty is a recurring problem for an older adult specifically, melatonin is the pharmacologically gentler starting point of the three compared here — though it carries its own fall/fracture risk signal and a real, documented content-accuracy problem in unregulated supplements (one cited study found actual melatonin content ranging from -83% to +478% of label).

---

## Why "occasional" matters

Every ingredient page in this comparison is scoped to *occasional* sleeplessness, not chronic insomnia — tolerance to diphenhydramine/doxylamine's hypnotic effect develops with regular use (undermining even short-term benefit), and melatonin's evidence base is strongest for sleep-onset/duration in the studied trial populations, not as a long-term nightly solution. Recurring, persistent sleep difficulty is a reasonable prompt to talk to a doctor rather than escalate self-treatment.

---

## Bottom lines for this specific symptom

1. **One bad night, need something now?** Diphenhydramine (up to 50mg) or doxylamine (25mg) are FDA-recognized OTC options — but weigh the Beers Criteria flag if the user is an older adult.
2. **Want the best-quantified evidence, and can plan ahead?** Melatonin at 4mg, dosed 3 hours before the desired bedtime, per the cited meta-analysis — not the common 2mg-at-bedtime pattern.
3. **Combining with a pain reliever?** "PM" combination products all use diphenhydramine as the sleep-aid component — the real choice is which analgesic (acetaminophen, ibuprofen, naproxen) to pair it with.
4. **Buying melatonin?** Look for a USP-Verified product given the documented content-accuracy problem, and keep it stored the way any medication would be — pediatric melatonin exposure cases rose sharply (2012–2021) per [[melatonin]].
