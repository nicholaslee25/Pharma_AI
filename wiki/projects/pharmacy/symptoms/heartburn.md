## How common this is, and the three-tier toolkit

Per [[antacids]], North American heartburn prevalence is 18–28%, with 25% of adults reporting daily heartburn — a large, mostly self-treated population. See [[gi-acid-reducers]] for the full head-to-head comparison; this page summarizes the decision logic specifically for the heartburn symptom itself.

Three classes, three speeds:
1. **Antacids** (Tums, Rolaids, Gaviscon, Mylanta) — neutralize acid already present, relief in minutes, gone in 1–2 hours. Best for an occasional, already-happening episode.
2. **H2 blockers** ([[famotidine]] — Pepcid) — dosed 10–60 minutes *before* a known trigger, covers ~10–12 hours. A middle ground between speed and duration.
3. **[[Proton-pump-inhibitors|PPIs]]** (omeprazole/Prilosec OTC and siblings) — not a rescue drug at all; built for frequent heartburn (≥2 days/week), takes 1–4 days for full effect, dosed once daily for a full 14-day course.

**A genuinely useful and lesser-known option:** [[bismuth-subsalicylate]] (Pepto-Bismol) is also FDA-approved specifically for heartburn/dyspepsia/indigestion in the same label as its antidiarrheal/nausea uses (see [[diarrhea]], [[nausea-and-motion-sickness]]) — a real multi-symptom option, though it carries the salicylate-specific Reye syndrome consideration in children/teens that the antacid/H2-blocker/PPI classes do not share.

## The honest efficacy ceiling, even at the top of the toolkit

Per [[proton-pump-inhibitors]], StatPearls reports that **roughly 50% of patients taking a PPI for non-erosive GERD do not achieve complete symptom resolution.** Even the most potent acid-suppression class available OTC has a real, substantial gap — worth knowing before assuming escalating to "the strongest option" guarantees relief.

---

## When frequency, not intensity, is the deciding factor

The FDA's own OTC PPI labeling frames the decision around **frequency**, not severity: heartburn ≥2 days/week for the omeprazole indication specifically. An occasional bad meal calls for an antacid or famotidine; heartburn that's become a near-daily pattern is the labeled use case for a PPI course — and per [[gi-acid-reducers]], that's also the point at which seeing a doctor about the underlying GERD, rather than repeatedly self-treating, becomes reasonable.

## Seek care if — pointer to Layer 5

From this database's own sourced content (see [[red-flags]] for full detail):
- Difficulty or pain swallowing, vomiting blood, or bloody/black stools — all three acid-reducer OTC labels (antacids, famotidine, PPIs) explicitly warn against self-treating these symptoms; they suggest a condition beyond simple heartburn.
- Chest pain with shortness of breath, or chest pain radiating to the arm/jaw — cardiac causes can mimic heartburn, and this is an emergency, not a self-treatment decision.
- Heartburn not improving after a PPI's full 14-day OTC course, or needing to repeat that course more often than the label allows (every 4 months) — time to see a doctor rather than continuing to self-treat.
- A child or teenager with a current/recent viral illness — avoid bismuth subsalicylate (Reye syndrome risk) — see [[red-flags]].
