## How common this is

Per [[senna]], chronic idiopathic constipation affects an estimated 8–12% of the US population. See [[laxatives]] for the full head-to-head comparison across mechanisms; this page summarizes the decision logic specifically for the constipation symptom.

## Matching the mechanism to the timeline

- **Need relief within hours?** Magnesium hydroxide (Milk of Magnesia, ½–6h) or bisacodyl (6–12h oral, 15–60min rectal) act fastest — but check kidney function first with magnesium hydroxide specifically (see below).
- **Ongoing/recurring constipation, want the best-evidenced daily approach?** PEG 3350 (MiraLAX) has a real placebo-controlled RCT behind it (n=203, p<0.0001) and is StatPearls' preferred agent for chronic constipation. Psyllium fiber (Metamucil) has a real, quantified meta-analysis benefit (RR 1.48, 16 RCTs) — but specifically for psyllium/pectin at >10g/day for ≥4 weeks, not fiber supplements generally.
- **Painful defecation to avoid straining (post-surgery, hemorrhoids, cardiac patients), not true constipation relief?** Docusate's rationale niche — but see the honest efficacy caveat below before reaching for it as a first choice.

## The most important honest finding in this symptom's toolkit

**Docusate (Colace) holds 61% of the Stool Softeners category** — the single largest share of any laxative brand in this database — **despite having no FDA-approved indication and a 2021 review of 7 RCTs finding no significant difference from placebo.** This is a real, sourced disconnect between how often it's recommended and what the trial evidence shows, worth knowing before defaulting to the most familiar name in this category. See [[laxatives]] and [[docusate]] for the full detail.

## Safety notes specific to this symptom

- **Magnesium hydroxide should be avoided by anyone with reduced kidney function** without physician direction — real, documented, sometimes-fatal hypermagnesemia case reports exist, particularly in renal impairment and the elderly.
- **Senna and bisacodyl** are not meant for open-ended daily use — OTC labels cap self-use at about 1 week, and long-term frequent use is associated with real structural colonic changes (loss of haustral folds, radiographic colonic redundancy/dilation in 45% of frequent long-term users per [[bisacodyl]]).
- **PEG 3350 carries a real FDA-reported pediatric neuropsychiatric signal** (seizures, tics, mood/behavior changes) with prolonged off-label pediatric use — worth monitoring for, not a reason to avoid outright given how commonly it's used in children.
- **Pregnancy:** PEG 3350 is AAFP's explicit drug of choice for chronic constipation in pregnancy; see [[pregnancy]] for the full tiered breakdown across this category's ingredients.

## Seek care if — pointer to Layer 5

From this database's own sourced content (see [[red-flags]] for full detail):
- Rectal bleeding, or no bowel movement at all after using a laxative as directed — both senna's and docusate's OTC labels specifically direct stopping and seeing a doctor for these signs.
- Severe abdominal pain, distension, vomiting, or suspected bowel obstruction — a contraindication for every laxative mechanism in this comparison, not a reason to try a different one.
- Constipation lasting beyond the OTC label's stated duration (commonly about 1 week) without improvement.
- Reduced kidney function and considering magnesium hydroxide — check with a doctor first given the real hypermagnesemia risk documented above.
