## How to read this page

Unlike pregnancy or kidney function, "on a blood thinner" covers a genuinely varied group — warfarin (a vitamin-K antagonist with routine INR monitoring), DOACs (apixaban, rivaroxaban, etc.), and antiplatelet drugs (clopidogrel, low-dose aspirin itself). This database's sourced ingredient pages mostly discuss "anticoagulants" or "warfarin" generically rather than distinguishing DOAC-specific data — that generic framing is preserved below rather than invented distinctions the sources don't support. The unifying practical concern across all of them is the same: **anything that adds bleeding risk or affects clotting-factor activity compounds with a blood thinner's own intended effect.**

## Tier 1 — Preferred / lowest added bleeding risk

| Ingredient | Note | Source |
|---|---|---|
| [[acetaminophen]] | The preferred OTC pain/fever option for someone on a blood thinner — no antiplatelet effect and no direct COX-mediated GI bleeding mechanism, a real structural advantage over the NSAID class. **Caveat, not a clean pass:** prolonged use at 4000mg/day is associated with elevated INR — warfarin monitoring frequency should increase even with this "safer" choice, not be abandoned entirely | [[acetaminophen]], [[high-risk-combinations]] |

## Tier 2 — Real, sourced interaction risk — use with caution and medical awareness

| Ingredient | Note | Source |
|---|---|---|
| [[ibuprofen]] | Does not significantly affect warfarin's PT/clotting-factor activity directly in controlled studies — but **combined GI bleeding risk is synergistic (higher than either drug alone)**. Also directly competes with aspirin at the platelet COX-1 binding site — relevant if the "blood thinner" in question is actually low-dose aspirin itself (see the timing rule on [[oral-pain-relievers]]) | [[ibuprofen]], [[high-risk-combinations]] |
| [[naproxen]] | Same NSAID-class bleeding-risk-compounding logic as ibuprofen; naproxen's own page does not independently confirm whether it shares ibuprofen's specific antiplatelet-timing interaction with aspirin, though it plausibly does as a same-class effect | [[naproxen]], [[oral-pain-relievers]] |
| Topical NSAIDs ([[topical-diclofenac]]) | Even at low systemic absorption, the label specifically calls out higher stomach-bleeding risk with anticoagulant use as a carried-over class warning — worth knowing that "topical" does not mean "risk-free" here | [[topical-diclofenac]] |
| [[zinc]] (lozenges, e.g. Cold-Eeze) | Manufacturer label specifically flags a warfarin interaction — ask a doctor before use | [[zinc]] |
| [[omega-3-fish-oil]] | A bleeding-time consideration is noted as relevant to concurrent anticoagulant/antiplatelet use, though not independently confirmed as a clinically significant interaction in the specific source reviewed for this database — a real, if not fully quantified, caution | [[omega-3-fish-oil]] |
| [[garlic]] (supplement form) | Historically discussed elsewhere as carrying potential antiplatelet/bleeding-risk interactions, but this was **not independently confirmed via a primary source** in this database's own research — flagged as an unconfirmed-but-plausible caution rather than an established one | [[garlic]] |
| [[coq10]] | General pharmacology knowledge suggests a theoretical warfarin interaction via vitamin-K-like structural similarity, but this was **not independently confirmed via a primary source** in this pass — same unconfirmed-but-plausible status as garlic above | [[coq10]] |

## Tier 3 — Avoid, or avoid without explicit medical direction

| Ingredient | Why | Source |
|---|---|---|
| [[aspirin]] (general OTC pain/fever use, distinct from doctor-directed low-dose cardioprotective use) | Aspirin's own Drug Interactions section names anticoagulants (warfarin, heparin) and antiplatelets (clopidogrel, dipyridamole) directly, with a synergistic bleeding-risk warning — and aspirin's own irreversible antiplatelet effect (lasting the ~7–10 day platelet lifespan) means it is functionally adding a second blood thinner on top of the first, not just a pain reliever | [[aspirin]], [[high-risk-combinations]] |
| [[bismuth-subsalicylate]] (Pepto-Bismol, Kaopectate) | A salicylate, same family as aspirin — its own Contraindications section specifically lists concurrent anticoagulants as a reason to avoid, and it further compounds if combined with an aspirin-containing product (salicylate stacking) | [[bismuth-subsalicylate]], [[high-risk-combinations]] |

## Aspirin's other specific, sourced interactions worth naming for this population

Per [[aspirin]] and [[high-risk-combinations]]: **methotrexate** (decreased renal clearance, increased toxicity), **SSRIs** (synergistic upper GI bleeding risk via platelet serotonin depletion — a real, separate mechanism from the anticoagulant interaction above), and **valproate/phenytoin** (aspirin displaces both from protein binding). None of these are specific to warfarin, but all are relevant if a patient on a blood thinner is also taking one of these other drug classes.

## Bottom lines

1. **Pain/fever while on a blood thinner:** acetaminophen is the clearly preferred OTC option — but don't treat it as risk-free at sustained high doses; INR monitoring frequency should still increase with regular use.
2. **Avoid the NSAID class (ibuprofen, naproxen) and aspirin for routine pain/fever** — all three compound bleeding risk with an anticoagulant, and aspirin specifically adds its own separate, irreversible antiplatelet effect on top of whatever blood thinner is already prescribed.
3. **Avoid Pepto-Bismol/Kaopectate (bismuth subsalicylate)** for upset stomach/diarrhea if on a blood thinner — it's a salicylate with a directly sourced anticoagulant contraindication; consider [[loperamide]] instead for diarrhea specifically (not independently flagged with an anticoagulant interaction in this database's sources).
4. **Cold remedies:** check zinc lozenges specifically — a real, labeled warfarin interaction exists, easy to overlook in a product that doesn't read as a "blood thinner risk" item.
5. **Supplements are not automatically exempt:** fish oil, garlic, and CoQ10 all carry a plausible-but-not-fully-confirmed bleeding-risk or warfarin-interaction concern in the sources reviewed — worth a pharmacist check before starting any of them alongside a blood thinner, even though this database can't assert the interaction as definitively established for garlic/CoQ10 specifically.
6. **Low-dose aspirin itself is often part of the "blood thinner" regimen, not separate from it** — see [[pregnancy]]'s and [[oral-pain-relievers]]'s own treatment of low-dose cardioprotective aspirin as a distinct, medically-directed use case from general OTC pain-relief dosing.
