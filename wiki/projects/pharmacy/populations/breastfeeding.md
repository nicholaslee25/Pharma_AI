# Breastfeeding — OTC Decision Guide

**Type:** Analysis (special-population synthesis)
**Verification Status:** AI-compiled from primary sources, unverified — not yet reviewed by a pharmacist/physician
**Last updated:** 2026-08-28
**Sources:** Synthesized entirely from the "Special Population Flags" (and related Contraindications/Key Safety Data) sections already sourced across `ingredients/*.md` pages in this database. No new primary research was performed for this page — every judgment below traces to a specific ingredient page, linked inline.

---

## How to read this page

Same structure and caveat as [[pregnancy]]: ingredients are grouped by what this database's own sourced content (StatPearls, FDA labels, manufacturer data) actually says about lactation, not a fresh clinical judgment made for this page. A real, recurring pattern in the sources reviewed for this page is that **breastfeeding-specific data is frequently thinner than pregnancy-specific data** for the same ingredient — several ingredient pages state plainly that no dedicated breastfeeding study was located, which is different from a finding of risk. That gap is preserved rather than papered over below.

**This page is not a substitute for a lactation consultant's or pediatrician's guidance** — it exists to organize what this database already knows.

## Tier 1 — Generally considered safe / preferred while breastfeeding

| Ingredient | Note | Source |
|---|---|---|
| [[acetaminophen]] | Considered suitable; breast milk levels significantly lower than typical infant doses | [[acetaminophen]] |
| [[ibuprofen]] | StatPearls (2024) calls it a **preferred NSAID for nursing mothers** — relative infant dose <0.38% of maternal dose (up to 0.6% during colostral phase). | [[bisacodyl]] | Safe without special precautions — active metabolite undetectable in breast milk | [[bisacodyl]] |
| [[docusate]] | Minimally absorbed from the GI tract, unlikely to be present in breast milk | [[docusate]] |
| [[peg-3350]] | Minimal systemic absorption implies low clinically significant breast milk excretion | [[peg-3350]] |
| [[famotidine]] | Appears in breast milk at **lower concentrations than other H2RAs** — a potentially preferable H2RA choice during lactation; available evidence suggests unlikely to harm the breastfed infant | [[famotidine]] |

| [[guaifenesin]] | No dedicated breastmilk-excretion studies located per StatPearls; a separate, not-independently-verified LactMed reference reportedly considers usual maternal OTC doses unlikely to harm a nursing infant, especially beyond 2 months postpartum | [[guaifenesin]] |

| Topical [[benzoyl-peroxide]] | Insufficient formal data; topical use generally considered safe given low systemic absorption, but avoid applying to areas of direct infant skin contact | [[benzoyl-peroxide]] |

## Tier 3 — Avoid, or avoid without explicit medical direction

| Ingredient | Why | Source |
|---|---|---|
| [[aspirin]] (general OTC pain/fever use) | Excreted into milk, disproportionately more at higher maternal doses; long-term high-dose maternal use can cause infant metabolic acidosis — use an alternative for high-dose maternal therapy | [[aspirin]] |
| [[bismuth-subsalicylate]] | Alternative treatments preferred, given potential infant salicylate absorption — the same salicylate-family concern as aspirin above | [[bismuth-subsalicylate]] |
| [[loperamide]] | Manufacturers recommend against use, due to variable breast-milk concentrations | [[loperamide]] |
| [[phenazopyridine]] | Avoid, especially if the infant is under 1 month old or has G6PD deficiency | [[phenazopyridine]] |
| Oral [[terbinafine]] | Excreted in breast milk — StatPearls advises use should follow completion of breastfeeding | [[terbinafine]] |
| [[melatonin]] | Insufficient safety evidence — avoidance recommended (same conclusion as [[pregnancy]]'s) | [[melatonin]] |

## A real, recurring data gap worth naming

Several widely-used ingredients in this database's ingredient pages simply have **no independently located breastfeeding-specific data** — [[proton-pump-inhibitors]] and [[naproxen]] are two notable examples where the ingredient page's Special Population Flags section does not address lactation at all. This is a genuine gap in what this database has sourced, not a finding that these are unsafe — but it means a nursing parent considering either should specifically ask a pharmacist or doctor rather than assume the absence of a stated concern means clearance.

## Bottom lines

1. **Pain/fever:** acetaminophen and ibuprofen are both well-supported for use while breastfeeding — ibuprofen in particular has favorable modern relative-infant-dose data per StatPearls. Aspirin and bismuth subsalicylate (same salicylate family) are the ones to avoid or use only at low/occasional doses.
2. **Heartburn:** famotidine's lower breast-milk concentration relative to other H2 blockers makes it a reasonable choice; calcium-based antacids are considered safe.
3. **Constipation:** bisacodyl, docusate, and PEG 3350 are all considered low-risk given minimal systemic absorption — consistent with their generally favorable pregnancy profile too (see [[pregnancy]]).
4. **Allergies:** cetirizine in occasional/small doses is reasonable, though the OTC label itself is more conservative ("not recommended") than the clinical literature — a real, stated tension rather than a settled answer. Prolonged larger doses risk decreased milk supply.
5. **Decongestants:** pseudoephedrine carries a real, if not independently re-verified in this pass, association with reduced milk supply — worth discussing with a doctor or lactation consultant before regular use.
6. **When a label just says "ask a health professional before use"** — as with [[pregnancy]], this is often because no dedicated breastfeeding-specific trial exists, not because a specific risk was found. Treat these as genuinely needing a real conversation, not a default red flag.
