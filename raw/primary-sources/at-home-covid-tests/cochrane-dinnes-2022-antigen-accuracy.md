# Cochrane Review — Rapid Antigen Tests for SARS-CoV-2 (condensed facts, not full text)

**Source:** Dinnes J, Deeks JJ, Berhane S, et al. Rapid, point-of-care antigen tests for diagnosis of SARS-CoV-2 infection. Cochrane Database Syst Rev. 2022. PMID: 35866452.

## Condensed facts
- Second update of a review first published 2020. Searched COVID-19 living evidence databases (search date 8 March 2021).
- 155 study cohorts (152 evaluations of single test applications), 100,462 unique samples, 16,822 confirmed SARS-CoV-2 cases, 49 different commercial antigen assays evaluated.
- Average sensitivity, symptomatic participants: **73.0%** (95% CI 69.3–76.4%; 109 evaluations, 50,574 samples, 11,662 cases).
- Average sensitivity, asymptomatic participants: **54.7%** (95% CI 47.7–61.6%; 50 evaluations, 40,956 samples, 2,641 cases).
- Sensitivity by symptom timing: first week after onset **80.9%** (95% CI 76.9–84.4%); second week **53.8%** (95% CI 48.0–59.6%).
- Asymptomatic + suspected epidemiological exposure: sensitivity 64.3% (95% CI 54.6–73.0%); asymptomatic + open community testing: 49.6% (95% CI 42.1–57.1%).
- Specificity: **99.1%** symptomatic, **99.7%** asymptomatic (both high and consistent across studies).
- By brand (per manufacturer instructions): sensitivity ranged **34.3%–91.3%** across 20 assays (symptomatic) and **28.6%–77.8%** across 12 assays (asymptomatic) — only 7 of 20 assays met the WHO ≥80% sensitivity bar for symptomatic use; 17 of 20 met the WHO ≥97% specificity bar.
- PPV illustration: at 5% prevalence, symptomatic, week 1 — PPV ≈89% (roughly 1 in 10 positives are false, ~1 in 5 cases missed). At 0.5% prevalence, asymptomatic — PPV 38–52% (2 in 5 to 1 in 2 positives are false).
- Authors' conclusion: antigen tests vary widely in sensitivity; highest in first week of symptomatic illness; many commercial tests lack independent validation; asymptomatic-population evidence has grown but sensitivity remains lower there.
