## Mechanism of Action
Non-systemic surfactant — decreases surface tension of gas bubbles in the GI tract, enabling coalescence and dispersion for removal via flatulence/belching. **Does not reduce actual gas production** — cannot prevent gas increases caused by lactose intolerance or medication side effects (a mechanistic ceiling worth stating plainly).

## Indications
FDA-approved 1952, for flatulence and bloating discomfort from excess GI gas. Explored (not established) for: skin protectant use, H. pylori treatment adjunct, exercise-related GI symptom reduction in endurance athletes. **Not suitable for ileus, small bowel obstruction, or constipation.** Symptoms suggesting gallstones or heart disease warrant further investigation rather than simethicone use.

**Infant colic:** studies found simethicone **NOT effective** for infantile colic treatment — an important negative finding given how commonly it is marketed/used for this purpose.

## Dosing
- Adults: 40–125mg PO four times daily after meals/bedtime; max 500mg/day
- Children 2–12y: 40mg four times daily; max 480mg/day
- Infants/children <2y: 20mg four times daily; max 240mg/day

## Pharmacokinetics
Not absorbed orally; excreted in feces — systemic side effects are essentially not possible given zero systemic absorption.

## Adverse Effects
Minimal toxicity profile: mild diarrhea and nausea reported occasionally. No kidney injury, hypertension, or hyperglycemia (mechanistically ruled out by non-absorption). No oral-administration silicosis cases reported.

## Contraindications
Simethicone allergy.

## Drug Interactions
Generally minimal. One case report of altered levothyroxine absorption in a child (concern judged minimal). One case report of potential carbamazepine toxicity elevation when combined with simethicone — warrants caution though mechanism/frequency unclear.

## Special Populations
Considered safe in pregnancy and breastfeeding (given lack of systemic absorption). Presumed safe in infants (though see Indications above regarding lack of colic efficacy).

## Monitoring
No serum level monitoring feasible or needed (non-absorbed). Abdominal imaging rarely necessary. No significant drug interactions typically occur.

## Efficacy Evidence (as cited in this StatPearls article)
A 1974 double-blind, placebo-controlled trial (n=41) found simethicone significantly improved all evaluated symptoms — gas, heartburn, bloating, distension — versus placebo at days 5 and 10 of treatment. This is an old, small trial cited by StatPearls itself; not independently re-verified against the original 1974 publication in this pass (pre-dates most modern PubMed indexing conventions and was not separately pulled as its own raw source).
