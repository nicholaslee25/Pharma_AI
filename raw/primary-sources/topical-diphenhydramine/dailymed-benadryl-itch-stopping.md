## Benadryl Extra Strength Itch Stopping Cream
**Source:** https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=a1c1b878-d58f-41ff-b7a8-477f28c52667&type=display
**Active Ingredients:** Diphenhydramine HCl 2% (20mg/1g) — Topical analgesic; Zinc acetate 0.1% (1mg/1g) — Skin protectant
**Uses:** Temporarily relieves pain and itching associated with insect bites, minor burns, sunburn, minor skin irritations, cuts, scrapes, and poison ivy/oak/sumac rashes; dries the oozing/weeping of poison plant reactions.
**Warnings:** External use only. Do not apply to large body areas. Do not use with other diphenhydramine-containing products (oral or topical). Ask a doctor before use on chicken pox or measles. Avoid eyes. Stop use and ask a doctor if condition worsens, persists beyond 7 days, or symptoms clear up and recur within days. Keep out of reach of children; contact Poison Control if ingested.
**Directions:** Adults/children 2+: apply to affected area not more than 3-4 times daily. Children under 2: ask a doctor.

## Safety literature — topical diphenhydramine systemic toxicity
- **Bhat et al., Am J Forensic Med Pathol 2009 (PMID 19901814): "Death of a Child From Topical Diphenhydramine."** First reported fatal case from topical diphenhydramine use in a toddler. Postmortem blood diphenhydramine level 1.06 mg/L — approximately 20x the expected therapeutic peak plasma concentration (0.066 mg/L) and within the reported lethal range for infants/toddlers (1.1-1.6 mg/L, per surrounding literature). Contributing factors cited: application immediately after a bath (vasodilation, open pores increasing absorption) and the child's eczema (impaired dermal barrier increasing absorption through damaged skin).
- Other pediatric case reports (ages 4-9) describe non-fatal toxic reactions — visual/auditory hallucinations, agitation, dilated pupils, flushed face, ataxic gait, urinary retention — following topical application to large body areas.
- General toxicology: toxic diphenhydramine levels in children can cause CNS excitation with hallucinations, agitation, dizziness, ataxia, seizures, coma, and cardiopulmonary arrest.
- Basis for the FDA label warning common to all topical diphenhydramine OTC products: "do not apply to large body areas" and "do not use with other diphenhydramine-containing products" (oral or topical) — these warnings map directly to the absorption/overdose mechanism documented in the case-report literature above.
