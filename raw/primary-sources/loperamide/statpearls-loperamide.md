## Mechanism of Action
Lipophilic synthetic phenylpiperidine opioid, mu-opioid receptor agonist. At therapeutic doses, acts on intestinal muscle mu-receptors to slow transit and inhibit peristalsis, with minimal CNS penetration (P-glycoprotein normally excludes it from the brain). At higher (abuse) doses, P-glycoprotein becomes saturated/inhibited, allowing loperamide to cross the blood-brain barrier and produce central opioid effects. Also directly inhibits cardiac Na+-gated channels (prolongs QRS) and the hERG channel (prolongs QTc) — the mechanistic basis for its dangerous high-dose cardiotoxicity (torsade de pointes, ventricular fibrillation).

## Indications
**FDA-approved:** traveler's diarrhea, IBS with chronic diarrhea, acute nonspecific diarrhea (≥2 years old), reducing ileostomy output.
**Off-label:** chemotherapy-related diarrhea (after excluding infection/colitis).
**Concerning trend:** 91% increase in reported loperamide exposures 2010–2015, including 8 deaths, driven by non-medical high-dose use for opioid-withdrawal self-management and euphoria-seeking ("loperamide as a poor man's methadone").

## Pharmacokinetics
Peak plasma 4–5h. Bioavailability <1% due to extensive first-pass metabolism — at recommended doses, almost no active loperamide reaches systemic circulation (this is why it's safe at labeled doses despite being an opioid). High protein binding, large Vd. Hepatic metabolism via CYP2C8 and CYP3A4 to desmethyl-loperamide. Half-life 9.1–14.4h (StatPearls gives two slightly different ranges: 7–19h and 9.1–14.4h in different sections). Primarily fecal excretion.

## Adult Dosing
- Acute diarrhea: 4mg initial, then 2mg after each unformed stool; OTC max 8mg/day; prescription max 16mg/day
- Chronic use: 2mg twice daily
- Chemotherapy-induced diarrhea (off-label): 4mg initial, then 2mg q2–4h; if persistent >24h, 2mg q2h (or 4mg q4h); continue until 12h without loose stool; doses >16mg/day provide no additional benefit

## Pediatric Dosing
**Contraindicated <2 years.** Weight-based: 13–21kg — 1mg initial then 1mg/unformed stool, max 3mg/day; 21–27kg — 2mg initial then 1mg/dose, max 4mg/day; 27.1–43kg — 2mg initial then 1mg/dose, max 6mg/day; ≥12y — 4mg initial then 2mg/dose, max 8mg/day. Not recommended for managing infectious diarrhea in pediatric patients.

## Special Populations
- **Hepatic impairment:** use with caution, no specific dose adjustment given
- **Renal impairment:** no dose adjustment needed, mild-severe
- **Pregnancy:** limited data; oral rehydration/dietary changes first-line, loperamide at lowest doses only if symptoms disabling
- **Breastfeeding:** manufacturers recommend against, due to variable breast-milk concentrations
- **Elderly:** use cautiously — paralytic ileus risk

## Adverse Effects
**Common:** dry mouth, flatulence, abdominal cramps, nausea, ileus, constipation, urinary retention, dizziness, drowsiness.
**Serious:** toxic megacolon, necrotizing enterocolitis, Stevens-Johnson syndrome, toxic epidermal necrolysis, QT/QTc prolongation, torsade de pointes, ventricular tachycardia ("VT storm" = ≥3 VT episodes within 24h), cardiac arrest, syncope.

## Drug Interactions
CYP3A4 substrate and P-glycoprotein substrate. **Lonafarnib** (CYP3A4 inhibitor): increases loperamide levels — limit to 1mg/day initially if combined. **P-gp inducers** (reduce loperamide levels/effect): carbamazepine, mitotane, phenytoin, rifampicin. **P-gp inhibitors** (raise loperamide levels/CNS penetration — use with caution): amiodarone, carvedilol, ritonavir, quinidine, itraconazole, verapamil, clarithromycin, ranolazine.

## Contraindications
Age <2 years; acute ulcerative colitis; bloody diarrhea; diarrhea from bacterial infection; hypersensitivity to loperamide/excipients.

## Boxed Warning (FDA)
Torsades de pointes and sudden death when used at higher-than-recommended doses.

## Toxicity/Overdose
High-dose use produces opioid-like effects: euphoria, miosis, CNS depression, respiratory depression, plus potentially life-threatening dysrhythmias via cardiac Na+/K+ channel blockade. ECG findings: QRS widening, QTc prolongation (>450ms males, >470ms females = prolonged). Risk factors: electrolyte abnormalities (magnesium, potassium, phosphate). **Management:** supportive care primary; naloxone (intranasal/IV bolus/infusion) for respiratory depression, with ≥24h observation after naloxone since loperamide's half-life outlasts naloxone's; sodium bicarbonate boluses/infusion for QRS widening; isoproterenol or transcutaneous pacing for QTc prolongation; standard ACLS for cardiac arrest. FDA approved new package-size limits and unit-dose packaging in September 2019 specifically to curb high-dose abuse access.
