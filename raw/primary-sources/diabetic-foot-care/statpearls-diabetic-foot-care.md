## Core Components of Care
Clinical examination has four major elements: dermatological assessment, vascular evaluation, neurological examination, musculoskeletal evaluation.

## Risk Factors
Peripheral neuropathy (reduces protective sensation), peripheral arterial disease (impairs tissue perfusion), previous ulceration or amputation, structural deformities (hammertoes, claw toes, ankle equinus), Charcot neuroarthropathy.
**Patients with diabetes and neuropathy have a 7–10% annual risk of foot ulceration, rising to 25–30% with additional complications.**

## Screening/Prevention Protocol
- Annual comprehensive assessment for all diabetic patients
- Every clinical encounter for those with sensory deficits, prior ulceration, or amputation history
- Regular podiatry follow-up for high-risk individuals
- Daily self-care: visual foot inspection (using mirrors if needed), skin/nail monitoring, proper hygiene and drying

## Footwear
Therapeutic footwear recommended for: loss of protective sensation, foot deformities, current/prior ulcers, poor circulation, history of amputation. Shoes must fit properly to prevent ulcers; individualized offloading (orthoses, toe spacers, specialized padding) as needed.

## Skin/Moisturizer Notes
Autonomic dysfunction reduces perspiration, causing xerosis (dryness) and fissuring. StatPearls does not give a specific moisturizer product/ingredient recommendation — focus is on protective footwear and skin monitoring, not a topical product.

## Patient Education
Protective footwear importance, daily self-exam technique, recognition of early warning signs, smoking cessation, compliance with specialist appointments. Education should be provided and reinforced at every visit.

## Key Statistics
- Global diabetes prevalence: ~589 million adults aged 20–79 (IDF figure, consistent with other StatPearls diabetes-related articles cited elsewhere in this vault)
- **Mortality post-amputation: 40–82% five-year mortality after below-knee amputation; 40–90% after above-knee amputation**
- **Non-compliant patients are 54 times more likely to develop pedal ulcerations and 20 times more likely to undergo amputation**
- **~85% of diabetes-related lower-extremity amputations result from complications of foot ulceration**
- Age >65: 12-fold increased amputation risk; ages 65–74 specifically: 23-fold risk
- Charcot neuroarthropathy: 17% annual ulceration risk; 12x higher amputation risk when ulceration is present
- **Interprofessional (team-based) care reduced major limb amputations by 75%**
- Each 1% increase in HbA1c raises peripheral vascular disease risk by 25–28%

## Complications of Inadequate Care
Ulceration, infection, osteomyelitis, tissue necrosis/gangrene, limb amputation, significantly reduced quality of life.
