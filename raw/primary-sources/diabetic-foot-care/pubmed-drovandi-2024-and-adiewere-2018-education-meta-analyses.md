# Efficacy Evidence — Patient Education for Diabetic Foot Ulcer Prevention (condensed facts, not full text)

## Source 1: Drovandi et al. 2024 (larger, more recent meta-analysis)
Drovandi A, et al. Effectiveness of educational interventions for diabetes-related foot disease: A systematic review and meta-analysis. Diabetes Metab Res Rev. 2024 Mar. PMID: 37926437.
**Retrieved:** 2026-08-26 (abstract viewed via PubMed, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37926437/)
**License note:** Open access (Diabetes/Metabolism Research and Reviews, Wiley, © 2023 The Authors) — condensed facts recorded here regardless, per this vault's general practice.

### Condensed facts
- Systematic review/meta-analysis of RCTs on educational programs for people with or at risk of diabetes-related foot disease (DFD). Random-effects models; risk of bias via Cochrane ROB-2.
- **29 RCTs, n=3,891.** Education programs reduced risk of a foot ulcer by roughly half: **OR 0.54 (95% CI 0.29–1.00, I²=65%)** — note the upper CI bound reaches exactly 1.00, meaning the result is borderline/not fully statistically decisive despite the point estimate.
- Reduced risk of any amputation: **OR 0.34 (95% CI 0.13–0.88, I²=38%)**.
- Reduced HbA1c: standardized mean difference **-0.73 (95% CI -1.26 to -0.20, I²=93%** — very high heterogeneity, interpret cautiously).
- No effect on all-cause mortality: OR 1.09 (95% CI 0.57–2.07, I²=0%).
- Education programs significantly improved DFD knowledge in 13 of 16 trials, and self-care behavior scores in 19 of 20 trials.
- Only ONE of 29 trials was rated low risk of bias overall — a real quality caveat.
- Authors' conclusion: education programs mostly improve knowledge/self-care and reduce ulceration/amputation risk, but larger higher-quality trials with longer follow-up are needed.

## Source 2: Adiewere et al. 2018 (smaller, earlier meta-analysis, more mixed results)
Adiewere P, et al. A systematic review and meta-analysis of patient education in preventing and reducing the incidence or recurrence of adult diabetes foot ulcers (DFU). Heliyon. 2018. PMID: 29872752.
**Retrieved:** 2026-08-26 (abstract viewed via PubMed, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29872752/)
**License note:** Open access (Heliyon) — condensed facts recorded per this vault's general practice.

### Condensed facts
- Systematic review/meta-analysis of RCTs (adults 18+, type 1 or 2 diabetes or existing DFU), searched to September 2017, English-language only.
- Only **6 RCTs** met inclusion criteria (5 reported DFU incidence, 2 reported amputation rates) — a notably small evidence base.
- Combining different educational approaches: no significant advantage, RR 0.50 (95% CI 0.21–1.17, P=0.11).
- Foot-care education alone vs. usual care (2 RCTs): non-significant effect (P=0.57), high heterogeneity (77%).
- **Intensive vs. brief educational approach: statistically reduced DFU incidence risk, RR 0.37 (95% CI 0.14–1.01, P=0.05)** — borderline significance, high heterogeneity (91%).
- Authors' interpretation: intensive educational intervention specifically (not education in general) was associated with reduced DFU incidence.

## Synthesis note
Both reviews converge on a similar message despite different eras/scope: general "any education" comparisons are inconsistent/borderline, but more INTENSIVE education programs show a more consistent (though still not always fully statistically decisive) protective effect against foot ulceration and amputation. Neither review evaluated any specific OTC topical cream or moisturizer product — the evidence base here is about educational/behavioral intervention, not about any product.
