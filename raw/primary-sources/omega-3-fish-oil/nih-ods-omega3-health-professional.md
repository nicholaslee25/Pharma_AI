## Introduction / Mechanism
Omega-3 (n-3) polyunsaturated fatty acids: ALA (18 carbons, plant oils — flaxseed/soybean/canola, chia, walnuts), and long-chain EPA (20 carbons) and DHA (22 carbons, from fish/fish oil/krill oil, originally synthesized by marine microalgae). ALA is an essential fatty acid (must come from diet); conversion of ALA to EPA/DHA in the liver is very limited (<15%), so **direct EPA/DHA intake from food or supplements is the only practical way to raise body levels**. Absorption ~95% (efficient, similar to other dietary fats). Omega-3s are structural components of cell-membrane phospholipids (DHA especially concentrated in retina, brain, sperm) and precursors of eicosanoids (signaling molecules) — omega-3-derived eicosanoids are generally LESS pro-inflammatory/pro-clotting than omega-6-derived ones, so a relatively higher EPA/DHA-to-arachidonic-acid ratio tips balance toward less inflammatory activity. The optimal omega-6:omega-3 ratio, if any, is NOT established — many experts consider it too nonspecific a metric; raising EPA/DHA directly matters more than manipulating the ratio.

## Recommended Intakes (Adequate Intake, ALA only for age ≥1; total omega-3s for infants)
0.5g (infants) up to 1.6g (adult males) or 1.1g (adult females), 1.4g (pregnancy), 1.3g (lactation) — the IOM did NOT establish specific intake recommendations for EPA/DHA specifically, only for ALA.

## Sources
Food: flaxseed, chia, walnuts, canola/soybean oil (ALA); fatty cold-water fish — salmon, mackerel, tuna, herring, sardines (EPA/DHA, amounts vary by species and, for farmed fish, by feed composition — one Scottish farmed-salmon analysis found EPA/DHA content fell significantly 2006–2015 due to feed changes). Supplements: fish oil, krill oil, cod liver oil (also provides vitamin A/D), algal oil (vegetarian, DHA ± EPA). Typical fish oil supplement: ~1,000mg fish oil containing 180mg EPA + 120mg DHA (doses vary widely by product). Omega-3 supplements have NOT been found to contain methylmercury (removed during processing) despite seafood itself containing variable methylmercury levels. Forms: natural triglycerides, ethyl esters, re-esterified triglycerides, free fatty acids, phospholipids (krill oil) — natural/re-esterified triglycerides and free fatty acids have somewhat higher bioavailability than ethyl esters, though all forms raise plasma EPA/DHA significantly.

## Deficiency
Essential fatty acid deficiency (omega-3 or omega-6) causes rough/scaly skin and dermatitis; no established cutoff DHA/EPA level below which functional (visual/neural/immune) endpoints are known to be impaired. Classical deficiency in healthy US individuals is virtually nonexistent — clinically seen almost only in patients on PUFA-lacking parenteral nutrition (documented in 1970s–80s case reports; modern feeding solutions contain adequate PUFAs).

## Cardiovascular Disease — Detailed Trial History (the best-studied omega-3 health outcome)

**Early positive signal:** epidemiological research (1970s) on Greenland Inuit and Japanese fish-eating populations showed low MI/coronary-event rates; observational studies/meta-analyses consistently link higher fish/omega-3 intake to lower heart failure, coronary disease, fatal CHD risk.

**Japan EPA Lipid Intervention Study (JELIS, 2007):** 18,645 people with hypercholesterolemia (total cholesterol ≥251 mg/dL), ± CAD history, randomized to 1.8g/day EPA + statin vs. statin alone. After mean 4.6 years: EPA group had **19% fewer major coronary events**; significant reduction in unstable angina/nonfatal coronary events, but NOT in sudden cardiac death or coronary death. **Subgroup split:** primary-prevention subgroup (no CAD history) — no significant effect; secondary-prevention subgroup (CAD history) — 28% reduction in unstable angina, 19% reduction in major coronary events. Separate analysis: no effect on total stroke, but 20% reduction in RECURRENT stroke in patients with prior stroke.

**Subsequent largely-null trials:**
- **ORIGIN (2012):** 12,536 diabetic/high-diabetes-risk patients, 1g/day omega-3 (375mg DHA + 465mg EPA) for ~6 years — lowered triglycerides but NO effect on MI, stroke, or CV death vs. placebo
- **Alpha Omega Trial (2010):** 4,837 older adults post-MI on standard cardioprotective meds, low-dose EPA+DHA (150mg DHA + 226mg EPA, in margarine) for 40 months — NO reduction in major CV events vs. placebo

**2018 primary-prevention trials:**
- **VITAL:** 25,871 adults (men ≥50, women ≥55) with NO prior heart attack/stroke/cancer, 1g/day omega-3 (460mg EPA + 380mg DHA) ± vitamin D 2000 IU/day vs. placebo. Median 5.3 years: NO significant reduction in composite major CV events. BUT: significant **28% reduction in total MI** (77% reduction in African Americans; 40% reduction in those eating <1.5 fish servings/week); significant reductions in fatal MI, total CHD, and coronary revascularization procedures. No stroke or CV-mortality reduction.
- **ASCEND:** 15,480 diabetic adults ≥40y, no CVD evidence, same 1g/day dose vs. placebo. Mean 7.4 years: NO significant effect on composite serious vascular events or revascularization. BUT: significant **19% reduction in cardiovascular death**.

**High-dose prescription omega-3 trials — a striking contrast:**
- **REDUCE-IT (2019):** 8,179 participants with CVD (or diabetes + 1 more risk factor), elevated triglycerides (135–499 mg/dL) despite statin therapy, LDL 41–100 mg/dL. **4g/day icosapent ethyl (IPE, purified EPA ethyl ester, i.e. prescription Vascepa) vs. MINERAL OIL placebo**, median 4.9 years. IPE significantly reduced composite CV events by **25%**, CV death by **20%**, fatal/nonfatal stroke by **28%**, fatal/nonfatal MI by **31%** — a strong, consistent, multi-endpoint benefit.
- **STRENGTH (2020):** 13,078 high-CV-risk participants (22 countries, mean age 62.5), **4g/day omega-3 carboxylic acid (EPA+DHA, prescription Epanova) vs. CORN OIL placebo**. Stopped early (~3.5 years median) for futility AND a HIGHER rate of atrial fibrillation in the treatment group. NO significant reduction in the composite CV endpoint.

**Why REDUCE-IT and STRENGTH conflict despite similar 4g/day doses (ODS's own analysis):** possible explanations include different omega-3 formulation (ethyl ester EPA-only vs. carboxylic-acid EPA+DHA), different baseline population health, and critically, **different placebo comparators — mineral oil (REDUCE-IT) is NOT a neutral placebo**, since it affects lipid levels/inflammatory markers and may inhibit statin absorption, potentially inflating the apparent IPE benefit; corn oil (STRENGTH) is more neutral. ODS explicitly flags this as a likely partial explanation for the divergent results — a genuinely important methodological caveat, not just "one drug works, one doesn't."

**Recent syntheses:**
- **2019 meta-analysis** (13 trials incl. ASCEND/VITAL/REDUCE-IT but NOT STRENGTH, 127,477 participants, doses 0.376–4g/day, mean 5 years): LC omega-3 supplementation reduces MI, CHD death, total CHD, CV death, and total CVD risk, dose-relatedly — but NO significant effect on fatal/nonfatal stroke (except REDUCE-IT's high 4g/day dose, suggesting stroke benefit may need the higher dose)
- **2020 Cochrane review** (86 RCTs, 1968–2019, 162,796 participants, 0.5g to >5g/day, 12–88 months): reduced serum triglycerides ~15% and slightly decreased cardiovascular mortality and CHD events — but NO effect on all-cause mortality, cardiovascular events overall, stroke, or arrhythmia
- Several earlier meta-analyses and a 2016 AHRQ report concluded omega-3 supplements do NOT significantly reduce risk of most cardiovascular events

## Safety (supplemented from related search, since this section loaded incompletely from the live page)
Common mild side effects: unpleasant/fishy taste, bad breath, heartburn, nausea, GI discomfort, diarrhea, headache, odoriferous sweat. Doses of 2–15 g/day EPA/DHA might increase bleeding time via reduced platelet aggregation, though these doses have not been shown to cause actual bleeding problems or affect immune function/glucose homeostasis/lipid peroxidation in the cited research. **FDA has concluded dietary supplements providing ≤5 g/day EPA+DHA are safe as recommended**; European Food Safety Authority reached a similar conclusion for long-term use up to ~5g/day combined EPA+DHA.
