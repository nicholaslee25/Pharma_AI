# PubMed Abstract — White Petrolatum vs. Bacitracin RCT (condensed facts, not full text)

**Source:** Smack DP, Harrington AC, Dunn C, et al. Infection and allergy incidence in ambulatory surgery patients using white petrolatum vs bacitracin ointment: a randomized controlled trial. JAMA. 1996;276(12):972-977. PMID: 8805732.
**Retrieved:** 2026-08-26 (abstract viewed via PubMed, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8805732/)
**License note:** Journal article, copyrighted. Condensed facts only from the free PubMed abstract, no full text reproduced.

## Condensed facts
- Randomized, double-blind, prospective trial. White petrolatum vs. bacitracin ointment for postprocedure wound care after dermatologic surgery. Setting: Walter Reed Army Medical Center outpatient dermatology/surgical clinics.
- 922 patients enrolled, 1,249 wounds total; 440 evaluable in the petrolatum group, 444 in the bacitracin group. Baseline characteristics comparable between groups.
- **Postprocedure infection: 9 (2.0%) in the white petrolatum group vs. 4 (0.9%) in the bacitracin group — 95% CI for the difference -0.4% to 2.7%, P=0.37 (NOT statistically significant).**
- Of the petrolatum-group infections, 8 of 9 (1.8% of the whole group) were specifically due to *Staphylococcus aureus*, vs. NONE in the bacitracin group (P=0.004 — this specific comparison WAS statistically significant, an important nuance: bacitracin's antimicrobial action did prevent staph infections specifically, even though overall infection rate wasn't significantly different).
- **Allergic contact dermatitis: 0 patients in the petrolatum group vs. 4 (0.9%) in the bacitracin group (P=0.12, not significant but numerically all in bacitracin's favor for petrolatum's safety).**
- No clinically significant differences in healing between groups at day 1 (P=0.98), day 7 (P=0.86), or day 28 (P=0.28).
- **Authors' conclusion: white petrolatum is a safe, effective wound care ointment for ambulatory surgery, with an equally low infection rate and minimal allergy risk compared to bacitracin.**
- This is the classic, frequently-cited trial supporting "plain petrolatum instead of topical antibiotic ointment for routine minor/surgical wound care" — see [[topical-antibiotics]] for the comparison-ingredient's own dedicated page.
