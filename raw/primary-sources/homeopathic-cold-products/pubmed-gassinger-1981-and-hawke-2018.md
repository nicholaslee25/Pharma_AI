# Efficacy Evidence — Homeopathic Cold/Respiratory Products (condensed facts, not full text)

## Source 1: Gassinger et al. 1981 (RCT, Eupatorium perfoliatum D2 vs. aspirin)
Gassinger CA, Wünstel G, Netter P. [A controlled clinical trial for testing the efficacy of the homeopathic drug eupatorium perfoliatum D2 in the treatment of common cold]. Arzneimittelforschung. 1981. PMID: 7195723. (Article in German.)
**Retrieved:** 2026-08-26 (abstract viewed via PubMed, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7195723/) — VERIFIED as a real, correctly-characterized citation carried forward from this vault's prior AI-compiled dossier.
**License note:** Journal article, copyrighted. Condensed facts from the free PubMed abstract.

### Condensed facts
- 53 outpatients with common cold/flu-like illness, randomized to acetylsalicylic acid (aspirin) or homeopathic Eupatorium perfoliatum D2.
- Assessed via symptom checklists and physical exam on days 1, 4, and 10.
- **Neither subjective complaints nor body temperature nor laboratory findings showed significant differences between groups** — interpreted by the original authors as evidence both were "equally effective," but note: this study had NO placebo arm, so "equally effective as aspirin" does not establish that either treatment outperforms no treatment/natural resolution of a self-limiting illness. This is a genuine interpretive limitation worth flagging rather than repeating the original "equally effective" framing uncritically.

## Source 2: Hawke et al. 2018 (Cochrane systematic review, children)
Hawke K, Ridd MJ, Herbert RJ, et al. Homeopathic medicinal products for preventing and treating acute respiratory tract infections in children. Cochrane Database Syst Rev. 2018. PMID: 29630715 (original 2018 publication; later updated with PMID 30196554).
**Retrieved:** 2026-08-26 (abstract viewed via PubMed, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29630715/) — VERIFIED as a real, correctly-characterized citation carried forward from this vault's prior AI-compiled dossier.
**License note:** Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews is copyrighted. Condensed facts only from the free PubMed abstract, no full text reproduced.

### Condensed facts
- Double-blind RCTs/cluster-RCTs, oral homeopathic products vs. identical placebo or self-selected conventional treatment, children aged 0–16, for acute respiratory tract infections (ARTIs — most children get 3–6/year).
- Searched CENTRAL, MEDLINE, Embase, CINAHL, AMED, CAMbase, British Homeopathic Library, WHO ICTRP, ClinicalTrials.gov.
- **8 RCTs included, 1,562 children total.** 4 treatment studies (recovery from URTI), 4 prevention studies (1–3 months treatment, followed for the rest of the year).
- Methodological problems: inconsistent methodology, high attrition, no intention-to-treat analysis in some, selective reporting, protocol deviations. 3 studies at high risk of bias in ≥1 domain. 3 studies funded by homeopathy manufacturers; 1 by an NGO; 2 government-supported; 1 university co-sponsored; 1 unreported funding.
- Heterogeneity precluded robust meta-analysis for most outcomes; only 4 outcomes were poolable across >1 study.
- **CRITICAL FINDING: all studies assessed as LOW risk of bias showed NO benefit from oral homeopathic products; trials at UNCERTAIN or HIGH risk of bias were the ones reporting beneficial effects.** This is a textbook bias-gradient pattern — the better-designed studies found nothing, the weaker ones found an effect.
- Non-individualized homeopathic products, prevention of ARTIs: **OR 1.14 (95% CI 0.83–1.57)** — low-quality evidence of little-to-no preventive effect.
- Individualized homeopathy, effect on antibiotic-use need: **OR 0.79 (95% CI 0.35–1.76)**, N=369 — low-quality evidence, wide CI crossing 1.
- Individualized treatment, short-term cure: **OR 1.31 (95% CI 0.09–19.54)**, N=155 — very-low-quality evidence, extremely wide CI (essentially uninformative).
- Individualized treatment, long-term cure: **OR 1.01 (95% CI 0.10–9.96)** — very-low-quality evidence, again an uninformative CI.
- Adverse events reported inconsistently; no serious adverse events reported; one study found increased non-severe adverse events in the treatment group.
