## Design
Systematic review to assess the maximum tolerable lactose dose and interventions for reducing lactose-intolerance symptoms. Searched MEDLINE, Cochrane Library, and others, English-language trials 1967–November 2009. RCTs of individuals with lactose intolerance/malabsorption.

## Scale
36 unique RCTs: 26 on lactase- or lactose-hydrolyzed milk supplements/lactose-reduced milk/tolerable lactose doses; 7 on probiotics; 2 on incremental lactose administration (colonic adaptation); 1 on another agent.

## Results
- **Moderate-quality evidence:** 12–15g of lactose (~1 cup of milk) is well tolerated by MOST adults — i.e., many people labeled "lactose intolerant" can handle a meaningful amount without any product
- **Evidence was INSUFFICIENT** that lactose-reduced solution/milk (0–2g lactose) is more effective than >12g at reducing symptoms
- **Evidence for probiotics, colonic adaptation, and other agents was also INSUFFICIENT**
- Most studies evaluated lactose malabsorption (a lab finding) rather than clinical lactose intolerance (symptomatic), and heterogeneous enrollment/dosing/reporting precluded pooling results

## Authors' Conclusion
Most individuals with presumed lactose intolerance or malabsorption can tolerate 12–15g of lactose. Additional studies are needed to determine the effectiveness of lactose intolerance treatments generally.

## Relevance
This is a rigorous (AHRQ-sponsored), broad systematic review that found the evidence base for treatment interventions — including lactase-related products broadly — insufficient to draw strong efficacy conclusions, while also finding many "lactose intolerant" individuals can tolerate more lactose than commonly assumed without any intervention at all. This is an important calibrating finding for a category with high consumer usage (see Pharmacy Times Context on the ingredient page).
