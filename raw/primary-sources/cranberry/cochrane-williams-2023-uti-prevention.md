## Background/Mechanism (per the review)
Cranberries contain proanthocyanidins (PACs), which inhibit adherence of p-fimbriated E. coli to urothelial bladder cells — the proposed mechanism for UTI prevention.

## Design
Fifth update of a Cochrane review (originally 1998; prior updates 2003, 2004, 2008, 2012). Searched Cochrane Kidney and Transplant Specialised Register through March 2023 (drawing on CENTRAL, MEDLINE, EMBASE, trial registries). RCTs/quasi-RCTs of cranberry products vs. placebo/no treatment/other intervention (antibiotics, probiotics) for UTI prevention in susceptible populations. GRADE used for evidence certainty.

## Scale
50 studies total (26 new in this update), 8,857 randomized participants. 45 studies compared cranberry vs. placebo/water/no treatment across 6 different susceptible-population groups; 26 of these were meta-analysable for symptomatic culture-verified UTI.

## Results (this is a substantially MORE POSITIVE update than the 2012 version)

**Overall (moderate-certainty evidence):** cranberry products **REDUCED UTI risk** — RR **0.70** (95% CI 0.58–0.84; I²=69%; 6,211 participants)

**By population subgroup:**
- **Women with recurrent UTIs:** RR 0.74 (95% CI 0.55–0.99; 8 studies, 1,555 participants) — probably reduced risk
- **Children:** RR **0.46** (95% CI 0.32–0.68; 5 studies, 504 participants) — probably reduced risk, the strongest subgroup effect
- **People susceptible due to a medical intervention** (e.g. catheterization): RR **0.47** (95% CI 0.37–0.61; 6 studies, 1,434 participants) — probably reduced risk
- **Elderly institutionalized men/women:** RR 0.93 (95% CI 0.67–1.30; 3 studies, 1,489 participants; moderate certainty) — **little or no benefit**
- **Pregnant women:** RR 1.06 (95% CI 0.75–1.50; 3 studies, 765 participants; moderate certainty) — **little or no benefit**
- **Adults with neuromuscular bladder dysfunction/incomplete emptying:** RR 0.97 (95% CI 0.78–1.19; 3 studies, 464 participants; low certainty) — **little or no benefit**

**Head-to-head comparisons:**
- **Vs. antibiotics:** little or no difference (RR 1.03, 95% CI 0.80–1.33, culture-verified; RR 1.30 for clinical symptoms without culture, 95% CI 0.79–2.14)
- **Vs. probiotics:** cranberry may REDUCE risk more than probiotics — RR 0.39 (95% CI 0.27–0.56; 3 studies, 215 participants)
- **Juice vs. tablets, or different PAC doses:** unclear — very low certainty evidence for these comparisons

## Relevance
This is a materially more favorable finding than the earlier (2012) version of this same Cochrane review, which found only a non-significant trend. The current (2023), much larger evidence base shows a REAL, moderate-certainty overall benefit — but one that is population-specific: strongest in children and intervention-susceptible patients, real but more modest in women with recurrent UTIs, and essentially absent in elderly institutionalized patients, pregnant women, and neuromuscular bladder dysfunction patients. This population-dependence is the single most important nuance for this ingredient page.
