# PubMed Abstract — Temporal Artery Thermometer Meta-Analysis (condensed facts, not full text)

**Source:** Geijer H, Udumyan R, Alfredsson J, Fransson SG, Fabi B. Temperature measurements with a temporal scanner: systematic review and meta-analysis. BMJ Open. 2016. PMID: 27033957.
**Retrieved:** 2026-08-26 (abstract viewed via PubMed, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27033957/)

## Condensed facts
- Systematic review/meta-analysis of temporal artery thermometer (TAT) diagnostic accuracy vs. core-temperature reference methods (rectal, bladder, etc.), searched PubMed/Embase/Cinahl/Web of Science, risk of bias via QUADAS-2, quality via GRADE.
- 37 articles, 5,026 participants (clinical + healthy, with/without fever).
- Pooled mean difference vs. reference: **-0.19°C** (95% limits of agreement -1.16 to 0.77°C) — moderate quality of evidence; large heterogeneity/wide limits of agreement across studies.
- Pooled sensitivity for detecting fever: **0.72** (95% CI 0.61–0.81).
- Pooled specificity: **0.94** (95% CI 0.87–0.97).
- Subgroup finding: trend toward UNDERESTIMATING temperature in febrile patients specifically.
- When tympanic thermometers were assessed in the same populations, TAT performance was similar to tympanic.
- **Authors' conclusion: TAT is NOT sufficiently accurate to replace reference methods (rectal, bladder, invasive)** — it may be usable as a substitute for tympanic thermometry, "with the caveat that both methods are inaccurate."
