# Norwegian Knowledge Centre for the Health Services — Thermometry Diagnostic Accuracy Report (background context)

**Source:** Holte TO, et al. The Diagnostic Accuracy of Infrared Tympanic, Oral, Axillary and Temporal Thermometry, Compared with Rectal Readings when Identifying Fever in Adult Hospitalized Patients. Norwegian Knowledge Centre for the Health Services (NOKC) Report No. 19-2009. Hosted on NCBI Bookshelf: https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK470017/
**Retrieved:** 2026-08-26 (via ?report=printable)
**License note:** Norwegian government health technology assessment report, included as general background/context since no StatPearls-branded article specific to digital thermometry was located in this search pass.

---

## Extracted content
Systematic review of 11 small cross-sectional studies (N=1,426) comparing non-rectal thermometry methods (mostly infrared tympanic; some oral, some axillary) to rectal reference standard in adult hospitalized patients. No studies in this review evaluated temporal artery thermometry. Key finding: infrared tympanic thermometry did NOT identify an acceptable proportion of patients with fever detected by rectal thermometry (low sensitivity), while showing high specificity (few false positives) — i.e., tympanic thermometers under-detect fever relative to rectal readings more than they over-detect it. Authors conclude diagnostic-accuracy documentation for tympanic thermometry was "sparsely documented" as of 2009 and call for further study of newer device models in clinical settings. (Note: this report predates the more recent Geijer 2016 and Shi 2020 meta-analyses in this same folder, which provide more specific pooled sensitivity/specificity numbers.)
