## Design
Umbrella review (a review of systematic reviews/meta-analyses) using PRISMA methodology, searching PubMed, Embase, Scopus, Web of Science, Cochrane, Google Scholar. Quality assessed via AMSTAR. Random-effects model for pooled estimates across included SRMs.

## Scale
10 systematic reviews/meta-analyses, 296,816 total study participants (across the underlying primary studies these 10 reviews synthesized).

## Results
- **Overall pooled effect (folic acid OR multivitamin) for NTD prevention:** relative risk/effect **0.43** (95% CI 0.29–0.58; I²=93.5%, high heterogeneity)
- **Folic acid alone:** effect 0.23 (95% CI 0.09–0.37) — i.e., a **77% reduction** in NTD risk
- **Multivitamin (without isolated folic acid emphasis):** effect 0.63 (95% CI 0.53–0.72) and 0.61 (95% CI 0.46–0.75) in two groupings — i.e., a **37% reduction**
- **Primary (occurrence) prevention:** effect 0.50 (95% CI 0.34–0.66) — 50% reduction
- **Secondary (recurrence) prevention:** effect 0.20 (95% CI −0.01–0.41) — ~80% reduction, though CI crosses null
- **RCTs specifically:** effect 0.43 (95% CI 0.32–0.54) — 57% reduction
- **Observational studies specifically:** effect 0.45 (95% CI 0.03–0.87) — 55% reduction

## Authors' Conclusion
Prenatal folic acid and/or multivitamin supplementation was associated with an overall **57% reduction in neural tube defects**. Folic acid alone showed a somewhat larger reduction (77%) than multivitamin supplementation (37%). Both are recommended for incorporation into NTD prevention strategies during the preconception period. More large-scale prospective/RCT research is needed to nail down optimal dose, duration, and timing.

## Relevance
This is a strong, current (2025), high-participant-count evidence base directly supporting the core rationale for prenatal vitamins/folic acid — one of the few OTC supplement categories in this database with genuinely large, consistent, well-quantified effect sizes across multiple independent systematic reviews.
