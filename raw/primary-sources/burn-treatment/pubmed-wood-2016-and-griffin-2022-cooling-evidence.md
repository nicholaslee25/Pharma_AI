# Efficacy Evidence — Cool Running Water First Aid for Burns (condensed facts, not full text)

## Source 1: Wood et al. 2016 (bi-national cohort study)
Wood FM, Phillips M, Jovic T, Cassidy JT, Cameron P, Edgar DW; on behalf of the BRANZ registry. Water First Aid Is Beneficial In Humans Post-Burn: Evidence from a Bi-National Cohort Study. PLoS One. 2016. PMID: 26808839.
**Retrieved:** 2026-08-26 (abstract viewed via PubMed, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26808839/)
**License note:** PLoS One is open access (CC BY); condensed facts recorded here regardless, consistent with this vault's general practice for journal articles.

### Condensed facts
- Prospective cohort study using the Burn Registry of Australia and New Zealand (BRANZ), 2009–2012 data, examining whether pre-admission water-cooling first aid affects burn severity outcomes. Regression adjusted with propensity score to control for confounding.
- Cooling was provided before burn-center admission for **68%** of patients; at least 20 minutes duration for **46%**.
- **Graft surgery probability fell by 0.070 from a baseline of 0.537 — a 13% relative reduction (p=0.014).**
- **ICU admission probability fell by 0.084 from a baseline of 0.175 — a 48% relative reduction (p<0.001).**
- **Hospital length of stay fell by 2.27 days from a baseline of 12.9 days — an 18% relative reduction (p=0.001).**
- All outcomes except death showed a dose-response relationship with duration of first-aid cooling (i.e., more cooling time, better outcome, up to guideline duration).
- Burn size and patient age interacted with these relationships (not further quantified in the abstract).

## Source 2: Griffin et al. 2022 (systematic review/meta-analysis)
Griffin B, et al. The effect of 20 minutes of cool running water first aid within three hours of thermal burn injury on patient outcomes: a systematic review and meta-analysis. Australas Emerg Care. 2022 Dec. PMID: 35688782.
**Retrieved:** 2026-08-26 (abstract viewed via PubMed, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35688782/)
**License note:** Journal article, copyrighted. Condensed facts from the free PubMed abstract, no full text reproduced.

### Condensed facts
- Systematic review/meta-analysis (PRISMA), searched PubMed/EMBASE/CENTRAL/CINAHL/PROQUEST + ANZ Clinical Trial Registry, no date restriction, English and Chinese language studies. Quality assessed via Downs and Black Checklist.
- 323 records screened, 7 studies included; majority (67%) conducted in Australia/New Zealand; methodological quality ranked "fair" to "good."
- Finding: 20 minutes of cool running water (CRW) within the first three hours of burn injury **significantly decreased the odds of requiring skin grafting and surgical intervention** for wound management (exact pooled odds ratio not stated in the abstract text extracted).
- Authors' conclusion: considerable evidence supports 20 min of CRW within 3 hours improving burn outcomes; call for consensus among burn organizations to standardize first-aid guidance globally (guideline duration recommendations vary by country/organization).
