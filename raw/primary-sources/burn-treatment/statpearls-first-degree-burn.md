## Definition
First-degree burns are superficial burns involving only the epidermal layer of skin — the most superficial burn classification.

## Evaluation
Follows ATLS protocol (airway, breathing, circulation, disability, exposure) as a primary survey. For first-degree burns specifically, patients present without associated airway symptoms; thorough history and detailed exam should still be performed.

## Treatment/Management
- Pain relief with oral analgesics and topical agents (e.g., Silvadene, 3% bismuth tribromophenate, or petroleum gauze)
- Clean the area to remove the superficial (stripping) skin layer
- Non-adhesive dressings recommended, especially for children in flexure regions
- Dressings changed three times per week in the absence of infection
- If infection develops: daily wound exam/dressing changes; 7-day course of flucloxacillin is first-line therapy, erythromycin as an alternative

## Complications
Pain and hypothermia (especially in children and elderly, due to higher body-surface-area-to-mass ratio increasing heat loss risk during wound care). Once pain subsides, first-degree burns rarely have adverse sequelae.

## Key Notes
- Avoid hypothermia in children during burn care
- Suspect non-accidental trauma (abuse) in children with burns on hands, feet, buttocks, or perineum
- Outpatient management is typical for first-degree burns
