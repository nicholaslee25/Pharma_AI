## Mechanism of Action
NMDA receptor antagonist that nonselectively inhibits reuptake of serotonin (5-HT) and catecholamines. Also agonizes σ-1 receptors in the medullary cough center, suppressing transmission of cough impulses.

## Indications
**FDA-approved:** productive cough from common cold; chest congestion; productive cough from allergies.
**Off-label:** acute URTIs, chronic bronchitis, productive cough in pneumonia.

## Pharmacokinetics
Well absorbed in small intestine; volume of distribution 5–6.7 L/kg. Lipophilic, structurally similar to alkaloid opioids but does not interact with the μ-opioid receptor. Hepatic metabolism via CYP2D6 to dextrorphan (active metabolite); also undergoes N-demethylation via CYP3A4. Dextrorphan/metabolites eliminated renally.

## Dosing
Common OTC syrup: 10 mL (dextromethorphan 20mg + guaifenesin 200mg) q4h, max 60mL/day. Extended-release tablets: 30-600mg or 60-1200mg strengths (DM-guaifenesin combination ratios).
**Pediatric:** FDA recommends against pediatric use of these combination medications; AAP advises against use under age 4; some sources support use ≥2y with careful liquid measurement.

## Special Populations
- **Pregnancy:** CYP2D6/CYP3A activity elevated during pregnancy — individualized consideration warranted
- **Breastfeeding:** dextromethorphan and dextrorphan minimally present in breast milk, do not affect the infant
- **Hepatic/renal impairment:** no dose adjustment advised per manufacturer label
- **Elderly:** no specific dose adjustment needed

## Contraindications
- MAOI use: to avoid serotonin syndrome, do not take within 14 days of MAOI use or any other serotonergic-neurotransmission-increasing drug
- Phenylketonuria (phenylalanine-containing formulations) — risk of seizures, intellectual disability, tremors in PKU patients
- Age <4 years — "this drug combination may cause death in this age group" (per StatPearls' own wording)
- Severe hepatic/renal disease
- Hypersensitivity to dextromethorphan or guaifenesin

## Adverse Effects
- **Neuropsychiatric:** PCP-like dissociative psychosis — paranoid feelings, hallucinations, delusions; insomnia; dissociation
- **Cardiovascular:** tachycardia, hypertension
- **Other:** dizziness, drowsiness, blurred vision, altered mental status; severe type I hypersensitivity (rash, dyspnea, oral/facial swelling)
- **Abuse potential:** high, despite OTC availability, due to dissociative/euphoric effects at supratherapeutic doses

## Drug Interactions
- Serotonergic agents (SSRIs, SNRIs, TCAs): increased serotonin syndrome risk
- MAOIs: contraindicated, major serotonin syndrome risk
- Respiratory depressants (benzodiazepines, opioids, alcohol): enhanced CNS/respiratory depression
- Antiarrhythmics (amiodarone, procainamide, quinidine): affect heart rhythm
- CYP2D6 inhibitors (e.g. rolapitant): can triple dextromethorphan exposure; CYP2D6 "poor metabolizers" need close monitoring due to narrow therapeutic index at high doses

## Toxicity & Overdose (Abuse "Plateaus")
- 1.5 mg/kg: mild stimulation
- 2.5–7.5 mg/kg: mild-moderate euphoria/hallucinations
- 7.5–15 mg/kg: dissociation, "out-of-body" state
- >15 mg/kg: complete dissociation, nonresponsiveness
- **Overdose symptoms:** dyspnea, seizures, vomiting, drowsiness, tachycardia, hyperthermia, anxiety, altered mental status
- **Management:** no specific antidote; symptomatic treatment; activated charcoal may help; supplemental O2/assisted ventilation if needed; naloxone can reverse coma/respiratory depression but should not replace supportive care
