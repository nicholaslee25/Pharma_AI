## Introduction / Mechanism
Cofactor in >300 enzyme systems: protein synthesis, muscle/nerve function, blood glucose control, blood pressure regulation. Required for energy production, oxidative phosphorylation, glycolysis; contributes to bone structural development; required for DNA/RNA/glutathione synthesis. Plays a role in active transport of calcium/potassium across cell membranes (nerve impulse conduction, muscle contraction, normal heart rhythm).

Adult body contains ~25g magnesium: 50–60% in bone, most of the rest in soft tissue. <1% in blood serum, tightly regulated. Normal serum range: 0.75–0.95 mmol/L; **hypomagnesemia = <0.75 mmol/L**. Kidneys control homeostasis, normally excreting ~120mg/day in urine (reduced when status is low).

## Assessing Status
Difficult to assess since most magnesium is intracellular/in bone. Serum magnesium is the most common test but does NOT accurately reflect total body magnesium or tissue-specific concentrations — comprehensive assessment may need both labs and clinical evaluation.

## Recommended Intakes
310–420mg for adults; 30–410mg for infants/children/adolescents, varies by age/sex/life stage.

## Sources
Food: green leafy vegetables, legumes, nuts, seeds, whole grains, certain beverages; some fortified cereals. ~30–40% of dietary magnesium is absorbed. Supplements: forms that dissolve well in liquid absorb better; **aspartate, citrate, lactate, chloride forms have higher bioavailability than magnesium oxide and magnesium sulfate**. Medicines: some heartburn/antacid remedies and laxatives contain magnesium.

## Intakes and Status
Many in the US consume less than recommended; men ≥71y and adolescent males/females have highest risk of low intake. No routine national magnesium-status surveillance data exists (unlike vitamin D/C).

## Deficiency
Early signs: loss of appetite, nausea, vomiting, fatigue, weakness. Progression: numbness, muscle contractions/cramps, seizures, abnormal heart rhythms, coronary spasms. Severe: hypocalcemia or hypokalemia. Symptomatic deficiency uncommon in healthy people — usually from specific health conditions or medications.

## Groups at Risk of Inadequacy
GI diseases, type 2 diabetes, alcohol dependence, older adults.

## Magnesium and Health

**Hypertension/cardiovascular disease:** clinical trials suggest supplementation only marginally lowers blood pressure. Observational data link higher intake/serum levels to lower sudden cardiac death, ischemic heart disease, stroke risk — but ODS states more research is needed to confirm supplementation's actual effect on CVD.

**Type 2 diabetes:** meta-analyses of cohort studies link higher magnesium intake to lower T2D risk, but only a few small clinical trials tested supplementation for glycemic control, with conflicting results. **American Diabetes Association: not enough evidence to recommend magnesium for glycemic control.**

**Osteoporosis:** essential for bone health; some studies link higher intake to higher BMD, others find low serum magnesium in osteoporotic women — but more research needed on whether supplements prevent/manage osteoporosis specifically.

**Migraine headaches:** low serum/tissue magnesium associated with migraine occurrence. Some research suggests regular supplementation may reduce migraine frequency. **The American Academy of Neurology and American Headache Society have concluded magnesium is "probably effective" for migraine prevention** (despite limited data) — this is the strongest, most specific efficacy conclusion in this fact sheet.

## Health Risks from Excessive Magnesium
Low risk from food alone in healthy people. High supplement/medication doses can cause diarrhea, nausea, abdominal cramping. Very high doses → magnesium toxicity: hypotension, vomiting, difficulty breathing, irregular heartbeat, cardiac arrest; a few fatal hypermagnesemia cases reported. **Tolerable Upper Intake Level (supplemental magnesium only, not food): 350mg for adults; 65–350mg for children/adolescents**, varies by age.

## Interactions with Medications
May interact with: oral bisphosphonates, tetracyclines, quinolone antibiotics (absorption interference). Some medications affect magnesium status: diuretics, proton pump inhibitors.
