# PubMed Abstract — Wieland et al. 2021 Systematic Review (condensed)

## Design
Systematic review (Cochrane risk-of-bias tools, GRADE certainty assessment), searched 6 databases + 4 trial registers + 2 preprint sites, screened 1187 records. Assessed elderberry for prevention/treatment of viral respiratory illness AND specifically investigated the COVID-19-era concern that elderberry might overstimulate the immune system and increase "cytokine storm" risk.

## Scale
5 RCTs included for treatment/prevention of viral respiratory illness; 3 additional studies measured cytokine production ex vivo after elderberry ingestion.

## Results
- **Common cold prevention:** elderberry may NOT reduce the risk of developing a cold
- **Common cold treatment:** may reduce duration/severity, but evidence is uncertain
- **Influenza treatment:** may reduce duration, but evidence is uncertain
- **Vs. oseltamivir (Tamiflu):** one elderberry-containing product may be associated with LOWER risk of influenza complications and adverse events compared to oseltamivir
- **Cytokine storm concern:** no studies found linking elderberry to clinical inflammatory outcomes; some evidence elderberry affects inflammatory markers, but this effect may decline with ongoing supplementation; one small study found elderberry as effective as or less effective than diclofenac (an NSAID) for cytokine reduction over time

## Authors' Conclusion
Elderberry may be a safe option for treating viral respiratory illness, with no evidence it overstimulates the immune system. However, evidence on both benefits and harms remains uncertain, and more research is needed for firm conclusions.

## Disclosed Conflict of Interest
Lead author (LSW) is a Systematic Review Editor for the publishing journal and Coordinator of the Cochrane Complementary Medicine Field — a role-based affiliation disclosed by the authors themselves, not necessarily a financial conflict.

## Relevance
This is a more recent, more methodologically rigorous (GRADE-assessed), and more cautious synthesis than the Hawkins et al. 2019 meta-analysis (separate raw file) — it should be weighted as the more current evidence summary, with Hawkins 2019's "large effect size" framing understood as coming from a smaller, less uncertainty-adjusted analysis.
