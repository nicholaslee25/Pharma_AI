## What It's Used For
Dark purple berries of the European black elder tree (Sambucus nigra). Historically used in folk medicine for colds and flu; promoted as a dietary supplement for colds, flu, and other conditions.

## Evidence for Cold/Flu
Research is limited but somewhat promising. Some studies suggest elderberry may relieve symptoms of flu, colds, or other upper respiratory infections. A meta-analysis found black elderberry supplementation "effectively treats upper respiratory symptoms" in clinical trials, though the overall evidence base remains small (see Hawkins et al. 2019 raw file).

**COVID-19:** NCCIH states there is no good evidence to support elderberry's use for COVID-19, and regulatory agencies have taken action against companies making unsubstantiated COVID-19 claims about elderberry products.

## Safety Concerns & Toxicity
**Raw/unripe berries:** Raw or unripe elderberries and other parts of the elder tree (leaves, stem) contain poisonous cyanide-producing (cyanogenic glycoside) substances that can cause nausea, vomiting, and severe diarrhea. **Cooking eliminates this toxin.** This is a real, distinct safety consideration from the standardized commercial extract products (e.g. Sambucol) sold as supplements, which use processed/cooked extract, not raw berries.

**Drug interactions:** NCCIH advises anyone taking medication to talk with a healthcare provider before using elderberry, noting some herbs and medicines interact in harmful ways (no specific interaction list given on this page).

**Pregnancy/breastfeeding:** Little is known about elderberry's safety for these populations.
