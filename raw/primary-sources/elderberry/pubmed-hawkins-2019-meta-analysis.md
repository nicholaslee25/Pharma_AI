## Design
Meta-analysis of RCTs of elderberry supplementation for upper respiratory symptoms (common cold, influenza). Evaluated moderators including vaccination status and underlying pathology (cold vs. flu).

## Scale
180 total participants across included trials (small evidence base, explicitly noted by the authors as a limitation — "no large-scale studies or meta-analyses" existed before this one).

## Results
Supplementation with elderberry substantially reduced upper respiratory symptoms; quantitative synthesis yielded a **large mean effect size** (specific numeric effect size not given in the abstract itself).

## Authors' Conclusion
Findings present elderberry as a potential alternative to antibiotic misuse for viral upper respiratory symptoms, and a potentially safer alternative to prescription drugs for routine common cold/influenza cases.

## Caveat
This is a small-N meta-analysis (180 participants total) — see the more recent and more rigorously GRADE-assessed Wieland et al. 2021 systematic review (separate raw file) for a more cautious, uncertainty-weighted synthesis of a similar evidence base.
