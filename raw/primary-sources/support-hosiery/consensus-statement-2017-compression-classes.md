## Condensed facts

### Compression class definitions (European standard cited in this consensus)
- Class 1: 18–21 mmHg
- Class 2: 23–32 mmHg
- Class 3: 30–35 mmHg (some other systems place Class 3 higher, ~34–46 mmHg for "high pressure")
- Note: European and US compression-class mmHg bands are not identical to the informal US retail bands (mild 8–15, moderate 15–20, firm 20–30, extra-firm 30–40) seen on consumer product labels — a real labeling-terminology gap consumers should be aware of, not a contradiction between sources.

### Graded recommendations (Grade 1 = strong recommendation)
- Medical compression stockings (MCS) for chronic venous disorder symptoms — Grade 1B
- MCS to improve quality of life in chronic venous disease — Grade 1B
- MCS to prevent leg swelling/edema — Grade 1B
- MCS to reduce existing leg edema — Grade 1B
- "Ulcer kits" (compression systems) for venous leg ulcer healing — Grade 1A
- MCS to reduce venous leg ulcer recurrence — Grade 1A
- Immediate compression in acute DVT to reduce pain and swelling — Grade 1B
- MCS for lymphedema maintenance therapy — Grade 1A

### Overall evidence characterization
- "Robust evidence" specifically for venous leg ulcer prevention/treatment and lymphedema maintenance.
- "Moderately robust" / moderate evidence for symptom relief and edema prevention/treatment more broadly.
- This is a stronger, more consistent evidence base than the Cochrane varicose-vein-specific RCT review (PMID 34271595) found on its own narrower question — the two are not contradictory, they evaluate different (though overlapping) clinical questions and evidence bases.
