# Cochrane Review — Compression Stockings for the Initial Treatment of Varicose Veins (condensed facts, not full text)

**Source:** Cochrane systematic review, graduated compression stockings for the initial treatment of varicose veins in people without venous ulceration. PMID: 34271595. DOI: 10.1002/14651858.CD008819.pub4.
**Retrieved:** 2026-08-26 (viewed via PMC free full text of Cochrane summary, PMC8407251)
**License note:** Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews is copyrighted. Only condensed facts recorded here — no full text reproduced.

## Condensed facts
- 13 RCTs, 1,021 participants total, adults with primary varicose veins (CEAP C2–C4), no healed/active venous ulcer.
- Interventions: graduated compression stockings (knee-length, full-length, or full tights), pressure range 10–50 mmHg, vs. no treatment/placebo stockings or vs. different stocking types/pressures.
- Symptom improvement: 4 studies reported subjective improvement; analyses often at risk of bias. One trial (Kakkos 2018) found pain reduction with active stockings (mean VAS decrease 2.8, p=0.02).
- Physiological measures were mixed: one trial found no clear difference in ankle circumference (Kakkos 2018); another found oedema significantly reduced with compression, 84.6% resolution by day 3 (Mariani 2013).
- Compliance highly variable across trials (33% dropout in one study vs. 86.7% good compliance in another); pregnancy-related non-compliance particularly high.
- **Evidence certainty rated low to very low across outcomes**, due to risk of bias, inconsistency, and heterogeneous reporting.
- Bottom line: compression stockings are a longstanding first-line conservative option for varicose veins, but the RCT evidence base supporting that practice is thinner and lower-certainty than the near-universal clinical recommendation might suggest.
