## Indications
**Osteoarthritis** (1–2% gel, affected joints). **Actinic keratosis** (3% gel, dermatological use to prevent progression toward malignancy — a distinct, higher-concentration, prescription-only formulation not the same product as the OTC arthritis gel).

## Mechanism of Action
Same COX-inhibitory pathway as oral diclofenac — competitively blocks COX-1 and COX-2, reducing prostanoid synthesis. Diclofenac shows modest COX-2 selectivity (~4x greater COX-2 vs. COX-1 inhibition in vitro), though far less selective than dedicated COX-2 inhibitors. For actinic keratosis specifically, proposed mechanism involves inhibiting epithelial growth factors that would otherwise promote angiogenesis and inhibit apoptosis in proliferating tissue (investigational).

## Dosing (Topical Gel)
**Osteoarthritis:** 1–2% diclofenac sodium gel, up to 16g/day for monoarthritis (single joint), up to 32g/day for polyarthritic joints. **Actinic keratosis:** 3% gel applied twice daily as hybrid therapy.

## Pharmacokinetics — Topical vs. Systemic
Topical application carries much lower systemic exposure risk than oral administration — "less risk of systemic exposure to the drug" is explicitly noted as the rationale for topical NSAID's better safety profile. (For comparison, oral diclofenac is rapidly absorbed, binds plasma albumin, and concentrates in synovial fluid at inflammation sites.)

## Adverse Effects — Topical vs. Systemic
**Topical-specific:** mild-to-moderate skin irritation at the application site, with minimal systemic exposure concern.
**Systemic (mainly relevant to ORAL diclofenac, included here for contrast):** cardiovascular (increased MI/heart failure/stroke/death risk — NSAID class effect); GI (acid-mediated gastric epithelial damage → erosion/ulceration); renal (AKI risk with extended use); hepatic (rare hepatitis, more prevalent with long-term use for rheumatoid arthritis); hematologic (increased bleeding risk via platelet inhibition). **The topical formulation avoids most of these systemic complications due to limited absorption** — this is the central safety rationale for choosing topical over oral NSAID therapy when feasible.

## Contraindications
Absolute: cardiovascular history (prior MI/stroke), severe renal insufficiency, prior NSAID anaphylaxis, active GI bleeding/ulcers. **FDA boxed warning for cardiovascular risk** (class-wide NSAID warning, applies to the drug generally). Diclofenac is on the **Beers List** — avoid in elderly patients due to cardiovascular/GI concerns (this Beers guidance is more relevant to oral/systemic use than the topical gel specifically, though StatPearls does not separately caveat this). Misoprostol-combination formulations are contraindicated in pregnancy (misoprostol-specific, not a topical-gel-specific issue).

## Special Populations
- **Elderly:** Beers Criteria recommend avoidance due to CV/GI effects (context: primarily oral formulation risk)
- **Pregnant women:** contraindicated, especially misoprostol-combination formulations
- **Renal insufficiency:** contraindicated
- **H. pylori infection history:** requires special monitoring
- **Cardiovascular disease:** heightened risk necessitates lowest effective dosing

## Monitoring
Blood pressure, GI symptoms (including GERD), renal function, CBC, liver function testing — again, more relevant to oral/systemic diclofenac use than to the topical gel specifically, though StatPearls presents this as general monitoring guidance for the drug overall.
