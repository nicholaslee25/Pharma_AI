# Aleve / Naproxen — FDA Cardiovascular Warning History

## 2004 FDA Warning
On 2004-12-20, the FDA issued a warning against naproxen (Aleve, Anaprox, Naprosyn) after the NIH halted a colon-adenoma-prevention clinical trial. Patients taking 2 tablets/day for 3 years (dose above OTC max) developed a 50% higher risk of heart attack and stroke. Of ~2,500 patients in the study, about 70 suffered stroke or heart attack, and 23 died. FDA recommended Aleve not be taken for more than 10 days without medical direction.
Source (via WebSearch): nbcnews.com "Government study led to warning about naproxen"

## 2014 FDA Joint Advisory Committee (Arthritis Advisory Committee + Drug Safety and Risk Management Advisory Committee, February 2014)
Reviewed cardiovascular data available after 2005. Many committee members expressed the view that data suggested a more favorable CV risk profile for naproxen vs. other NSAIDs. However, the committee's official recommendation (not unanimous) was that current data does NOT support concluding naproxen has lower thrombotic-event risk than other NSAIDs.
Source (via WebSearch): blogs.nejm.org/cardioexchange "FDA advisers: meta-analysis does not prove that naproxen carries lower CV risk" (2014-02-11)

## July 2015 FDA Label Update
Following the 2014 advisory committee findings, FDA updated label warnings on non-aspirin NSAIDs generally (strengthened heart attack/stroke warning), applied uniformly rather than distinguishing naproxen as safer.
Source (via WebSearch): health.harvard.edu "FDA strengthens warning that NSAIDs increase heart attack and stroke risk"
