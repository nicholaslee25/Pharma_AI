# OFF! — Regulatory Status and Active Ingredients (EPA, not FDA)

**Retrieved:** 2026-08-26
**Sources:**

## Regulatory Status
OFF! is a skin-applied insect REPELLENT, regulated by the **EPA as a pesticide** under FIFRA — not by the FDA as a drug. No DailyMed / FDA Drug Facts listing exists for OFF! products because they are not drugs. EPA registration confirms the company submitted safety and efficacy data to the EPA, not the FDA. This is a fundamentally different regulatory pathway and product category than every other brand in the Insect Bite and Sting Management list, which are all FDA-regulated OTC drugs treating existing bites/stings.

## Active Ingredients (span multiple, by product line)
- **DEET-based lines** (e.g., OFF! Deep Woods, OFF! FamilyCare): DEET (N,N-diethyl-meta-toluamide), consumer products generally 5-99% DEET depending on formulation/product.
- **Picaridin-based** (DEET-free): OFF! Clean Feel, OFF! Defense.
- **PMD-based** (plant-derived, "OFF! Botanicals"): ~10% concentration, ~2 hours protection per manufacturer claim.
- **Essential-oil-based** (OFF! Kids): rosemary, citronella, geranium, cedarwood, peppermint, lemongrass, cornmint, or clove oils; ~90 minutes protection per manufacturer claim.

## EPA Safety Position on DEET
EPA concluded (1998 and 2014 reviews) that DEET-containing repellents "do not present a health concern" when used as directed; no age restriction for children, though standard label precautions apply (do not apply over cuts/wounds/irritated skin; avoid eyes/mouth/hands in young children; wash treated skin after returning indoors; for sprays, apply to hands first then face rather than spraying directly on face).

## Category-placement discrepancy
OFF! is filed in this database under "Insect Bite and Sting Management" (First Aid) — a treatment category for existing bites/stings — but it is fundamentally a PREVENTION product (repels insects before a bite occurs) with no drug-facts label, no active-ingredient overlap with any treatment product in the same category, and a different regulator entirely. This mirrors, in spirit, the [[petrolatum]] page's A&D/A+D category-placement discrepancy (a product that does something different from what its category implies) but is more fundamental here: it is not a drug at all.
