## Product Type & Regulatory Classification
Band-Aid Hydro Seal is a **hydrocolloid gel bandage/patch** — a medical DEVICE, not an FDA drug. No DailyMed Drug Facts listing exists. Occlusive wound dressings (the FDA device category covering hydrocolloid dressings) are classified under **21 CFR 878.4020, Class II** — a higher classification than the simple adhesive-strip bandages covered on [[bandages-covers-and-gauze]] (Class I, 21 CFR 880.5240 per that page's Nexcare research).

## Mechanism
Hydrocolloid gel absorbs wound exudate and maintains a moist wound-healing environment; per manufacturer marketing, a visible white "bubble" forms under the bandage within 24 hours, said to indicate the gel is absorbing fluid and healing is underway. Waterproof/shower-proof, designed for multi-day wear.

## Evidence (general hydrocolloid class, not brand-specific RCT)
Hydrocolloid dressings have an established, real evidence base for moist wound healing generally (well-documented in wound-care literature). Manufacturer-cited comparative claims (Compeed brand marketing, a hydrocolloid blister-patch competitor): "heals 20% faster than ordinary bandages," "stays in place 50% longer," "immediate pain relief" — these are manufacturer-stated figures, not independently verified against a specific peer-reviewed RCT with PMID in this pass, and are cited here as a competitor's marketing claim illustrating the general hydrocolloid-vs-standard-bandage comparison space, not verified data for Band-Aid Hydro Seal specifically.

## Category-placement note
Band-Aid Hydro Seal is filed under "Liquid Bandages" in this database's Pharmacy Times-derived category data, but it is a solid hydrocolloid PATCH, not a liquid at all — a different physical form and mechanism from both sub-classes described on [[liquid-bandages]] (antiseptic liquid film-formers like New-Skin, and cyanoacrylate liquid adhesives like the RCT-tested product on that page).
