# Banana Boat — Regulatory Status and Aloe Evidence Notes

**Retrieved:** 2026-08-26

## DailyMed search results
Direct search for "Banana Boat" on DailyMed returned ONLY sunscreen products (avobenzone, octocrylene, oxybenzone, homosalate, octisalate, zinc oxide, titanium dioxide combinations across various SKUs — Kids Max Protect & Play, Baby Mineral, Kids Mineral, Sheer Sensitive, Kids Sport, Simply Protect Kids, Light as Air Face, Protection Plus Vitamins Face). **No after-sun/aloe product appears in DailyMed at all.**

## After-sun aloe products — confirmed NON-DRUG cosmetics
Per multiple third-party ingredient-listing sources (incidecoder, skinsort, CVS ingredient pages) for "Banana Boat Soothing After Sun Gel with Aloe," "Banana Boat Aloe After Sun Spray," and "Banana Boat Aloe After Sun Lotion": ingredient lists (water, alcohol denat., glycerin, aloe barbadensis leaf juice, polysorbate 20, carbomer, triethanolamine, fragrance, colorants, etc.) show **no FDA-recognized active drug ingredient** (no camphor, menthol, lidocaine, benzocaine, or hydrocortisone). These products are marketed with names including "Sunburn Relief" and "After Sun Care for Sunburn Relief" despite containing no active ingredient recognized under any OTC drug monograph.

## Contrast — a competitor that DOES add an active ingredient
Ocean Potion's "Cooling After Sun Gel" (a different brand, cited only for contrast) reportedly contains lidocaine, menthol, aloe vera, and tea tree oil — illustrating that after-sun products CAN contain genuine active analgesic ingredients; Banana Boat's does not, per the sources reviewed.

## Aloe vera evidence — burn wound healing (not sunburn-specific)
A systematic review/meta-analysis of aloe vera for burn wounds (4 controlled trials, 371 patients, first/second-degree burns) found healing time significantly shorter with aloe vera vs. control (8.79 days shorter, pooled analysis of 1 RCT + 1 non-RCT) — but the two RCTs scored only 2/5 on the Jadad quality scale, indicating meaningful methodological limitations. This evidence concerns burn wounds generally, not sunburn specifically, and was not conducted on any Banana Boat product.

## Category-placement/product-type discrepancy
Banana Boat's FDA-regulated product line is entirely SUNSCREEN (a PREVENTION product, arguably belonging under a "Sunscreen" category rather than "Sun Burn Relief"). Its actual presence in the "Sun Burn Relief" (TREATMENT) category in this database's Pharmacy Times data is most plausibly driven by its non-drug aloe after-sun gel/spray/lotion line — which has no FDA Drug Facts panel, no confirmed active ingredient, and only weak/indirect aloe vera evidence (from burn-wound literature, not sunburn-specific literature) supporting any therapeutic claim.
