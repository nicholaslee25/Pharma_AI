# Dr. Scholl's Freeze Away - Sourcing Notes

Pulled: 2026-08-27

## FDA 510(k) Clearance
Source: https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf3/k031697.pdf ; https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf5/K052259.pdf
Dr. Scholl's Freeze Away Wart Remover, Model 40226 — FDA 510(k) K031697 (cleared 2003), related model K052259 (cleared 2005).
Classified as Class II medical device, substantially equivalent to predicate cryosurgical devices — not an OTC monograph drug.
Cryogen: dimethyl ether + propane. Average applicator-surface temperature after activation: -42°C.

## MAUDE Adverse Event Report
Source: https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/scripts/cdrh/cfdocs/cfmaude/detail.cfm?mdrfoi__id=17912907&pc=GEH
Adverse event report exists for "Dr. Scholl's Freeze Away Max Plantar Wart Remover" (cryogenic wart remover) — not independently characterized in detail in this pass.

## Dual Action SKU
Per manufacturer/retail sourcing: "Dr. Scholl's Dual Action Freeze Away Wart Remover" combines the cryogenic device with a separate salicylic acid 17% liquid wart remover as a post-cryo treatment.

## Efficacy cross-reference
Per Kwok et al. 2012 Cochrane review (already documented in ingredients/salicylic-acid.md): cryotherapy vs placebo RR 1.45 (95% CI 0.65-3.23, not significant); cryotherapy vs salicylic acid RR 1.23 (95% CI 0.88-1.71, not significant); aggressive vs gentle cryotherapy RR 1.90 (95% CI 1.15-3.15); SA + cryotherapy combined vs SA alone RR 1.24 (95% CI 1.07-1.43).

## Safety guidance
Per manufacturer/drugs.com: do not use if diabetic or with poor blood circulation (frozen tissue may not heal as quickly or completely); common side effects: minor skin irritation, rash, itching, stinging, dryness.
