# VOLTAREN Gel - DailyMed Drug Facts / Prescribing Information

Source: https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=b04009ca-7545-4cd4-af63-f15aebf20f18&audience=consumer
Pulled: 2026-08-27

## Active Ingredient
Diclofenac sodium 10 mg in 1 g (1% topical gel)

## Indications
Relief of the pain of osteoarthritis of joints amenable to topical treatment, such as the knees and those of the hands. Not evaluated for spine, hip, or shoulder use.

## Dosage & Administration
- Upper extremities (hands, wrists, elbows): Apply 2g four times daily; maximum 8g per day per joint
- Lower extremities (knees, ankles, feet): Apply 4g four times daily; maximum 16g per day per joint
- Total daily maximum: 32g across all affected joints
- Use the provided dosing card to measure amounts.
- Avoid showering/bathing for at least 1 hour after application; do not wash treated hands for one hour if hands are the treatment site.

## Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to diclofenac
- History of asthma, urticaria, or allergic reactions to aspirin or NSAIDs
- Contraindicated for peri-operative pain in coronary artery bypass graft (CABG) surgery

## Warnings & Precautions
- Cardiovascular Risk: NSAIDs may cause increased risk of serious cardiovascular thrombotic events, myocardial infarction, and stroke, which can be fatal.
- Gastrointestinal Risk: NSAIDs cause increased risk of serious gastrointestinal adverse events including bleeding, ulceration, and perforation.
- Hepatic Effects: Elevations of one or more liver tests may occur during therapy. Monitor transaminases within 4-8 weeks of initiation.
- Skin Reactions: Can cause serious skin adverse events such as exfoliative dermatitis, Stevens-Johnson Syndrome (SJS), and toxic epidermal necrolysis (TEN).
- Pregnancy: Avoid in late pregnancy due to risk of premature ductus arteriosus closure.

## Adverse Reactions
Most common in clinical trials: application site reactions (7% vs. 2% placebo), particularly application site dermatitis (4% vs. <1% placebo). In long-term studies, application site dermatitis occurred in 11% of patients.
