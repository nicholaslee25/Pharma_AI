## Historical Context — Hexachlorophene (1950s-1972)
Both Phisoderm and sister product Phisohex (Winthrop Laboratories) were originally antibacterial/antiseptic skin cleansers formulated with hexachlorophene. In September 1972, the FDA restricted hexachlorophene concentrations above 1% in OTC products following documented neurotoxicity — cited sources report 39 infant deaths in France and 15 in the United States linked to hexachlorophene exposure. Both products were removed from general retail distribution at that time. The Phisoderm brand was subsequently reformulated (Winthrop Laboratories/Sterling Drug, per sources located) and continued under new, non-hexachlorophene formulations, eventually arriving at today's salicylic-acid-based acne-cleanser positioning.
