# DailyMed — Pomada Dragon Pain Relieving/Pain Relief Cream, FDA Drug Facts

**Source:** https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=c283291d-feaa-4f89-8fde-9e3957ea8b9f&type=display
**Retrieved:** 2026-08-26

## Active Ingredients & Strengths
- Camphor (synthetic) 4% — Topical analgesic
- Menthol 10% — Topical analgesic
- Methyl Salicylate 30% — Topical analgesic

## Uses
Temporary relief of minor pain from arthritis, muscle cramps, sprains/strains, bruising, backache.

## Warnings
Do not apply to open wounds or sunburned/windburned/dry/irritated skin; ask a health professional before use if pregnant/breastfeeding; keep out of reach of children, contact Poison Control if swallowed.

## Directions
Adults/children 12+: apply to affected area 3-4 times daily max.

## Inactive Ingredients
Ethylparaben, glyceryl monostearate, polysorbate 80, purified water, sorbitan monooleate, stearic acid.

## MAJOR FINDING — identical formulation to BENGAY Ultra Strength
Pomada Dragon's active-ingredient profile (camphor 4% + menthol 10% + methyl salicylate 30%) is **IDENTICAL** to [[BENGAY]] Ultra Strength Non-Greasy Cream's confirmed formulation (camphor 4%, menthol 10%, methyl salicylate 30%) — the exact "identical formulations under two brand names" pattern this database's research process specifically watches for. Both exceed the FDA's 2012 burn-risk thresholds (>3% menthol, >10% methyl salicylate) on every ingredient, and both carry the full methyl-salicylate NSAID-class risk profile (see [[topical-counterirritants]]).

## Category-placement discrepancy
Pomada Dragon is filed in this database under "Topical Anesthetics" (1% share, per the category file) alongside genuinely anesthetic (lidocaine/benzocaine-containing) products like Aspercreme with Lidocaine, Salonpas Lidocaine, and Dermoplast. **Pomada Dragon contains NO anesthetic ingredient at all** (no lidocaine, no benzocaine) — its actual mechanism is the counterirritant class (camphor/menthol/methyl salicylate) already documented on [[topical-counterirritants]], mechanistically identical to Icy Hot, BENGAY, and Tiger Balm, all of which are filed under "Thermal Relief Products" instead. This is a real category-placement mismatch, in the same family as the [[A&D]]/[[A+D First Aid Ointment]] petrolatum-filed-as-antibiotic discrepancy documented elsewhere in this database.
