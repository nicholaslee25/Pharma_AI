# ThermaCare — Device Classification, Pivotal RCT, and Recall

**Retrieved:** 2026-08-26

## Product Type & Mechanism
ThermaCare HeatWraps use "HeatCell" technology: iron powder + oxygen (from air, once package opened) + water + salt, reacting exothermically (iron oxidation). Reaches ~40°C (104°F) within ~30 minutes, sustained ~8 hours. **FDA Class II medical DEVICE, not a drug** — no active pharmaceutical ingredient, no DailyMed Drug Facts listing.

## Pivotal Efficacy RCT — Nadler et al. 2002 (PMID 12004166, Spine 27(10):1012-7)
Prospective, randomized, single (investigator) blind, comparative-efficacy trial, funded in whole or part by The Procter & Gamble Company (ThermaCare's original manufacturer). Continuous low-level heat wrap therapy (40°C, 8 hours/day) vs. ibuprofen (1200mg/day) vs. acetaminophen (4000mg/day), subjects with acute nonspecific low back pain.
**Results:**
- Day 1 pain relief (mean score): heat wrap 2.0 > ibuprofen 1.51 > acetaminophen 1.32
- Days 3-4 mean pain relief: heat wrap 2.61 > acetaminophen 1.95 > ibuprofen 1.68
- **Conclusion (per study title): continuous low-level heat wrap therapy provided MORE efficacy than both ibuprofen and acetaminophen for acute low back pain** — a genuinely strong, positive, quantified finding for a non-drug device outperforming standard oral analgesics in a head-to-head trial.

Additional related trials located (not independently detailed in this pass): Nadler et al., "Treating acute low back pain with continuous low-level heat wrap therapy and/or exercise" (Spine Journal); "Continuous Low-Level Heat Wrap Therapy for the Prevention and Early Phase Treatment of Delayed-Onset Muscle Soreness of the Low Back" (Arch Phys Med Rehabil); a separate trial on heat wrap vs. acetaminophen for primary dysmenorrhea (PMID 15493566) — directly relevant to ThermaCare's separate Menstrual Pain Relief category appearance.

## Real FDA Recall History
Pfizer Consumer Healthcare (ThermaCare's manufacturer at time of recall) voluntarily recalled SIX LOTS of ThermaCare HeatWraps — spanning Muscle Pain Therapy, Menstrual, and Back Pain Therapy product lines (lots distributed June 2017 - March 2018) — due to two identified defects: (1) some heat cells reaching higher-than-specified temperatures, and (2) some wraps leaking heat-cell contents, both risking burns/blisters/skin irritation. **FDA Class 2 recall classification.** Source: FDA.gov company announcement + multiple corroborating news/consumer-safety sources.
