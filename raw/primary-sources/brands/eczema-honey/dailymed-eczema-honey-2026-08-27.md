## Drug Facts
**Active Ingredient:** Colloidal Oatmeal 1%
**Purpose:** Skin Protectant
**Uses:** Temporarily protects and helps relieve minor skin irritation and itching due to rashes and eczema.
**Warnings:** External use only. Do not get into eyes. Keep out of reach of children; contact Poison Control if swallowed. Stop use if condition worsens or symptoms persist longer than 7 days, or clear up and reappear within a few days.
**Directions:** Apply evenly to skin. Apply lightly and use 1-3 times a day as needed. Store below 30°C (68°F).
**Inactive Ingredients:** Olive oil, sweet almond oil, honey, aloe vera leaf juice, beeswax, water, sodium bicarbonate, potassium sorbate, sunflower seed oil, phenoxyethanol, ethylhexylglycerin, hexylene glycol, caprylyl glycol.

## Note on brand-name/active-ingredient mismatch
Despite the brand name "Eczema Honey" and honey-forward marketing, honey is listed as an INACTIVE ingredient only. The sole FDA-recognized active ingredient (the one actually regulated and required to meet the OTC skin-protectant monograph) is colloidal oatmeal 1% — the same active ingredient as Aveeno Eczema Therapy (see colloidal-oatmeal.md). The honey used is generic "organic honey," not manuka or kanuka honey (the two varietals with any dedicated clinical-trial evidence for eczema).

## Separate brand-name-confusion finding
A different, seemingly unrelated product line, "Kenkoderm Eczema Honey Soothing Body" / "Kenkoderm Eczema Honey Intensive Soothing Balm" (labeler: Guangzhou Ariel Biotech Co., Ltd.), also uses the words "Eczema Honey" in its product name and lists colloidal oatmeal (1.0%, plus allantoin 0.5% in one SKU) as its active ingredient(s). DailyMed setid=4865c697-05fc-eac8-e063-6294a90ae541. This is NOT the Pharmacy Times-tracked "Eczema Honey" brand (Eczema Honey CO LLC) — flagged as a real, findable source of consumer brand-name confusion between two unaffiliated-appearing product lines sharing near-identical naming.

## Brand-specific trial search
No Eczema-Honey-brand-specific clinical trial was located. General honey-for-atopic-dermatitis evidence found:
- Al-Waili human studies + Alangari et al. 2017 (Immun Inflamm Dis, PMC5418133) — small mechanistic study (n=14), manuka honey applied to one AD lesion site vs. untreated contralateral site for 7 days; found reduced IL-4-induced CCL26 release and mast cell degranulation in vitro/ex vivo — authors call for RCT confirmation, not itself a definitive clinical efficacy trial.
- Fingleton et al. 2014 (J R Soc Med Cardiovasc Dis, pilot single-blind RCT) — topical medical-grade Kanuka honey vs. aqueous cream control for eczema: feasible, moderately acceptable, but **no evidence of efficacy above the aqueous cream control**.
Neither trial used manuka/kanuka honey in a colloidal-oatmeal base matching this brand's actual formulation, and the brand's own active ingredient (colloidal oatmeal) evidence is covered separately on colloidal-oatmeal.md.
