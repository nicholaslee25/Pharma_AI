## Active Ingredient
Diphenhydramine HCl 25 mg (Benadryl Allergy Ultratab and tablet forms)

## Purpose
Antihistamine

## Uses
Temporarily relieves: runny nose; sneezing; itchy, watery eyes; itching of the nose or throat — due to hay fever or other upper respiratory allergies. Also labeled for allergic reactions and cough due to minor throat/airway irritation (some formulations).

## Warnings
May cause marked drowsiness; alcohol, sedatives, tranquilizers may increase drowsiness. Do not use with other diphenhydramine-containing products (including topical). Ask a doctor before use with glaucoma, breathing problems (emphysema/chronic bronchitis), or trouble urinating (enlarged prostate). Overdose: hallucinations, unable to be awakened, seizure, trouble breathing, collapse — call Poison Control (1-800-222-1222) or get emergency help immediately.

## Directions
Adults/children ≥12y: 25–50mg (1-2 tablets) every 4-6h, max 6 doses/24h. Children 6-11y: 12.5-25mg every 4-6h, max 6 doses/24h. Children <6y: ask a doctor.

## Brand-specific notes beyond generic diphenhydramine
- **The TIRED study** (PMID 16818836, JAMA Pediatrics) — a randomized, placebo-controlled trial of diphenhydramine for infant nighttime awakening — found diphenhydramine was **no more effective than placebo**: only 1 of 22 infants on diphenhydramine improved vs. 3 of 22 on placebo; the data safety monitoring board stopped the trial early for futility. This directly contradicts diphenhydramine's common off-label reputation as a sleep aid and is worth flagging on the ingredient page since it's product-class-relevant, not Benadryl-brand-specific (the study used diphenhydramine generically).
- A meta-analysis of 18 RCTs found diphenhydramine produces significantly worse cognitive performance (attention, working memory, speed, vigilance) than second-generation antihistamines or placebo — reinforces the sedation profile already known.
- No Benadryl-brand-specific trial (as opposed to generic diphenhydramine) was found.
