# DailyMed — Icy Hot, FDA Drug Facts

**Source:** https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=40c8c02f-fa5f-4e80-81a0-48271fa6f94b
**Retrieved:** 2026-08-26

## Active Ingredients & Strengths (vary by format)
- **Icy Hot Pain Relieving Balm (ointment):** Menthol 7.6%, Methyl Salicylate 29%
- **Icy Hot Pain Relieving Stick:** Menthol 10%, Methyl Salicylate 30%
- **Icy Hot Pain Relieving Cream:** Menthol 10%, Methyl Salicylate 30% (per separate retail listing)

## Purpose
Topical analgesic (both ingredients).

## Uses
Temporary relief of minor pain associated with arthritis, simple backache, muscle strains, sprains, bruises, and cramps.

## Warnings
External use only; avoid eyes and mucous membranes; do not use with a heating pad; do not bandage tightly; do not apply to wounds or damaged skin. **Rare cases of serious burns have been reported with products of this type** (per label language reflecting the FDA's 2012 Drug Safety Communication — see [[topical-counterirritants]]). Stop use and consult a doctor if condition worsens or symptoms persist beyond 7 days.

## Directions
Apply to affected area not more than 3-4 times daily (adults/children per label age restriction).

## Cross-reference — FDA 2012 burn-risk communication
Icy Hot was one of the brands explicitly named in press coverage of the FDA's September 2012 Drug Safety Communication on rare-but-serious burns from topical menthol/methyl salicylate/capsaicin products. Icy Hot's menthol (7.6-10%) and methyl salicylate (29-30%) concentrations both exceed the FDA-identified risk thresholds (>3% menthol, >10% methyl salicylate) by a wide margin. See [[topical-counterirritants]] for full detail.
