### Stridex Maximum — full Drug Facts panel
(setid=a3b16d52-76a5-438b-a120-bb59ce062cc1)

**Active Ingredient:** Salicylic acid 2.0% (w/w)
**Purpose:** Acne medication
**Uses:** Treatment of acne; reduces pimples and blackheads while promoting skin healing; helps prevent new acne pimples from forming
**Warnings:** For external use only. Do not use if allergic to salicylic acid. Skin irritation and dryness more likely when combined with other topical acne medications. Keep away from children. If swallowed, get medical help or contact Poison Control right away.
**Directions:** Cleanse skin thoroughly before application. Use pad to apply a thin layer to affected area one to three times daily. Because too much of this medication can cause excessive skin irritation, start with one application daily, then gradually increase as needed or as directed. Reduce frequency if excessive dryness or peeling occurs. Do not leave pad on skin for extended periods. Keep away from eyes, lips, and mucous membranes.
**Inactive Ingredients:** Ammonium lauryl sulfate, ammonium xylenesulfonate, citric acid, ethylhexylglycerin, fragrance, menthol, phenoxyethanol, purified water, simethicone, sodium borate, tetrasodium EDTA

## Brand-Specific Clinical Trial (secondary citation)
Journal of Clinical and Aesthetic Dermatology (JCAD) review, "Over-the-counter Acne Treatments: A Review" (jcadonline.com), cites: a 12-week, double-blind, randomized, vehicle-controlled study by Shalita et al., testing Stridex-brand 0.5% salicylic-acid pads (attributed in the review to "Blistex, Oak Brook, Illinois") applied twice daily vs. vehicle pads in mild-to-moderate acne. Result per the review: "the actively treated group demonstrated greater reduction of both inflammatory lesions and open comedones." Full primary citation (journal/year/PMID) not independently pulled in this pass — flagged as a minor sourcing gap on wiki/projects/pharmacy/drugs/Stridex.md.

Note: a separate, NOT independently confirmed as directly relevant, possible citation trail: Zander E, Weisman S. "Treatment of acne vulgaris with salicylic acid pads." Clin Ther. 1992 Mar-Apr;14(2):247-53. PMID 1535287 — search-summarized as a 180-patient randomized investigator-blinded trial of 2% salicylic acid pads vs. 10% benzoyl peroxide cream (43% inflammatory lesion reduction at 12 weeks, fewer side effects than BPO) — this citation was NOT found quoted inside the JCAD review itself when checked, so it is not confirmed to be Stridex-brand-specific (could be a generic/different-brand SA pad study). Not used as brand-specific evidence on the Stridex page for this reason.
