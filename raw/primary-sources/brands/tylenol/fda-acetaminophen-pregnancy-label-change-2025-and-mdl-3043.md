# Tylenol / Acetaminophen — FDA Pregnancy Label-Change Process (2025) and MDL 3043 Litigation

## FDA announcement (2025-09-22)
FDA announced it had initiated the process for a label change for acetaminophen regarding pregnancy use, citing an association (not causation) between prenatal acetaminophen exposure and later autism spectrum disorder / ADHD diagnoses in children. Cited studies include the Nurses' Health Study II and the Boston Birth Cohort, in addition to the Alemany et al. cohort (73,881 mother-child pairs) already documented in this database's acetaminophen ingredient page. FDA sent a "physician's notice" to doctors. FDA's own announcement explicitly states "a causal relationship has not been established" and acknowledges "there are contrary studies in the scientific literature."

## Pushback
- Kenvue (Tylenol's manufacturer) has publicly urged the FDA to reject the label change.
- ACOG and other medical societies maintain that high-quality evidence does not support a causal link, and that acetaminophen remains the only OTC antipyretic/analgesic considered appropriate for use during pregnancy, since NSAIDs and aspirin carry well-documented adverse fetal effects.

## Litigation — MDL No. 3043 (S.D.N.Y., Judge Denise L. Cote)
Federal multidistrict litigation consolidating Tylenol-specific autism/ADHD prenatal-exposure injury claims, formed October 2022. Dismissed August 2024 (plaintiffs' scientific experts excluded). On 2026-07-13, the U.S. Second Circuit Court of Appeals vacated that dismissal, reviving approximately 550 federal cases, holding the experts were wrongly excluded.
