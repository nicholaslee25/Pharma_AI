# PubMed Abstract — Mortensen et al. 2014 Q-SYMBIO Trial (condensed)

**Citation:** Mortensen SA, et al. The effect of coenzyme Q10 on morbidity and mortality in chronic heart failure: results from Q-SYMBIO: a randomized double-blind trial. JACC Heart Fail. 2014 Dec. PMID: 25282031. Free article. ISRCTN94506234.

## Design
Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, multicenter trial. Patients with moderate-severe chronic heart failure randomized to CoQ10 100mg three times daily (300mg/day total) vs. placebo, added to standard HF therapy, followed 2 years. Short-term (16-week) endpoints: NYHA class change, 6-minute walk test, NT-proBNP. Long-term (2-year) primary endpoint: composite major adverse cardiovascular events (time-to-first-event).

## Scale
420 patients.

## Results
- **Short-term (16 weeks):** no significant changes in NYHA class, 6-min walk, or NT-proBNP
- **Primary long-term endpoint (2 years):** reached by **15%** (CoQ10) vs. **26%** (placebo) — HR **0.50** (95% CI 0.32–0.80, p=0.003), intention-to-treat
- **Cardiovascular mortality:** 9% vs. 16% (p=0.026)
- **All-cause mortality:** 10% vs. 18% (p=0.018)
- **HF hospitalization incidence:** significantly lower with CoQ10 (p=0.033)
- **NYHA class:** significant improvement in CoQ10 group after 2 years (p=0.028)

## Authors' Conclusion
Long-term CoQ10 treatment of chronic heart failure patients is safe, improves symptoms, and reduces major adverse cardiovascular events.

## Relevance
This is a genuinely strong, well-powered (relative to prior underpowered CoQ10-in-HF trials, per the authors' own framing), positive RCT for a specific indication (adjunctive chronic heart failure therapy at 300mg/day) — a notably different and more favorable picture than CoQ10's much weaker evidence for statin-myopathy prevention (see separate raw file). The two use cases should not be conflated.
