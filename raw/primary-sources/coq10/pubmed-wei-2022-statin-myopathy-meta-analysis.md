# PubMed Abstract — Wei et al. 2022 Meta-Analysis (condensed)

**Citation:** Wei H, et al. Effects of coenzyme Q10 supplementation on statin-induced myopathy: a meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials. Ir J Med Sci. 2022 Apr. PMID: 33999383.

## Design
Meta-analysis of RCTs of oral CoQ10 vs. placebo in patients with statin-induced myopathy. Searched PubMed, Embase, Cochrane Library. Outcomes: creatine kinase (CK) activity and degree of muscle pain.

## Scale
8 studies, 472 statin-treated patients total: 6 studies (281 participants) assessed CK activity; 4 studies (220 participants) assessed muscle pain.

## Results
- **CK activity:** mean difference +3.29 U/L (95% CI −29.58 to 36.17, p=0.84) — **not significant** (CoQ10 did not lower CK vs. placebo)
- **Muscle pain:** standardized mean difference −0.59 (95% CI −1.54 to 0.36, p=0.22) — **not significant**

## Authors' Conclusion
CoQ10 supplementation did NOT show significant benefit for improving statin-induced myopathy on either objective (CK) or subjective (pain) measures.

## Relevance
This is a genuinely negative finding for one of the most common marketed uses of CoQ10 (taking it alongside a statin to prevent/treat muscle aches) — contrasting with CoQ10's much stronger, positive evidence specifically in chronic heart failure (Q-SYMBIO trial, separate raw file). Other meta-analyses on this exact question (Banach 2015 PMID 25440725, Qu 2018 PMID 30371340, Kennedy 2020 PMID 32179207) are noted in this PubMed record's "similar articles" list as addressing the same question — not independently opened in this pass, but their existence indicates this is a well-studied, still-debated question rather than a single outlier study.
