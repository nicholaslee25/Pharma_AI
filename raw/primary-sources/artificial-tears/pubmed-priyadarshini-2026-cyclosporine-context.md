## Design
Cochrane systematic review/meta-analysis (update of a 2019 review), search date 17 March 2026. RCTs of topical cyclosporine A (CsA) vs. vehicle, artificial tears, or alternative CsA formulations/concentrations in dry eye disease (DED).

## Scale
58 RCTs, 10,225 participants. 60% of studies from South Korea, USA, China. Typical follow-up 3 months (range 3 days–12 months); median age 53; 46–100% female (median 80%).

## Key Findings
- CsA 0.05% vs. vehicle/artificial tears: may improve DED symptoms at 3 months (SMD −0.33, 95% CI −0.65 to −0.01; low certainty) and corneal staining (SMD −0.20, 95% CI −0.31 to −0.09; low certainty)
- CsA 0.05% users were probably more likely to discontinue due to adverse events (RR 1.99, 95% CI 1.02–3.87; moderate certainty)
- CsA 0.1% probably improves corneal staining (SMD −0.16, 95% CI −0.24 to −0.08) and conjunctival staining (SMD −0.20, 95% CI −0.28 to −0.12), both moderate certainty; probably more discontinuation due to AEs (RR 1.58, 95% CI 1.04–2.41)
- Higher CsA concentrations generally associated with more treatment discontinuation
- Overall certainty of evidence: very low to moderate across comparisons (downgraded for imprecision, risk of bias, heterogeneity)

## Authors' Conclusion
Low-certainty evidence suggests topical cyclosporine A may improve DED symptoms and some clinical signs vs. vehicle/artificial tears, but results are inconsistent/heterogeneous; higher concentrations trade efficacy for more discontinuation due to adverse events.
