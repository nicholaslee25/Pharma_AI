## Design
Systematic review of RCTs comparing efficacy of artificial tears in dry eye disease (PROSPERO CRD42022369619). Searched Web of Science, PubMed, Medline. 64 relevant articles identified.

## Key Findings
- Good evidence artificial tears improve dry-eye **symptoms** within 1 month of regular use, applied about **4 times daily**; **signs** generally take several months to improve
- Not all DED patients benefit — if no benefit after 1 month of use, alternative management should be considered
- **Combination formulations outperform single-active-ingredient artificial tears**
- **Polyethylene glycol**-containing tears outperform those containing **carboxymethylcellulose/carmellose sodium** and **hydroxypropyl methylcellulose**
- Evaporative dry eye disease specifically benefits from **liposome**-containing tears, especially higher concentration
- Limitations: variable DED definitions across studies, variable disease severity examined, compliance with artificial tears rarely quantified
