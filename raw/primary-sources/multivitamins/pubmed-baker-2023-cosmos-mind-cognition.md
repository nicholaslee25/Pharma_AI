# PubMed Abstract — Baker et al. 2023 COSMOS-Mind RCT (condensed)

**Citation:** Baker LD, et al. Effects of cocoa extract and a multivitamin on cognitive function: A randomized clinical trial. Alzheimers Dement. 2023 Apr. PMID: 36102337. Free PMC article.
**Retrieved via:** PubMed abstract (browser), https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36102337/
**Retrieved:** 2026-08-25
**Verification note:** Confirmed real via live PubMed lookup — a companion trial to the Sesso 2022 COSMOS cancer/CVD trial, same overall study infrastructure, different outcome focus.
**License note:** Copyrighted journal article (noted as published open-access/Free PMC) — condensed abstract facts only, per this vault's policy.

## Design
COSMOS-Mind: large, pragmatic, 2×2 factorial, 3-year RCT (cocoa extract 500mg flavanols/day vs. placebo; multivitamin-mineral vs. placebo) testing cognitive outcomes. Cognition assessed by phone at baseline and annually via a composite of standardized tests (Telephone Interview of Cognitive Status, Word List/Story Recall, Oral Trail-Making, Verbal Fluency, Number Span, Digit Ordering). Primary endpoint: change in global cognition composite with cocoa extract. Pre-specified secondary endpoint: change in composite with MVM supplementation.

## Scale
2,262 participants (mean age 73y, 60% women, 89% non-Hispanic White); 92% completed baseline + ≥1 annual assessment.

## Results
- **Cocoa extract:** NO effect on global cognition (mean z=0.03, 95% CI −0.02 to 0.08, p=0.28)
- **Multivitamin-mineral (MVM):** statistically significant BENEFIT on global cognition vs. placebo (mean z=0.07, 95% CI 0.02–0.12, p=0.007)
- MVM effect was MORE PRONOUNCED in participants with a cardiovascular disease history (no CVD history: z=0.06, 95% CI 0.01–0.11; CVD history: z=0.14, 95% CI −0.02–0.31; interaction nominal p=0.01)
- MVM benefits also observed for memory and executive function composites specifically
- No significant cocoa-extract × MVM interaction on any cognitive measure

## Authors' Conclusion
Cocoa extract did not benefit cognition. COSMOS-Mind provides the first evidence from a large, long-term, pragmatic RCT supporting potential MVM efficacy for improving cognition in older adults. Confirmation needed in a more diverse cohort; mechanisms unclear.

## Disclosed Funding
NIH National Institute on Aging funded; Mars (cocoa) and Pfizer (multivitamin manufacturer) did NOT contribute to trial design, conduct, data analysis/interpretation, or manuscript preparation/approval/submission decision — disclosed explicitly as an independence safeguard given both companies' commercial interest in the tested products.

## Relevance
This is a genuinely positive, reasonably rigorous finding for daily multivitamin use — cognitive benefit (global cognition, memory, executive function) in older adults over 3 years, more pronounced in those with cardiovascular disease history — from the SAME overall COSMOS study population that showed no cancer/CVD/mortality benefit in Sesso et al. 2022 (separate raw file). Read together, these two papers give a balanced, real picture: no benefit for cancer/CVD/mortality, but a real (if modest, single-trial, not-yet-replicated) cognitive benefit.
