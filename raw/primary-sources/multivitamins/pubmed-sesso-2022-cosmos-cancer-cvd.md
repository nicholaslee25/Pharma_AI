## Design
Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, 2×2 factorial trial (multivitamin-multimineral × cocoa extract) for cancer/CVD prevention. Intervention phase June 2015–December 2020. Primary outcome: total invasive cancer (excluding nonmelanoma skin cancer). Secondary: major site-specific cancers, total CVD, all-cause mortality, cancer risk in those with baseline cancer history.

## Scale
21,442 US adults (12,666 women ≥65y, 8,776 men ≥60y), free of major CVD and recent cancer diagnosis at enrollment. Median follow-up 3.6 years.

## Results
- **Total invasive cancer:** 518 (MVM) vs. 535 (placebo) — HR 0.97 (95% CI 0.86–1.09, p=0.57) — **no significant effect**
- **Breast cancer:** HR 1.06 (95% CI 0.79–1.42) — no significant effect
- **Colorectal cancer:** HR 1.30 (95% CI 0.80–2.12) — no significant effect
- **Lung cancer:** HR **0.62** (95% CI 0.42–0.92) — a significant PROTECTIVE effect (though a secondary/exploratory outcome, interpret cautiously)
- **Composite CVD:** 429 (MVM) vs. 437 (placebo) — HR 0.98 (95% CI 0.86–1.12) — no significant effect
- **All-cause mortality:** HR 0.93 (95% CI 0.81–1.08) — no significant effect
- No safety concerns identified

## Authors' Conclusion
A daily multivitamin-multimineral supplement did NOT significantly reduce total cancer incidence in older adults compared to placebo. Further research needed on other aging-related outcomes.

## Relevance
This is a large, rigorous, government/foundation-funded RCT (not industry-run) directly testing the most common marketed rationale for daily multivitamin use (general disease prevention) — and it found no benefit for its primary cancer outcome or for cardiovascular disease/overall mortality. See the companion COSMOS-Mind cognitive-outcomes trial (separate raw file) for a different, genuinely positive secondary finding from the same overall study population.
