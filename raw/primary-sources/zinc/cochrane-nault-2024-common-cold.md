## Design
Cochrane systematic review/meta-analysis of RCTs (children or adults) testing any form of zinc vs. placebo for prevention or treatment of the common cold/URTI. Excluded zinc combined with other minerals/vitamins/herbs. Searched CENTRAL, MEDLINE, Embase, CINAHL, LILACS to 22 May 2023; Web of Science + 2 trial registries to 14 June 2023.

## Scale
34 studies (15 prevention, 19 treatment), 8526 participants (22 adult studies, 12 pediatric). Most (17/34) used lozenges (acetate, gluconate, orotate forms; gluconate most common, 9/17). Zinc gluconate lozenge doses ranged 45–276 mg/day for 4.5–21 days across included trials. Most studies at unclear/high risk of bias in ≥1 domain.

## Prevention Results
- Risk of developing a cold: RR 0.93 (95% CI 0.85–1.01; I²=20%; 9 studies, 1449 participants; **low-certainty** — little/no reduction)
- Mean number of colds over 5–18 months: MD −0.90 (95% CI −1.93 to 0.12; I²=96%; 2 studies, 1284 participants; low-certainty)
- Duration of colds when they occur: MD −0.63 days (95% CI −1.29 to 0.04; I²=77%; 3 studies, 740 participants; **moderate-certainty** — little/no difference)
- Global symptom severity: SMD 0.04 (95% CI −0.35 to 0.43; low-certainty, little/no difference)

## Treatment Results
- **Mean duration of cold reduced**: MD **−2.37 days** (95% CI −4.21 to −0.53; I²=97%; 8 studies, 972 participants; **low-certainty**)
- Risk of ongoing cold at end of follow-up: RR 0.52 (95% CI 0.21–1.27; I²=65%; 5 studies, 357 participants; **very low-certainty** — uncertain)
- Global symptom severity: SMD −0.03 (95% CI −0.56 to 0.50; very low-certainty)
- Risk of a change in global symptom severity: RR 1.02 (95% CI 0.85–1.23; 1 study, 114 participants; low-certainty)

## Adverse Events
- Prevention trials: non-serious AE RR 1.11 (95% CI 0.84–1.47; very low-certainty); serious AE RR 1.67 (95% CI 0.78–3.57; low-certainty)
- **Treatment trials: non-serious AEs probably increased**, RR **1.34** (95% CI 1.15–1.55; **moderate-certainty**, 2084 participants, 16 studies) — e.g., unpleasant taste, loss of smell, vomiting, stomach cramps, diarrhea; no treatment study reported serious AE data

## Authors' Conclusion
Zinc supplementation may have little/no effect on cold prevention but may reduce duration of an ongoing cold, at the cost of increased non-serious adverse events. Wide variation in interventions/outcomes and incomplete reporting across studies limits confidence in the treatment-duration finding specifically.

## Note on Controversy
A subsequent commentary — Hemilä H, et al. "Shortcomings in the Cochrane review on zinc for the common cold (2024)." Front Med (Lausanne). 2024. PMID 39478818 — critiques this review's methodology (found via "Cited by" on PubMed). Not independently evaluated in this pass; flagged for awareness that this Cochrane review's conclusions are not undisputed within the field.
