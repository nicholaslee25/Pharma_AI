## Mechanism of Action
Neutralize stomach acid and inhibit pepsin (a proteolytic enzyme). Goals: alleviate pain, relieve pylorospasm, prevent digestion/corrosion of mucosa by acid.

**Aluminum hydroxide:** neutralizes acid and inhibits pepsin. When complexed with sucrose octasulfate (forming sucralfate) loses direct buffering capacity but heals chronic ulcers/prevents acute mucosal damage via reduced pepsin/acid access plus stimulated angiogenesis/granulation tissue. Also binds GI phosphate (used for hyperphosphatemia).

**Calcium salts:** neutralize gastric acidity, raise gastric/duodenal bulb pH; inhibit pepsin above pH 4; increase lower esophageal sphincter tone; increase esophageal peristalsis (pushes acid downward). Also form insoluble compounds with dietary phosphate, preventing its absorption.

Other ulcer-healing mechanisms across the class: promoting angiogenesis, binding bile acids, inhibiting peptic activity, suppressing H. pylori growth.

## Indications
Primary: mild intermittent GERD-associated heartburn. (Context: North American heartburn prevalence 18–28%, with 25% of adults reporting daily heartburn.) Other uses: duodenal/gastric ulcers, stress gastritis, pancreatic insufficiency, non-ulcer dyspepsia, bile-acid diarrhea, biliary reflux, constipation, osteoporosis (calcium), urinary alkalinization, phosphate binding in chronic renal failure.

## Dosing
- **Aluminum hydroxide (antacid):** 640mg up to 5–6x/day, max 3840mg/24h; suspension 320mg/5mL
- **Aluminum hydroxide (hyperphosphatemia):** 300–600mg three times daily, limited to 4 weeks, for serum phosphorus >7 mg/dL
- **Calcium carbonate (antacid):** max 8000mg/day (1–4 tablets daily) for up to 2 weeks symptomatic relief
- **Calcium carbonate (hyperphosphatemia):** total dose not to exceed 2000mg/day
- General: 10–15mL (1 tbsp) liquid or 1–2 tablets, 3–4x/day

## Contraindications
Hypersensitivity to any formulation component (absolute). Caution: renal failure, heart failure, edema, cirrhosis, low-sodium diets, uremia, GI hemorrhage, hyperparathyroidism, renal calculus, achlorhydria.

## Adverse Effects

**Aluminum hydroxide:** increased toxicity risk in renal failure patients and infants — osteopenia/osteomalacia, microcytic anemia, neurotoxicity, constipation/fecal impaction, nausea/vomiting/abdominal cramps, hypomagnesemia, hypophosphatemia.

**Calcium carbonate:** abdominal pain, anorexia, constipation, acid rebound, nausea, vomiting, flatulence, xerostomia, headache, hypercalcemia, hypophosphatemia, **milk-alkali syndrome**.

Adverse effects are prominent in infants and elderly; chronic use is not recommended for either population due to safety concerns.

## Drug Interactions
- **Acidic drugs (digoxin, chlorpromazine, isoniazid):** impaired absorption — reduced blood levels/therapeutic effect
- **Pseudoephedrine, levodopa:** increased absorption — possible toxicity from elevated serum concentrations
- **Tetracycline, fluoroquinolone antibiotics:** magnesium trisilicate/magnesium hydroxide bind these drugs, impeding absorption
- **Sodium bicarbonate specifically:** significantly alters urine pH — inhibits excretion of basic drugs (amphetamines, quinidine) while increasing excretion of acidic drugs (aspirin)

## Special Populations
- **Pregnancy:** aluminum-containing antacids considered safe, including during labor for aspiration prophylaxis. Calcium-containing antacids require caution — avoid excessive use since calcium crosses the placenta (amount reaching fetus depends on maternal physiologic changes)
- **Breastfeeding:** aluminum-containing antacids not well studied in lactation, though aluminum is endogenous to breast milk; calcium-containing antacids considered safe during breastfeeding, with maternal intake affecting milk calcium levels

## Monitoring
Periodic calcium and phosphorus plasma concentration monitoring suggested for patients on chronic antacid therapy.
