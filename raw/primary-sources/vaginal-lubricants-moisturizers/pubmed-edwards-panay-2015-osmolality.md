## Condensed facts — WHO osmolality/pH standards
- **WHO recommends vaginal/rectal lubricant osmolality NOT exceed 380 mOsm/kg.**
- In practice, because most commercially available products greatly exceed this, an upper limit of **1,200 mOsm/kg** is generally deemed acceptable.
- Normal vaginal pH: 3.8-4.5. Rectal pH: ~7.0 (relevant for products marketed for both uses).

## Condensed facts — hyperosmolar product harm (real product-specific data)
Using slug mucosal irritation assays (a standard model for epithelial irritancy testing):
- **Hypo-osmotic lubricants (32-316 mOsm/kg): no adverse effects.**
- **Moderately hyperosmotic products — Replens: 2,143 mOsm/kg; K-Y Jelly: 2,463 mOsm/kg — caused mild to moderate irritation.**
- **Very hyperosmotic lubricant — Astroglide: 5,848 mOsm/kg — caused SEVERE irritation and tissue damage.**
- Separate studies confirmed hyperosmolar lubricants cause epithelial cell damage and increase susceptibility to infections including HSV-2 — a real, biologically-grounded infection-risk mechanism, not just discomfort.
- The article's product table lists 30+ commercial products with measured pH/osmolality; examples cited: Yes Water-Based (154 mOsm/kg — well within WHO's recommended range), Balance Activ (309 mOsm/kg — also within range), Gynomunal (>2,000 mOsm/kg — well above).

## Why this matters for this ingredient page
This is real, product-name-specific, quantified evidence that NOT ALL water-based lubricants/moisturizers are equivalent — some widely-sold, market-leading brands (Replens, K-Y Jelly, Astroglide) measured far above the WHO-recommended osmolality ceiling in this study, with a documented dose-response relationship between osmolality and tissue irritation/damage. This directly supports (and sharpens, with real brand names) this database's existing "lubricant type/composition matters, products are not interchangeable" framing.
