## Definition
Genitourinary syndrome of menopause (GSM) — chronic genital, sexual, and urinary manifestations from estrogen deficiency affecting vulva, vagina, urethra, bladder.

## Prevalence
14-87% reported prevalence among postmenopausal women (wide range across studies); ~40-54% report BOTHERSOME symptoms specifically. Menopause causes a 95% reduction in estrogen production. **75% of women experience vaginal dryness; 40% report pain during intercourse (dyspareunia); 30-40% experience urinary urgency/frequency.**
**Real care-gap data: over 50% of affected patients use NO treatment at all; ~70% don't discuss the concern with a healthcare provider; less than 25% of affected women receive appropriate care.** This is a substantial, quantified undertreatment gap directly relevant to an OTC-focused database.

## Symptoms
Genital dryness, burning, irritation, discomfort, dyspareunia, reduced lubrication, dysuria, urinary urgency, recurrent infections.

## Evaluation
Primarily clinical history and physical exam; serum estrogen testing and routine labs have limited diagnostic utility (current assays lack sufficient sensitivity).

## Treatment Options
**Nonhormonal (first-line):** vaginal moisturizers and lubricants, regular sexual activity, pelvic floor physical therapy, vaginal dilators.
**Local estrogen therapy:** creams, tablets, vaginal rings; improvement typically within 2-4 weeks; minimal-to-no increased endometrial hyperplasia risk.
**Vaginal DHEA:** low-dose daily insert; tissue converts DHEA to estradiol/androgens locally.
**Systemic HRT:** effective for widespread menopausal symptoms; ~75% symptomatic improvement, vs. **80-90% success with local therapy** — local therapy actually outperforms systemic for this specific symptom set.
**Ospemifene:** oral SERM, 60mg daily; not shown to increase endometrial cancer, VTE, breast cancer, or breast cancer recurrence risk.
**Emerging:** laser/radiofrequency therapies show promise but lack current FDA approval specifically for GSM.

## Special Populations — Breast Cancer Survivors
Low-dose vaginal estrogen may be reasonable despite FDA contraindication labeling, per clinical evidence showing no increased mortality risk; DHEA carries a cautionary label; ospemifene shows weak antiestrogenic effects in breast tissue.

## Prognosis
Without treatment, GSM is progressive (does not spontaneously resolve); effective management significantly improves symptoms/quality of life.
