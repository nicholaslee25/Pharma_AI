## Design
Cochrane systematic review/meta-analysis, RCTs of probiotics (any strain/dose) vs. placebo/active alternative/no treatment for preventing antibiotic-associated diarrhea (AAD) in children (0–18y) receiving antibiotics. Searched MEDLINE, EMBASE, CENTRAL, CINAHL, AMED, Web of Science through Nov 2014, plus specialized registries and author/industry contact for unpublished data. GRADE used for evidence quality.

## Scale
23 studies, 3,938 participants. Probiotic strains tested: Bacillus spp., Bifidobacterium spp., Clostridium butyricum, Lactobacilli spp., Lactococcus spp., Leuconostoc cremoris, Saccharomyces spp., Streptococcus spp., alone or combined (single-strain to 10-strain combinations across different trials). Risk of bias high/unclear in 13 studies, low in 10.

## Results
- **Incidence of AAD:** probiotic group 8% (163/1992) vs. control group 19% (364/1906) — **RR 0.46 (95% CI 0.35–0.61)**, I²=55%, based on 3,898 participants across 22/23 trials reporting this outcome — a precise, consistent benefit
- No serious adverse events observed in otherwise-healthy children
- **Important safety caveat:** serious adverse events HAVE been observed in severely debilitated or immunocompromised children with underlying risk factors — the review recommends AVOIDING probiotic use in pediatric populations at risk for such events until further research clarifies safety in those groups specifically

## Authors' Conclusion
Probiotics are associated with a moderate-to-large, fairly consistent reduction in antibiotic-associated diarrhea risk in children — roughly halving AAD incidence (RR 0.46) — with no safety signal in healthy children, but a real, specific safety caveat for immunocompromised/severely debilitated pediatric populations.

## Relevance
This is one of the stronger, more specific, well-quantified efficacy findings in the OTC-supplement space in this database — but it is specific to AAD prevention in children taking antibiotics, not a general "probiotics are good for gut health" claim, and carries a real, named safety caveat for immunocompromised children.
