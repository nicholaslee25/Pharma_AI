# PubMed Abstract — Goodman et al. 2021 Systematic Review (condensed)

## Design
Systematic review/meta-analysis of RCTs evaluating probiotics for AAD prevention specifically in ADULTS. Searched CINAHL Plus, EMBASE, MEDLINE, Web of Science (inception–May 2021) plus trial registries/reference lists. Random-effects model; subgroup analyses by species and dose.

## Scale
42 studies, 11,305 participants.

## Results
- **Overall:** probiotics + antibiotics reduced AAD risk by **37%** — RR 0.63 (95% CI 0.54–0.73, p<0.00001); GRADE-rated **moderate** quality evidence
- **Dose-response:** high-dose probiotic outperformed low-dose of the same probiotic — RR 0.54 (95% CI 0.38–0.76, p<0.01)
- **Species-specific:** mainly **Lactobacillus** and **Bifidobacterium** genera species were found effective — not all probiotic species show this benefit
- **Baseline-risk-dependent:** studies with LOW baseline AAD risk showed NO significant difference; studies with MODERATE or HIGH baseline AAD risk showed significant risk reduction — i.e., probiotics help most in patients already likely to get AAD, not universally

## Authors' Conclusion
Probiotics are effective for preventing AAD in adults, with higher dosages and certain species (Lactobacillus/Bifidobacterium) showing increased effectiveness. Results may not generalize to lower-baseline-AAD-risk clinical scenarios.

## Disclosed Conflicts of Interest
None declared.

## Relevance
This adult-population finding (37% risk reduction, moderate-quality evidence) is directionally consistent with but somewhat more modest than the pediatric Cochrane finding (Goldenberg 2015, RR 0.46 = 54% reduction) — together they give a reasonably strong, real, dose- and species-dependent evidence base for this specific indication (AAD prevention), while NOT supporting probiotics as a universal "gut health" intervention regardless of baseline risk or species/strain used.
