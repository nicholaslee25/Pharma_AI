## Mechanism of Action
Direct-acting sympathomimetic amine, alpha-1 adrenergic agonist with minimal beta activity. Raises mean arterial pressure via venous/arterial vasoconstriction and increased cardiac preload. Effects on cardiac output vary by dosing method, volume status, baseline heart rate, cardiac status.

## Indications
**FDA-approved:** IV for clinically significant hypotension from vasodilation (septic shock, anesthesia); ophthalmic for mydriasis/conjunctival vasoconstriction; **intranasal for uncomplicated nasal congestion**; OTC topical hemorrhoid products.
**Off-label:** adjunct to neuraxial/peripheral nerve blockade, acute ischemic priapism (AUA-endorsed), anesthetic vasopressor in normotensive hypotension.

## FDA 2023 GRASE Determination — Oral Phenylephrine Ineffectiveness
FDA determined that oral phenylephrine as a decongestant is **ineffective, even at the standard 10mg q4h dose and at higher doses**. This determination applies specifically to ORAL formulations — nasal and topical (ophthalmic, hemorrhoid) preparations are NOT affected. Metabolism: primarily sulfate conjugation in the intestinal wall plus oxidative deamination via MAO-A/MAO-B, plus glucuronidation — this extensive first-pass/gut-wall metabolism is the mechanistic basis for oral phenylephrine's poor systemic bioavailability (StatPearls does not give an explicit oral bioavailability percentage in this article; see the Hatton 2007 meta-analysis raw file for the actual efficacy data underlying the ineffectiveness finding).

## Dosing (non-oral routes, for context)
IV bolus 50–100mcg aliquots; infusion 0.1–1.5 mcg/kg/min; onset 1–3min, duration 5–20min. Ophthalmic 1% (neonates, combined w/ cyclopentolate), 2.5% (dilated fundus exam), 10% (less common, systemic absorption concern), OTC drops as low as 0.12%. Nasal formulations 0.125–1%.

## Pharmacokinetics
Ophthalmic: rapid conjunctival/corneal absorption, onset minutes. IV: immediate, complete bioavailability. Nasal: rapid mucosal absorption, peak plasma 15–30min. Rectal: slower, peak ~30–60min. Vd 340L (exceeds total body volume — highly tissue-distributed). Renal excretion primary, mostly unchanged. Half-life ~2.5–3h.

## Contraindications
No definitive contraindications except hypersensitivity to the drug/components. IV formulation contains sodium metabisulfite — can cause allergic reactions including anaphylaxis/asthma in susceptible individuals.

## Warnings/Precautions
Caution with bradycardia history, severe cardiac dysfunction, hypovolemia; may worsen angina/heart failure/pulmonary arterial hypertension; autonomic dysfunction patients may have heightened pressor response; extravasation risk of tissue necrosis; concurrent sympathomimetics intensify pressor effects and increase hemorrhagic stroke risk.

## Adverse Effects
Most common: nausea, vomiting, headache, nervousness (in awake patients). Can cause baroreceptor-mediated reflex bradycardia. In hypovolemic patients with cardiac dysfunction, may raise afterload more than preload, reducing cardiac output and worsening angina/heart failure/pulmonary hypertension.

## Drug Interactions
**Increase pressor effect:** propranolol, clonidine, amitriptyline, atomoxetine, prednisone/steroids, ergot alkaloids, MAOIs.
**Decrease pressor effect:** phenothiazines (e.g. chlorpromazine, via alpha-1 antagonism), alpha-1 antagonists.

## Special Populations
- **Hepatic impairment:** caution, individualized dosing/monitoring (liver synthesizes BP-regulating factors)
- **Renal impairment:** ESRD patients show heightened sensitivity — lower IV doses recommended
- **Pregnancy:** ASA guidelines support IV phenylephrine for neuraxial-anesthesia hypotension; may be preferred over ephedrine for fetal acid-base balance absent maternal bradycardia concern
- **Breastfeeding:** systemic administration may reduce milk production; nasal spray/ophthalmic drops less likely to affect lactation
- **Pediatric:** IV safety/effectiveness not established
- **Elderly (≥65y):** limited effectiveness data; start at lower end of dosing range given hepatic/renal/cardiac impairment + polypharmacy risk

## Toxicity/Overdose
No known antidote for IV phenylephrine. Overdose hypertension typically transient given short duration — manage by discontinuing + chronotropic agents/vasodilators as needed. Extravasation: fluid aspiration, warming, elevation; subcutaneous phentolamine may be considered.

## Clinical Note
Accounts for up to 10% of perioperative medication errors (IV context) — closed-loop communication protocols reduce this.
