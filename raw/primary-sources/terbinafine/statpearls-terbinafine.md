The oral-form data (dosing, hepatotoxicity) is included here for completeness/contrast even though this vault's ingredient page is anchored on the OTC topical product — the two forms have very different risk profiles and this distinction is preserved explicitly on the ingredient page.

## Mechanism of Action
Allylamine antifungal — non-competitive inhibitor of squalene epoxidase, acting early in the ergosterol synthesis pathway. Not directly fungicidal by itself; intracellular squalene accumulation to toxic levels is what kills the fungal cell.

## Indications
**Oral (FDA-approved):** onychomycosis from dermatophytes — proximal subungual onychomycosis; distal lateral subungual onychomycosis affecting >50% of the nail plate with matrix involvement and thickness >2mm; ≥3 nails involved.
**Oral off-label:** pediatric onychomycosis (first-line per British Association of Dermatologists); tinea capitis (particularly Trichophyton infections in North America).
**Topical (OTC, per DailyMed):** tinea pedis, tinea cruris, tinea corporis.

## Dosing — Oral
250mg tablet once daily. Duration: **6 weeks for fingernails, up to 12 weeks for toenails**. Absorption/bioavailability similar regardless of food intake. Unapproved pulse-dosing regimen exists: 250mg/day x4 weeks, then 4 weeks off, then another 4 weeks on.

## Pharmacokinetics
Hepatic AND renal clearance. Inhibits hepatic CYP2D6.

## Contraindications (Oral)
Chronic or active liver disease; prior allergic reaction to oral terbinafine.

## Special Populations (Oral)
**Pregnancy:** former Category B, but NOT recommended for use during pregnancy despite that classification. **Breastfeeding:** excreted in breast milk — use should follow completion of breastfeeding.

## Adverse Effects (Oral)
**Common (mild, self-limited):** headaches, GI symptoms, rash.
**Uncommon:** visual disturbances, dysgeusia (taste disturbance), mild transaminitis.
**Severe/rare:** dysgeusia can be severe or PERMANENT; reports of fulminant liver failure; Stevens-Johnson syndrome, toxic epidermal necrolysis; cutaneous or systemic lupus erythematosus.

## Drug Interactions (Oral)
CYP2D6 inhibition — potential interactions with cimetidine, fluconazole, cyclosporine, rifampin, caffeine, paroxetine, codeine, metoprolol, simvastatin, nifedipine, digoxin, phenytoin.

## Monitoring (Oral)
No standardized lab monitoring protocol exists, but **FDA recommends measuring serum transaminases before drug initiation**. Average time to drug-induced liver injury is nearly 30 days, usually within 3 months. CBC monitoring for rare blood dyscrasias. **Liver enzymes >2x normal require immediate discontinuation** — supportive care, including liver transplantation in severe cases, may be needed.

## Efficacy
"Level-I" evidence shows oral terbinafine's superiority compared to placebo and alternative systemic antifungal agents (for its approved onychomycosis indication).
