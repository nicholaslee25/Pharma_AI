# TENS Devices — Cochrane Evidence + FDA Classification

## Gibson et al. 2019, Cochrane overview of reviews (CD011890)
Overview of Cochrane Reviews of TENS for chronic pain in adults. 9 reviews eligible (7 specifically on TENS for pain/function in chronic pain conditions), comparing TENS vs. sham TENS, usual care/no treatment, or TENS-plus-active-treatment vs. active treatment alone.
Conclusion: not possible to confidently state whether TENS is effective in relieving chronic pain in any comparison. Reviews themselves were high quality, but underlying trial evidence was very low certainty. No reliable evidence that effectiveness varies by delivery mode (frequency, intensity, electrode placement).
Source (via WebSearch, not fetched in full): cochranelibrary.com/cdsr/doi/10.1002/14651858.CD011890.pub3/full; cochrane.org overview page.

## FDA Regulatory Pathway
TENS devices cleared via 510(k) since 1977 based on substantial equivalence to devices marketed before May 1976. 510(k) clearance does not require clinical efficacy data. Standard OTC TENS units for muscle/joint pain generally Class II devices. Cefaly (a different TENS device) received a distinct 2014 de novo FDA clearance specifically for migraine prophylaxis — separate pathway/indication from general OTC pain-relief TENS units.
