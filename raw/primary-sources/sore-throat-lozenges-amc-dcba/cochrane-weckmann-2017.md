## Design
Systematic review/meta-analysis of RCTs comparing AMC/DCBA (amylmetacresol + 2,4-dichlorobenzyl alcohol, e.g. Strepsils) lozenges vs. placebo/alternative local treatment for acute uncomplicated sore throat. Searched Medline, Embase, Cochrane + hand search. Fixed-effects meta-analysis, standardized mean difference (SMD) with 95% CI.

## Scale
77 citations screened, 3 RCTs met inclusion criteria, 660 adults total. Formulation: amylmetacresol 0.6mg + 2,4-dichlorobenzyl alcohol 1.2mg, compared with unflavored non-medicated lozenges (one trial's active formulation also contained lidocaine; another had flavoring additives).

## Results
- **Primary outcome** (pain intensity reduction from baseline, 2h post-dose): SMD **−0.60** (95% CI −0.75 to −0.45) on an 11-point ordinal scale, favoring AMC/DCBA
- Secondary outcomes (sore throat relief, swallowing difficulty, throat numbness): direction of results not separately quantified in the abstract, described only qualitatively as secondary outcomes assessed
- **Adverse events:** no serious side effects; mild side effects (headache, cough, nasal congestion, oral cavity irritation) reported in up to 16% of subjects in BOTH groups (active and placebo) — i.e., not clearly attributable to the drug itself
- **All included trials were sponsored by a manufacturer of AMC/DCBA-containing lozenges**

## Authors' Conclusion
AMC/DCBA lozenges "can be a safe treatment option to relieve pain in patients with uncomplicated sore throat looking for local treatment options and valuing the modest additional effect compared with non-medicated lozenges." Note the authors' own characterization of the effect as "modest."

## Relevance
Manufacturer sponsorship of all 3 included trials is the single most important limitation to weigh against the otherwise consistent, statistically significant effect size.
