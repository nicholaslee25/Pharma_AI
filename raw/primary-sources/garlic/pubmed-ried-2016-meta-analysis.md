## Design
Updated meta-analysis (blood pressure) + narrative review (cholesterol, immunity). Searched MEDLINE 1955–Dec 2013 for RCTs of garlic preparations on blood pressure.

## Blood Pressure Results
- **20 trials, 970 participants (all-comers, not just hypertensive):** systolic BP decreased 5.1±2.2 mmHg (p<0.001); diastolic BP decreased 2.5±1.6 mmHg (p<0.002) vs. placebo
- **Hypertensive subgroup specifically** (baseline SBP/DBP ≥140/90): larger effect — SBP decreased **8.7±2.2 mmHg** (p<0.001, n=10 trials); DBP decreased **6.1±1.3 mmHg** (p<0.001, n=6 trials)

## Cholesterol Results (citing a separate previously-published meta-analysis, 39 RCTs, 2,300 adults, ≥2 weeks treatment)
Garlic reduced total and LDL cholesterol by **~10%** if taken for **>2 months**, specifically in individuals with slightly elevated cholesterol (total cholesterol >200 mg/dL / >5.5 mmol/L) — i.e., duration and baseline cholesterol level both matter for this outcome.

## Immunity
Garlic has immunomodulating effects (increased macrophage activity, natural killer cells, T/B cell production). Clinical trials cited show garlic significantly reduces the number, duration, and severity of upper respiratory infections (specific trial data not detailed in this review's abstract).

## Safety (per broader search context, not itself in this abstract)
Side effects reported by roughly one-third of participants across garlic trials generally, described as mild: burping, flatulence, reflux, mainly in the first few weeks of use.

## Authors' Conclusion
Garlic supplements have potential to lower blood pressure in hypertensive individuals, regulate slightly elevated cholesterol, and stimulate the immune system. Well tolerated; may be considered a complementary treatment for hypertension, mildly elevated cholesterol, and immune support. Long-term trials on cardiovascular morbidity/mortality outcomes (not just surrogate BP/lipid markers) are still needed.

## Relevance
This is a genuinely solid, quantified, multi-outcome positive evidence base — one of the stronger findings among the "general wellness" supplement category in this database — though it is specifically about BLOOD PRESSURE and CHOLESTEROL surrogate markers, not hard cardiovascular event outcomes, which the author explicitly flags as still needing confirmation.
