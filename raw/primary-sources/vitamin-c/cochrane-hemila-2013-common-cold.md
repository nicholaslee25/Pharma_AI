## Design
Cochrane systematic review/meta-analysis. Searched CENTRAL, MEDLINE, EMBASE, CINAHL, LILACS, Web of Science through Nov 2012, plus trial registries. Restricted to placebo-controlled trials using ≥0.2g/day vitamin C. Assessed both REGULAR (preventive) supplementation and THERAPEUTIC (at symptom onset) use.

## Results

**Incidence (regular supplementation), 29 comparisons, 11,306 participants:**
- **General community** (10,708 participants): pooled RR 0.97 (95% CI 0.94–1.00) — **essentially no reduction in cold risk** for the general population
- **Marathon runners, skiers, soldiers on subarctic exercises** (5 trials, 598 participants): pooled RR **0.48** (95% CI 0.35–0.64) — **cold incidence roughly HALVED** in people under heavy acute physical/cold stress — a genuinely different, much larger effect in this specific subgroup

**Duration (regular supplementation), 31 comparisons, 9,745 episodes:**
- Adults: duration reduced 8% (95% CI 3–12%)
- Children: duration reduced 14% (95% CI 7–21%); specifically 1–2g/day in children shortened colds by 18%
- Severity also reduced with regular supplementation

**Therapeutic use (started at symptom onset), 7 comparisons, 3,249 episodes:**
- **No consistent effect** on duration or severity — the preventive-supplementation benefit did NOT replicate when vitamin C was started after symptoms began

## Authors' Conclusion
Failure to reduce cold incidence in the general population means **routine vitamin C supplementation for cold prevention is not justified**, though it may help people exposed to brief periods of severe physical/environmental stress (athletes, cold-exposed workers). Regular supplementation reduces cold duration/severity, but this was not replicated in therapeutic (post-onset) trials. Given the consistent regular-supplementation effect plus low cost/safety, individual patients might reasonably test whether therapeutic vitamin C helps them personally — further therapeutic RCTs are warranted.

## Disclosed Conflicts of Interest
None declared by either review author.

## Relevance
This is a nuanced, evidence-graded finding directly relevant to how vitamin C is marketed for colds: general prophylactic use does NOT prevent colds in ordinary people, but DOES modestly shorten/soften them if taken regularly (not started after symptoms appear), and has a much larger preventive effect specifically in people under heavy physical/cold stress (athletes). This directly contradicts the common "take vitamin C when you feel a cold coming on" marketing pattern, since therapeutic (post-onset) use showed no consistent benefit in this review.
