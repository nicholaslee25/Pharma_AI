# PubMed Abstract — Docosanol 10% Cream Pivotal RCT (condensed facts, not full text)

**Source:** Sacks SL, Thisted RA, Jones TM, et al. Clinical efficacy of topical docosanol 10% cream for herpes simplex labialis: A multicenter, randomized, placebo-controlled trial. J Am Acad Dermatol. 2001 Aug. PMID: 11464183.
**Retrieved:** 2026-08-26 (abstract viewed via PubMed, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11464183/)

## Condensed facts
- Background: recurrent herpes simplex labialis (HSL) occurs in 20-40% of the US population; self-limiting in immunocompetent people, but patients seek treatment for discomfort/visibility.
- Two identical double-blind, placebo-controlled trials across 21 sites total. Adults with documented HSL history randomized to docosanol 10% cream or polyethylene glycol placebo, started during the prodrome or erythema stage. Applied 5x daily until healing, with twice-daily clinic visits.
- **370 docosanol-treated patients vs. 367 placebo-treated patients.**
- **Median time to healing: 4.1 days (docosanol) — 18 hours shorter than placebo (P=0.008, 95% CI 2–22 hours).**
- Docosanol also reduced time to: cessation of pain/itching/burning/tingling (P=0.002, 95% CI 3–16.5 hours); complete healing of classic lesions (P=0.023, 95% CI 1–24.5 hours); cessation of ulcer/soft-crust stage (P<0.001, 95% CI 8–25 hours).
- **Aborted episodes (lesion never progressed past early stage): 40% docosanol vs. 34% placebo (P=0.109, 95% CI for OR 0.95–1.73)** — NOT statistically significant, an important limitation to note rather than omit.
- Adverse experiences with docosanol were mild and similar to placebo.
- Authors' conclusion: docosanol applied 5x daily is safe and effective for recurrent HSL; healing-time differences compared favorably to the only other FDA-approved HSL treatment at the time (topical penciclovir, prescription).
- **Note on effect size:** the headline benefit is an ~18-hour (well under one day) reduction in median healing time — a real but modest effect, consistent with this database's original characterization of docosanol as producing "modest shortening," not a dramatic cure.
- A published comment (Spruance SL, 2002, PMID 12196766, "problems and questions") raised critical questions about this trial — noted here as an existing point of scientific debate, though not independently reviewed in this pass.
