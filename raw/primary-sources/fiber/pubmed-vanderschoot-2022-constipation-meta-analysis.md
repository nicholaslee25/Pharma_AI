## Design
Systematic review/meta-analysis of RCTs of fiber supplementation in adults with chronic constipation. Outcomes: stool output, gut transit time, symptoms, quality of life. Cochrane RoB 2.0 for bias assessment; random-effects model for synthesis.

## Scale
16 RCTs, 1,251 participants.

## Results
- **Overall response to treatment:** 66% (311/473, fiber) vs. 41% (134/329, control) — **RR 1.48** (95% CI 1.17–1.88, p=0.001; I²=57%)
- **Which fibers worked:** **psyllium and pectin** had significant effects (not all fiber types shown effective)
- **Dose matters:** higher response apparent regardless of treatment duration, but ONLY at higher fiber doses (**>10 g/day**)
- **Stool frequency:** significantly increased (SMD 0.72, 95% CI 0.36–1.08, p=0.0001); again psyllium/pectin significant, and improvement apparent only at higher doses AND longer duration (**≥4 weeks**)
- **Stool consistency:** improved (SMD 0.32, 95% CI 0.18–0.46, p<0.0001), particularly at higher doses
- **Flatulence:** significantly HIGHER in fiber groups vs. control (SMD 0.80, 95% CI 0.47–1.13, p<0.00001) — a real, quantified tradeoff

## Authors' Conclusion
Fiber supplementation is effective for improving constipation. **Psyllium specifically, at doses >10 g/day, for at least 4 weeks, appears optimal** — though the authors caution about considerable heterogeneity across included trials. This provides an evidence base for standardizing dose/duration recommendations rather than a vague "take more fiber" instruction.

## Relevance
This gives concrete, actionable, quantified guidance (psyllium, >10g/day, ≥4 weeks) rather than a generic fiber-is-good claim — and it also quantifies the real, common flatulence tradeoff that patients experience, which is worth stating rather than omitting.
