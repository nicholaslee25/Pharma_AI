## BOXED WARNING
**Cardiovascular thrombotic events:** NSAIDs increase risk of serious CV events including MI and stroke, which can be fatal; risk may occur early and increases with duration of use. Contraindicated in CABG surgery setting.
**GI risk:** NSAIDs cause increased risk of serious GI adverse events including bleeding, ulceration, perforation of stomach/intestines, potentially fatal; can occur anytime without warning; elderly at greater risk.

## Indications and Usage
Relief of signs/symptoms of rheumatoid arthritis and osteoarthritis; relief of mild-to-moderate pain; treatment of primary dysmenorrhea. Use lowest effective dose for shortest duration.

## Contraindications
Known hypersensitivity to ibuprofen; history of asthma, urticaria, or allergic reaction to aspirin/NSAIDs; CABG surgery setting.

## Dosage and Administration
- Max total daily dose: 3200 mg
- RA/OA: 1200–3200 mg/day (400/600/800 mg, 3–4x daily)
- Mild-moderate pain: 400 mg q4–6h PRN
- Dysmenorrhea: 400 mg q4h PRN

## Warnings

**Cardiovascular:** CV thrombotic risk most consistent at higher doses; avoid post-MI unless benefit outweighs recurrent-event risk; contraindicated in CABG setting; can worsen hypertension (monitor BP); approximately 2x increase in heart failure hospitalizations vs. placebo.

**Gastrointestinal:** Upper GI ulcer/bleed/perforation in ~1% of patients treated 3–6 months, ~2–4% at one year; >10-fold increased GI bleed risk with prior ulcer/GI bleed history. Risk factors: oral corticosteroids, anticoagulants, longer NSAID duration, smoking, alcohol, older age.

**Renal:** Can cause renal papillary necrosis and other renal injury via dose-dependent reduction in prostaglandin formation; avoid in advanced renal disease; highest risk in impaired renal function, heart failure, liver dysfunction, diuretic/ACE-inhibitor use, elderly.

**Anaphylactoid reactions:** Can occur without prior NSAID exposure; contraindicated in aspirin triad (asthma + rhinitis/nasal polyps + severe bronchospasm with NSAIDs).

**Serious skin reactions:** Stevens-Johnson syndrome, toxic epidermal necrolysis, exfoliative dermatitis possible; fixed drug eruption (including generalized bullous variant) may be life-threatening; discontinue at first rash.

**DRESS (Drug Reaction with Eosinophilia and Systemic Symptoms):** Reported with NSAIDs, some fatal/life-threatening. Presents with fever, rash, lymphadenopathy, facial swelling; may include hepatitis, nephritis, hematologic abnormalities, myocarditis, myositis. Discontinue and evaluate immediately if signs present.

**Fetal toxicity:** Avoid ~30 weeks gestation and later (premature ductus arteriosus closure risk); ~20 weeks or later may cause fetal renal dysfunction/oligohydramnios; if necessary 20–30 weeks, use lowest effective dose/shortest duration and consider ultrasound monitoring if treatment >48h.

## Precautions

**Hepatic:** Borderline liver test elevation in ~15% of NSAID users; notable ALT/AST elevation (~3x ULN) in ~1% of clinical trial patients; rare severe hepatic reactions reported, some fatal. Discontinue if signs of liver dysfunction.

**Hematological:** Anemia sometimes observed. Postmarketing: ≥1 g/dL hemoglobin decrease in 17.1% (1600 mg/day OA) and 22.8% (2400 mg/day RA) of patients. NSAIDs inhibit platelet aggregation — effect is quantitatively less, shorter duration, and reversible vs. aspirin. Monitor patients with coagulation disorders or on anticoagulants.

**Preexisting asthma:** Aspirin-sensitive asthma patients at risk for severe, potentially fatal bronchospasm; cross-reactivity between aspirin and NSAIDs reported; use with caution in preexisting asthma generally.

**Ophthalmological:** Blurred/diminished vision, scotomata, color vision changes reported; discontinue and get ophthalmologic exam if symptoms develop.

**Aseptic meningitis:** Rare, with fever and coma; more likely in SLE/connective tissue disease patients.

## Drug Interactions

- **ACE inhibitors:** NSAIDs may diminish antihypertensive effect.
- **Aspirin:** Ibuprofen 400 mg TID interferes with antiplatelet activity of enteric-coated aspirin; interference also occurs with once-daily 400 mg ibuprofen, especially if dosed before aspirin. Timing data (thromboxane B2 inhibition, see Pharmacodynamics below) shows how to avoid the interaction. Consider alternative NSAID/non-NSAID analgesic for patients relying on low-dose aspirin cardioprotection.
- **Diuretics:** Can reduce natriuretic effect of furosemide/thiazides in some patients; monitor for renal failure signs, confirm diuretic efficacy.
- **Lithium:** Elevates plasma lithium, reduces renal clearance — one study: mean minimum lithium concentration +15%, renal clearance −19%. Monitor for lithium toxicity.
- **Methotrexate:** NSAIDs competitively inhibit methotrexate accumulation, may enhance methotrexate toxicity — use with caution.
- **Warfarin-type anticoagulants:** Controlled studies showed no significant effect on PT/clotting factors, but bleeding reported when combined; GI bleeding risk is synergistic — combined use carries higher risk than either drug alone.
- **H2 antagonists:** Cimetidine/ranitidine co-administration had no substantive effect on ibuprofen serum levels.

## Use in Specific Populations

**Pregnancy:** Avoid ~30 weeks+ (premature ductus arteriosus closure); ~20 weeks+ risk of oligohydramnios/neonatal renal impairment; 20–30 weeks limit dose/duration if necessary, consider ultrasound monitoring if >48h treatment. No adequate well-controlled human studies; use only if benefit justifies fetal risk. Animal studies: prostaglandin inhibitors caused increased pre/post-implantation loss and impaired kidney development. Background population risk cited: 2–4% major birth defects, 15–20% miscarriage in the general US population (context, not drug-attributable).

**Nursing mothers:** Unknown if excreted in human milk; potential for serious adverse reactions in nursing infant — decide whether to discontinue nursing or drug. (Compare StatPearls, which cites more recent data suggesting ibuprofen is actually a preferred NSAID for nursing mothers with low relative infant dose — this FDA label language is more conservative/dated; both are preserved here since they reflect genuinely different source vintages.)

**Pediatric:** Safety/effectiveness not established in this label; controlled trials in pediatric patients not conducted (per this specific label — note other ibuprofen products/labels do carry pediatric dosing, e.g. Children's Motrin/Advil; this particular label is conservative).

**Geriatric:** Caution warranted ≥65 years.

**Renal/hepatic impairment:** No specific dosing guidance beyond general warnings.

## Clinical Pharmacology

**Mechanism:** Analgesic/antipyretic activity, mode of action likely related to prostaglandin synthetase inhibition. Comparable to aspirin for pain/inflammation control in RA/OA with statistically significant reduction in milder GI side effects vs. aspirin. More effective analgesic than propoxyphene for episiotomy pain, post-dental-extraction pain, and primary dysmenorrhea. Reduces elevated prostaglandin activity in menstrual fluid; reduces uterine pressure/contractions.

**Pharmacodynamics — ibuprofen/aspirin timing interaction (thromboxane B2 inhibition data):**
- 400 mg ibuprofen once daily, 2h before immediate-release aspirin 81 mg: 53% serum thromboxane B2 inhibition (interaction present)
- 400 mg once daily, 8h before aspirin: 90.7% inhibition (interaction minimized)
- 400 mg once daily, 2h after aspirin: 99.2% inhibition (no interaction)
- 400 mg TID (1h, 7h, 13h post-aspirin) with aspirin 81 mg daily: 98.3% mean inhibition (no interaction); individual range down to 90.2%
- Enteric-coated aspirin 81 mg + ibuprofen 400 mg TID (2h, 7h, 12h post-aspirin): 67% inhibition at 24h on day 6 (interaction present)

**Pharmacokinetics:** Rapidly absorbed; peak serum levels at 1–2h. Linear dose-response for single doses up to 800 mg; above 800 mg, AUC increases less than proportionally. No accumulation or enzyme induction evidence. Fasting vs. pre-meal dosing: similar serum profiles; immediately post-meal: reduced absorption rate but not appreciable extent; food minimally alters bioavailability overall. Antacids (Al/Mg hydroxide) don't interfere with absorption. Rapidly metabolized, excreted in urine; excretion essentially complete 24h after last dose. Serum half-life 1.8–2.0h. 24h urinary recovery: ~25% as metabolite A (2-[p-(2-hydroxymethyl-propyl)phenyl]propionic acid), ~37% as metabolite B (2-[p-(2-carboxypropyl)phenyl]propionic acid), free ibuprofen ~1%, conjugated ~14% (total ~45–79% dose recovered as these forms).

## Adverse Reactions

**Most frequent:** GI complaints, 4–16% in controlled trials.

**Incidence >1% (controlled trials):**
- GI: nausea, epigastric pain, heartburn, diarrhea, abdominal distress, indigestion, constipation, cramping, bloating, flatulence
- CNS: dizziness, headache, nervousness
- Dermatologic: rash (incl. maculopapular), pruritus
- Special senses: tinnitus
- Metabolic: decreased appetite
- Cardiovascular: edema, fluid retention

**<1% (probable causal relationship):**
- GI: gastric/duodenal ulcer with bleeding/perforation, GI hemorrhage, melena, gastritis, hepatitis, jaundice, abnormal LFTs, pancreatitis
- CNS: depression, insomnia, confusion, emotional lability, somnolence, aseptic meningitis
- Dermatologic: vesiculobullous eruptions, urticaria, erythema multiforme, Stevens-Johnson syndrome, alopecia
- Special senses: hearing loss, amblyopia
- Hematologic: neutropenia, agranulocytosis, aplastic anemia, hemolytic anemia, thrombocytopenia, eosinophilia, Hgb/Hct decreases
- Cardiovascular: CHF (in marginal cardiac function), elevated BP, palpitations
- Allergic: abdominal pain/fever/chills/nausea/vomiting syndrome, anaphylaxis, bronchospasm, serum sickness, lupus erythematosus syndrome, Henoch-Schönlein vasculitis, angioedema
- Renal: acute renal failure, decreased creatinine clearance, polyuria, azotemia, cystitis, hematuria

**Causal relationship unknown (<1%):** paresthesias, hallucinations, dream abnormalities, pseudotumor cerebri, toxic epidermal necrolysis, photoallergic reactions, conjunctivitis, diplopia, optic neuritis, cataracts, arrhythmias (sinus tachycardia, bradycardia), renal papillary necrosis, dry eyes/mouth, gingival ulcer, rhinitis.

**Postmarketing:** exfoliative dermatitis, Stevens-Johnson syndrome, toxic epidermal necrolysis, fixed drug eruption.

**Higher doses (3200 mg/day):** slight incidence increases, still within reported ranges.

## Overdosage

**Case reports (as printed in this label):**
- 19-month-old (12 kg), ingested 7–10× 400 mg tablets: apneic/cyanotic initially; blood level 102.9 mcg/mL at 8.5h; complete recovery by 12h.
- Two children (~10 kg each), ingested ~120 mg/kg: no acute intoxication/sequelae; blood levels ~700 mcg/mL at 90 min (10x normal peak).
- 19-year-old, ingested 8000 mg over several hours: dizziness, nystagmus; recovered after hospitalization/hydration/rest.

**Management:** Empty stomach (vomiting/lavage) if <1h since ingestion; alkali administration/diuresis theoretically beneficial (acidic drug, urinary excretion); oral activated charcoal may reduce absorption/reabsorption; supportive measures otherwise.
