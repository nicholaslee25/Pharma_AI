# Cochrane Review Summary — Single Dose Oral Ibuprofen for Acute Postoperative Pain (raw extraction, condensed)

**Citation:** Derry C, Derry S, Moore RA, McQuay HJ. Single dose oral ibuprofen for acute postoperative pain in adults. Cochrane Database Syst Rev. 2009;(3):CD001548. DOI: 10.1002/14651858.CD001548.pub2. PMID: 19588326. (Update of Collins SL, et al. 2000, PMID: 10796811.)
**Retrieved via:** PubMed abstract, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19588326/
**Retrieved:** 2026-08-24

## Design
Systematic review/meta-analysis of randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trials of single-dose oral ibuprofen (any formulation) in adults with moderate-to-severe acute postoperative pain. Searched Cochrane CENTRAL, MEDLINE, EMBASE, Oxford Pain Relief Database through May 2009.

## Scale
72 studies compared ibuprofen vs. placebo, 9186 total participants. Predominantly high reporting quality.

## Primary Results (≥50% pain relief over 4–6h vs. placebo)
- **Ibuprofen 200 mg** (2690 participants): NNT 2.7 (95% CI 2.5–3.0); 46% achieved ≥50% pain relief
- **Ibuprofen 400 mg** (6475 participants): NNT 2.5 (95% CI 2.4–2.6); 54% achieved ≥50% pain relief

## Secondary Results
- Remedication within 6h: 48% (200 mg) vs. 42% (400 mg) — higher doses reduced need for remedication
- Median time to remedication: 4.7h (200 mg) vs. 5.4h (400 mg)
- Sensitivity analysis: dental-impaction pain models and soluble ibuprofen salts produced better efficacy estimates than other models/formulations

## Adverse Events
Uncommon; not different from placebo.

## Authors' Conclusion
"The very substantial amount of high quality evidence demonstrates that ibuprofen is an effective analgesic in treating postoperative pain." NNTs for 200mg/400mg did not change significantly from the prior (2000) review despite substantial new data.

## Limitations / Conflict of Interest (per the paper's own disclosure)
Two of four review authors (RAM, HJM) disclosed consulting/lecture fees from pharmaceutical companies related to analgesics; review funding came from Oxford Pain Research, NHS Cochrane Collaboration Programme Grant Scheme, and NIHR Biomedical Research Centre Programme (academic/public funding, not industry-funded directly, but author-level industry ties disclosed).
