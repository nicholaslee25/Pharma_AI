## Mechanism of Action
Non-selective cyclooxygenase (COX) inhibitor. Reduces expression of prostaglandin precursors by blocking both COX-1 and COX-2 pathways. COX-1 is inhibited approximately 2.5x more strongly than COX-2. Produces analgesic, antipyretic, and anti-inflammatory effects.

## FDA-Approved Indications
- Oral: osteoarthritis, rheumatoid disorders
- Intravenous: mild-to-moderate pain; moderate-to-severe pain as opioid adjunct (adults and pediatric ≥3 months); fever (adults and pediatric ≥3 months)
- IV ibuprofen lysine: patent ductus arteriosus (PDA) closure in premature infants
- OTC: fever, musculoskeletal pain, primary dysmenorrhea, headache/migraine, sore throat, cold/flu, arthralgia, odontalgia

## Adult Dosing
- Pain relief: 200–400 mg PO q4–6h; max 1200 mg/day (OTC/unsupervised)
- Higher-dose regimen (physician-directed): 400–800 mg q6h; max 3200 mg/day
- Joint inflammation (RA/OA/ankylosing spondylitis): 1800–2400 mg/day
- Acute pericarditis (per ACC): 600–800 mg q8h
- Take with food or milk; IV infusion ≥30 min (adults)

## Pediatric Dosing
- Standard: 5–10 mg/kg PO q6–8h; max 400 mg/dose or 40 mg/kg/day
- PDA treatment (ibuprofen lysine): 10 mg/kg IV initial, then 5 mg/kg at 24h and 48h
- IV infusion over 10 min (pediatric)

## Pharmacokinetics
- Rapid, complete oral absorption; food reduces Cmax by 30–50% and delays Tmax by 30–60 min
- ~99% plasma protein-bound
- Metabolized by CYP2C9 and CYP2C8 to hydroxylated/carboxylated metabolites
- Primarily renal excretion of metabolites; ~1% excreted unchanged

## Special Populations

**Renal impairment:**
- CKD stage 1–2 (stable, no risk factors): standard monitoring
- CKD stage 3 (minimal risk factors): short-term use up to 5 days acceptable; labs + follow-up in 2–3 weeks
- GFR <30 mL/min/1.73m²: avoid NSAIDs (KDIGO 2012)
- GFR <60 mL/min/1.73m²: prolonged therapy not recommended
- Elderly, eGFR-based max daily dose: eGFR ≥60 → 1600 mg; eGFR 30–59 → 1200 mg; eGFR ≤29 → contraindicated

**Hepatic impairment:** No specific dose modification given; use with caution in preexisting liver disease (risk of drug-induced liver injury).

**Pregnancy:** After 20 weeks — risk of fetal renal dysfunction, oligohydramnios, delayed lung development; restrict to lowest effective dose/shortest duration weeks 20–30; avoid entirely ≥30 weeks (premature ductus arteriosus closure risk).

**Breastfeeding:** Preferred NSAID for nursing mothers; relative infant dose <0.38% of maternal dose (up to 0.6% during colostral phase).

**Elderly (AGS 2023 Beers Criteria-style guidance):** Long-term use only when necessary, with gastroprotection (PPI or misoprostol). Avoid short-term use with corticosteroids/anticoagulants/antiplatelets unless no effective alternative and gastroprotection feasible.

## Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to ibuprofen, other NSAIDs, or aspirin
- IV ibuprofen lysine (PDA use): congenital heart disease requiring PDA patency, active bleeding, thrombocytopenia, renal impairment, coagulation defects, necrotizing enterocolitis
- Canada-specific: active GI/cerebrovascular bleeding, uncontrolled heart failure, lupus, renal/hepatic impairment

## Box Warnings
1. Cardiovascular thrombotic events — increased MI/stroke risk
2. Not advised perioperatively for CABG surgery
3. GI bleeding/perforation/ulceration risk, heightened in elderly and those with prior GI disease

## Adverse Effects
- GI: bleeding, gastritis, ulceration, hemorrhage, perforation (most common category)
- Renal: reduced kidney function; AKI in dehydrated patients — number needed to harm (NNH) of 5.5 in ultramarathon runners
- Dermatologic: hypersensitivity rash; rare DRESS syndrome
- Cardiovascular: hypertension (independent risk factor in older adults)
- IV PDA treatment: hyperbilirubinemia, bilirubin displacement (high protein binding)

## Drug Interactions
- ACE inhibitors: may reduce antihypertensive effect — monitor
- Aspirin: interferes with antiplatelet effect — space immediate-release low-dose aspirin ≥2h before ibuprofen
- Diuretics (furosemide, thiazides): diminished natriuretic effect; increased AKI risk
- Lithium: increases plasma lithium, reduces renal clearance — monitor for toxicity
- Warfarin: increased GI bleeding risk — close monitoring

## Monitoring
Pain relief and GI symptoms; blood pressure (esp. older adults/HTN); renal function; liver function in high-risk individuals.

## Toxicity & Overdose
- Max daily dose: 3200 mg
- Severe pediatric toxicity threshold: ≥400 mg/kg
- Complications: seizures, apnea, hypertension, renal/hepatic dysfunction, MI (chronic high-dose)
- No antidote; supportive care. CRRT/hemodialysis may help stabilize metabolic balance despite limited acute drug removal. Selective Plasma Adsorption (SPAD) shows promise for severe overdose with multi-organ failure.
