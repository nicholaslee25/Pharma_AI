## Design
Systematic review and dose-response meta-analysis of double-blind RCTs of exogenous melatonin in patients with insomnia and healthy volunteers. Outcomes: standardized mean difference in sleep onset latency (SOL) and total sleep time (TST) vs. placebo. Dose-response and meta-regression models estimated effect of administration timing, dose, and other treatment parameters.

## Scale
26 RCTs published 1987–2020, 1689 total observations.

## Results
- Melatonin gradually reduces sleep onset latency and increases total sleep time in a dose-dependent way, **peaking at 4 mg/day**
- Meta-regression: insomnia status was a significant predictor of SOL benefit (β=0.50, p<0.001); time between administration and sleep episode was also significant for SOL (β=−0.16, p=0.023); time of day was the only significant predictor of TST (β=−0.086, p<0.01)

## Authors' Conclusion
Advancing administration timing to **3 hours before desired bedtime** and using a **4 mg/day** dose may optimize melatonin's sleep-promoting efficacy, compared with the most common real-world clinical practice pattern of 2mg taken 30 minutes before bedtime.

## Relevance
This directly challenges the common real-world dosing convention (2mg, 30min before bed) as suboptimal per the pooled dose-response data — a genuinely actionable, non-obvious finding worth foregrounding on the ingredient page rather than just citing "some evidence melatonin helps sleep."
