## Mechanism of Action
Endogenous melatonin synthesized in the pineal gland from tryptophan via serotonin; conversion is light-dependent, regulated by the suprachiasmatic nucleus (SCN). Sympathetic stimulation of the pineal gland upregulates arylalkylamine N-acetyltransferase (AA-NAT), the rate-limiting synthesis enzyme. Promotes sleep via MT1/MT2 receptor interactions in the hypothalamus and retina, inhibiting wake-promoting signals.

## Indications/Uses
**FDA status:** melatonin itself is not FDA-approved for any indication (sold as a dietary supplement); related synthetic melatonin-receptor agonists (ramelteon, tasimelteon) ARE FDA-approved drugs.
**Clinical applications (per StatPearls):** primary insomnia (first-line per AAFP), REM sleep behavior disorder, circadian rhythm disorders, delayed sleep-wake phase disorder, non-24-hour sleep-wake rhythm disorder, jet lag, shift work sleep disorder, post-traumatic brain injury, migraine prophylaxis, neurodegenerative disorders. Also under research for cancer, metabolic disorders, cardiovascular disease, GI conditions (not established indications).

## Dosing
Studied range 0.1–10 mg, typically administered up to 2 hours before bedtime; maximum dose undefined. For circadian disorders, timing varies by condition — typically 1 hour before preferred bedtime.

## Pharmacokinetics
Bioavailability 1–74% (highly formulation-dependent). ~90% hepatic metabolism via CYP1A2 (minor CYP2C19 involvement). 61–78% albumin protein binding. Elimination half-life 1–2 hours. Primarily urinary excretion, minor fecal.

## Adverse Effects
Mild: drowsiness, daytime sedation, nausea, headaches. Impaired glucose tolerance reported in some cases. No documented tolerance development.

## Drug Interactions
- **CYP1A2 inhibitors** (e.g. fluvoxamine): caution — can raise melatonin levels
- **CNS depressants** (benzodiazepines, zolpidem, eszopiclone): avoid combining — additive sedation risk

## Contraindications
Rarely associated with angioedema despite general tolerability. Caution advised in autoimmune conditions due to potential immune stimulation via interleukin production.

## Special Populations
- **Hepatic impairment:** reduced metabolism (slower clearance), though melatonin itself does not appear to cause hepatotoxicity
- **Renal impairment:** caution in dialysis patients due to impaired elimination
- **Pregnancy/breastfeeding:** insufficient safety evidence — avoidance recommended
- **Pediatric:** supplementation risks exceeding natural physiologic levels; StatPearls notes concerns raised in postmortem case reports
- **Elderly:** potential fall and fracture risk reported

## Quality/Regulation Note
A cited study examining 31 melatonin supplements found actual melatonin content varied from −83% to +478% of labeled content — a substantial real-world quality-control problem for this specific supplement class, given it is unregulated as a drug. USP verification (where present on a product) provides some quality assurance.

## Toxicity
Remarkably low acute toxicity — animal studies show no established LD50 even at 800 mg/kg. Long-term human exposure may reduce semen quality (limited evidence). Pediatric overdose/exposure cases increased dramatically: ~8,000 cases (2012) to >52,000 (2021), per US poison control data cited in the article; ~15% of these required hospitalization.
