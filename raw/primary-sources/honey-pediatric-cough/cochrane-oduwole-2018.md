# Cochrane Review — Honey for Acute Cough in Children (condensed facts, not full text)

**Source:** Oduwole O, Udoh EE, Oyo-Ita A, Meremikwu MM. Honey for acute cough in children. Cochrane Database Syst Rev. 2018. PMID: 29633783.

## Condensed facts
- Update of reviews previously published 2010/2012/2014. RCTs comparing honey (alone or with antibiotics) vs. no treatment, placebo, honey-based cough syrup, or other OTC cough medications, in **children aged 12 months to 18 years**, for acute cough in ambulatory settings. **This inclusion criterion (12 months+) directly matches this database's existing "age 1+ only" framing for honey.**
- 6 RCTs (899 children) originally included; 3 more studies (331 children) added in this 2018 update.
- Comparator drugs studied against honey: dextromethorphan, diphenhydramine, salbutamol, bromelin (pineapple-derived enzyme), no treatment, placebo. Cough symptom relief measured mostly via 7-point Likert scale (lower score = better relief).
- **Honey vs. no treatment:** mean difference (MD) **-1.05** (95% CI -1.48 to -0.62; I²=0%; 2 studies, 154 children; MODERATE-certainty evidence) — honey probably reduces cough frequency better than no treatment.
- **Honey vs. placebo:** MD **-1.62** (95% CI -3.02 to -0.22; I²=0%; 2 studies, 402 children; MODERATE-certainty evidence) — honey probably reduces cough frequency better than placebo.
- **Honey vs. dextromethorphan:** MD -0.07 (95% CI -1.07 to 0.94; I²=87% high heterogeneity; 2 studies, 149 children; LOW-certainty) — may have SIMILAR effect, not clearly better or worse.
- **Honey vs. diphenhydramine:** MD -0.57 (95% CI -0.90 to -0.24; 1 study, 80 children; LOW-certainty) — honey MAY BE BETTER than diphenhydramine specifically.
- **Duration matters:** honey up to 3 days is probably more effective than placebo/salbutamol; BEYOND 3 days, honey probably had NO advantage over salbutamol or placebo for cough severity, bothersome cough, or sleep impact (moderate-certainty evidence) — a real time-boundary on the benefit.
- Honey vs. bromelin-mixed-with-honey: probably little or no difference (5-point scale studies).
- **Adverse events:** nervousness/insomnia/hyperactivity in 7 of 75 children (9.3%) on honey vs. 2 of 75 (2.7%) on dextromethorphan — RR 2.94 (95% CI 0.74–11.71, NOT statistically significant given wide CI crossing 1, but numerically a real signal worth noting; low-certainty evidence, 2 studies, 149 children combined denominator). Diphenhydramine group: 3 of 40 (7.5%) had somnolence, RR 0.14 (95% CI 0.01–2.68, 1 study, 80 children). GI complaints: honey vs. placebo — 34 of ~283 (12%) honey vs. 13 of ~118 (11%) placebo — similar rates.
