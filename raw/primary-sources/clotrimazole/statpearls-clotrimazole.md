## Mechanism of Action
Imidazole antifungal — inhibits ergosterol biosynthesis in a concentration-dependent manner by interfering with 14-alpha-lanosterol demethylation. Blocking ergosterol synthesis disrupts fungal cell membrane integrity, inhibiting growth. Also affects calcium signaling and potassium/calcium channels through mechanisms unrelated to antifungal activity. Limited activity against gram-positive bacteria and Trichomonas at very high concentrations.

## Indications
Oral candidiasis (thrush); vulvovaginal candidiasis; dermatomycoses (athlete's foot, jock itch, ringworm, pityriasis/tinea versicolor, intertrigo, erythrasma); combined with betamethasone propionate for inflammatory tinea (adults/children >12y).

## Pharmacokinetics
**Oral troches:** minimal systemic bioavailability; inhibitory saliva levels persist up to 3h after the troche dissolves. **Topical:** negligible absorption through intact skin. **Intravaginal:** ~3–10% of dose absorbed; fungicidal vaginal concentrations persist up to 3 days post-application.

## Dosing by Indication
- **Vulvovaginal candidiasis (adults):** 1% cream (50mg) x7 days OR 2% cream (100mg) x3 days; tablets 200mg/day x3 days or 500mg single dose; complicated infection 7–14 days
- **Oropharyngeal candidiasis:** 10mg dissolved in mouth 5x/day x7–14 days (active infection); prophylaxis 10mg 3x/day during immunosuppression
- **Tinea versicolor:** 1% cream/solution twice daily x2 weeks
- **Tinea corporis/cruris:** 1% formulations twice daily x1–4 weeks
- **Tinea pedis:** 1% formulations twice daily x4 weeks total, continuing 1 week post-resolution
- **Cutaneous candidiasis:** cream/solution twice daily; ointment twice daily x2 weeks
- **Pediatric oropharyngeal (3–17y):** 10mg five times daily x7–14 days; adolescent females 12–17y use same vaginal dosing as adults

## Contraindications
**Topical clotrimazole is NOT effective for onychomycosis** (nail fungal infection) — an important limitation given consumer expectations. Azole hypersensitivity. Ocular use contraindicated (requires immediate flushing if accidental contact occurs). Not suitable for systemic fungal infections.

## Special Populations
- **Pregnancy:** only topical formulations recommended; not known to cross the placenta; former FDA Class C; safe in 2nd/3rd trimester, use only if indicated in 1st trimester; CDC recommends 7 days topical azole therapy for pregnant women with vulvovaginal candidiasis
- **Breastfeeding:** topical use poses minimal risk (poor absorption); oral troches only when needed; do not apply topically to breasts
- **Hepatic impairment:** no specific dose adjustment given, but periodic LFT monitoring recommended
- **Renal impairment:** no specific dose adjustment given

## Adverse Effects
**Oral:** itching, nausea, vomiting; **>10% of oral clotrimazole patients may have abnormal LFTs** — a notably high incidence for a monitoring parameter.
**Vaginal:** <10% experience vulvar/vaginal burning; also rash, hives, blisters, irritation.
**Topical:** local irritation, rash, hives, burning, itching, peeling, redness, swelling.

## Drug Interactions
**Tacrolimus:** clotrimazole may significantly increase tacrolimus levels — caution required. **Progesterone:** vaginal antifungals may reduce progesterone effectiveness — concurrent use not recommended.

## Monitoring
LFTs for oral formulation users; monitor for hypersensitivity/local irritation with topical/vaginal use; seek medical care if no improvement within 2 weeks (jock itch) or 4 weeks (athlete's foot/ringworm); minimum 30-minute dissolution time for oral lozenges.
