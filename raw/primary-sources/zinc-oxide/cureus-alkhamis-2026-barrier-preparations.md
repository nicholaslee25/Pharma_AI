## Condensed facts
- Systematic review/meta-analysis of barrier preparations vs. placebo/other therapies for diaper dermatitis (DD) in infants/toddlers aged 0-2. Searched MEDLINE/PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science, CENTRAL, Google Scholar, Jan 2004–Jan 2024. Analyzed with RevMan 5.3.
- **6 studies included total: 4 RCTs + 2 cohort studies.** Barrier preparations examined across the 6 studies: clotrimazole, petroleum jelly, water and soap, zinc oxide (ZnO) paste, and diaper creams/moisturizers.
- DD background incidence cited: 7-35% (varies by study/definition) — a different framing than StatPearls' "~50% of infants" figure (likely reflecting point-prevalence vs. cumulative-incidence measurement differences; both are reported here rather than reconciled, since reconciling them would require access neither source provides).
- **Incidence comparison across ~4 of the 6 studies: odds ratio 0.77 (95% CI 0.37–1.61), reported by the authors as statistically significant with p=0.0002, with notable heterogeneity (df=3, I²=79%).**
- **Important transparency note: this is exactly as the source itself reports it — but a 95% CI spanning 0.37 to 1.61 (crossing 1.0) is not normally consistent with a p-value as small as 0.0002 for that same effect estimate.** This appears to be an internal inconsistency in the published article (the p=0.0002 may actually belong to the heterogeneity/Cochran's Q test rather than to the pooled OR itself, a common mislabeling in meta-analysis reporting) rather than a typo introduced in this summary. Recorded transparently rather than "corrected" without being able to verify which number is right.
- **Zinc oxide paste specifically:**
  - Showed effectiveness in managing diaper dermatitis among neonates/infants (general finding across included studies).
  - One specific included study (Hoeger et al.) found ZnO paste was LESS effective than clotrimazole.
  - A separate cited study found a 2% eosin treatment group had significantly higher full/partial healing rates than a corticosteroid+ZnO paste combination; **in that same study, ZnO paste alone achieved 22% complete healing and 44% partial healing.**
  - Another study reported zinc oxide produced quicker recovery than talcum powder.
- Clotrimazole emerged as an effective strategy in the review overall, particularly for suspected fungal involvement, with lower dermatitis incidence vs. placebo across 3 of the studies.
- Diaper cream/moisturizer plus soap was found effective for PREVENTING nappy rash across 2 studies.
- **Only 1 study reported NO efficacy for petroleum jelly/Vaseline** in reducing severity/incidence of dermatitis symptoms — a real, specific negative finding for that particular barrier agent in that particular study.
- Quality caveat: methodological quality assessment revealed missing critical information across studies, raising bias potential; overall evidence graded as low-level (Grade C) per the authors, urging caution in implementation.
- Authors' overall conclusion: barrier-preparation effectiveness varies by specific preparation and study design; clotrimazole shows particular potential for suspected fungal cases; further research needed on factors influencing efficacy and use in high-risk patients.
