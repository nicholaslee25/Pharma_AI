## Definition
Inflammatory reaction of the skin in the perineal/perianal (diaper) area — the most prevalent skin condition in young infants.

## Causes/Pathophysiology
Primary cause: irritant contact dermatitis from moisture, prolonged contact with bodily waste, irritating substances. Secondary causes: fungal infection (Candida albicans most common), bacterial infection (S. aureus, S. pyogenes), seborrheic dermatitis, atopic dermatitis. Key mechanisms: increased moisture causing maceration, elevated skin pH from urine breakdown, enhanced fecal enzyme activity damaging skin.

## Statistics
**Affects ~50% of infants. Peak incidence 9-12 months. Accounts for ~25% of dermatologic visits in first-year pediatric care.**

## Evaluation
Primarily clinical (history + physical exam); labs generally unnecessary; KOH exam may confirm suspected Candida.

## Treatment
**Behavioral/preventive (StatPearls' own framing puts these first):** frequent diaper changes with superabsorbent diapers, gentle cleansing with soap-free products, appropriate (pH-buffered, fragrance-free) wipe selection.
**Topical:** barrier emollients — zinc oxide, petrolatum, cod liver oil, lanolin — applied with each diaper change. Low-potency corticosteroids (hydrocortisone 0.5%) for moderate cases not improving within 2-3 days. Antifungals (nystatin or azoles) for Candida infection. Topical antibiotics for bacterial superinfection.

## Complications
Primarily superimposed fungal or bacterial infection, particularly serious in immunocompromised patients (risk of invasive disease).
