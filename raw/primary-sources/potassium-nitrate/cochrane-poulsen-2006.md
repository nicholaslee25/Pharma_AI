# Cochrane Review — Potassium-Containing Toothpastes for Dentine Hypersensitivity (condensed facts, not full text)

**Source:** Poulsen S, Errboe M, Lescay Mevil Y, Glenny AM. Potassium containing toothpastes for dentine hypersensitivity. Cochrane Database Syst Rev. 2006. PMID: 16855970. (Update of the original 2001 review, PMID 11405992.)
**Retrieved:** 2026-08-26 (abstract viewed via PubMed, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16855970/)
**License note:** Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews is copyrighted. Condensed facts only from the free PubMed abstract, no full text reproduced.

## Condensed facts
- RCTs comparing potassium-containing toothpastes (potassium nitrate and related potassium salts) vs. control (non-potassium) toothpastes for dentine hypersensitivity. Searched Cochrane Oral Health Trials Register, CENTRAL, EMBASE/MEDLINE, PubMed, Web of Science through Aug/Sep 2005.
- Sensitivity assessed via thermal, tactile, air blast, and subjective methods.
- **6 studies included in meta-analysis.**
- **Air blast sensitivity: standardized mean difference (SMD) -1.25 (95% CI -1.65 to -0.851) in favor of treatment at 6-8 weeks — statistically significant.**
- Tactile sensitivity: also showed a statistically significant effect at 6-8 weeks (specific SMD not separately quoted in the abstract text extracted, but described as significant alongside air blast).
- **Subjective assessment (i.e., what the patient actually reports experiencing) FAILED to show a significant effect at 6-8 weeks** — an important disconnect between objective instrument-measured sensitivity and patient-perceived benefit.
- **Authors' conclusion: "The evidence generated by this review is based on a small number of individuals. Furthermore, the effect varies with the methods applied for assessing the sensitivity. Thus no clear evidence is available for the support of potassium containing toothpastes for dentine hypersensitivity."** — This is a genuinely more cautious/mixed verdict than a simple "moderate-to-strong evidence" summary would suggest: objective measures show a real, sizeable effect, but the review's own authors call the overall evidence base weak and are unwilling to declare the ingredient clearly proven effective.
- Disclosed conflict of interest: one review author (Sven Poulsen) received a prize from Zendium Inc. (a toothpaste manufacturer, Denmark) in 1997 — an old, disclosed, but real conflict worth noting.
- The original 2001 version of this review (PMID 11405992) found a similar pattern with 4 pooled studies: air blast SMD -1.51 (95% CI -2.09 to -0.94), again significant on objective measures but not on subjective assessment, with the same "no strong evidence" conclusion language.
