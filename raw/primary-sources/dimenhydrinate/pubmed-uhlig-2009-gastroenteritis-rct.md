# PubMed Abstract — Dimenhydrinate in Pediatric Gastroenteritis RCT (condensed facts, not full text)

**Source:** Uhlig U, Pfeil N, Gelbrich G, et al. Dimenhydrinate in children with infectious gastroenteritis: a prospective, RCT. Pediatrics. 2009 Oct. PMID: 19752076.
**Retrieved:** 2026-08-26 (abstract viewed via PubMed, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19752076/)

## Condensed facts
- Prospective, randomized, placebo-controlled, multicenter trial. 243 children with presumed gastroenteritis and vomiting (no/mild dehydration only) randomized to RECTAL dimenhydrinate or placebo; all received oral rehydration therapy.
- **Note on indication match:** this trial studies dimenhydrinate for gastroenteritis-related vomiting, NOT motion sickness specifically — the ingredient page's core indication in this database is children's motion sickness, but this is the most directly relevant pediatric RCT with hard numbers located in this search pass. Flagged explicitly rather than presented as motion-sickness-specific evidence.
- Primary outcome (weight gain at 18–24h): did NOT differ between dimenhydrinate and placebo groups.
- Secondary outcome — vomiting frequency: mean **0.64 episodes** (dimenhydrinate) vs. **1.36 episodes** (placebo) between randomization and follow-up.
- **69.6% of dimenhydrinate group vs. 47.4% of placebo group were completely free of vomiting** in that interval.
- Hospital admission rate, fluid intake, general well-being, and adverse effects (including diarrhea episode count) were similar between groups.
- **Authors' conclusion: dimenhydrinate reduces vomiting frequency in children with mild dehydration, but overall benefit is LOW because it does not improve oral rehydration success or overall clinical outcome (weight gain).** This is a genuinely mixed/modest verdict, not a strong endorsement.
