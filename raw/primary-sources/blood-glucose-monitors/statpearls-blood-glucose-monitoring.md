## Technique & Mechanism
**Capillary blood glucose (CBG) / glucometers:** enzymatic reaction linked to electrochemical detection, using glucose oxidase or glucose dehydrogenase enzymes; generates electrical current proportional to glucose concentration.
**Continuous glucose monitoring (CGM):** measures interstitial fluid glucose via subcutaneous sensor, typically every 1–5 minutes, using glucose oxidase immobilized on the electrode surface. Sensors last 10–15 days, provide real-time trend data unavailable from point-of-care fingerstick testing.
**Venous plasma testing:** lab-based venipuncture, superior precision, serves as the diagnostic reference standard for diabetes.

## Interfering Substances & Technical Factors
- **Glucose oxidase-based meters:** sensitive to ambient oxygen. Arterial blood or supplemental oxygen therapy may produce falsely LOW readings; low oxygen tension may yield falsely ELEVATED results.
- **Glucose dehydrogenase-based meters:** generally oxygen-insensitive, but subject to interference from maltose, galactose, xylose, acetaminophen, dopamine, vitamin C, and N-acetylcysteine.
- **Hematocrit effects:** high hematocrit → falsely LOW readings; low hematocrit → falsely HIGH readings.
- **CGM interferents:** acetaminophen (above 1000 mg/6h in some systems), high-dose ascorbic acid, hydroxyurea, select antibiotics.

## Accuracy Standards
- **FDA guidance, home use:** 95% of results must fall within 15% of the reference value across the usable glucose range.
- **FDA guidance, hospital point-of-care:** stricter — 95% of results within 12% for glucose ≥75 mg/dL, and within 12 mg/dL for levels <75 mg/dL.
- **ISO 15197:2013:** widely referenced international accuracy standard.
- **CGM standard:** ≥95% of measured values expected to fall within zones A and B of the Clarke-Parkes error grid.

## Clinical Significance & Complications
- Hyperglycemic crises (DKA, HHS) require emergent IV fluids, insulin, electrolyte monitoring.
- Long-term hyperglycemia drives microvascular (retinopathy, nephropathy, peripheral neuropathy) and macrovascular (coronary artery disease) complications.
- Hypoglycemia: brain requires continuous glucose supply; acute/chronic/recurrent hypoglycemia can cause cognitive impairment, seizures, loss of consciousness.
- CGM lag-time limitation: interstitial glucose can lag blood glucose by an average of 5–15 minutes, especially during rapid glycemic change — a real limitation distinct from meter "accuracy" per se.

## Epidemiological context
International Diabetes Federation: ~589 million adults aged 20-79 living with diabetes in 2024, projected to rise to 852.5 million by 2050.
