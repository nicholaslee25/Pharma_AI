## Condensed facts
- Literature review of publications 2010-2017 assessing blood glucose monitor (BGM) accuracy against ISO 15197:2003 and/or ISO 15197:2013 standards.
- 58 publications found, describing performance of 143 unique BGM systems, of which 59 were FDA-cleared.
- **FDA-cleared BGMs significantly more likely to pass ISO 15197:2003** — OR 2.39 (95% CI 1.45–3.92, P<0.01) — and **ISO 15197:2013** — OR 2.20 (95% CI 1.51–3.27, P<0.01) — vs. non-FDA-cleared meters.
- Newer meters more likely to pass both standards than older ones.
- **Manufacturer-supported studies showed dramatically higher odds of an FDA-cleared BGM passing** than independent studies: OR 22.4 (95% CI 8.73–21.57 [as reported in abstract], P<0.001) for ISO 2003, OR 23.08 (95% CI 10.16–60.03, P<0.001) for ISO 2013 — a substantial industry-funding bias signal.
- Authors' conclusion: BGM accuracy should be assessed independently after regulatory clearance, since failure to meet mandated performance levels can have real clinical/economic consequences.
