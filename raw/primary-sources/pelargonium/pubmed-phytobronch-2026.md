## Design
Pragmatic, open-label RCT across 36 primary care practices + 5 walk-in clinics in Switzerland. Adults (≥18y) with a new episode of acute bronchitis, cough ≤8 days' duration.

## Scale
332 participants: 155 randomized to EPs® 7630, 177 to usual care.

## Co-Primary Outcomes
1. Days to 50% reduction in symptoms from peak (Acute Bronchitis Severity Score, ABSS)
2. Proportion using antibiotics

## Results
- Time to 50% symptom reduction: no significant difference (adjusted regression coefficient 0.05, 95% CI −0.13 to 0.23; p=0.578)
- Antibiotic use: 17.4% (20/155) EPs®7630 vs. 25.2% (33/177) usual care — 7-point / 31% relative reduction, but NOT statistically significant (adjusted RR 0.78, 95% CI 0.49–1.26; p=0.309)
- Adverse events: 32.3% (50/155) EPs®7630 vs. 21.5% (38/177) usual care (HR 1.40, 95% CI 1.03–1.89; p=0.030) — statistically significant increase; all adverse drug events were mild and previously described

## Authors' Conclusion
EPs®7630 did not significantly reduce symptom duration or antibiotic use, and was associated with more frequent (mild) adverse events. The observed reduction in antibiotic use, though not significant, is flagged as worth further study for antimicrobial stewardship.
