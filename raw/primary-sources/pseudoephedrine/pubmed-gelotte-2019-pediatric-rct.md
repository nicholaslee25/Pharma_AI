## Design
Multicenter, double-blind, placebo-controlled RCT. Pseudoephedrine HCl 30mg tablets (current OTC monograph dose) in children 6–11 years, for temporary relief of nasal congestion from the common cold. Primary endpoint: weighted sum of change from baseline in instantaneous nasal congestion severity, 1–8h after first dose (day 1). Dosing continued as needed up to 7 days.

## Results
- Pseudoephedrine superior to placebo on the primary endpoint: least-squares mean treatment difference 1.2 (p=0.029)
- Day 1 secondary endpoints: supportive; Day 2 secondary endpoints: only numerically favorable (not necessarily statistically significant)
- Somnolence: 71.9% (pseudoephedrine) vs. 63.9% (placebo)
- Insomnia: 34.4% vs. 38.9%
- Nervousness: 20.0% vs. 23.6%

## Authors' Conclusion
Pseudoephedrine provides temporary relief of nasal congestion in children 6 to <12 years at the current OTC monograph dose; multiple dosing up to 7 days as-needed was generally safe in this population.
