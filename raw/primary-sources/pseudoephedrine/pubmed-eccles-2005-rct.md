## Design
Double-blind, randomized, parallel-group, placebo-controlled trial, single center, 3 study days. Total nasal airway resistance (NAR) measured by posterior rhinomanometry; subjective congestion scores also collected. Single dose measured hourly over 4h on day 1; multiple-dose effect assessed on day 3.

## Scale
238 patients with nasal congestion from acute URTI, mean age ~20 years. Pseudoephedrine 60 mg single dose (day 1), repeated dosing through day 3.

## Results
- **Day 1, objective (NAR AUC 0–3h, primary endpoint):** significantly lower (better) with pseudoephedrine vs. placebo, p=0.006. NAR AUC 0–4h: p=0.001.
- **Day 3, objective:** NAR AUC 0–3h and 0–4h both p<0.001 vs. placebo.
- **Day 1, subjective (VAS congestion):** AUC 0–3h p=0.029, AUC 0–4h p=0.021 (favoring pseudoephedrine).
- **Day 3, subjective:** VAS differences NOT statistically significant.
- **Summary congestion/stuffiness score** over full study duration: greater improvement with pseudoephedrine, p=0.016.
- **Heart rate:** ~2–4 bpm higher with pseudoephedrine vs. placebo.
- **Adverse events:** 5 in each group, judged unrelated to treatment.

## Authors' Conclusion
Single 60 mg dose of pseudoephedrine is superior to placebo on both objective and subjective nasal-congestion measures. Multiple-dose benefit is supported by objective measures (day 3) and subjective measures pooled over 3 days, but NOT by day-3 VAS scores specifically — i.e., objective decongestant effect persisted with repeated dosing more reliably than the subjective symptom-score benefit.
