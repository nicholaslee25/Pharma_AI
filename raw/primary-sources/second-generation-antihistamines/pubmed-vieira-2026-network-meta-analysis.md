## Design
Systematic review and network meta-analysis of RCTs in adults with perennial or seasonal allergic rhinitis (AR), comparing oral H1-antihistamines (OAHs) vs. placebo and vs. each other. Outcomes: Total Nasal Symptom Score, Total Ocular Symptom Score, Rhinoconjunctivitis Quality-of-Life Questionnaire, adverse events, withdrawals due to AEs. GRADE approach used for certainty of evidence on the most clinically relevant second-generation OAH comparisons.

## Scale
74 RCTs (21 perennial AR, 53 seasonal AR).

## Results
- **Cetirizine, ebastine, bilastine, and rupatadine** were among the individual OAHs associated with the highest efficacy for improving nasal symptoms
- Most-efficacious agent varied across other outcome measures (ocular symptoms, QoL)
- Similar adverse-event frequency across different second-generation OAHs; serious adverse events rare
- **For most comparisons, certainty of evidence was rated "low" or "very low"** — substantial uncertainty about true treatment effects

## Authors' Conclusion
Although some OAHs appear more efficacious than others, most differences between individual second-generation medications are trivial or small, and there were no relevant safety-profile differences found among second-generation OAHs.

## Relevance
This is a genuinely balanced, current (2026) finding: cetirizine performs among the best for nasal symptoms specifically, but the overall message is that second-generation antihistamines are largely interchangeable in practice, with low-certainty evidence underlying most head-to-head efficacy claims — useful context against overclaiming any one brand's superiority.
