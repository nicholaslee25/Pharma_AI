## Mechanism of Action
Fast-acting, highly selective peripheral H1-receptor antagonist. Metabolite of hydroxyzine (first-generation antihistamine) but does not cross the blood-brain barrier to a large extent, producing minimal sedation. Reduces vascular permeability (decreases capillary fluid leakage into tissue) and has anti-inflammatory effects, notably minimizing neutrophil/eosinophil migration.

## FDA-Approved Indications
Allergic rhinitis (seasonal/perennial), chronic urticaria (reduces hives/pruritus severity), allergic conjunctivitis (ophthalmic formulation), acute urticaria (IV formulation). AAAAI/ACAAI guidelines recognize second-generation antihistamines like cetirizine as first-line for chronic urticaria.

## Dosing
- Adults/children ≥12y: 5–10mg once daily (oral); IV 10mg for acute urticaria
- Children 6–11y: 5–10mg once daily (syrup)
- Children 2–5y: 2.5mg once daily (syrup)
- Infants 6–23mo: 2.5mg once daily (syrup)
- Ophthalmic: 1 drop (0.24%) twice daily

## Pharmacokinetics
Rapid GI absorption, peak plasma ~1h. Onset 20–60min, persists ≥24h. 93% plasma protein binding. Oxidative O-dealkylation metabolism; NOT a CYP450 substrate (relevant to its relatively low drug-interaction potential vs. many drugs); is a P-glycoprotein substrate. Half-life 8.3h. Primarily renal excretion. Food doesn't affect total exposure but delays peak concentration by 1.7h.

## Contraindications
Known hypersensitivity to cetirizine or its ingredients; known hypersensitivity to hydroxyzine (cetirizine's parent compound).

## Adverse Effects
**Adults (most common):** somnolence (dose-related), fatigue, pharyngitis, dizziness, dry mouth.
**Pediatric:** similar profile; more likely than adults to report headaches; in ages 2–11, most AEs mild/moderate, abdominal pain considered treatment-related in some cases.
**Ophthalmic:** conjunctival hyperemia, instillation site pain.
**Rare/severe (postmarketing):** severe hypotension, anaphylaxis, hemolytic anemia, cholestasis, orofacial dyskinesia, glomerulonephritis, hepatitis, stillbirth, thrombocytopenia.

## Drug Interactions
- **CNS depressants** (alcohol, benzodiazepines, opioids): dose-related sedation risk
- **Pitolisant:** cetirizine may diminish its therapeutic efficacy
- **Gabapentin:** cetirizine decreases gabapentin plasma concentrations; combined CNS depression possible
- **Verapamil** (P-gp inhibitor): increases cetirizine CNS penetration
- **Erdafitinib** (P-gp inhibitor): avoid concurrent use

## Special Populations
- **Hepatic impairment:** reduce to 5mg/day (≥12y); lower doses for ages 6–11; monitor LFTs
- **Renal impairment:** reduce to 5mg/day for CrCl 11–31 mL/min or hemodialysis patients (≥12y); dose adjustment needed for ages 6–11
- **Pregnancy:** former FDA Category B; ACOG/ACAAI recommend cetirizine when antihistamine treatment is needed in pregnancy, used only when necessary (first-generation antihistamines still considered safest by some guidance, a nuance worth preserving)
- **Breastfeeding:** occasional/small doses acceptable; prolonged larger doses risk decreased milk supply and infant drowsiness
- **Elderly (≥65y):** inadequate efficacy-specific data; safety data show no significant difference vs. younger adults, though individual sensitivity to ADRs remains possible
- **Pediatric (2–11y):** moderate-to-low certainty evidence of clinical benefit; generally well tolerated with increased somnolence risk

## Overdose/Toxicity
Animal studies: minimal lethal dose in rats ~460x the max recommended adult daily dose; primary acute toxicity target CNS, chronic target liver. Human case data: adult 150mg — somnolence only, normal labs; infant 180mg — restlessness/irritability/drowsiness; 6-year-old accidental ingestion — fixed/dilated pupils, tachycardia, agitation, hyperthermia, hallucinations consistent with anticholinergic toxicity. No known specific antidote; not effectively removed by dialysis; management is supportive/symptomatic.
