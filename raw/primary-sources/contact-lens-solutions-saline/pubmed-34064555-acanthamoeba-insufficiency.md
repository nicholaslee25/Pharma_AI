# Acanthamoeba Keratitis and Multipurpose Solution Insufficiency (condensed facts, not full text)

**Source:** Study on silver nanoparticle-conjugated contact lens solutions for Acanthamoeba keratitis protection. Pathogens. 2021. PMID: 34064555. DOI: 10.3390/pathogens10050583.
**Retrieved:** 2026-08-26 (viewed via PMC free full text, PMC8151187)
**License note:** Open-access journal article (Pathogens/MDPI) — condensed facts recorded here.

## Condensed facts
- More than 90% of Acanthamoeba keratitis (AK) cases occur in contact lens wearers; global AK infection rates among wearers have risen from an estimated 17 to 70 per million.
- **Standard multipurpose contact lens solutions (the disinfectant category most brands in this database's "Contact Lens Solutions, Saline" list belong to) are explicitly stated by the authors, citing their own prior research, to be "not effective against Acanthamoeba"** — these solutions' antimicrobial/antifungal disinfectant systems are not fully efficient against Acanthamoeba trophozoites and cysts specifically, even though they are validated (via ISO 14729) against standard bacterial/fungal panel organisms.
- AK symptoms (eye pain, vision loss) overlap with more common bacterial keratitis, complicating early diagnosis and delaying appropriate treatment.
- Study explored silver-nanoparticle conjugation as an experimental (not yet commercially available) approach to close this gap — an active area of research, not a solved problem as of this paper's publication.
- Practical implication: no OTC multipurpose contact lens solution (MPS) in this database's Contact Lens Solutions, Saline category should be assumed to offer meaningful anti-Acanthamoeba protection based on ISO 14729 bacterial/fungal-panel certification alone.
