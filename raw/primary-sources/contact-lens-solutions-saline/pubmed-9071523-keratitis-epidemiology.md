## Condensed facts
- Foundational epidemiological study establishing annual microbial-keratitis incidence rates by contact lens wear modality, still cited as the primary reference for these figures decades later.
- Estimated annual incidence per 10,000 wearers: ~1.1 (daily-wear rigid gas-permeable), ~3.5–4.1 (daily-wear soft lenses), ~13.3–20.9 (extended-wear soft lenses), ~52 (therapeutic/bandage contact lenses).
- Later reviews describe the overall rate as relatively stable over ~25+ years at roughly 2–5 per 10,000 regular contact lens wearers.
- Key risk factors identified across the literature building on this study: overnight/extended wear, smoking, male sex, socioeconomic status, and poor lens hygiene/solution compliance.
- Bacterial pathogens (predominantly Pseudomonas aeruginosa, then Staphylococcus aureus) account for ~90% of microbial keratitis cases; amoebae (Acanthamoeba) and fungi account for the remaining ~10%.
