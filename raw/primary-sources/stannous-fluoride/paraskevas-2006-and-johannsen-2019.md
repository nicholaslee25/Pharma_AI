# Efficacy Evidence — Stannous Fluoride for Gingivitis/Plaque/Calculus (condensed facts, not full text)

## Source 1: Paraskevas & van der Weijden 2006 (systematic review, DARE-quality-assessed)
Paraskevas S, van der Weijden GA. A review of the effects of stannous fluoride on gingivitis. J Clin Periodontol. 2006;33(1):1-13. Hosted in DARE on NCBI Bookshelf: https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK72762/
**Retrieved:** 2026-08-26 (via ?report=printable)
**License note:** Journal article summarized via DARE (Database of Abstracts of Reviews of Effects), Centre for Reviews and Dissemination — condensed facts recorded here.

### Condensed facts
- 15 studies included (n=4,989 total), of which 14 were RCTs (4,924 participants).
- **Gingival index: 6 pooled studies, weighted mean difference (WMD) -0.15 (95% CI -0.20 to -0.11)** — statistically significant reduction favoring stannous fluoride.
- **Gingivitis severity: 2 studies, WMD -0.21 (95% CI -0.27 to -0.14)** — also significant.
- **Plaque (Turesky index): 4 studies, WMD -0.31 (95% CI -0.54 to -0.07)** — significant, favoring stannous fluoride.
- **Notable increase in tooth staining with stannous fluoride formulations compared to sodium fluoride** — a real, documented tradeoff.
- Conclusion: stannous fluoride dentifrices reduce gingivitis and plaque formation compared to conventional dentifrices, though heterogeneity in outcome measures made assessing the true magnitude of effect difficult.

## Source 2: Johannsen et al. 2019 (systematic review)
Johannsen A, Emilson CG, Johannsen G, Konradsson K, Lingström P, Ramberg P. Effects of stabilized stannous fluoride dentifrice on dental calculus, dental plaque, gingivitis, halitosis and stain: A systematic review. Heliyon. 2019. PMID: 31872105.
**Retrieved:** 2026-08-26 (abstract viewed via PubMed, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31872105/)
**License note:** Open access (Heliyon, © 2019 The Author(s)) — condensed facts recorded regardless per this vault's general practice.

### Condensed facts
- Searched Medline OVID, Embase.com, Cochrane Library, database inception to June 2017. 6 researchers independently selected studies/extracted data/assessed quality. Risk of bias via Swedish Agency for Health Technology Assessment (SBU 2018) checklist.
- **Studies meeting inclusion: 2 on dental calculus, 21 on dental plaque/gingivitis, 4 on halitosis, 5 on stain.**
- Risk of bias HIGH for calculus, halitosis, and stain studies; VARIED for plaque/gingivitis studies.
- Significant reductions in dental calculus and halitosis reported for SnF2 dentifrice.
- **No significant differences in stain reduction were noted** — an interesting partial contrast with the Paraskevas 2006 review's staining-increase finding; the two reviews examined somewhat different comparisons/outcomes and should not be treated as contradictory without more detail.
- Gingivitis meta-analysis favored SnF2 dentifrice vs. other dentifrices, but individual trials showed SUBSTANTIAL heterogeneity.
- Conclusion: stabilized SnF2 toothpaste had a positive effect on calculus, plaque, gingivitis, stain, and halitosis, with a tendency toward more pronounced effect vs. non-SnF2 toothpaste — but the authors explicitly call for a new generation of well-conducted RCTs to further support these findings, i.e., they do not consider the evidence base definitive yet.
