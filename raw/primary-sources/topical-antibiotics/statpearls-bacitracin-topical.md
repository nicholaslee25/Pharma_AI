## Indications
Minor skin injuries (cuts, scrapes, burns). FDA-approved 1948 for preventing/treating acute and chronic localized skin infections. Available as single agent or combined with neomycin and polymyxin B (triple antibiotic).

## Mechanism
Cyclic polypeptide, bacteriostatic AND bactericidal — inhibits transfer of mucopeptides into bacterial cell walls, preventing cell wall synthesis/replication. Effective against gram-positive bacteria; most gram-negative organisms are resistant (this is why triple-antibiotic combinations add polymyxin B/neomycin for broader gram-negative coverage).

## Dosing
Topical: apply sufficient ointment (500 units/g) to cover affected area after cleansing with mild soap/water. Ophthalmic: apply to conjunctival sac 1-3x/day.

## Contraindications
Hypersensitivity to bacitracin or formulation components. Cross-sensitivity with neomycin is possible even though the two are chemically unrelated (likely due to coreactivity from similar exposure patterns/combination-product use, per related contact-dermatitis literature).

## Adverse Effects & Allergic Reactions
Common: fever, hives, itching, swelling, difficulty breathing, nausea, vomiting. **Anaphylactic reactions to bacitracin have been reported.**
**Bacitracin was designated "Contact Allergen of the Year" in 2003** by the American Contact Dermatitis Society. Between 2005-2006, it was the 6th most common allergen in patch testing.
Simultaneous contact allergy to neomycin, bacitracin, and polymyxin is well documented despite their differing chemical structures.
Neomycin, though less common a sensitizer than bacitracin in some reports, has been implicated in life-threatening contact urticaria and anaphylaxis.
Common reaction sites: legs/feet, particularly when applied to stasis dermatitis or leg ulcers (chronic wound populations).

## Drug Interactions
Intramuscular bacitracin (not the topical form) should avoid concurrent use with streptomycin, kanamycin, polymyxin E, and neomycin due to nephrotoxicity risk — included here for completeness though less relevant to OTC topical use.

## Monitoring
Do not use topically for more than 7 days unless directed by a physician. Monitor application area; discontinue if symptoms worsen.

## Comparison Data
This StatPearls article does not itself provide a head-to-head comparison to plain petrolatum — see the separate Smack et al. 1996 JAMA RCT (PMID 8805732), already saved at raw/primary-sources/petrolatum/pubmed-smack-1996-jama-rct.md, for that specific comparison (white petrolatum vs. bacitracin, 922 patients: no significant difference in overall infection rate, but bacitracin prevented all staph-specific infections while petrolatum had zero allergic contact dermatitis cases vs. bacitracin's 0.9%).
