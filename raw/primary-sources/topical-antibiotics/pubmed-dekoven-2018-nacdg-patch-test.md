## Condensed facts
- Multicenter study, 13 North American centers, standardized patch testing with a 70-allergen screening series (including neomycin and bacitracin as standard-series allergens), Jan 2015–Feb 2017.
- **5,597 patients tested. 3,725 (66.6%) had at least one positive reaction; 2,798 (50.2%) had a primary diagnosis of allergic contact dermatitis (ACD); 572 (10.2%) had occupationally related skin disease.** 10,983 total positive allergic reactions recorded.
- Nickel remained the most common allergen overall (17.5%); methylisothiazolinone second (13.4%).
- **Per secondary reporting of this same NACDG dataset (specific figures not independently re-confirmed word-for-word from the abstract excerpt viewed): neomycin and bacitracin both placed among the top-10 most frequent allergens in this cycle, at approximately 7.0% and 6.9% prevalence respectively among tested patients.** This is consistent with, and continues, a well-documented multi-decade trend of both being persistent, clinically significant topical-antibiotic contact allergens in North America (StatPearls separately notes bacitracin was "Contact Allergen of the Year" in 2003 and the 6th most common allergen in 2005-2006 patch testing — the same general magnitude, corroborating rather than contradicting this 2015-2016 figure).
- Authors' broader conclusion (unrelated to bacitracin/neomycin specifically): the epidemic of methylisothiazolinone sensitivity continued; testing beyond a basic screening tray is necessary for complete ACD evaluation.
