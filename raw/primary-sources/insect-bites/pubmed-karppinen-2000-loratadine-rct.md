# PubMed Abstract — Loratadine for Mosquito Bites in Children RCT (condensed facts, not full text)

**Source:** Karppinen A, Kautiainen H, Petman L, et al. Loratadine in the treatment of mosquito-bite-sensitive children. Allergy. 2000 Jul. PMID: 10921468.
**Retrieved:** 2026-08-26 (abstract viewed via PubMed, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10921468/)

## Condensed facts
- Double-blind, placebo-controlled, crossover study. Loratadine 0.3 mg/kg in 28 mosquito-bite-sensitive children (aged 2-11), exposed to Aedes aegypti laboratory mosquitoes. Bite lesion size and pruritus (VAS) measured at 15 min, 2h, 6h, 24h.
- **Loratadine decreased wheal size by 45% (P<0.001, 25 children) at 15 minutes vs. placebo.**
- **Loratadine decreased accompanying pruritus by 78% (P=0.011, 12 children) at 15 minutes vs. placebo.**
- The 24-hour delayed bite lesion size was also significantly reduced (P=0.004), but there was no significant change at 2h or 6h — the antihistamine effect was strongest at the immediate (15-min) and delayed (24h) timepoints specifically, not the intermediate ones.
- Loratadine was well tolerated; no marked side effects recorded.
- Authors' conclusion: prophylactic loratadine significantly reduces both whealing/pruritus AND 24h delayed bite lesion size — its therapeutic effect spans both the immediate and delayed allergic-response phases of mosquito-bite sensitivity in children.
