## Mechanism & Pathophysiology
Four primary injury mechanisms: mechanical trauma, allergic response to salivary antigens, toxic venom injection, disease vector transmission. Mechanical injury causes pain/swelling and a portal of entry for bacteria. Venom composition varies widely: centipede (histamine, serotonin), Hymenoptera/bees-wasps (bradykinin, acetylcholine), bark scorpion (opens neuronal sodium channels, prolonged depolarization), brown recluse (hemolytic enzymes), black widow (α-latrotoxin neurotoxin). Histology: early dermal edema + neutrophilic infiltrate, progressing to dense perivascular inflammation with eosinophils as a hallmark.

## Clinical Presentation
Most common: erythematous papules, urticarial wheals, pruritus, edema, localized pain.
- Mosquitoes/flies: minimal trauma, urticarial wheals, intense pruritus
- Brown recluse: often painless initially, may progress to full-thickness necrosis requiring debridement/grafting
- Black widow: sharp pinprick then muscle spasms, chest/abdominal pain, onset 1-6h, persisting 12-48h
- Lice/scabies: intense pruritus, papules, visible nits/burrows
- Bedbugs: small papules in linear arrangement
- Caterpillars: erythema, pruritus, papules, grid-like hemorrhagic pattern (puss caterpillar)

## Evaluation
Labs unnecessary for uncomplicated localized reactions. Severe envenomation: CBC, metabolic panel, LFTs, coagulation studies, creatine kinase, urinalysis. ECG for Lyme/Chagas/arrhythmia concern. Anaphylaxis is a clinical diagnosis requiring emergent treatment.

## Treatment
**Supportive/first-line:** wound cleansing (soap/water), ice, tetanus update, acetaminophen/NSAIDs for pain, elevation of edematous areas.
**Topical:** calamine lotion for pruritus, intermediate-potency corticosteroids, topical antibiotics for secondary infection.
**Systemic:** oral antihistamines (H1 and H2 blockers) for moderate-severe pruritus, systemic corticosteroids for severe itching, benzodiazepines for muscle spasm/myoclonus, beta-blockers for tachyarrhythmia/hypertension, antibiotics for bacterial infection.
**Emergency:** anaphylaxis requires immediate IM epinephrine, repeat doses q5-15min if needed; adjuncts (steroids, albuterol, antihistamines) should never delay epinephrine.
**Antivenom:** FDA-approved for Centruroides scorpions and Latrodectus spiders, reserved for severe systemic toxicity, requires poison control consultation.
**Specific:** scabies — permethrin 5% cream (neck-down, 2 treatments 1 week apart), oral ivermectin for failures. Lice — pediculicides (pyrethrins, permethrin 1%), oral ivermectin, fine-tooth comb. Brown recluse — ice, early surgical excision NOT recommended. Botflies — surgical extraction, or petroleum jelly occlusion induces larval emergence within 24h.

## Complications
**Local:** secondary bacterial infection (S. aureus, S. pyogenes), impetiginization, cellulitis, lymphangitis, excoriation, ulceration, scarring, postinflammatory pigmentation, necrosis (brown recluse).
**Systemic:** anaphylaxis (can be rapidly fatal), serum sickness-like reactions, vasculitis, vector-borne disease transmission (malaria, Lyme, dengue, yellow fever, Chagas, plague, typhus, leishmaniasis, West Nile), autonomic instability, neurotoxicity, coagulopathy, organ failure, psychological distress/sleep disturbance from persistent/recurrent bites.

## Statistics
American Association of Poison Control Centers: 28,087 arthropod-exposure cases documented in 2015 (likely undercounts actual encounters substantially). Risk factors for severe outcomes: extremes of age, immunocompromise, cardiovascular comorbidities, allergic conditions.
