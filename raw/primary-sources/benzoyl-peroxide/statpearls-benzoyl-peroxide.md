## Mechanism of Action
Bactericidal activity against Cutibacterium acnes. Upon skin absorption, converts to benzoic acid, releasing active free-radical oxygen species and oxidizing bacterial proteins. ~5% absorbed systemically, renally excreted. Also has mild sebostatic and keratolytic effects. 10% benzoyl peroxide applied daily for two weeks reduced P. acnes by 98% and free fatty acids by 50% (cited study finding).

## Indications
**FDA-approved:** acne vulgaris (primary); rosacea (microencapsulated 5% cream for moderate-severe papulopustular cases).
**Off-label:** folliculitis variants, pseudofolliculitis barbae, progressive macular hypomelanosis, pressure ulcers, perforating diseases, pitted keratolysis.

## Dosing & Administration
Available strengths: 2.5%, 5%, 10% in lotions/gels/creams/cleansing bars/masks/combination products. Cleansing bars/washes: 1-3x daily. Masks: thin layer 15-25 min weekly. Combination with antibiotics: twice daily. Combination with adapalene: once daily.

## Contraindications & Warnings
Known hypersensitivity to benzoyl peroxide; caution with cinnamon/benzoic-acid-derivative sensitivity (cross-reactivity risk). Avoid eyes/nose/mouth/mucous membranes — rinse ≥15 minutes with water on contact. Minimize UV exposure, use sunscreen. Fabric/hair bleaching risk. Some formulations contain propylene glycol or dimethyl sulfoxide, associated with toxicity risks.

## Adverse Effects
Most common: bleaching of colored fabrics/hair. At higher concentrations: mild dryness, erythema, scaling. Contact dermatitis reported in a small percentage of patients with marked erythema/irritation at application site.

## Drug Interactions
- Topical sulfones (dapsone): skin/facial hair yellowing
- Topical hydroquinone: increased irritation, transient epidermal staining
- Oral isotretinoin: increased skin irritation/dryness
- Topical tretinoin: decreases tretinoin efficacy (adapalene is compatible)
- Topical anesthetics: decreased anesthetic efficacy

## Special Populations
**Pregnancy:** former FDA Category C, but "generally considered safe" given minimal systemic absorption; 5% BID considered safe throughout all stages of pregnancy.
**Breastfeeding:** insufficient data, but topical use generally considered safe given low systemic absorption; avoid application areas with direct infant contact.
**Pediatric:** little data supports safety/efficacy in children, though generally considered safe given minimal systemic absorption.
**Hepatic/Renal impairment, elderly:** no dosage adjustments specified.

## Monitoring
No routine monitoring parameters recommended. Monitor for contact dermatitis; discontinue if marked erythema/irritation occurs. Recommend test-area treatment before full use.

## Toxicity & Overdose
Not to be taken by mouth. For ingestion: contact Poison Control (1-800-222-1222), call 911, or go to nearest ED. No known antidote.
