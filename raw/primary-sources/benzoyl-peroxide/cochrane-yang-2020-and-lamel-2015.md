# Efficacy Evidence — Benzoyl Peroxide (condensed facts, not full text)

## Source 1: Cochrane systematic review
Yang Z, Zhang Y, Lazic Mosler E, et al. Topical benzoyl peroxide for acne. Cochrane Database Syst Rev. 2020. PMID: 32175593.
**Retrieved:** 2026-08-26 (abstract viewed via PubMed, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32175593/)
**License note:** Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews is copyrighted. Condensed facts only from the free PubMed abstract, no full text reproduced.

### Condensed facts
- 120 trials, 29,592 participants randomized (116 trials reported clear randomization numbers). 72 trials mild-moderate acne, 26 severe acne; mean age 18-30.
- Treatment duration: >8 weeks in 80 trials, up to 12 weeks in 108 trials.
- **Participant-reported acne improvement:** BPO may be more effective than placebo/no treatment — RR **1.27** (95% CI 1.12–1.45; 3 RCTs, 2,234 participants, 10-12 week treatment; low-certainty evidence).
- **BPO vs. adapalene:** little to no difference — RR 0.99 (95% CI 0.90–1.10; 5 RCTs, 1,472 participants).
- **BPO vs. clindamycin:** RR 0.95 (95% CI 0.68–1.34; 1 RCT, 240 participants).
- **Withdrawal due to adverse effects:** risk may be HIGHER with BPO vs. placebo/no treatment — RR **2.13** (95% CI 1.55–2.93; 24 RCTs, 13,744 participants, low-certainty evidence). Most common causes: erythema, pruritus, skin burning.
- BPO vs. adapalene withdrawal comparison: RR 1.85 (95% CI 0.94–3.64; 11 RCTs, 3,295 participants) — very low-certainty evidence.
- Authors note high/unclear risk of performance, detection, attrition bias across many trials; 50 trials industry-funded, 63 did not report funding.

## Source 2: Vehicle-controlled trial design review
Lamel SA, Rahvar M, Maibach HI. Evaluating clinical trial design: systematic review of randomized vehicle-controlled trials for determining efficacy of benzoyl peroxide topical therapy for acne. Arch Dermatol Res. 2015 Nov. PMID: 26048131.
**Retrieved:** 2026-08-26 (abstract viewed via PubMed, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26048131/)
**License note:** Journal article, copyrighted. Condensed facts only from the free PubMed abstract.

### Condensed facts
- 12 RCTs, 2,818 patients on BPO monotherapy vs. 2,004 on vehicle (placebo) treatment.
- Average % reduction, total acne lesions: **44.3%** (SD 9.2) active vs. **27.8%** (SD 21.0) vehicle.
- Average % reduction, non-inflammatory lesions: 41.5% (SD 9.4) active vs. 27.0% (SD 20.9) vehicle.
- Average % reduction, inflammatory lesions: **52.1%** (SD 10.4) active vs. **34.7%** (SD 22.7) vehicle.
- Average % of participants achieving study-defined "success": 28.6% (SD 17.3) active vs. 15.2% (SD 9.5) vehicle.
- Authors' point: vehicle (placebo) response rates are substantial and variable, meaning trial design/vehicle effects can distort apparent BPO efficacy — a caution about interpreting any single trial's numbers in isolation.
