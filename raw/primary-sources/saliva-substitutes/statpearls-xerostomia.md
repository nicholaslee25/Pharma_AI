## Definition
The sensation of oral dryness, which may occur with or without a measurable decrease in actual saliva production.

## Primary Causes
Medication side effects (most frequent cause), Sjögren syndrome, head/neck radiation therapy, autoimmune diseases.

## Epidemiology
Swedish study: 21.3% of men, 27.3% of women reported xerostomia. Prevalence ranges 0.9%–64.8% depending on population studied. Nearly 100% of patients receiving head/neck radiation or diagnosed with Sjögren syndrome experience xerostomia.

## Medications Associated
Eight drug classes show ≥10% incidence of xerostomia as a side effect: anticholinergics, antidepressants, diuretics, antihypertensives, sedatives, muscle relaxants, analgesics, antihistamines.

## Evaluation (Sialometry)
Normal stimulated salivary flow: 1.5–2.0 mL/min. Normal unstimulated flow: 0.3–0.4 mL/min. Hyposalivation diagnosis: stimulated flow <0.5–0.7 mL/min or unstimulated <0.1 mL/min. Additional diagnostics: sialography, salivary gland biopsy when systemic cause suspected.

## Treatment
**Non-pharmacological:** patient education (water sipping, avoiding caffeine/tobacco), sugar-free gum/candy, artificial saliva products (sprays, lozenges, gels) — indicated before meals and as needed. **Disadvantage explicitly noted: saliva substitute products do not work reliably, have limited duration of action, unpleasant taste, and high monetary cost.**
**Pharmacological (prescription):** FDA-approved sialogogues — pilocarpine 5-10mg three times daily, cevimeline 30mg three times daily. Alternative agents: topical physostigmine, malic acid, anethole trithionate.

## Complications of Untreated Xerostomia
Dental caries and periodontal disease, candidiasis/fungal infections, halitosis, enamel erosion, poor nutrition from eating difficulty, anxiety/depression from chronic discomfort.
