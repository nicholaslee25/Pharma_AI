# Cochrane Review — Topical Therapies for Dry Mouth (condensed facts, not full text)

**Source:** Furness S, Worthington HV, Bryan G, Birchenough S, McMillan R. Interventions for the management of dry mouth: topical therapies. Cochrane Database Syst Rev. 2011. PMID: 22161442.
**Retrieved:** 2026-08-26 (abstract viewed via PubMed, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22161442/)
**License note:** Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews is copyrighted. Condensed facts only from the free PubMed abstract, no full text reproduced.

## Condensed facts
- RCTs of topical interventions (lozenges, sprays, mouthrinses, gels, oils, chewing gum, toothpastes) for dry mouth symptom, classified as saliva stimulants vs. saliva substitutes, vs. placebo or each other.
- **36 RCTs, 1,597 participants.** Only 1 trial at LOW risk of bias; 17 at HIGH risk of bias — most of the evidence base is low quality.
- Breakdown: 2 trials stimulants vs. placebo; 9 trials substitutes vs. placebo; 5 trials stimulants vs. substitutes directly; 18 trials comparing two-or-more substitutes directly; 2 trials comparing two-or-more stimulants directly.
- Meta-analysis was only possible for a few comparisons due to heterogeneity in interventions/outcomes.
- **Oxygenated glycerol triester (OGT) saliva substitute spray showed effectiveness vs. an electrolyte spray: SMD 0.77 (95% CI 0.38–1.15)** — approximately equivalent to a 2-point difference on a 10-point visual analogue scale (VAS) for mouth dryness. This is the one positive, quantified head-to-head finding in the whole review.
- Integrated mouthcare systems (toothpaste + gel + mouthwash) and oral reservoir devices showed "promising" results but insufficient evidence to recommend.
- Chewing gum increased saliva production in those with residual secretory capacity, but there was NO evidence gum is more or less effective than saliva substitutes.
- **Authors' overall conclusion: "There is no strong evidence from this review that any topical therapy is effective for relieving the symptom of dry mouth."** The one exception noted is the specific OGT-vs-electrolyte-spray comparison above — a narrow, product-specific finding, not a blanket endorsement of saliva substitutes as a class.
- Authors call for well-designed, adequately powered, CONSORT-compliant RCTs evaluating palatability, symptom reduction, AND long-term quality-of-life effects, since dry mouth is often a chronic problem for patients.
