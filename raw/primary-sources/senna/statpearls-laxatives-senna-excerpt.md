## Mechanism of Action
Stimulant laxative — stimulates the myenteric (Auerbach) plexus, increasing intestinal secretions and motility while decreasing water absorption from the bowel.

## Dosing
Available as an 8.6mg tablet; 1–2 tablets orally, 1–2 times per day.

## Adverse Effects
Stimulant laxatives like senna are known to cause abdominal pain. In vitro research has suggested potential neoplastic effects, though human data are lacking. Long-term use correlates with loss of haustral folds in the colon, potentially indicating neuronal or muscular injury. ## General Laxative Class Data (applies to senna as a stimulant laxative)
Osmotic or stimulant laxatives are typically first-line treatment for constipation. Chronic idiopathic constipation affects ~8–12% of the US population. Bulk laxatives are considered safe during pregnancy (this specific safety statement is about bulk laxatives, not stimulants like senna specifically — not extrapolated here). Continuous serum electrolyte monitoring is recommended especially for osmotic laxatives used long-term (again, senna is a stimulant not osmotic laxative — included for class context, not senna-specific claim).
