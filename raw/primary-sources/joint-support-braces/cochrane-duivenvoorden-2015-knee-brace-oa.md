# Cochrane Review — Braces and Orthoses for Treating Osteoarthritis of the Knee (condensed facts, not full text)

**Source:** Duivenvoorden T, Brouwer RW, van Raaij TM, Verhagen AP, Verhaar JAN, Bierma-Zeinstra SMA. Braces and orthoses for treating osteoarthritis of the knee. Cochrane Database Syst Rev. 2015;3:CD004020. PMID: 25773267. DOI: 10.1002/14651858.CD004020.pub3.
**Retrieved:** 2026-08-26 (abstract/summary viewed via PubMed and Cochrane Library listing)
**License note:** Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews is copyrighted. Only condensed facts from the free abstract/summary are recorded here — no full text reproduced.

## Condensed facts
- Second update of a review first published 2005, updated 2007 and 2015.
- Evaluates knee braces (valgus/unloader braces, neoprene sleeves) and foot/ankle orthoses (lateral wedge insoles) for medial compartment knee osteoarthritis.
- Primary outcomes assessed: pain, stiffness, physical function, quality of life.
- **Overall conclusion: evidence was inconclusive** for benefit of bracing on pain, stiffness, function, and quality of life in medial compartment knee OA.
- Moderate-quality evidence found no clear difference between laterally wedged insoles and neutral insoles.
- Low-quality evidence found no clear difference between valgus knee braces and laterally wedged insoles.
- Long-term (>12 months) evidence was especially limited/low quality; short-term studies were inconclusive on advantage.
- Authors note the optimal choice of orthosis type remains unclear and that patient-reported compliance/tolerance issues (skin irritation, discomfort, bulkiness) affect real-world brace use, independent of any efficacy question.
