# Systematic Review — Compression Wrapping for Acute Closed Extremity Joint Injuries (condensed facts, not full text)

**Source:** Systematic review of compression bandaging/wrapping (e.g. ACE-style elastic wraps) for acute closed extremity joint injuries (ankle sprain population predominant). PMID: 32688372. Searched Cochrane Library, PubMed, Embase.
**Retrieved:** 2026-08-26 (abstract viewed via PubMed listing; full text paywalled)
**License note:** Journal article — only condensed facts from the freely available abstract are recorded here.

---

## Condensed facts
- 8 studies included examining compression (elastic bandage/wrap) in the treatment of acute ankle sprains.
- No difference demonstrated on major outcomes: pain reduction, swelling, ankle-joint function, or range of motion, comparing compression vs. no compression.
- No benefit shown for compression on recovery time.
- All evidence rated **low to very low certainty** — small trials, heterogeneous methods, high risk of bias.
- Clinical takeaway used in patient-facing guidance (not itself part of the review's formal conclusion, but consistent with it): an elastic wrap may still offer a subjective sense of support/comfort and help control visible swelling, but should not be relied on as sole ankle protection for weight-bearing, and should never be wrapped tightly enough to cause numbness, tingling, or cold/blue toes.
