# Cochrane Review — Fluoride Toothpastes for Preventing Dental Caries (condensed facts, not full text)

**Source:** Marinho VC, Higgins JP, Sheiham A, Logan S. Fluoride toothpastes for preventing dental caries in children and adolescents. Cochrane Database Syst Rev. 2003. PMID: 12535435.
**Retrieved:** 2026-08-26 (abstract viewed via PubMed, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12535435/)
**License note:** Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews is copyrighted. Condensed facts only from the free PubMed abstract, no full text reproduced.

## Condensed facts
- RCTs/quasi-RCTs with blinded outcome assessment, fluoride toothpaste vs. placebo, children up to age 16, ≥1 year duration. Outcome: caries increment via change in decayed/missing/filled tooth surfaces (D(M)FS).
- Searched Cochrane Oral Health Group Trials Register, CENTRAL, MEDLINE (1966–Jan 2000), plus other databases/handsearching.
- **74 studies included; 70 contributed to meta-analysis, involving 42,300 children.**
- **D(M)FS pooled prevented fraction (PF): 24% (95% CI 21–28%, p<0.0001)** — a real, precisely quantified caries-reduction effect.
- **NNT (number needed to treat): 1.6 children need to brush with fluoride toothpaste (vs. non-fluoride) for 3 years to prevent one D(M)FS in a HIGH-caries population (2.6 D(M)FS/year baseline); 3.7 children needed in a LOWER-caries population (1.1 D(M)FS/year baseline).** NNT varies meaningfully with baseline disease burden — a genuinely important nuance.
- Clear statistical heterogeneity (p<0.0001) across studies.
- Effect INCREASED with: higher baseline D(M)FS, higher fluoride concentration, higher frequency of use, and supervised brushing. Effect was NOT influenced by concurrent exposure to water fluoridation.
- Little information available on effects in the deciduous (primary) dentition or on adverse effects (fluorosis) specifically — an acknowledged evidence gap even in this large, high-quality review.
- Reviewers' conclusion: "supported by more than half a century of research, the benefits of fluoride toothpastes are firmly established" — trials of relatively high overall quality providing clear evidence of a caries-preventive effect.
