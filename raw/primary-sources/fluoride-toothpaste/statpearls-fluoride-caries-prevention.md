## Epidemiology
Dental caries is the most common disease worldwide, affecting 60–90% of the population; it is non-communicable and preventable.

## Mechanism (three pathways)
1. **Demineralization inhibition:** fluoride in plaque fluid travels with acid into the enamel subsurface, forming fluorapatite, which is much more resistant to acid dissolution than hydroxyapatite.
2. **Remineralization enhancement:** fluoride accelerates growth of fluorapatite crystals on partially damaged tooth surfaces, making them less soluble in plaque acids.
3. **Bacterial inhibition:** at low pH, fluoride penetrates bacterial cell walls as HF, disrupting enzyme activity, producing cumulative antimicrobial effects.

## Evidence-Based Concentrations
- **Community water fluoridation:** recommended 0.7 mg/L to prevent caries while limiting fluorosis risk.
- **Standard toothpaste:** 1,000–1,100 ppm.
- **High-risk children (age 7+):** 1,350–1,500 ppm.
- **Professional varnish:** 22,600 ppm (suitable for children under 6 specifically because it's professionally applied, not swallowed at volume).
- **Professional gels:** 5,000–12,300 ppm (for children 6+).

## Efficacy Rates (StatPearls-cited figures)
- Daily fluoride toothpaste vs. placebo: prevented fraction (PF) of **24.9%** in young permanent dentition
- Toothpaste with 1,500 ppm fluoride had superior preventive effect vs. standard 1,000 ppm dentifrices
- Fluoride varnish twice yearly: reduces caries by **37% in primary teeth, 43% in permanent teeth**
- Fluoride gels: reduce incidence by **26% in permanent teeth**

## Age-Based Oral Fluoride Supplementation (non-fluoridated areas, <0.3 ppm water)
- 6 months–3 years: 0.25 mg/day
- 3–6 years: 0.5 mg/day
- 6+ years: 1.0 mg/day

## Fluorosis Risk & Safety
- Children under 4: risk to permanent incisors and first molars if overexposed
- Children 4–6: risk to premolars and second molars
- Precautions: pea-sized toothpaste amount for ages 3–6; smear amount for under 3; avoid fluoride rinses under age 6 (swallowing risk)
- Fluoride varnish is the only high-concentration fluoride formulation appropriate for children under 6, specifically because it is professionally applied in controlled amounts

## Clinical Recommendations
USPSTF strongly recommends fluoride varnish application to primary teeth starting at tooth eruption. Providers should ensure supervised use of any fluoride mouthwash and coordinate with pediatric providers for early-childhood-caries prevention.
