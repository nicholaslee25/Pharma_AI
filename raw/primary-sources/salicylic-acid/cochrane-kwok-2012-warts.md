# Cochrane Review — Topical Treatments for Cutaneous Warts (condensed facts, not full text)

**Source:** Kwok CS, Gibbs S, Bennett C, Holland R, Abbott R. Topical treatments for cutaneous warts. Cochrane Database Syst Rev. 2012. PMID: 22972052.
**Retrieved:** 2026-08-26 (abstract viewed via PubMed, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22972052/)

## Condensed facts
- RCTs of topical treatments for cutaneous non-genital warts, healthy immunocompetent adults/children. Search updated to May 2011 across Cochrane Skin Register, CENTRAL, MEDLINE, EMBASE, AMED, LILACS, CINAHL.
- **85 trials, 8,815 randomized participants** (26 new studies added in this update). Many studies judged high risk of bias in ≥1 area.
- **Salicylic acid (SA) vs. placebo, all sites: RR 1.56 (95% CI 1.20–2.03) — significantly increased chance of wart clearance.**
- Pooled cure rate from 5 placebo-controlled trials (per earlier WebSearch synthesis of this same review, corroborating the RR figure): 117/160 (73%) SA vs. 78/162 (48%) control.
- **Subgroup by site: hands RR 2.67 (95% CI 1.43–5.01); feet RR 1.29 (95% CI 1.07–1.55)** — salicylic acid appears considerably more effective for hand warts than foot (plantar) warts, though both are statistically significant.
- **Cryotherapy vs. placebo, all sites: RR 1.45 (95% CI 0.65–3.23) — NOT significant, favored neither.** Subgroup: hands RR 2.63 (95% CI 0.43–15.94), feet RR 0.90 (95% CI 0.26–3.07) — also not significant either way.
- One trial showed cryotherapy better than both placebo and SA, but only for hand warts specifically.
- No significant difference in cryotherapy cure rates across 2-, 3-, or 4-weekly treatment intervals.
- **Aggressive cryotherapy more effective than gentle cryotherapy: RR 1.90 (95% CI 1.15–3.15), but with increased adverse effects** — a real efficacy/tolerability tradeoff.
- **Cryotherapy vs. SA head-to-head: no significant difference in effectiveness, RR 1.23 (95% CI 0.88–1.71)** (all sites; also no significant difference in hand/foot subgroups).
- **SA + cryotherapy COMBINED more effective than SA alone: RR 1.24 (95% CI 1.07–1.43)** (2 trials, 328 participants) — combination therapy has a real edge over salicylic acid monotherapy.
- Intralesional bleomycin benefit remains uncertain — evidence inconsistent; the most informative trial (31 participants) showed no significant difference vs. saline injection.
