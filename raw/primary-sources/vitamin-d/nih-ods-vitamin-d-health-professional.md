## Introduction / Mechanism
Fat-soluble vitamin, naturally in few foods, added to others, available as supplement; also produced endogenously via UV skin exposure. Biologically inert until two hydroxylations: liver converts to 25-hydroxyvitamin D [25(OH)D, calcidiol]; kidney converts to physiologically active 1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D [1,25(OH)2D, calcitriol]. Promotes gut calcium absorption, maintains serum calcium/phosphate for bone mineralization, prevents hypocalcemic tetany. Needed for bone growth/remodeling (osteoblasts/osteoclasts). Prevents rickets (children) and osteomalacia (adults); with calcium, helps protect against osteoporosis. Other roles: anti-inflammatory, cell growth/differentiation/apoptosis modulation, neuromuscular and immune function, glucose metabolism.

Two supplement/food forms: D2 (ergocalciferol) and D3 (cholecalciferol) — both well absorbed in the small intestine; **D3 raises and maintains serum 25(OH)D levels more effectively than D2.**

## Assessing Status
Serum 25(OH)D is the main status indicator, though deficiency thresholds aren't definitively established. FNB (Food and Nutrition Board): ≥50 nmol/L (20 ng/mL) sufficient for most people; deficiency risk increases <30 nmol/L (12 ng/mL).

## Recommended Intakes (RDA/AI)
15–20 mcg (600–800 IU) for adults; 10–15 mcg (400–600 IU) for infants/children/adolescents, varying by age.

## Sources
Food: fatty fish flesh, fish liver oils, beef liver, egg yolks, cheese; US diet mostly from fortified foods (milk, cereals). Sun exposure: varies by season/time of day/cloud cover/skin melanin. Supplements: D2 or D3, both raise serum 25(OH)D, D3 more effective/longer-lasting.

## Vitamin D Deficiency
More common in: lactose intolerance, milk allergy, ovo-vegetarian/vegan diet, limited sun exposure, impaired renal 25(OH)D-to-active-form conversion, malabsorption. Manifests as rickets (children), osteomalacia (adolescents/adults).

## Groups at Risk of Inadequacy
Breastfed infants, older adults, people with limited sun exposure, dark skin, fat-malabsorption conditions, obesity, or gastric bypass surgery history.

## Vitamin D and Health (per FNB/AHRQ review of ~250 studies, 2009–2013)
FNB and AHRQ both concluded evidence was inadequate/contradictory to establish vitamin D effects on health outcomes **other than bone health**. Per-condition summaries:

- **Bone health/osteoporosis:** vitamin D+calcium may increase BMD in some postmenopausal women/older men, but unclear if this reduces falls/fracture rates; muscle-strength/function-decline data inconsistent
- **Cancer:** some evidence vitamin D might inhibit carcinogenesis/slow tumor progression, but most research found no relationship between serum 25(OH)D and cancer risk; clinical trials generally failed to show supplementation (with or without calcium) reduces cancer incidence; adequate/higher levels MIGHT reduce cancer mortality (more research needed)
- **Cardiovascular disease:** deficiency associated with vascular dysfunction, arterial stiffening, LVH, hyperlipidemia in observational data; but clinical trials have NOT confirmed CVD risk reduction from supplementation, even in low-25(OH)D populations
- **Depression:** low 25(OH)D associated with higher depression risk observationally, but supplementation has NOT been shown to prevent or treat depression in clinical trials
- **Multiple sclerosis:** epidemiological/genetic association with low 25(OH)D; observational data suggest adequate levels might reduce MS risk/progression/relapse, but clinical trial evidence is limited and supplementation does NOT appear to mitigate signs/symptoms or reduce relapse rates
- **Type 2 diabetes:** inverse association between vitamin D status and glucose observationally, but supplementation does NOT appear to help glucose homeostasis, prediabetes progression, or diabetes management (particularly in vitamin-D-replete individuals)
- **Weight loss:** higher body weight associated with lower vitamin D status, but evidence does NOT support vitamin D supplements for promoting weight loss

**Methodological caveat (ODS's own):** most studies measured 25(OH)D using non-standardized assays, raising questions about result accuracy and meta-analysis validity.

## Health Risks from Excessive Vitamin D
Toxicity causes hypercalcemia, hypercalciuria, high serum 25(OH)D; extreme cases: renal failure, soft tissue calcification, cardiac arrhythmias, death. Almost always from excessive supplement intake (not food/sun). Combining calcium + vitamin D supplements may increase certain adverse effect risks. **Tolerable Upper Intake Level: 25–100 mcg (1,000–4,000 IU), depending on age.**

## Interactions with Medications
Vitamin D supplements may interact with: orlistat, statins, steroids, thiazide diuretics (both directions — some meds affect vitamin D levels, vitamin D may affect drug function).
