## Design
Cochrane systematic review/meta-analysis. Searched Cochrane Bone/Joint/Muscle Trauma Group registry, CENTRAL, MEDLINE, EMBASE, CINAHL, BIOSIS through Dec 2012/Jan 2013. RCTs/quasi-RCTs of vitamin D or related compounds (± calcium) vs. placebo/no intervention/calcium alone, reporting fracture outcomes in older people. Primary outcome: hip fracture.

## Scale
53 trials, 91,791 total participants. 31 trials (70–36,282 participants each) examined vitamin D (including 25-hydroxy vitamin D) ± calcium in community/nursing home/hospital populations (12 of these had mean/median age ≥80). 22 smaller trials examined calcitriol/alfacalcidol, mostly in established-osteoporosis patients in institutional/hospital settings.

## Results

**Vitamin D ALONE (no calcium) — high-quality evidence of NO benefit:**
- Hip fracture: RR 1.12 (95% CI 0.98–1.29) — 11 trials, 27,693 participants — **not effective**
- Any new fracture: RR 1.03 (95% CI 0.96–1.11) — 15 trials, 28,271 participants — **not effective**

**Vitamin D PLUS calcium — high-quality evidence of small benefit:**
- Hip fracture: RR **0.84** (95% CI 0.74–0.96, p=0.01) — 9 trials, 49,853 participants. Translates to: 1 fewer hip fracture per 1000 older adults/year in low-risk (community) populations (~8 hip fractures/1000/year baseline); 9 fewer hip fractures per 1000/year in high-risk (institutional) populations (~54/1000/year baseline)
- Any fracture: RR **0.95** (95% CI 0.90–0.99) — 10 trials, 49,976 participants
- Non-vertebral fractures: statistically significant reduction (high-quality evidence)
- Clinical vertebral fractures: moderate-quality evidence of NO significant preventive effect

**Adverse effects:**
- Mortality: NOT adversely affected by vitamin D or vitamin D+calcium — RR 0.97 (95% CI 0.93–1.01), 29 trials, 71,032 participants
- Hypercalcemia (usually mild, 2.6–2.8 mmol/L): more common with vitamin D/analogue ± calcium — RR 2.28 (95% CI 1.57–3.31), 21 trials, 17,124 participants; especially with **calcitriol** specifically — RR 4.41 (95% CI 2.14–9.09), 4 trials, 988 participants
- GI symptoms: small increased risk — RR 1.04 (95% CI 1.00–1.08), 15 trials, 47,761 participants; especially calcium+vitamin D — RR 1.05 (95% CI 1.01–1.09)
- Renal disease: significant increase — RR 1.16 (95% CI 1.02–1.33), 11 trials, 46,548 participants
- Other systematic reviews (cited, not independently verified here) found increased MI with supplemental calcium alone, and increased MI/stroke but decreased cancer with calcium+vitamin D, without an overall mortality effect

## Authors' Conclusion
Vitamin D ALONE is unlikely to prevent fractures at doses/formulations tested. Vitamin D PLUS calcium may prevent hip or any fracture. Small but significant increase in GI symptoms and renal disease with vitamin D+calcium. No increased death risk from calcium+vitamin D.

## Disclosed Conflicts of Interest
Lead author (Avenell) was a principal investigator or participant in two of the included trials (Avenell 2004, RECORD 2005) and was involved in related calcium/cardiovascular meta-analyses (Bolland 2010, Bolland 2011b) — did not personally extract data or assess quality for trials she was involved in.

## Relevance
This is the single most important efficacy finding for this page: **vitamin D supplementation alone does not prevent fractures** — a very common but not well-supported use case — and only the vitamin D+calcium combination shows a real, modest, high-quality-evidence benefit specifically for hip and overall fracture risk, with real tradeoffs (hypercalcemia, GI symptoms, renal disease risk).
