## Mechanism of Action
Expectorant via two mechanisms: relaxes bronchial smooth muscle tone, and irritates the respiratory tract to increase respiratory-tract fluid volume — both reduce mucus viscosity.

## Indications
**FDA-approved:** productive cough from the common cold; chest congestion; productive cough from allergies.
**Off-label:** chronic bronchitis; productive cough in pneumonia patients.

## Pharmacokinetics
Well absorbed in the small intestine. Mean volume of distribution 116 L. Hepatic metabolism — oxidized to β-(2-methoxyphenoxy)-lactic acid and unmethylated to hydroxy-guaifenesin (both inactive metabolites). Metabolites renally excreted.

## Adult Dosing
- Syrup: 10 mL (200 mg) q4h, max 60 mL/day
- Tablets: 1–2 tablets q12h, max 4/day
- Granules: 2–4 packets q4h, max 6 doses/24h

## Pediatric Dosing
FDA recommends against pediatric use; AAP advises avoiding under age 4. Some studies support use ≥2y in accurately-measured liquid form.

## Special Populations
- **Pregnancy:** normal OTC doses acceptable
- **Breastfeeding:** no studies on breastmilk excretion or infant effects
- **Hepatic/renal impairment:** no manufacturer dose adjustment recommended

## Adverse Effects
- Renal: acute renal failure, nephrolithiasis, flank pain, hematuria, oliguria
- GI: nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, diarrhea
- Other: dizziness, drowsiness, tachycardia, hypertension, hypersensitivity reactions

## Contraindications
Phenylketonuria (some formulations contain phenylalanine); history of kidney stones (guaifenesin increases urine supersaturation/crystallization risk); severe liver or kidney disease; age <4 years; known hypersensitivity; concomitant MAOI use.

## Monitoring
Can falsely elevate vanillylmandelic acid (VMA) lab levels.

## Drug Interactions
Medications affecting CYP enzyme metabolism (e.g., rolapitant — this is more relevant to the dextromethorphan component); medications causing respiratory depression; medications affecting heart rhythm.

## Toxicity/Overdose
No guaifenesin-specific toxicity profile separate from the combination product is given in this article. Management is symptomatic; activated charcoal may help prevent absorption.
