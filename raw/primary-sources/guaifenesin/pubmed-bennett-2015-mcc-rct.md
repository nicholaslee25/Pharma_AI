## Design
Randomized, parallel-group, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial in non-smoking adults with acute upper respiratory tract infection. Single dose Mucinex 1200mg (2×600mg extended-release guaifenesin tablets) vs. placebo. Measured: mucociliary clearance (MCC) and cough clearance (CC) via radioactive tracer particle removal from the lung; sputum rheology; interfacial tension; subjective symptoms. Morning (period 1) vs. afternoon 4h post-dose (period 2) comparison, n=19 per arm (sputum subanalysis n=8–12/cohort).

## Results
- **MCC through 60min:** placebo 8.3% (period 1) vs. 11.8% (period 2), NOT significant; drug 9.7% vs. 11.1%, NOT significant
- **Cough clearance:** placebo 9.9% vs. 9.1%, NS; drug 10.8% vs. 5.6%, NS
- No significant difference in sputum biophysical properties between drug and placebo

## Authors' Conclusion
**No significant effect** of a single 1200mg extended-release guaifenesin dose on mucociliary clearance, cough clearance, or sputum biophysical properties compared to placebo in adults with acute RTI.

## Relevance
This is a mechanistic/objective-clearance negative result specifically for guaifenesin, consistent with StatPearls' own characterization elsewhere (in its separate "Cough: Evaluation and Management" article) that guaifenesin has "weak evidence" for effectiveness. Should be weighted alongside — not overridden by — the FDA's approval of guaifenesin as an expectorant, which long predates modern RCT standards.
