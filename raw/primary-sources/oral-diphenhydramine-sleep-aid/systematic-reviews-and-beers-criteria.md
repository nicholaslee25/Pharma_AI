# Oral Diphenhydramine (Sleep Aid) — Systematic Reviews + AGS Beers Criteria

## Efficacy
- "Over-the-Counter Agents for the Treatment of Occasional Disturbed Sleep or Transient Insomnia: A Systematic Review of Efficacy and Safety" (psychiatrist.com/pcc) — commonly used OTC sleep-aid agents, especially diphenhydramine, described as lacking robust clinical evidence for efficacy/safety.
- "A Systematic Review of the Efficacy and Safety of Over-the-Counter Medications Used in Older People for the Treatment of Primary Insomnia" (PubMed 33509331) — diphenhydramine and melatonin appeared efficacious on some sleep measures with minimal adverse effects specifically in older people in the studies reviewed.
- Standard OTC dosing: diphenhydramine HCl up to 50mg at bedtime for adults (FDA-recognized safe/effective sleep-aid dose); some evidence a 100mg dose is not substantially more effective than 50mg while anticholinergic effects may increase.

## Safety — AGS Beers Criteria
2023 American Geriatrics Society Beers Criteria lists first-generation antihistamines (diphenhydramine by name) as medications to avoid for most/all older adults. Reasons: highly anticholinergic, clearance reduced with age, tolerance develops with hypnotic use, risk of confusion/dry mouth/constipation/anticholinergic toxicity. Cumulative anticholinergic exposure associated with increased risk of falls, delirium, and dementia (reported even in younger adults). Exception noted for acute treatment of severe allergic reactions, not routine sleep-aid/combination-analgesic use.
