## Mechanism of Action
Competitive H2-receptor antagonist, selectively binds H2 receptors on the basolateral membrane of gastric parietal cells, blocking histamine's effect and suppressing gastric acid secretion (volume and acidity both reduced). Inhibits basal, nocturnal, and food-stimulated acid secretion.

## Indications
**Prescription:** duodenal and gastric ulcers, GERD (adults and children), pathological hypersecretory conditions (adults).
**OTC:** prevention/treatment of heartburn related to GERD.
**Off-label:** refractory urticaria, anaphylaxis/allergies, stress ulcer prophylaxis in critically ill patients, gastritis symptoms; combined with NSAIDs to mitigate GI risk.

## Adult Dosing by Indication
- Active duodenal ulcer: 40mg PO at bedtime up to 8 weeks, OR 20mg PO/IV q12h (IV short-term only)
- Maintenance (duodenal ulcer): 20mg at bedtime
- Active gastric ulcer: 40mg once daily at bedtime up to 8 weeks, OR 20mg IV q12h (short-term)
- Nonerosive symptomatic GERD: 20mg PO twice daily up to 6 weeks
- Erosive esophagitis: 20–40mg PO twice daily up to 12 weeks, OR 20mg IV q12h (short-term)
- Hypersecretory conditions: 20–60mg PO q6h, starting at 20mg q6h
- Intractable ulcer: 20mg IV q12h

## Pediatric Dosing
1 mg/kg/day divided into 2 doses of 0.5mg/kg each. OTC strengths: 10 or 20mg, taken 10–60min before triggering foods; OTC use should not exceed 2 weeks without provider guidance.

## Pharmacokinetics
Oral bioavailability 40–45%; onset within 1h, peak 1–3h; IV peak ~30min. Antisecretory effect lasts ~10–12h. Plasma protein binding 15–20%; Vd 1.0–1.3 L/kg. Minimal first-pass metabolism, primarily via CYP1A2. Half-life 2.5–3.5h normally; **>20h in severe renal impairment (CrCl <10 mL/min)**. ~65–70% excreted unchanged in urine.

## Special Populations
- **Renal impairment:** CrCl <50 mL/min — reduce dose 50% or extend interval to 36–48h; in anuria, half-life extends to ~24h, raising CNS adverse-effect risk
- **Hepatic impairment:** no dosage adjustment per manufacturer labeling
- **Pregnancy:** crosses placenta; former FDA Category B; ASA supports use in obstetric anesthesia for aspiration prophylaxis; consider after risk-benefit assessment for GERD (lifestyle changes/antacids first-line)
- **Breastfeeding:** appears in breast milk at lower concentrations than other H2RAs, making it a potentially preferable H2RA option during lactation; available evidence suggests unlikely to harm breastfed infants
- **Elderly:** per 2023 Beers Criteria, reduce dose if CrCl <50 mL/min; avoid in elderly patients experiencing delirium

## Contraindications
Hypersensitivity to famotidine or formulation components; due to potential H2RA cross-sensitivity, avoid in patients with cimetidine hypersensitivity history. OTC formulations specifically contraindicated with difficulty swallowing, hematemesis, melena, renal impairment, or concurrent use of other acid-reducing medications.

## Boxed Warning
IV formulations containing benzyl alcohol are contraindicated in neonates and pregnant women — "gasping syndrome" risk; benzyl alcohol readily crosses placental and blood-brain barriers.

## Adverse Effects
Agitation: 14% in infants, <1% in adults. Headache 5%. Dizziness 1%. Diarrhea 2%. Constipation 1%. IV: local injection-site irritation. Pediatric use associated with increased risk of community-acquired pneumonia and acute gastroenteritis.
**Thrombocytopenia:** documented famotidine-induced cases, sometimes causing prolonged hospital stays and higher injury severity scores; mechanism involves bone marrow suppression or platelet autoantibodies. Regular CBC monitoring recommended; discontinue and transition to a PPI if thrombocytopenia develops.

## Drug Interactions
Minimal inhibitory effect on most drug metabolism, but is a mild CYP1A2 inhibitor — significantly increases tizanidine levels (a CYP1A2 substrate); avoid combination, or monitor for hypotension/bradycardia/drowsiness if necessary. Reduces absorption of (avoid coadministration): cefuroxime, dasatinib, delavirdine, neratinib, pazopanib, risedronate.

## Monitoring
Renal function (especially elderly). For GI bleeding: CBC, gastric pH, occult blood testing.

## Overdose/Toxicity
Risk increases with renal impairment — dose adjustment imperative for moderate-severe impairment. Oral doses up to 640mg/day (far exceeding FDA-approved limits) have been given for hypersecretory conditions without severe adverse outcomes in some cases. Management: eliminate unabsorbed drug from GI tract, close monitoring, supportive care.
