# Efficacy Evidence — Boric Acid for Vulvovaginal Candidiasis (condensed facts, not full text)

## Source 1: Van Slyke et al. 1981 (RCT)
Van Slyke KK, Michel VP, Rein MF. Treatment of vulvovaginal candidiasis with boric acid powder. Am J Obstet Gynecol. 1981. PMID: 7282789.
**Retrieved:** 2026-08-26 (abstract viewed via PubMed, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7282789/)
**License note:** Journal article, copyrighted. Condensed facts from the free PubMed abstract, no full text reproduced.

### Condensed facts
- Double-blind comparative trial: 14 daily intravaginal gelatin capsules of 600mg boric acid powder vs. identical capsules with 100,000 U nystatin (diluted with cornstarch), for vulvovaginal candidiasis albicans.
- **Cure rates, boric acid:** 92% at 7–10 days, 72% at 30 days.
- **Cure rates, nystatin:** 64% at 7–10 days, 50% at 30 days.
- Speed of symptom relief similar between the two.
- No untoward side effects reported; cervical cytology unaffected.
- In vitro: boric acid fungistatic, effectiveness unrelated to pH.
- Blood boron analysis: little vaginal absorption, half-life <12 hours.
- Cost/acceptance note (historical, 1981): patient acceptance better than "messy" vaginal creams; self-made capsules ~31 cents for 14, cheaper than prescription alternatives of that era.

## Source 2: Iavazzo et al. 2011 (systematic review)
Iavazzo C, Gkegkes ID, Zarkada IM, Falagas ME. Boric acid for recurrent vulvovaginal candidiasis: the clinical evidence. J Womens Health (Larchmt). 2011 Aug. PMID: 21774671.
**Retrieved:** 2026-08-26 (abstract viewed via PubMed, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21774671/)
**License note:** Journal article, copyrighted. Condensed facts from the free PubMed abstract, no full text reproduced.

### Condensed facts
- Searched PubMed/Scopus for clinical evidence on intravaginal boric acid for vulvovaginal candidiasis (VVC).
- 14 studies identified: 2 RCTs, 9 case series, 4 case reports. Boric acid compared against nystatin, terconazole, flucytosine, itraconazole, clotrimazole, ketoconazole, fluconazole, buconazole, miconazole; studied as monotherapy in 7 studies.
- **Mycologic cure rates ranged 40%–100%** across included studies (wide range — heterogeneous study quality/design).
- 4 of 9 case series reported statistically significant cure-rate outcomes.
- No included study found a statistically significant difference in RECURRENCE rates vs. comparators.
- **Adverse effects:** vaginal burning sensation (<10% of cases), watery discharge during treatment, vaginal erythema — identified across 7 studies.
- Authors' conclusion: boric acid is a safe, alternative, economical option specifically for recurrent/chronic vaginitis symptoms when conventional azole treatment fails due to non-albicans Candida species or azole-resistant strains — explicitly NOT framed as a general first-line replacement for azole antifungals.
