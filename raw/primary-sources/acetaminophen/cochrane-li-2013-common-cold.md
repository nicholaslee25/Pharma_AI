## Design
Cochrane systematic review, RCTs comparing acetaminophen to placebo/no-treatment in adults with the common cold (including acetaminophen as one ingredient of a combination therapy). Excluded studies with complicated participants. Searched CENTRAL, Ovid MEDLINE, EMBASE, CINAHL, LILACS through Feb 2013.

## Scale
4 RCTs, 758 participants. Data not pooled due to heterogeneity in design/outcomes/timepoints. 3 of 4 studies were short (4–6h) trials — sparse data on effects beyond a few hours.

## Results
- Nasal obstruction: significant improvement with acetaminophen in 2 of 4 studies
- Rhinorrhea: one study found acetaminophen superior to placebo for severity; NOT superior for sneezing/coughing in that same study
- Sore throat, malaise: NOT improved in 2 of 4 studies
- Headache/achiness: improved more with acetaminophen in 2 studies; no difference in 1 study
- **No study reported on duration of cold symptoms**
- Minor side effects (GI events, dizziness, dry mouth, somnolence, increased sweating) reported in 2 of 4 studies; one used an acetaminophen+pseudoephedrine combination

## Authors' Conclusion
Acetaminophen may help relieve nasal obstruction and rhinorrhea but does not appear to improve sore throat, malaise, sneezing, or cough. Evidence is insufficient to inform practice — 2 of 4 studies were small, allocation concealment unclear in all four. Large, well-designed trials are needed.

## Relevance
This is a notably honest/limited-evidence finding for a near-ubiquitous OTC drug — the review explicitly states current data are insufficient to guide practice for this specific indication (common cold symptom relief), even though acetaminophen's fever/pain-reducer role generally is well established via other evidence (see StatPearls raw file for FDA-approved indications and general dosing).
