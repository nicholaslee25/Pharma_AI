# Cochrane Review — Chlorhexidine Mouthrinse for Gingival Health (condensed facts, not full text)

**Source:** James P, Worthington HV, Parnell C, Harding M, Lamont T, Cheung A, Whelton H, Riley P. Chlorhexidine mouthrinse as an adjunctive treatment for gingival health. Cochrane Database Syst Rev. 2017. PMID: 28362061.
**Retrieved:** 2026-08-26 (abstract viewed via PubMed, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28362061/)
**License note:** Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews is copyrighted. Condensed facts only from the free PubMed abstract, no full text reproduced.

---

## Condensed facts
- RCTs of chlorhexidine (CHX) mouthrinse as an adjunct to mechanical oral hygiene (toothbrushing ± flossing) for ≥4 weeks, vs. mechanical hygiene alone or plus placebo/control rinse, in children and adults.
- Searched to September 2016 across Cochrane Oral Health Trials Register, CENTRAL, MEDLINE, Embase, CINAHL; no language/date restrictions.
- 51 studies, 5,345 participants total. 1 study unclear risk of bias, 50 at HIGH risk of bias — but review authors judged further research unlikely to change confidence in the gingivitis/plaque effect estimates.

### Gingivitis
- At 4–6 weeks: CHX reduced Gingival Index (GI, 0–3 scale) by **0.21** (95% CI 0.11–0.31) vs. placebo/control/no rinse (10 trials, 805 participants with MILD gingival inflammation, mean baseline GI ~1; **high-quality evidence**).
- Similar effect size at 6 months.
- Insufficient data for moderate/severe gingivitis (GI 1.1–3).
- **Authors' own characterization: this reduction, while statistically robust, was NOT considered clinically relevant** — an important nuance often lost when only the "high-quality evidence" label is quoted.

### Plaque
- At 4–6 weeks: standardized mean difference (SMD) **1.45** (95% CI 1.00–1.90) standard deviations LOWER plaque in the CHX group (12 trials, 950 participants, **high-quality evidence**) — a LARGE reduction. Similar large reduction at 6 months.

### Extrinsic tooth staining (harm)
- At 4–6 weeks: SMD **1.07** (95% CI 0.80–1.34) standard deviations HIGHER staining in the CHX group (8 trials, 415 participants, moderate-quality evidence) — a LARGE increase in staining. Similarly large increase at 7–12 weeks and 6 months.

### Calculus
- Results inconclusive.

### Concentration/frequency
- Insufficient data to show any CHX concentration or rinsing frequency (once vs. twice daily) is more effective than another.

### Other adverse effects (by number of studies reporting)
- Taste disturbance/alteration: 11 studies
- Oral mucosal effects (soreness, irritation, mild desquamation, ulceration/erosions): 13 studies
- Burning sensation/burning tongue: 9 studies

### Authors' conclusions
High-quality evidence of a gingivitis reduction that is statistically real but NOT clinically relevant in mild disease; high-quality evidence of a large plaque reduction; no evidence one concentration beats another; large, real staining increase is the tradeoff.
