## Design
Cochrane systematic review/meta-analysis, RCTs comparing a cerumenolytic ear drop/spray vs. no treatment, water/saline, an alternative liquid (oil/almond oil), or another cerumenolytic, in adults/children with obstructing or impacted ear wax. Searched Cochrane ENT Trials Register, CENTRAL, PubMed, Embase, CINAHL, Web of Science, ClinicalTrials.gov, ICTRP through March 2018. GRADE used for evidence quality.

## Scale
10 studies, 623 participants (900 ears). Interventions: oil-based (triethanolamine polypeptide, almond oil, benzocaine, chlorobutanol), water-based (docusate sodium, carbamide peroxide, phenazone, choline salicylate, urea peroxide, potassium carbonate), saline/water, no treatment. 9 of 10 studies were >15 years old. Overall risk of bias low/unclear.

## Results (primary outcome: complete ear-wax clearance)
- **Active treatment vs. no treatment** (1 study, 117 ears): 22% clearance vs. 5% after 5 days, RR 4.09 (95% CI 1.00–16.80), NNTB=8 (**low-quality evidence**)
- **Active treatment vs. water/saline** (3 studies, 213 participants/257 ears): no evidence of difference, RR 1.47 (95% CI 0.79–2.75) (**low-quality evidence**)
- **Water/saline vs. no treatment** (1 study, 76 ears): no evidence of difference, RR 4.00 (95% CI 0.91–17.62) (**low-quality evidence**)
- **Active treatment A vs. active treatment B:** no evidence any one cerumenolytic is superior to another (multiple head-to-head single studies)
- **Oil-based vs. non-oil-based:** no evidence of a difference

## Adverse Effects
Only 2 of 7 studies planning to measure this provided usable data (141 participants, 176 ears) — no significant difference between drop types. Remaining 5 studies summarized narratively: all events mild, reported in <30 participants across 7 studies. No emergencies or serious adverse effects in any of the 10 studies. Occasional reports (3 studies): dizziness, unpleasant smell, tinnitus, hearing loss — no significant between-group differences.

## Authors' Conclusion
No high-quality evidence exists to determine whether one type of cerumenolytic is more effective than another — the answer remains uncertain. A single study suggests 5 days of ear drops may increase complete wax clearance vs. no treatment at all. No evidence that saline/water alone is better or worse than commercial cerumenolytics, or better than no treatment.

## Relevance
This is a genuinely limited, mostly-null evidence base for a very commonly used, high-recommendation-share OTC product category (see Pharmacy Times context on the ingredient page) — most head-to-head and vs.-water comparisons show no significant difference, and only one small, low-quality study showed benefit over no treatment at all.
