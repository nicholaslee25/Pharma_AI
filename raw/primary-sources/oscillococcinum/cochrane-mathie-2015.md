## Product Description (per the review itself)
Oscillococcinum is a patented homeopathic medicine made from a 1% solution of wild duck heart and liver extract, then serially diluted 200 times with water and alcohol.

## Design
Systematic review/meta-analysis, RCTs of Oscillococcinum vs. placebo for prevention and/or treatment of influenza/influenza-like illness, adults or children. Searched CENTRAL, MEDLINE, AMED, Web of Science, LILACS, EMBASE through Sept 2014; also contacted the manufacturer (Boiron) for unpublished trials. No new trials found vs. the 2012 update.

## Scale
6 studies: 2 prophylaxis trials (327 adults, Russia), 4 treatment trials (1196 teenagers/adults, France/Germany). Overall trial-reporting quality was poor; many methodological aspects had unclear risk of bias.

## Results
- **Prevention:** no statistically significant difference vs. placebo — RR 0.48 (95% CI 0.17–1.34, p=0.16)
- **Treatment, 48h symptom relief** (2 "low quality" trials): absolute risk reduction 7.7% (RD 0.077, 95% CI 0.03–0.12); RR 1.86 (95% CI 1.27–2.73, p=0.001)
- **Treatment, 3 days:** smaller but still significant effect — RR 1.27 (95% CI 1.03–1.56, p=0.03)
- **Treatment, 4 days:** not significant — RR 1.11 (95% CI 0.98–1.27, p=0.10)
- **Treatment, 5 days:** not significant — RR 1.06 (95% CI 0.96–1.16, p=0.25)
- **Adverse events:** one patient (of 6 studies) reported headache attributed to Oscillococcinum; no other harms

## Authors' Conclusion
"Insufficient good evidence" to draw robust conclusions about Oscillococcinum for influenza prevention or treatment. Findings do NOT rule out a possible clinically useful treatment effect, but given the low quality of eligible studies, the evidence is "not compelling." No evidence of clinically important harm.

## Disclosed Conflicts of Interest
All three review authors are research-active in homeopathy; all were members of the International Scientific Committee for Homeopathic Investigations (ISCHI, dissolved July 2013), whose membership also included two Boiron (the manufacturer's) employees — though the review itself was drafted independently of ISCHI communications, and ISCHI never ran/sponsored Oscillococcinum research. Individual disclosures: Mathie is Research Development Adviser to the British Homeopathic Association; Frye's salary was partly funded by a Standard Homeopathic Company research grant (unrelated manufacturer, ended June 2013); Fisher is Expert Adviser to NICE on complementary/alternative medicine and Editor-in-Chief of a homeopathy journal, with some of his conference travel supported by grants from commercial interests including Oscillococcinum's manufacturer.

## Relevance / Interpretation Note for This Vault
This is a genuinely mixed, low-certainty result for an ultra-high-dilution (200CK) homeopathic product where, per standard chemistry, essentially no molecule of the original substance remains in the final product. The reviewers themselves — homeopathy researchers, several with ties to homeopathy organizations — concluded evidence is insufficient/not compelling, not that the product works. This should be read as a notably cautious result from a source with, if anything, priors favorable to homeopathy, not a validation of efficacy.
