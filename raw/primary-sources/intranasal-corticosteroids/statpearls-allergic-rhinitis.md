## First-Line Status
"A topical intranasal steroid should be the first-line treatment for AR [allergic rhinitis]." Intranasal corticosteroids are superior to antihistamines in reducing nasal inflammation and improving mucosal pathology.

## Efficacy vs. Antihistamines
More beneficial than antihistamines in relieving sneezing, rhinorrhea, and nasal pruritus/blockage.

## Specific Agents (commonly available in the US, per this article)
Beclomethasone, budesonide, fluticasone propionate, mometasone furoate, triamcinolone acetonide.

## Onset
Should be used regularly — peak effect may take multiple days to develop (not an immediate-relief product class).

## Adverse Effects
Most common: nasal irritation, followed by epistaxis. Preventable largely through proper technique — spray away from the nasal septum to minimize product contact with it.
