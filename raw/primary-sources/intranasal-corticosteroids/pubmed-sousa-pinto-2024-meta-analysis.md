## Design
Systematic review of RCTs in adults with seasonal or perennial allergic rhinitis (AR), comparing individual intranasal corticosteroids or antihistamines vs. placebo. Outcomes: Total Nasal Symptom Score, Total Ocular Symptom Score, Rhinoconjunctivitis Quality-of-Life Questionnaire. Random-effects meta-analyses per medication/outcome; GRADE evidence-certainty assessment.

## Scale
151 primary studies (most on seasonal AR); most studies at unclear/high risk of bias.

## Results
- Most assessed treatments (both perennial and seasonal AR) were more effective than placebo
- **Seasonal AR:** azelastine-fluticasone (combination), fluticasone furoate, and fluticasone propionate had the highest probability of producing moderate-large improvement in Total Nasal Symptom Score and Rhinoconjunctivitis QoL
- Azelastine-fluticasone had the highest probability of moderate-large Total Ocular Symptom Score improvement
- **Evidence certainty:** "high" in 6/46 analyses, "moderate" in 23/46, "low"/"very low" in 17/46

## Authors' Conclusion
Most intranasal medications are effective for rhinitis symptoms and quality of life, but there are relevant differences in the certainty of the underlying evidence across specific agents/outcomes.

## Relevance
Fluticasone propionate (the Flonase active ingredient) is specifically named among the top-performing agents for the primary nasal-symptom outcome in seasonal AR, with combination azelastine-fluticasone performing best overall — useful context on where plain fluticasone propionate monotherapy sits relative to combination products.
