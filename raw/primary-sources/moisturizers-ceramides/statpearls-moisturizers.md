## Composition & Types
**Occlusives:** form a physical barrier reducing water evaporation — petrolatum, mineral oil, paraffin, squalene, dimethicone. **Petrolatum reduces water loss by nearly 99%** — the single hardest number in this article.
**Humectants:** attract/bind water — glycerin, hyaluronic acid. Alpha-hydroxy acids (glycolic, lactic) promote desquamation while increasing ceramide levels.
**Emollients:** shea butter, ceramides — smooth/soften skin by filling gaps between skin cells.
**Formulations:** creams contain more occlusives; lotions primarily humectants; modern products combine multiple types.

## Mechanism
Hydrate and restore natural skin barrier function. Stratum corneum water content is 10-30% in healthy skin vs. 75-85% in stratum basale — water enables enzymatic reactions that break down corneodesmosomal connections during normal skin-cell shedding (desquamation).

## Indications
Eczema, xerosis, ichthyosis, psoriasis, atopic dermatitis. Liberal moisturizer use is a fundamental component of managing chronic atopic dermatitis.

## Clinical Evidence
Consistent moisturizer use in atopic dermatitis can reduce symptoms and flare frequency, per StatPearls, supported by RCTs (see the dedicated Cochrane reviews in this same folder for the specific quantified numbers, which show a more nuanced picture depending on TREATMENT of existing eczema vs. PREVENTION of eczema onset).

## Adverse Effects
No specific adverse effects listed in this article. Note: humectants used ALONE (without an occlusive) may actually increase transepidermal water loss rather than reduce it — an important formulation nuance.
