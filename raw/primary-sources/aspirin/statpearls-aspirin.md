## Mechanism of Action
Irreversibly inhibits COX-1 (abolishes activity) and acetylates COX-2 (altering function) — distinct from reversible-COX-inhibiting NSAIDs. Platelets cannot synthesize new COX, so aspirin's thromboxane A2 suppression gives prolonged (7–10 day, platelet-lifespan) antiplatelet effect. ~90% COX inhibition at 160–325mg doses. Redirects arachidonic acid toward the lipoxygenase pathway, generating pro-resolving mediators (aspirin-triggered lipoxins, resolvins) while increasing leukotriene production — explains aspirin-exacerbated respiratory disease (AERD).

## Indications
**Cardiovascular/cerebrovascular:** ACS, MI treatment/prophylaxis, unstable angina/NSTEMI, secondary ASCVD prevention, primary ASCVD prevention (selected populations), acute ischemic stroke, ischemic stroke prophylaxis, post-PCI.
**Inflammatory/pain:** ankylosing spondylitis, RA (off-label), fever, OA pain, acute migraine.
**Other (off-label):** colorectal cancer prevention, Kawasaki disease, SLE.

## Dosing by Indication
- **ACS:** loading 162–325mg chewed; maintenance 75–100mg/day
- **Acute ischemic stroke:** 325mg within 24–48h of onset (delay 24h if treated with IV alteplase)
- **Secondary ASCVD prevention:** 75–100mg once daily
- **Primary ASCVD prevention:** 75–100mg/day, ages 40–70 with elevated CV risk and no bleeding risk; not recommended ≥60y or with elevated bleeding risk
- **RA/inflammation:** 2–4g/day total in divided doses
- **Acute migraine:** 1000mg
- **Colorectal cancer prevention:** 160mg/day for 3 years post-surgery
- **Pain/fever (general OTC use):** 325–650mg q4–6h PRN, max 4g/day
- **DAPT:** 81mg/day with a P2Y12 inhibitor, ≥12 months minimum in ACS without high bleeding risk

## Pharmacokinetics
Liquid forms absorb faster than tablets; hydrolyzes to salicylic acid, mostly absorbed in stomach. Peak plasma 0.3–2h. Extensive albumin binding; Vd 0.16 L/kg; crosses placenta, enters breast milk. Low-dose kinetics are first-order; higher doses saturate metabolism → pseudo-zero-order kinetics, explaining the sharp rise in salicylate levels with high/repeated dosing and the narrow therapeutic-toxic margin. Renal clearance of salicylate increases with higher urinary pH (basis of alkalinization therapy for toxicity).

## Contraindications
NSAID hypersensitivity/allergy; NSAID-associated bronchospastic asthma; peptic ulcer/gastritis; inherited coagulopathies (hemophilia); acquired bleeding diatheses (dengue, yellow fever); G6PD deficiency; viral infection in children (Reye syndrome risk); severe hepatic insufficiency (generally contraindicated); severe renal impairment (CrCl <10 mL/min). Precautions: avoid in gout (even low doses reduce uric acid excretion); discontinue if tinnitus develops.

## Adverse Effects
Most common: GI irritation (gastritis to GI bleeding). Hypersensitivity ~1–2% prevalence, mild rash to angioedema/anaphylaxis. AERD (Samter triad: asthma + recurrent nasal polyps + NSAID hypersensitivity) — aspirin desensitization may improve pulmonary outcomes. Reye syndrome: rare, 30–45% mortality, encephalopathy + fatty liver degeneration; incidence has fallen substantially due to preferential acetaminophen use in children. Increased intracerebral hemorrhage risk, especially low-BMI or Asian-ethnicity patients.

## Drug Interactions
- **Methotrexate:** aspirin decreases renal MTX clearance, increases toxicity — avoid concomitant use
- **Uricosurics (probenecid, sulfinpyrazone):** salicylates reverse their uricosuric effect
- **Anticoagulants (heparin, warfarin):** increased bleeding via platelet inhibition + mucosal injury
- **Antiplatelets (clopidogrel, dipyridamole):** synergistic GI bleeding risk
- **SSRIs:** limit platelet serotonin uptake, combined with aspirin increases upper GI bleeding risk
- **Digoxin:** decreases renal digoxin clearance — monitor levels
- **Lithium:** reduces clearance, increases toxicity — monitor levels
- **Diuretics:** increased AKI risk — ensure hydration
- **Acetazolamide/carbonic anhydrase inhibitors:** may cause severe metabolic acidosis (synergistic bicarbonate lowering)
- **Systemic corticosteroids, other NSAIDs:** synergistic GI bleeding/ulceration
- **Nephrotoxic drugs (cyclosporine, tacrolimus):** increased nephrotoxicity
- **Valproate, phenytoin:** aspirin decreases protein binding, increases free drug concentration — monitor free phenytoin levels
- **Alcohol:** increased GI bleeding risk
- **ACE inhibitors:** reduced antihypertensive effect, increased AKI risk

## Special Populations
- **Renal impairment:** reduces renal prostaglandin synthesis, may worsen perfusion; caution + monitoring in mild-moderate impairment; avoid if CrCl <10 mL/min. KDIGO recommends low-dose aspirin for secondary ASCVD prevention in CKD patients with established ischemic CV disease.
- **Hepatic impairment:** use cautiously — impaired metabolism raises salicylate levels/bleeding risk; monitor LFTs during prolonged/high-dose therapy
- **Pregnancy:** low-dose aspirin in the 3rd trimester NOT associated with ductal closure (unlike NSAIDs generally). 81mg/day recommended from 12–28 weeks gestation for high preeclampsia risk (prior early-onset preeclampsia, multifetal gestation, renal disease, autoimmune disease, diabetes, chronic hypertension); may be considered for multiple moderate-risk factors. Current evidence does NOT support prophylactic low-dose aspirin for preventing early pregnancy loss, growth restriction, stillbirth, or preterm birth absent preeclampsia risk.
- **Breastfeeding:** excreted into milk, disproportionately higher with higher maternal doses; long-term high-dose maternal use can cause infant metabolic acidosis; Reye-syndrome link via breast milk unclear; use alternatives for high-dose therapy; monitor infant for bruising/bleeding with any use.
- **Pediatric/Reye syndrome:** aspirin not used in children/adolescents during or after viral illness due to Reye syndrome risk (rapid-progressing encephalopathy + acute liver failure).
- **Kawasaki disease:** standard-risk patients get IV immunoglobulin + aspirin, but evidence shows no significant advantage of medium/high-dose over low-dose aspirin, and similar coronary outcomes reported even when aspirin is omitted in the acute phase.
- **Elderly:** AGS Beers Criteria advise against initiating aspirin for primary CV prevention in older adults (bleeding risk rises with age, benefit minimal); consider deprescribing if already on it for primary prevention; remains appropriate for secondary prevention.

## Monitoring
Therapeutic salicylate concentration: 150–300 mcg/mL; toxic: >300 mcg/mL. Peak levels 1–3h post-dose; steady state 5–7 days. Routine level monitoring not required for most patients; consider baseline creatinine; measure salicylate levels with prolonged high-dose anti-inflammatory therapy (RA, JRA, Kawasaki, SLE-associated arthritis/pleurisy).

## Toxicity/Overdose
**Mild:** tinnitus, dizziness, lethargy, nausea, vomiting. **Severe:** hyperthermia, tachypnea, respiratory alkalosis, high anion-gap metabolic acidosis, hypokalemia, hypoglycemia, seizures, coma, cerebral edema; death often from cardiopulmonary failure via pulmonary edema.
**Mechanism:** high anion-gap acidosis from salicylate/lactic acid accumulation (uncoupled oxidative phosphorylation); respiratory alkalosis from direct respiratory-center stimulation; acidemia increases nonionized (lipophilic) salicylate fraction, worsening CNS penetration/symptoms.
**Management:** based on level, acid-base/volume/electrolyte status, decontamination, airway, ventilation, enhanced elimination.
- **Urinary alkalinization** (cornerstone): bicarbonate drip (150 mEq/1000mL D5W) + potassium supplementation (prevents hypokalemia-driven acidemia worsening)
- **GI decontamination:** activated charcoal and/or bowel irrigation for acute/chronic ingestions including extended-release products
- **Airway protection:** for worsening mental status, sedation, difficult airway, acute lung injury
- **Ventilation:** increase minute ventilation to avoid CO2 retention/maintain alkaline pH; during intubation maintain pH ≤7.5 with bicarbonate
- **Hemodialysis:** highly effective, especially once protein binding saturates (reduces clearance from days to hours); peritoneal dialysis ineffective. Indications: acute level ≥100 mg/dL (any symptoms), chronic level ≥40 mg/dL (any symptoms), neurotoxicity at any level, renal failure, acute pulmonary edema, cardiovascular compromise/volume overload
