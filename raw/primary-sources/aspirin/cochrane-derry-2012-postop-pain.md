# Cochrane Review Summary — Single Dose Oral Aspirin for Acute Postoperative Pain (raw extraction, condensed)

**Citation:** Derry S, Wiffen PJ, Moore RA. Single dose oral aspirin for acute postoperative pain in adults. Cochrane Database Syst Rev. 2012;(4):CD002067. PMID: 22513905. (Update of Edwards JE, et al. 2000, PMID 10796855.)

## Design
Systematic review/meta-analysis of single oral dose, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trials of aspirin for moderate-to-severe established postoperative pain in adults. Searched CENTRAL, MEDLINE, EMBASE, Oxford Pain Relief Database through January 2012 (original search to 1998).

## Scale
68 studies; doses 300–1200mg, but vast majority received 600/650mg (2409 participants, 64 studies) or 990/1000mg (380 participants, 8 studies). Only 1 new study since the prior (2000) review. Studies overwhelmingly adequate/good methodological quality.

## Primary Results (≥50% pain relief over 4–6h vs. placebo)
- **600/650mg:** NNT **4.2** (95% CI 3.9–4.8)
- **900/1000mg:** NNT **3.8** (95% CI 3.0–5.1)
- **1200mg:** NNT **2.7** (95% CI 2.0–3.8)
- Lower doses (below 600mg) were NOT significantly different from placebo
- Type of pain model (dental, gynecologic, etc.) had no significant impact on results

## Secondary Results
- Fewer participants required rescue medication with aspirin than placebo over 4–8h post-dose; by 12h, no difference
- Adverse events: not significantly different from placebo at 600/650mg; at 900/1000mg, **NNH 7.5** (95% CI 4.8–17)
- Most common AEs: dizziness, drowsiness, gastric irritation, nausea, vomiting — nearly all mild-to-moderate severity

## Authors' Conclusion
Aspirin is an effective analgesic for acute moderate-to-severe pain. Higher doses are more effective but come with more adverse events (drowsiness, gastric irritation). Pain relief achieved was "very similar milligram for milligram" to acetaminophen/paracetamol. No change to conclusions from the earlier (2000) review.
