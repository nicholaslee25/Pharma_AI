# PubMed Abstract — Clegg et al. 2006 GAIT Trial (condensed)

**Citation:** Clegg DO, et al. Glucosamine, chondroitin sulfate, and the two in combination for painful knee osteoarthritis. N Engl J Med. 2006. PMID: 16495392. Free article. ClinicalTrials.gov NCT00032890. (Glucosamine/chondroitin Arthritis Intervention Trial, GAIT — NIH-sponsored.)

## Design
Multicenter, double-blind, placebo- AND active-comparator (celecoxib)-controlled RCT. 5 arms: glucosamine 1500mg/day, chondroitin sulfate 1200mg/day, both combined, celecoxib 200mg/day, or placebo, for 24 weeks. Rescue analgesia (up to 4000mg/day acetaminophen) allowed in all arms. Stratified by baseline knee-pain severity (mild, n=1229; moderate-severe, n=354). Primary outcome: 20% decrease in knee pain from baseline to week 24.

## Scale
1,583 patients with symptomatic knee osteoarthritis; mean age 59, 64% women.

## Results
- **Placebo response rate:** 60.1%
- **Glucosamine:** +3.9 percentage points over placebo (p=0.30) — NOT significant
- **Chondroitin sulfate:** +5.3 percentage points (p=0.17) — NOT significant
- **Combined glucosamine+chondroitin:** +6.5 percentage points (p=0.09) — NOT significant (borderline)
- **Celecoxib (positive control):** +10.0 percentage points (p=0.008) — significant, confirming the trial was sensitive enough to detect a real effect when one exists
- **Subgroup — moderate-to-severe baseline pain specifically:** combined therapy response rate 79.2% vs. placebo 54.3% (p=0.002) — a SIGNIFICANT benefit in this specific subgroup
- Adverse events: mild, infrequent, evenly distributed across all groups (no safety signal)

## Authors' Conclusion
Glucosamine and chondroitin sulfate, alone or combined, did NOT effectively reduce pain in the OVERALL knee osteoarthritis population. Exploratory subgroup analysis suggests the COMBINATION may be effective specifically in patients with moderate-to-severe knee pain at baseline — flagged explicitly as an exploratory (not primary, not pre-registered as the main hypothesis) finding, so it carries less weight than a confirmed primary result.

## Relevance
This is one of the most well-known "negative" (for the primary/overall population) supplement trials in this database, notable for including a positive control (celecoxib) that DID work, ruling out "the trial just couldn't detect anything" as an explanation for the null glucosamine/chondroitin result. The one silver lining — a significant subgroup effect in moderate-to-severe pain patients specifically — is real but exploratory, and subsequent literature (per broader search context) remains genuinely split on whether this subgroup finding replicates.
